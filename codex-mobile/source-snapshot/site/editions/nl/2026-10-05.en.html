Phones locked away all school day. Cabinet wants better online protection for children

From January 1, 2027, mobile phones should also be out of sight during breaks, the cabinet advises. It is not a law. In Europe, the Netherlands wants a minimum age of 15 for social media.

On Monday the cabinet presented a strategy to better protect children online. The most visible part concerns school. From January 1, 2027, pupils in primary and secondary education are to put their phones away for the whole school day, including during breaks and in the schoolyard. The agreement is called ‘At home or in the locker’. A national agreement to keep phones out of lessons has applied since 2024; it is now being widened. According to the cabinet, many schools already go further than the lesson and keep devices out of the schoolyard too. The new line is meant to make that the same everywhere, so that pupils and parents do not have to learn a different rule for each school. NOS, NU.nl, de Volkskrant, NRC and Trouw all reported the news. It is not a law. The cabinet does not itself decide on phone use in schools, and so speaks of an urgent recommendation. State Secretary Tielen of Education is making agreements on this with the education sector, and the central government calls the start date an aim. According to de Volkskrant, secondary schools reacted sceptically. A school that already has a full ban told NOS that pupils have since spent less time in the toilets.

The phone rule is only one of about forty measures from various ministries; according to State Secretary Aerdts, fifteen are new. Digital services are to make less use of techniques that keep children online for a long time, such as endlessly scrolling videos and addictive algorithms. The cabinet wants a reliable form of age verification, without collecting an unnecessary amount of young people’s data in the process. At European level, the Netherlands is pushing for a minimum age of 15 for social media. The cabinet is also having research done into what services such as TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, YouTube, Roblox, Fortnite, Netflix and ChatGPT do to children’s wellbeing. Screen time of five to six hours a day is no longer an exception among young people, the cabinet writes. A more extensive response to European legislative proposals follows at the end of October; only then will it become clear how an age limit would work in practice. The same evening, scientists in NRC called for action against social media to protect young people’s health. Other European countries are already further ahead. France wants to ban social media for children under 15, and the European Commission is working on rules for age checks. For the Netherlands, the outcome in Brussels is therefore more important than what The Hague can arrange on its own. For schools the work begins now: lockers, agreements with parents about reachability, and exceptions for pupils who need their phone for medical reasons.

ACM: the market for school textbooks does not work. Publishers concede one point at once

Regulator ACM concludes that the market for teaching materials does not work well. Trouw summed it up as a confirmation of what schools have been saying for years; NRC wrote that the school textbook is still a disposable product. Schools can often buy a method only in combination with an online environment, and books leave the door after one year per pupil. That makes teaching materials expensive and hard to reuse, and it makes switching to another publisher difficult. A few large publishers control most of the market. A school that wants to change methods often has to buy new books, licences and teaching materials at the same time, and so pays twice.

The publishers responded the same day. According to Trouw, schools may from now on buy paper books without also having to take an online method. The FD reported that the publishers are taking action after the critical report. That removes one form of tied sales, although it is not yet clear what this saves schools per pupil. Which prices will change exactly, and from when, we could not yet find in two sources. If agreements do not work, the ACM can take further steps. For schools the main thing is that they will soon be able to choose: books only, digital only, or both.

Nobel Prize in Medicine for optogenetics: a light switch for brain cells

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine goes to three brain researchers from the United States and Germany: Karl Deisseroth (Stanford), Peter Hegemann (Humboldt University of Berlin) and Georg Nagel (University of Würzburg). They receive the prize for optogenetics, a technique with which researchers can switch nerve cells on and off with light.

It began with an alga that reacts extremely fast to light, NRC wrote. In that alga, Hegemann and Nagel discovered channelrhodopsin, a protein that opens a small channel in the cell membrane as soon as blue light falls on it. Deisseroth brought the protein into brain cells of mice. That makes it possible to establish precisely which group of cells drives which behaviour. The technique is used worldwide in research into anxiety, sleep and addiction, among other things. Physics follows on Tuesday, chemistry on Wednesday.

Spanish Prime Minister Sánchez calls early elections. The housing crisis is at the centre

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez announced early elections on Monday. All major Dutch media reported the news. The trigger is the housing shortage: rents and house prices have risen sharply in Spanish cities, and the anger over it is great. According to Trouw, Sánchez is gambling on that indignation; de Volkskrant writes that he hopes voters will punish the right for the housing crisis.

For the Netherlands, Spain is more than a holiday country. It is the fourth-largest economy in the eurozone, and a government that is caretaker for months makes European decisions slower. On top of that, France is also restless: school students’ protests got out of hand there on Monday, and investors are worried about French national debt. NRC pointed out that few things matter more than a roof over your head – a theme that also dominates politics in the Netherlands.

AkzoNobel sells Asian paint unit for €1.2 billion. Shares among the risers in a higher AEX

Paint maker AkzoNobel is selling its decorative paint activities in Southeast Asia and Australia to Japan’s Nippon Paint, for €1.2 billion. NOS, NRC, de Volkskrant and the FD reported the deal. The Amsterdam company now focuses on the planned merger with the American Axalta. The share led the AEX on Monday afternoon with a gain of more than 3 percent, according to market reports; the index closed higher at 1,123.88 points.

The sale price is high in the eyes of analysts, but the real test is the merger: it still has to get through shareholders and regulators. On the currency market the euro temporarily fell to $1.116, its lowest point since May 2025. Investors are worried about France, where the interest rate on ten-year government bonds is the highest since 2011. A weak US jobs report came in: 29,000 jobs added, against 84,000 expected.
