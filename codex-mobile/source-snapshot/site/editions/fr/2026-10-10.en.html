2027 budget: finance committee rejects the revenue section by 31 votes to 3. Debate resumes Tuesday in the Hemicycle

The finance committee rejected the revenue section by 31 votes to 3. The text returns to the Hemicycle on Tuesday in its original version, and the Lecornu government will have to look for a majority.

On Friday, Oct. 9, the National Assembly’s finance committee rejected the first part of the draft 2027 budget, the revenue section, which brings together taxes and levies. The vote was almost unanimous: 31 against, 3 in favor and 2 abstentions, according to Le Figaro, while Le Monde speaks of a broad rejection. The opposition parties lambast the government’s draft, while most of the governing camp deplores the amendments they added during three days of debate. The contribution asked of retirees and local authorities concentrated the tensions (Le Monde). The rejection does not stop the timetable: in plenary session, MPs will start again from the government’s original text, as soon as Tuesday, Oct. 13 in the Hemicycle (Le Monde, Le Figaro). For franceinfo, these three days were only a rehearsal, and the result points to long haggling for Sébastien Lecornu’s government.

This budget is meant to reduce the public deficit, so every saving and every revenue measure is negotiated with groups whose interests clash. Neither of the two parts of the text has been adopted at this stage: the only vote secured is a refusal. The points of friction go beyond the committee alone. Le Figaro ranks inheritance tax among the hottest topics of the budget debate, and Le Monde notes the anger of training providers over the retroactivity of the reform of the personal training account, the CPF. The Constitution frames this timetable: the Assembly has forty days to decide, and Parliament as a whole seventy days. What follows obeys a simple rule: if the first part of the text is rejected in the Hemicycle, the Assembly does not vote on the second, devoted to spending, and the text goes to the Senate. The government must therefore look for a majority, or find a way to do without one, in a country already going through a high-school student movement, a surge in fuel prices and the start of a presidential campaign. Friday’s vote says nothing about the balance of power in plenary session, because the text starts again from a different page: the amendments adopted in committee fall, and they will have to be tabled again or replaced. Zone Militaire adds an example of these trade-offs: the chief of the defense staff, General Mandon, calls the abolition of the child-related pension supplement “disastrous” for the armed forces. Above all he recalls that the 2027 budget will be decided on very concrete measures: pensions, resources for town halls and départements, inheritances, training. The social-democratic left, which this weekend picks its candidate for the presidential election, is also looking for resources in inheritances and tax loopholes (Le Figaro), so that Tuesday’s debate will also serve as a rehearsal for the 2027 campaign.

Trump announces massive deliveries of Russian diesel with Putin. Kyiv calls it an “investment in the war”

On Friday, after talking with Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump announced that Russia would supply “immediately” 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the American and world markets (Reuters, Le Figaro, BFM). France 24 and franceinfo speak of several million tonnes over several months. The Kremlin confirmed Russia’s willingness to supply oil and petroleum products (Le Monde). Volodymyr Zelensky sees an “investment in the war” (Reuters). According to the BBC, the price of U.S. diesel has doubled since the start of the war with Iran in February. Reuters notes that Donald Trump’s measures have not yet brought prices down, three weeks before the midterm elections. On Wall Street, the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83% to 51,654.95. The yield on the 10-year Treasury bond settled at 5.244%. WTI gained 0.15%, to $91.63, and gold 1.51%, to $4,219.9. The CAC 40 had closed at 7,803.33 points (+0.95%).

Nobel Peace Prize for Navi Pillay, former international court judge. A few hours later, Washington sanctions the ICC

The 2026 Nobel Peace Prize was awarded on Friday to South African Navanethem “Navi” Pillay, 85 (Reuters, AP, Le Monde). She sat on the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda in the 1990s and chaired a UN commission of inquiry that concluded a “genocide” was under way in Gaza, Le Monde recalls. The Quai d'Orsay hailed a figure of the fight against apartheid and an advocate for human rights (Le Figaro).

A few hours later, the United States announced sanctions against the International Criminal Court, the ICC (Reuters, AP, Le Monde, Le Figaro, RFI). Washington cites the defense of American sovereignty. The Court denounces an “attack on the rule of law” (Le Monde), and the European Union calls the sanctions “deeply regrettable.” Le Figaro notes that the Court is backed by 125 states.

Left primary: voting extended to Sunday 6 p.m. after a cyberattack on the platform

Voting in the social-democratic left’s primary, which has five candidates (Le Monde), is extended to Sunday 6 p.m. after a cyberattack that targeted the platform (Le Monde, franceinfo). The organizing committee insists that the integrity of the votes is guaranteed.

A complaint has been filed. Its chairman, Patrick Mennucci, sees “something much more organized” than an isolated act (franceinfo). Le Figaro details the programs: return of the ISF, the Zucman tax, “comprehensive” Sécurité sociale.

Society · EducationPress ✓ 3 outlets · police and justice tallies not cross-checked by a second outlet

High schoolers: the 3,000 promised substitutes raise doubts, the movement says it goes on

A day after the announcement of 3,000 substitute teachers, students and teachers remain skeptical (franceinfo). Le Figaro notes that these teachers were already in post but not assigned. Reuters reports that the students vow to continue, and franceinfo quotes the Union syndicale lycéenne (high school students’ union): the movement is not running out of steam.

According to franceinfo, 6,877 people, 86% of them minors, have been placed in police custody since the protests began, and 24 investigations target the police before the IGPN. Le Figaro points to a mobilization on Saturday, Oct. 17.
