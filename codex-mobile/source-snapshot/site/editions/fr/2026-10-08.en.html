Brent jumps 5% to around $105. Paris stocks fall and French yields climb again

Attacks on ships are multiplying in the Gulf. Bond yields are rising on both sides of the Atlantic, and the ECB is standing pat.

A barrel of Brent was up about 5% on Thursday and trading around $105, according to AFP and Reuters, amid tensions in the Middle East. Reuters cites the growing number of attacks on ships in the Gulf and the possibility that Washington could resume large-scale military operations against Iran. The Houthis have struck Saudi airports, with three dead according to Riyadh. European stock markets are down. The CAC 40 lost nearly 1% during the session (7,700.73 points at 1:50 p.m.) before closing at 7,729.69 points, down 0.51%, after −1.22% on Wednesday. U.S. WTI was up nearly 3.7% in the evening, near $91.5. Shares in OPmobility plunge about 16% after cutting their forecasts and announcing job cuts, according to Zonebourse and Les Échos. On Wall Street, futures are down about 0.5% and the yield on 10-year Treasuries stays close to its highest since 2002.

In France, borrowing rates are rising again. The 10-year OAT yield stood at about 4.90% the day before, after an intraday peak of 4.96% on Oct. 1. The governor of the Banque de France, Emmanuel Moulin, says geopolitical shocks are now turning into a financial shock. According to AFP, the ECB is standing pat for now: the economy minister and one governor consider intervention of little use, while another raises the possibility of a rate hike. In Le Monde, twenty-three economists urge elected officials to act to “stop France’s slide,” and the paper explains why the country’s situation worries the United States.

Nobel Prize in Literature goes to Canadian poet Anne Carson

The Swedish Academy on Thursday honored Canadian Anne Carson, 76, in Stockholm for a body of work it called “bold and inventive.” A poet, essayist, translator and specialist in classical studies, she draws on Greek and Roman literature to blend poetry, essay and fiction. Her verse novel “Autobiography of Red,” which reinvents the myth of Geryon, and the essay “Eros the Bittersweet,” on desire in ancient Greece, are among her best-known books. She is the 19th woman to receive the prize and succeeds Hungarian László Krasznahorkai. The prize carries 12 million Swedish kronor, about €1.07 million. She was in Iceland when the announcement was made. Her next book, “Eros the Bittersweet” (« Éros doux-amer »), is due out in French on Nov. 6 from Arche Éditeur.

Strike at TotalEnergies: fuel shipments blocked at several refineries, according to the CGT

The CGT has called a 24-hour strike at all TotalEnergies sites in France. According to franceinfo, workers have been blocking fuel shipments since dawn at several of the group’s refineries and biorefineries, including those in Normandy, Donges and La Mède, as well as Le Havre. The union is demanding jobs and pay: it denounces departures that are not replaced, heavy use of temp agency staff, repeated fixed-term contracts and subcontracting, and wages that the minimum wage (Smic) is catching up with in some subsidiaries.

Turnout figures differ. Éric Sellini, the CGT’s group coordinator, reports “more than 50% on strike” among employees on shift at these sites, while management counts “less than 2%” across all of its sites and does not dispute the blocking of shipments. At Donges, the CGT claims 60%. Management says supplies to stations and customers are being maintained. On the station side, 27 stations of the Argedis subsidiary are said to be closed according to the CGT, 11 according to management, out of about 180. The movement comes the day after the Prime Minister announced the release of diesel stocks.

“Act IV” of the high school movement: 375 schools closed in whole or in part. Police interventions under scrutiny

A fourth major day of blockades and demonstrations is under way across the country on Thursday. At 8 a.m., 375 schools were partly or fully closed, including 224 fully and 151 in part, according to preliminary figures from the Education Ministry relayed by Les Échos. Franceinfo notes that the movement is spreading to new schools and to the overseas territories. Le Monde describes Sébastien Lecornu as “overwhelmed by the pile-up of crises,” from the high school movement to falling purchasing power, and asks about the “questions raised by two weeks of police interventions.”

Investigations are multiplying. An investigation for assault has been opened in Marseille after a high school student suffered a head injury, along with a disciplinary inquiry in Dunkirk into a police “rodeo” in the middle of students (Le Figaro). In Orléans, three minors are in police custody for glorifying terrorism after a video filmed in a school (Le Monde). Launcher-fired stun grenades remain provisionally suspended. On Thursday evening, Édouard Geffray announced the arrival of 3,000 substitute teachers (franceinfo, live), while a poll relayed by franceinfo indicates that one in two French people supports the movement.

Drought: agriculture minister announces €2 billion in zero-rate loans

Annie Genevard on Thursday presented new aid for farmers hit by drought and heat waves at the Livestock Summit in Cournon-d’Auvergne. The zero-rate loans, meant in particular for buying fodder and farm inputs, can cover an outstanding total of €2 billion: banks lend at about 2.5% and the state covers the interest. A rescheduling scheme is also planned for recently established farmers, and an envelope of €235 million is being made available to prefects for the hardest-hit farms.

The mechanism will go through an amendment to the 2027 budget, so it depends on a vote in Parliament. The announcements extend the emergency plan of more than €1 billion presented in September; the minister specifies that it is “not a full and final settlement.” Le Monde sees it as an attempt to defuse the social crisis in the farming world, as the Confédération paysanne has called on members to join the high school students.
