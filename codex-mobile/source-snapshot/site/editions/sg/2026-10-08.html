The STI falls 3.5%, its second big drop in two days. DBS, OCBC and UOB lead a rout driven by inflation and rate fears

Oil's climb and signs of more US rate rises unsettled markets across Asia. Singapore's index has lost about 5 per cent since Tuesday's close.

Singapore shares had their worst day in a long while on Thursday. The Straits Times Index closed at 5,412.96, down 3.49 per cent, and the Business Times reported that the three local banks led the fall. It came a day after a 1.6 per cent drop led by OCBC and UOB, so the index has now lost close to 5 per cent in two sessions. The selling began at the open: BusinessToday reported that the STI fell 1.22 per cent to 5,539.75 in the first minutes, with DBS down 2.32 per cent at S$75.69. By late morning the Business Times said a rout in the banks and other blue chips had cut more than 2 per cent from the index and wiped billions of dollars off their value.

The Business Times put the cause plainly: inflation and interest-rate concerns spooked investors. Two things fed them overnight. The minutes of the US Federal Reserve's September meeting, released early on Thursday Singapore time, showed that most officials expected another rate rise to be appropriate by the end of the year. And oil prices rose again. The Business Times reported that oil climbed on concern over Middle East supply and on a report that President Trump may order strikes on Iran before the US midterm elections, and that attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz had reached their highest weekly level since the Iran war began.

The pressure was regional. The Business Times reported that Japanese and South Korean shares fell as Asian stocks dropped on inflation fears, and CNA said Asian bonds were swamped by a wave of AI-related debt. In Europe, bank shares fell to a three-month low. Gold, which usually gains in times of stress, was near a nine-week low because higher interest rates make it less attractive. In the evening, Wall Street opened lower as the jump in oil and high bond yields fed inflation concerns, the Business Times reported.

Why the banks? They are the largest stocks in the STI, and they are the most exposed to a change in the interest-rate outlook. Higher rates can lift their lending margins, but a sharp rise in borrowing costs raises the risk that companies and households fall behind on loans, and it cuts the value of bonds that banks hold. Foreign funds that own large stakes in the banks can sell them quickly when they reduce their exposure to Asia.

There was no sign of trouble inside any of the banks themselves; the moves tracked global markets. For retail investors, two days like this test nerves more than fundamentals. DailyDrop will watch whether foreign selling continues on Friday, what the next US inflation figures show, and how MAS describes the risks in its policy statement later this month.

LTA proposes one COE category for cars, with rebates and surcharges of up to S$15,000 based on a model's value

The Land Transport Authority proposed on Thursday to merge COE Categories A and B into a single category for cars, CNA, the Straits Times and the Business Times reported. The price paid for each car would then be adjusted by a rebate or a surcharge, depending on the median open market value of the model over a recent period, such as the past year.

The adjustment would be capped at S$15,000 a car, so the gap between a mass-market car and a higher-end one would be at most S$30,000. LTA set out two options: three bands (a S$15,000 rebate, no adjustment, a S$15,000 surcharge) or five bands, with S$7,500 steps on the inner bands. It is also asking whether the system should apply to COE renewals and how the Open Category should change.

The review was prompted by premiums for smaller cars in Category A at times exceeding those for larger cars in Category B, which defeats the purpose of having two categories. Higher-end electric cars that now qualify for Category A could pay more under the plan. The public consultation runs until 2 November. Whether COE prices fall overall is another matter: the number of certificates, not how they are grouped, sets the supply.

Open market valueCars

The value of a car assessed by Customs, based on its purchase price, freight and other costs before taxes. In Singapore it also sets the Additional Registration Fee, and under LTA's proposal it would decide each model's COE rebate or surcharge.

Full-time national serviceman dies after being found unconscious on Pulau Tekong

A full-time national serviceman died after he was found unconscious at a building at Basic Military Training Centre School 3 on Pulau Tekong, the Straits Times and CNA reported on Thursday. The reports we checked did not give the cause of death or the circumstances in which he was found.

Deaths during national service lead to an investigation and, in many cases, a Committee of Inquiry. Training safety is also a question this week because of the haze and heat. DailyDrop will report the findings when they are published and offers its condolences to the family.

Sport · EnvironmentPress ✓ 3 outlets

FIA warns Grand Prix entertainment may be curbed if the haze worsens, and declares a heat hazard

The FIA said entertainment at the Singapore Grand Prix may be curbed if the haze worsens, the Straits Times, CNA and the Business Times reported on Thursday. The Straits Times also reported that the FIA has declared a heat hazard for the race, which allows measures to protect drivers in extreme heat.

There was better news in the evening: the Straits Times reported that hazy conditions may ease on Friday, 9 October. The race weekend brings visitors and spending to the city, and organisers will be watching the PSI as closely as the weather forecast.

Revolut will invest S$350 million in Singapore and grow its staff here to 300

Digital bank Revolut will open a new office in the central business district at Collyer Quay, invest S$350 million in its Singapore expansion and grow its headcount here to 300, the Straits Times and the Business Times reported on Thursday.

The announcement shows that Singapore remains a regional base for global financial technology firms even as the market turns rough. It also adds competition for the local banks in payments and foreign-exchange services, the areas where Revolut has built its business with travellers and expatriates.
