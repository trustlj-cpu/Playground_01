Haze persists: 38 of 68 monitoring stations unhealthy on Thursday morning, with a slight easing by evening

Johor was worst hit, with every one of its eight stations in the unhealthy band and Segamat the highest in the country.

Thirty-eight of the country’s 68 air-quality stations recorded unhealthy readings at 9am on Thursday 8 October, led by Segamat in Johor at 187, Malay Mail reported from the Department of Environment’s portal. All eight monitored locations in Johor were unhealthy. The Star’s evening update at 7pm carried a headline saying there was a slight improvement, with fewer areas recording unhealthy readings, but DailyDrop did not see the count behind it.

The Air Pollutant Index treats 101 to 200 as unhealthy and above 200 as very unhealthy. Under the Education Ministry guidance reported in the coverage, outdoor activities should be suspended above 100 and schools in areas above 200 should close. None of the stations was in the very unhealthy band in the reports DailyDrop saw. The smoke comes from fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan; the Star’s regional roundup said Jakarta was struggling to contain them.

KLCI eases 10.77 points to 1,601.01 as oil and Budget jitters outweigh optimism

The FBM KLCI closed at 1,601.01 on Thursday, down 10.77 points or 0.67% from 1,611.78, Bernama reported, and Business Today and Malay Mail agreed on the figures. An analyst said Brent rose more than 3% on growing concerns over a possible escalation between the United States and Iran, and that foreign investors had been net sellers for ten consecutive sessions.

Apex Securities said Brent December futures jumped past US$103 overnight and expected the market to stay volatile, with Budget 2027 as the next key event, The Star and Business Today reported on Friday morning. Bernama quoted the ringgit at 4.0890/0940 to the dollar at Thursday’s close, according to its report on Friday. In two sessions the index has lost about 2% from Tuesday’s 1,633.35.

Anwar inspects final preparations for Budget 2027; cost of living and investment are the stated themes

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim inspected the final preparations for Budget 2027 on Thursday, The Star reported in a headline saying he plans to strengthen the economy and aid the rakyat. DailyDrop has the headline and not the full text, so it does not give detail. In August, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said the Budget would focus on cost-of-living issues and attracting investment, according to Malay Mail.

On 22 September Business Today reported that higher oil prices would not disrupt the Budget’s development plans. Budget 2027 will be tabled at 3.30pm today. The Supply Bill is expected to follow the 13th Malaysia Plan, the Madani Economy framework and the National Semiconductor Strategy, according to pre-sitting coverage. Today’s speech will confirm what was in the plan.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 3 outlets · closes from market data; cause attributed to analysts

WTI jumps 3.6% to US$91.50 and Brent tops US$103 as analysts point to US-Iran escalation risk

US crude settled at US$91.50 on Thursday, up 3.60%, and Brent December futures rose past US$103 overnight, according to market data and Apex Securities as quoted by The Star and Business Today. An analyst cited by Bernama said Brent rose more than 3% on growing concerns over a possible escalation between the United States and Iran. DailyDrop did not find a wire report explaining the specific trigger, so the cause is analysts’ view only.

The S&P 500 fell 0.47% to 7,765.36 and gold rose 0.20% to US$4,157.0. For Malaysia, higher oil lifts Petronas and state revenue but also raises costs for airlines, plantations and fuel subsidies, and it was cited as a reason for the KLCI’s fall.

Environment · JohorPress ✓ 3 outlets · readings per NST and Malay Mail

Johor’s schools stay shut for a second day as Segamat reaches 189 and flights are delayed at Senai

All of Johor’s 1,198 schools and 617,258 students were out of class on Thursday, the first of two days under the regent’s decree, NST and Malay Mail reported. NST said the haze worsened in Johor on Thursday, with Segamat at 189 by 4pm, up from 179 at 8pm on Wednesday, and all eight state stations recording unhealthy readings. Five flights were delayed at Senai because of poor visibility.

Malay Mail’s 9am count put Segamat at 187, so the reading peaked later in the day. The closure runs to Friday 9 October. DailyDrop did not find an announcement on whether it will be extended or whether other states will close schools, and it will not guess.
