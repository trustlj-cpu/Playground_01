Tuesday’s Dewan Rakyat agenda: the RM2,000 minimum wage, Myanmar refugee registration and Gaza

Commentators spent the day weighing what the June 2027 wage floor means for small firms. DailyDrop saw previews of the sitting, not the replies given in the House.

The Dewan Rakyat, the lower house of Parliament, was due on Tuesday to discuss how the RM2,000 minimum wage will be put into effect from June 2027, how Myanmar refugees are to be registered, and what can be done about the humanitarian crisis in Gaza, the New Straits Times and Malay Mail reported on the morning of the sitting. Both pieces were previews of the day’s questions. DailyDrop did not see a report of what ministers answered, so this edition does not describe the replies. The wage floor comes from Budget 2027, which Malay Mail says carries an allocation of RM510 billion. Malay Mail also quoted Johan Merican as saying that a higher minimum wage is not a silver bullet but is crucial for income growth; DailyDrop saw that headline and the opening of the article, not the full remarks.

Berita Harian reported that micro, small and medium enterprises are still expected to feel pressure to raise pay and to compete for workers, even though the sector has been given an exemption from the increase. The agenda reached beyond the Budget. On refugees, the Straits Times reported that Johor police are investigating a Molotov cocktail attack on a Rohingya settlement, which started a minor fire at a house belonging to Rohingya residents, according to the Johor police chief, Ab Rahaman Arsad; no second outlet carried it in what DailyDrop saw. On Gaza, Antara quoted Foreign Minister Mohamad Hasan as saying Malaysia is tired of the use of vetoes on the Palestine issue at UN forums and wants reform. It was not clear whether he said this in the House.

Haze leaves 19 areas unhealthy and Pasir Gudang worst at 180; Johor, Melaka and Negeri Sembilan schools shut again today

Nineteen areas recorded unhealthy Air Pollutant Index (API) readings at 8am on Tuesday, with Pasir Gudang in Johor the highest at 180, the NST and Free Malaysia Today reported. Malay Mail’s later update counted 20 stations in the unhealthy band and put Pasir Gudang at 181, the worst in the country. The Klang Valley was moderate, though FMT said Putrajaya, Johan Setia and Klang were close to unhealthy, and Sabah and Sarawak were hit again. The Straits Times reported that schools in Johor, Melaka, Negeri Sembilan and several other districts would close on Wednesday 14 October; DailyDrop saw that outlet’s headline only, so check your state education notice. A parents’ group told FMT that more than 70% of Johor primary pupils did not follow online lessons, citing working parents and a lack of devices. Firefly extended its Subang–Seletar cancellations to 14 October (Berita Harian, CNA). Mohamad Hasan said Malaysia will raise cross-border haze at an Asean foreign ministers’ meeting because the bloc’s disaster mechanism does not cover it (CNA, Malay Mail), and Berita Harian reported that Indonesia will not declare its fires a national disaster for now.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 8 outlets · Aramco hit reported by unnamed sources only

Missile reported to hit Aramco’s headquarters as Saudi forces strike a fuel depot near Sanaa and oil holds above US$105

The Saudi–Houthi fighting escalated on Tuesday. A Houthi missile hit the headquarters of Saudi Aramco in Dhahran and started a fire in part of the complex, Bloomberg reported, citing people with knowledge of the matter. Reuters, citing six sources, reported loud booms near the headquarters, which had been evacuated. DailyDrop saw no statement from Saudi Arabia or Aramco, so both reports rest on unnamed sources. Earlier in the day the Saudi-led coalition told civilians to leave the Sabah central station, a fuel depot of the Yemen Petroleum Company on the outskirts of Sanaa, and then struck it (Reuters, the Guardian, the BBC). The BBC said the Houthis vowed to retaliate against Riyadh and Dammam, and Al Jazeera said they threatened Aramco facilities and airports. President Trump said the Houthis were “behaving very badly” and, according to Reuters and Al Jazeera, that they were trying to push oil prices up before the US midterm elections.

The airport attacks earlier in the week still shape travel. The NST put the toll at 18 in a headline; earlier DailyDrop editions carried 12 dead in the first strike and one in the second, so treat 18 as an unreconciled figure from one outlet. Reuters said Saudi Arabia’s warning system stayed silent before the strikes. Air India, IndiGo and other Indian airlines cancelled Saudi flights until 17 October (Hindustan Times), and Singapore’s foreign ministry told citizens to defer travel to the kingdom (CNA, Straits Times). Oil moved with the news: the NST said it climbed above US$106 on Tuesday morning, and the FinancialJuice feed put Brent’s settlement at US$105.69, down 0.9%, and WTI at US$94.14, up 0.43%; that is one source, so treat the settlements as unconfirmed.

Economy · Budget 2027Press ✓ 4 outlets · official and analyst views attributed

RM510 billion Budget needs no new taxes if leakages are cut, says Amir Hamzah, as analysts weigh its pull with voters

Amir Hamzah said the government is confident it can spend the RM510 billion in Budget 2027 without new taxes by cutting leakages, Malay Mail reported. BMI kept its forecast that Malaysia’s fiscal deficit will narrow to 3.4 per cent, Malay Mail also reported; DailyDrop saw the opening of that article only, so it cannot say what the figure is measured against. Berita Harian carried economists calling the Budget smart and balanced between people’s needs and fiscal discipline, while saying structural reform is still needed. The NST reported that the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission and analysts expect the Budget to strengthen the digital economy through connectivity, artificial intelligence and cybersecurity.

On voters, FMT quoted analysts saying the Budget’s benefits are no guarantee of votes for Pakatan, because the cost of living, the economy and public perception of the government will count for more before the next general election. FMT’s market report said the ringgit edged higher on Budget optimism, quoted at 4.0815/4.0900 to the dollar at the open, with trading expected to stay in a narrow range. In Johor, the NST reported that the masterplan and blueprint for the Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone are due to be unveiled in December, and that this and the RTS cross-border rail link are lifting investor confidence in the state’s property market.

Chip rout drags Asia and Wall Street ends Tuesday slightly lower; Bursa opened down at 1,611.80

Chip stocks in Asia fell for a second day. South Korea’s Kospi sank more than 5% on a sell-off in semiconductor shares over worries about the AI boom, AP reported; Reuters said Asian stocks dived on AI jitters, and Bloomberg tied the slide to a breakthrough by China’s memory-chip maker CXMT. On Bursa Malaysia, FMT said the main index opened lower on Tuesday at 1,611.80, as geopolitical risks and oil weighed on sentiment. That compares with Monday’s close of 1,618.01, reported in DailyDrop’s previous edition. DailyDrop could not confirm Tuesday’s closing level of the FBM KLCI or of the ringgit from two sources, so both ears show a dash. FMT said the dollar stayed near an 18-month high.

The latest completed U.S. session was Tuesday’s. Wall Street ended slightly lower on a quiet day, pulled down by technology shares while banks held up, Valor Econômico and De Telegraaf reported. DailyDrop could not confirm index closing levels from two sources, so it gives none. Reuters said Goldman Sachs and Citigroup beat profit estimates and Wells Fargo’s profit rose, while JPMorgan posted a rare profit miss. After the close, the Financial Times and De Telegraaf reported that Bank of America was fined US$1.2 billion over money-laundering failings. The International Monetary Fund forecast global growth of 3.1% for 2026 and 3.4% for 2027 and said AI investment is propping up growth while war and debt threaten it (Straits Times, CNA); Reuters and Bloomberg reported that it also flagged an AI market correction and surging bond yields as risks. Fed officials Collins and Williams each said another rate hike this year is possible or appropriate (Reuters, Bloomberg). DailyDrop will not forecast the direction.
