Aaron-Wooi Yik start their LA 2028 push with a winning reunion at the Denmark Open

The pair cleared the first round after getting back together, and Berita Harian reported Pang Ron-Pei Jing into round two. DailyDrop saw headlines only, so no scores or opponents are given.

Bukit Aman to send a team to Japan after five Malaysians were held over 5kg of cocaine hidden in shoe insoles

Bukit Aman, the police headquarters, said it would send officers to Japan to look into the case, the Malay Mail, the NST and FMT reported on Wednesday. The five Malaysians were held at a Tokyo airport, and the NST and Malay Mail said the cocaine, about 5kg, was hidden in shoe insoles. Our last edition carried the arrests; the new fact is the investigation team. The NST also ran two features of its own, one on how social media and disappearing messages help recruit drug mules and one on international rings eyeing other airports as KLIA tightens its checks. Those are the paper’s reporting, not a police statement, and DailyDrop could not test them.

Politics · EconomyPress ✓ 3 outlets · minister not named in the headlines

Anti-rent-seeking law now targeted for 2027, and “Ali Baba” is not yet a crime, the economy minister says

The government is aiming to bring in an anti-rent-seeking law next year, Malaysiakini, the Malay Mail and Free Malaysia Today reported on Wednesday. Malaysiakini’s headline attributed it to the economy minister, said the law had been pushed back to 2027, and added that “Ali Baba” arrangements are not yet a crime. FMT wrote that an “anti-Ali Baba” bill would be tabled next year. DailyDrop saw the headlines only, so it does not know why the date moved or exactly what the bill would penalise.

“Ali Baba” is local shorthand for a deal in which the holder of a licence or contract lends the name to someone else, who then runs the business and shares the profit. The subject was already on the Dewan Rakyat’s agenda: the NST and the Malay Mail both listed Alibaba licences among Wednesday’s focus. Rent-seeking, in economics, means earning money from access, licences or connections rather than from making or selling something. The minister’s remark, as headlined, is that the arrangement is not yet an offence in itself.

Nation · HazePress ✓ 5 outlets · counts differ by outlet

Haze: 27 stations unhealthy across the Peninsula, Johor worst hit, as Jakarta mulls Malaysia’s offer of help

Unhealthy air persisted on Wednesday. The New Straits Times counted 27 monitoring stations with unhealthy readings on the Air Pollutant Index (API) across Peninsular Malaysia. The Malay Mail reported an API of 177 at Pasir Gudang in Johor with readings rising in the Klang Valley, and FMT likewise said Johor remained the worst hit. An API from 101 to 200 is in the unhealthy band, so 177 is unhealthy but below the 180 level above which, SAYS reported on 9 October, schools must close. Earlier editions counted areas rather than stations, so the numbers are not a like-for-like trend.

On the cost, the Malay Mail quoted experts saying the haze could cost the economy as much as RM1.5 billion a month as business and health bills mount; that is a projection from one outlet, not a measured loss. On the cross-border row, the NST reported that Jakarta was considering Malaysia’s offer of help against the fires, and FMT reported that residents of fire-ravaged West Kalimantan had sued their state government for clean air. AFP Fact Check warned that AI-generated images exaggerate the scale of the Indonesian wildfires.

Markets · ClosePress ✓ 4 outlets · local closes and U.S. index levels not confirmed

Hotter US inflation lifts Fed-hike bets and sinks chip stocks; Bursa and the ringgit opened firmer but their closes are not confirmed

Bursa Malaysia opened higher on Wednesday on bargain-hunting and what the NST and FMT called resilient domestic fundamentals, and both outlets said the ringgit opened marginally higher against the US dollar on a weaker dollar index. DailyDrop did not find the FBM KLCI’s or the ringgit’s closing level in two sources, so both ears show a dash. In Singapore, the NST reported, the central bank kept monetary policy unchanged.

The latest completed U.S. session was Wednesday’s. US consumer prices rose 3.7% in September from a year earlier, according to the Financial Times headline and a market-data feed, FinancialJuice, which gave a forecast of 3.6% and a previous reading of 3.4% and put core inflation at 2.6%. Reuters said prices accelerated on costlier gasoline, and Bloomberg called it the fastest pace in five months. Traders added to bets on Federal Reserve rate rises (Bloomberg, FinancialJuice), Reuters said a rate-hike call looks tight, Citigroup moved its forecast to a December hike (FinancialJuice), and Bloomberg said the dollar reached its highest since May 2025. On Wall Street, Bloomberg reported that the S&P 500 ended its longest stretch of calm since 2020 as AI shares sank, the FT said chip stocks slid on inflation and AI-debt worries, and Bank of America fell on a US$1.2 billion fine (Bloomberg). DailyDrop did not see the index closing levels and gives none. FinancialJuice, a single feed, put the Brent settle at US$106.06 and WTI at US$95.09. DailyDrop will not forecast the direction.
