Haze rebounds in 22 areas as Johor, Melaka, Negeri Sembilan and Putrajaya schools close again on Tuesday

Kuala Lumpur and Jakarta traded words over who should do more about the fires, while the education minister defended deciding school closures day by day.

Haze returned to 22 areas of Malaysia on Monday, with Johor the worst hit, Malay Mail and the Straits Times reported; Free Malaysia Today counted 23 areas at a different hour. The New Straits Times said Kota Tinggi in Johor recorded the highest Air Pollutant Index (API) reading, 170, on Monday morning, which falls in the unhealthy band of 101 to 200. The readings rose just as schools in several states were due to reopen after the weekend. By Monday afternoon the Straits Times reported that schools in Johor, Melaka, Negeri Sembilan and Putrajaya would close again on Tuesday 13 October. DailyDrop saw that list in that outlet’s headline only, so parents should check the notice from their state education department before leaving home.

Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek said schools cannot be shut for days at a time while the haze fluctuates, the NST reported, and CNA reported her defending decisions taken day by day. The Johor education department said its daily closure notices are routine updates that do not conflict with the state haze order, Malay Mail reported. Under the ministry’s rule, reported by SAYS on 9 October, schools in areas with an API above 180 must close. The row centres on fires in Indonesia, and Indonesian officials have pushed back on criticism from Malaysia and Singapore: an official’s remark that “we send oxygen when there’s no smoke” was reported by Malay Mail, and the South China Morning Post said Malaysians mocked it. The Straits Times reported that President Prabowo ordered tougher action on the fires and that foreign offers of help had not been discussed; an Antara headline said Malaysia’s offer was still being discussed. Singapore, where air was also unhealthy, rolled out haze subsidies, the SCMP reported.

Genting shares slump to 2007 lows after a rights issue plan, even as the KLCI edges up

Genting Berhad shares fell sharply on Monday, three days after the group proposed a rights issue to raise about RM1.25 billion, to repay debt and add working capital. Under a rights issue a company offers new shares to existing shareholders, usually at a discount, so the share count rises. The Edge’s headline put the fall at 17% to 2007 lows, the Business Times called it the biggest drop since 1998, and BusinessToday listed Genting among the day’s biggest Bursa losers. The Star’s market wrap carried different percentages for Genting Berhad and Genting Malaysia, and DailyDrop could not reconcile them, so no single figure is given here. The filing said the issue price would be set at a 25% to 30% discount to the theoretical ex-rights price, and major shareholders have promised to take up their share.

Airlines cancel Riyadh flights for weeks and Malaysia tells umrah pilgrims to postpone after the airport attacks

The aftermath of the Houthi strikes on Riyadh’s airport shaped travel plans on Monday. CNA reported that the airport extended its suspension of flights, and Reuters said airlines cancelled services to Saudi Arabia. The NST reported that the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) warned American airlines over Saudi flights, the Inquirer said Philippine Airlines cancelled Riyadh flights until 29 November, and CNBC reported that Delta delayed the debut of its Saudi service until March. The NST reported one person dead and 11 wounded in the Sunday attack, and an Indian national died, the embassy said. AP reported that the Saudis were investigating how air defences failed to stop the attack.

For Malaysians, CNA and the Straits Times reported that the government urged citizens to postpone umrah trips; DailyDrop saw those two headlines but not the full advisory, so it does not describe the wording. Indonesia called for vigilance, CNA said, and Indonesian outlets reported pilgrim groups delaying departures. Separately, DW published a fact-check saying viral videos were falsely linked to the Riyadh attacks. Bloomberg reported that the Saudis said new missiles were intercepted.

Budget 2027 debate opens in the Dewan Rakyat as analysts weigh the cost of the RM2,000 minimum wage

The Dewan Rakyat, the lower house of Parliament, began debating Budget 2027 on Monday, with the gap in access to healthcare among the day’s topics, Malay Mail reported. The NST carried a report that the RM69 billion education allocation is meant to close an infrastructure gap and lift welfare, and another saying plantation earnings face a 7% hit from the RM2,000 minimum wage. Another NST report said emoluments and pensions drive higher government operating spending. The Health Ministry approved 400 more ambulances, the NST reported.

Markets leaned positive. The NST and FMT reported that the ringgit opened higher on the Budget’s pledge of fiscal discipline, and FMT said Bursa opened higher on budget sentiment. The SCMP wrote that the Budget tackles graft but that critics ask whether it is too late. The Malaysian Dental Association urged that contract dentists not be left behind (Malay Mail). DailyDrop did not find an opposition reply in the reports it saw.

KLCI closes 0.59% higher on bank buying while Genting sinks; Wall Street’s Monday close is still to come

The FBM KLCI closed at 1,618.01 on Monday, up 9.56 points or 0.59% from Friday’s 1,608.45, according to The Star and Free Malaysia Today, helped by bargain-hunting in banking shares and optimism over Budget 2027. Decliners outnumbered gainers 778 to 344, The Star reported, with Genting among the heaviest losers. The ringgit opened higher against the US dollar (NST, FMT), but DailyDrop could not confirm its Monday closing rate from two sources, so the ringgit ear shows a dash.

Monday’s U.S. session was still trading when this edition was written and closes after it, so the latest completed Wall Street figures are Friday’s: the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq Composite 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average 0.83% to 51,654.95. WTI crude settled on Friday at US$91.85 (+0.39%) and gold at US$4,216.3 (+1.43%). During Monday’s U.S. session a market-data feed showed WTI near US$93.7, about 2% higher; that is an intraday quote, not a close. The NST reported oil climbing in Asian trade on the Middle East conflict and tanker attacks, and Reuters said the dollar stayed near an 18-month high. DailyDrop will not forecast the direction.
