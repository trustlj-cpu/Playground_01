Budget 2027 lifts the minimum wage to RM2,000 and the personal tax relief to RM12,000

CIMB Securities called the tax relief and wage rise larger than expected, and warned of higher costs for business. Policy debate on the Budget begins in Parliament today.

Budget 2027, tabled on Friday 9 October, raises the national minimum wage from RM1,700 to RM2,000 a month from June 2027, and lifts the individual tax relief from RM9,000 to RM12,000, the first revision since 2010, according to reports on the Budget speech. The government says the higher relief, together with lower tax rates in some income bands, could leave about five million taxpayers with up to RM1,600 more in disposable income. Free Malaysia Today carried a broking firm’s analysis repeating that figure. More than four million workers are expected to gain from the wage rise. One report said firms with annual sales below RM50 million are exempt from the new rate; DailyDrop saw that exemption in that report only.

CIMB Securities said on Sunday that the Budget delivered larger-than-expected tax relief and a higher minimum wage, which supports household spending but raises cost pressures for businesses, the New Straits Times reported. Cash aid under the Sumbangan Tunai Rahmah (STR) and Sumbangan Asas Rahmah (SARA) schemes rises to RM16 billion, reports said. HRD Corp will use RM3 billion of its levy funds to support three million training places. Political analyst James Chin called the Budget a bid to win votes before GE16, while ministers welcomed the focus on living costs. The total is reported two ways: Bernama and Sinar Daily cite RM459.8 billion for the federal budget, up 3.6%, while The Star’s Saturday front page called it a record RM510 billion. DailyDrop has not reconciled the two, and did not find an opposition statement.

Haze keeps every Johor school shut until 16 October while other states reopen today

Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek said all schools in Johor, and those under four district education offices in Selangor, would stay closed on Monday because the haze remained hazardous, the New Straits Times reported on Sunday evening. Johor’s schools will stay shut until 16 October, and the Special School Admission Assessment (PKSK) planned for Monday has been postponed. Schools under the Education Ministry in Melaka, Negeri Sembilan, Pahang, Putrajaya and parts of Kuala Lumpur and Selangor reopen today, Malay Mail and the NST reported. In Sarawak, the Department of Environment said air quality in Kuching and Miri had improved to moderate. Firefly cancelled Subang–Seletar flights from 10 to 12 October because of haze and weather. Earlier in the week all 1,198 Johor schools were closed by a Regent’s decree, Malay Mail reported. Check the ministry’s notice before sending children to school.

Second strike hits Riyadh airport a day after an attack that killed 12; Malaysia condemns the violence

A projectile hit Terminal 4 at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Sunday and started a fire, the Saudi-led coalition said, a day after a missile attack there killed 12 people and wounded more than 300, according to Saudi authorities (AP, BBC, Livemint). Yemen’s Houthis, who control much of northern Yemen, claimed responsibility for the Saturday attack and renewed a warning to airlines and travellers against using Saudi airports (Reuters, FinancialJuice). Saudi Arabia extended the airport’s closure (AP) and the coalition vowed retaliation (The Guardian). The Indian Embassy said one Indian died and others were among the injured (Livemint, The Hindu).

Malaysia condemned the attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure and urged a peaceful resolution, the NST reported. Families of Malaysians on umrah said they were anxious after some flights were diverted to Madinah. Reuters reported that Saudi stocks slid after the attack, and the FT said oil executives pulled out of an energy congress in Riyadh. A Houthi claim to have hit Dammam airport and Aramco facilities in the Eastern Province came from one news service only and is not confirmed. Bloomberg said the strikes raise the risk of a wider war.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · Trump’s post and Zelensky’s posts attributed; no Russian confirmation seen

Trump declares a Russia–Ukraine “energy ceasefire”; Kyiv backs it but says the post was news to it

President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social on Sunday that an energy ceasefire in the war between Russia and Ukraine was effective immediately, that both parties had agreed, and that they should not break it (Reuters, BBC, Bloomberg). Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky first said the declaration was news to him, according to Axios as relayed by The Week. He then wrote on Telegram that Ukraine supports every real and credible ceasefire format, and Reuters reported he is ready to stop striking Russian refineries if Russia stops hitting Ukraine’s energy grid. Kyiv awaits details from Washington. DailyDrop found no Russian confirmation at the time of writing.

Earlier, Trump had said it was time for Ukraine to get a new president after Zelensky criticised the US deal for Russian diesel (Bloomberg). Germany’s foreign minister, Johann Wadephul, backed Zelensky (Reuters), and the FT reported that Washington had threatened to block weapons for Ukraine over its strikes on Russian refineries. Zelensky said on Telegram that Russian attacks killed 23 people in Zaporizhzhia, a figure DailyDrop has not seen from a second source. The link for Malaysia is oil: WTI closed on Friday at US$91.85, before the post, and Monday is the first test of whether markets believe the ceasefire.

Bursa reopens today facing a new Budget, two Riyadh strikes and a ceasefire headline

Bursa Malaysia was closed on Sunday and reopens on Monday 12 October. DailyDrop could not confirm the FBM KLCI’s close on Friday. The last confirmed close was 1,601.01 on Thursday, 10.77 points lower, according to Bernama. Friday’s ringgit rate could not be confirmed from two sources, so the ringgit ear is blank rather than guessed.

On Wall Street on Friday the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.83% to 51,654.95. WTI crude settled at US$91.85, up 0.39%, and gold rose 1.43% to US$4,216.3. All of that closed before Trump’s ceasefire post and Sunday’s second Riyadh strike, so Monday’s open is the first reaction. Reuters reported that Saudi stocks slid after Saturday’s attack. DailyDrop will not forecast the direction.
