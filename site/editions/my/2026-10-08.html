KLCI drops 1.3% to 1,611.78 as oil prices and Budget nerves hit banks

It was the sharpest one-day fall since this edition began on 5 October, with foreign investors said to be net sellers for ten sessions in a row.

The FBM KLCI closed at 1,611.78 on Wednesday 7 October, down 21.57 points or 1.32% from 1,633.35, according to Bernama’s figures reported the next day. The Star said the index dropped sharply on rising oil prices, and Business Today said it slipped at the open as Budget 2027 loomed. At 12.30pm it was down 19.39 points at 1,613.96, with heavyweight banks leading the fall, The Star reported.

An analyst quoted in the coverage said foreign investors had been net sellers on Bursa Malaysia for ten consecutive trading days. Brent briefly topped US$102 in Asian morning trading before settling lower in New York, according to an AP-based market report. DailyDrop did not find a single official explanation, so the oil and Budget links are the reasons analysts gave, not facts. The October futures contract fell 17.50 points to 1,611.0 shortly after the open.

Melaka votes on 14 November, with nomination on 31 October and early voting on 10 November

The Election Commission set polling for the 16th Melaka state election for Saturday 14 November, with nomination on Saturday 31 October and early voting on 10 November, Malay Mail and New Straits Times reported on Wednesday. Commission chairman Datuk Seri Ramlan Harun said the campaign period will run for 14 days.

About 687,697 voters can vote: 675,166 ordinary voters, 9,769 military personnel and spouses and 2,762 police personnel. There will be 271 polling centres with 1,263 channels, including 31 early voting centres, at an estimated cost of RM28.45 million. The Star’s roundup the same day added that Barisan Nasional intends to contest 21 seats; DailyDrop saw that figure in one outlet, and has not seen the coalition’s own announcement.

Johor orders every school shut for two days as haze deteriorates in all districts

All schools in Johor will close on Thursday 8 and Friday 9 October by decree of the state regent, Malay Mail reported on Wednesday. The state’s education committee chairman said the closure responds to the ongoing haze and worsening air quality across all districts. It covers 617,258 students at 1,198 schools under the Education Ministry.

The Vibes reported that parents were worried about their children’s exposure as the haze worsened. The Education Ministry’s guideline reported in the coverage is that outdoor activities should be suspended when the Air Pollutant Index passes 100, and that schools in areas above 200 should close. A day earlier Business Today counted 28 unhealthy areas nationwide. DailyDrop did not find a nationwide count for Wednesday.

Wall Street slips from a record as oil jumps above US$102 in the morning

The S&P 500 closed at 7,801.77 on Wednesday, down 17.16 points or about 0.2% from 7,818.93, after having moved above its August record, according to a Crypto Daily report citing AP. The Nasdaq composite fell 61.20 points to 27,538.69. Brent topped US$102 in morning trading and settled at US$100.20, down 0.4%. The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.36% intraday.

WTI settled at US$88.28, down 1.30%, and gold fell 1.11% to US$4,140.7, according to market data. DailyDrop has the AP-based detail from one secondary source only, so the figures other than the closes should be treated as indicative. For Malaysia, high US bond yields and oil are the two channels that reach Bursa and the ringgit.

Economy · BudgetPress ✓ 3 outlets · analyst views attributed; nothing official

Markets turn cautious as Budget 2027 nears: what analysts say they are waiting for

Business Today reported that the KLCI slipped at the open on Wednesday as Budget 2027 loomed, and The Star said investors were watching oil prices while keeping a close eye on the tabling. Foreign investors were said to be net sellers for ten days. Bernama’s earlier schedule shows the Budget will be tabled at 3.30pm on Friday and debated for weeks.

DailyDrop has not seen an official preview of the contents. What analysts quoted in the coverage said they were waiting for was the deficit target, the cost of subsidies and any change to taxes. The ringgit was close to RM4.09 to the dollar on Bank Negara’s 5pm rate, slightly weaker than on Tuesday. The index is now about 1.1% below its close of 1,630.36 a week earlier.
