PM orders flood relief paid daily; DDPM has moved 4.7 billion baht to the Government Savings Bank, with money due in victims’ accounts this week

Anutin Charnvirakul met the governors of 76 provinces by video on Oct. 5 and ordered them to verify eligibility and pay at once, day by day, without waiting for the whole area to be finished. He also told them to speed up pumping and look after vulnerable groups.

Thansettakij, Infoquest and Money and Banking report in agreement that Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul chaired a meeting of the National Disaster Prevention and Mitigation Command Center on Oct. 5, with the governors of 76 provinces joining by video conference. He ordered provinces to verify eligibility and then pay flood relief day by day, without waiting for data from the whole area. Earlier, the Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) had transferred a 4.7 billion baht allocation to the Government Savings Bank, and the money will begin reaching victims’ accounts within this week.

The prime minister stressed the approach of “send it fast, transfer it fast” and told officials to cut steps so that money reaches people as quickly as possible (Infoquest). Official reports say the aid covers disasters that occurred between May 15 and Sept. 30, 2026, and that registration through the “Thang Rat” app has been open since Oct. 2 (Thai PBS). No figure has been reported for the number of people whose eligibility has been verified.

At the meeting, the prime minister told governors to look after vulnerable groups, make sure food at shelters is of good quality, and give clear flood warnings (Siam Rath). He also ordered faster drainage and asked them to report their needs for pumps and boats to the DDPM (Post Today). Meanwhile, the South must prepare for heavy rain through Oct. 14.

What is still unknown is the first day money actually reaches accounts and the number of households paid each day, because the 4.7 billion baht is an allocation the DDPM has transferred to the bank, not an amount already paid out to people. Today, watch for the first-batch announcement from the DDPM and the Government Savings Bank.

Thailand prepares four flood scenarios for the IMF–World Bank meetings on Oct. 12–18; cancellation to be proposed if water reaches 3 meters in the convention center

Nation Thailand, The Star, Bernama and SCMP report that organizers and Thai agencies have drawn up flood plans for the IMF–World Bank Annual Meetings at the Queen Sirikit National Convention Center on Oct. 12–18. There are four scenarios, ranging from changing travel routes to a decision procedure for the worst case: water about 3 meters high inside the convention building, in which case cancellation would be proposed, with the final decision made jointly by IMF and World Bank management and the Thai side. If water on roads reaches 51 cm, attendees will be directed to use the electric trains.

The Meteorological Department warns that Bangkok could see about 50 mm of rain a day on Oct. 11–13. About 15,000 attendees from 191 countries are expected (Bernama), and the prime minister has ordered airports and transport readied for more than 10,000 attendees (Malay Mail). The BOT confirms it does not expect the floods to affect the event.

World · US marketsPress ✓ 4 outlets · figures cited from financial media

US stocks close higher and the Nasdaq sets a record despite a 10-year bond yield near 5.3%, while WTI crude falls 1.84%

Wall Street closed higher on Monday, Oct. 5. The S&P 500 ended at 7,773.95, up 51.23 points (+0.66%), the Dow Jones at 51,267.90 (+0.18%) and the Nasdaq at 27,477.31, a new high, led by big technology shares (CNBC, Reuters, BBN Times). The market looked past the 10-year US bond yield of about 5.3%.

WTI crude closed at $89.43 (−1.84%) and Brent at about $100.32. Reuters attributes the fall to the return of Middle East oil exports and a pledge by the G7 to increase supply, but worry remains over the US–Israel war with Iran. Gold closed at $4,156.8 (−0.13%). The market gives the Fed about a 24% chance of raising rates this month, down from about 64% a week earlier, after weak jobs data.

Money markets · Thai stocksPress ✓ 4 outlets · baht quotes differ by the timing of each source

SET closes up 0.31% at 1,576.45, but foreigners are net sellers of 2.26 billion baht, while the baht weakens from the morning to 33.68

The SET index closed on Oct. 5 at 1,576.45 points, up 4.83 points (+0.31%), with a high of 1,582.90 and a low of 1,568.61, on turnover of 55,047.95 million baht. On the day, net foreign selling came to about 2.26 billion baht, while institutions and retail investors were net buyers, and DELTA had the highest turnover (Kaohoon, Thansettakij, Bangkok Insight). Analysts give a support level of 1,560–1,565 and a resistance level of 1,585–1,590.

The baht opened on the morning of Oct. 5 at about 33.56, a stronger level, but closed weaker at 33.68, touching 33.71 during the day, its weakest in two months (Daily News), and opened on Oct. 6 at 33.68 (Prachachat Turakij). The move followed the region, where the dollar was firm and the euro weak because markets worried about French public finances.

PoliticsPress ✓ 3 outlets · statement by the person accused

Worasit baffled by opposition accusations, says it is as if they were “living in different worlds”; says he is ready to explain if grilled in a censure debate

Matichon, Amarin TV and Siam Rath report that Deputy Interior Minister Worasit Liangprasit, commenting on the opposition’s announced plan to file a censure motion against the government, said he did not understand the accusations, as if they lived in different worlds, and did not yet know the details because they were written broadly. But he insisted he was ready to explain and confident he could answer. He is not worried about the issues the opposition raised, including the local inquiry (Siam Rath).

Politically, a censure debate is a mechanism of oversight in parliament; the debate needs members’ endorsement, and the date depends on the House Speaker. DailyDrop has not seen the text of the motion or a debate date, so the accusations that have appeared remain only the opposition’s claims.
