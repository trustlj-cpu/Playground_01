Thammasat marks 50 years since October 6, 1976; government, opposition, students and bereaved families attend

A merit-making ceremony and wreath-laying at the sculpture garden at Tha Phra Chan, followed by performances and music from early morning into the evening; attendees raised the three-finger salute

Thammasat University’s Tha Phra Chan campus held “Thammasat and the Struggle for Democracy” on Oct. 6, marking 50 years since the October 6, 1976 events, when students were dispersed by force inside the university and a coup followed the same day. In the morning, alms were offered to 19 monks and wreaths were laid at the Historic Sculpture Garden. Representatives of the government, the opposition, students and bereaved families attended (The Standard, Siam Rath, Thammasat University).

The Artists for Peace group, the Chaiwana Foundation and their network staged a live performance, “Nattakam Lamnam: 50 Years of October 6,” from early morning, while Prachatai reports that art, music and civil-society activities continued into the evening, closing with music and the raising of the three-finger salute.

Varasarn Press reports that the campaign “Erase the Legacy of the October 6 Coup” has closed its list at more than 60,000 signatories, to propose repealing National Administrative Reform Council Order No. 41. The figure comes from a single source, and we have not seen whether the proposal has been submitted to Parliament.

In the reports we checked, there is no new policy proposal or timetable from the government or Parliament. What can be confirmed is that the commemoration was held openly, with representatives from both the government and the opposition attending.

Commerce Ministry asks to hold farm-gate eggs at 4 baht and extend hens’ retirement age to 85 weeks after floods affect about 3 million birds on Chachoengsao farms

The Department of Internal Trade said on Oct. 6 that egg farms in Chachoengsao have been affected, with about 3 million birds hit. It has therefore asked associations and large farms to hold the farm-gate price of mixed-size eggs at 4 baht per egg and to source eggs from areas not hit by the floods for distribution centers, and has asked the Department of Livestock Development to extend the retirement age of caged hens from 80 to 85 weeks (Daily News, Amarin TV, Thai PBS, The Standard).

Some vegetables are in short supply because growing areas in Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya, Nakhon Pathom and Chachoengsao were damaged. The department is bringing vegetables from Phetchabun and Nakhon Ratchasima into Bangkok and is holding Blue Flag (Thong Fa) fairs in Bangkok on Oct. 9–18 and in other provinces from Oct. 16. The department’s hotline, 1569, takes reports of overpricing (Siam Rath, The Reporters).

Disasters · FloodsPress ✓ 6 outlets · figures from the DDPM Operations Center

Floods in 27 provinces and Bangkok: 3.14 million people affected, 23 deaths in all; North easing, Central region still hard hit

The Disaster Prevention and Mitigation Operations Center (DDPM Operations Center) reported as of 6:00 a.m. on Oct. 6 that flooding covers 27 provinces and Bangkok, in all 126 districts, 999 sub-districts and 7,379 villages, with 1,149,285 households, or 3,143,962 people, affected. By comparison, the Oct. 4 report gave 154 districts and 3.26 million people, so the figures have fallen (Thai PBS and Prachatai, Bangkok Biz News, Daily News, Naewna).

Media report 23 deaths in all (Naewna). Bangkok Biz News says water levels in the North have started to fall, but the Central region is still affected by surging water. People affected should follow announcements from the DDPM and local authorities.

Security · Southern borderPress ✓ 6 outlets · Cabinet resolution

Cabinet extends serious emergency rule in 3 southern border provinces by another 3 months, the 86th extension, to Jan. 19, 2027

On Oct. 6 the Cabinet approved extending the declaration of a serious emergency situation in Narathiwat, Pattani and Yala by another 3 months, from Oct. 20, 2026 to Jan. 19, 2027. It is the 86th extension, following the 85th, which ran to Oct. 19 (Thansettakij, Thai PBS, Manager Online).

Some districts are exempt, such as, in Narathiwat, Yi-ngo, Waeng and Sukhirin; in Pattani, Yaring, Panare, Mayo, Mai Kaen, Thung Yang Daeng, Kapho and Mae Lan; and in Yala, Betong, Yaha, Raman, Kabang and Krong Pinang. The deputy spokesperson of the Prime Minister’s Office announced the resolution.

World · US marketsPress ✓ 4 outlets · figures from financial media

S&P 500 closes above 7,800 for the first time, at 7,818.93 (+0.58%); gold up 0.73%, WTI crude steady

Wall Street closed Tuesday, Oct. 6. The S&P 500 ended at 7,818.93 (+0.58%), its first close above 7,800, as technology stocks led and bond yields cooled (CNBC). WTI closed at $89.44 (+0.01%) and gold at $4,187.1 (+0.73%).

In Asia in the morning, Brent was around $100.17 and WTI $88.67, according to Reuters, pressured by data showing that Persian Gulf oil exports in parts of September were higher than before the Iran war, and by the G7’s agreement on Oct. 2 to release up to 100 million barrels of reserve oil and diesel over 4 months, diesel first (Spokesman, Al Jazeera).
