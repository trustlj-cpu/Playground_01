Democratic Party moves for parliamentary investigation of Chief Justice Cho Hee-de… aims to pass it at Nov. 5 plenary session

The Democratic Party of Korea decided at a caucus meeting to hold a parliamentary investigation into the allegations surrounding Chief Justice Cho Hee-de, while the People Power Party demanded it stop, calling it ‘killing Cho Hee-de’.

Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the Democratic Party of Korea, after a closed-door caucus meeting on the 27th, decided to pursue a parliamentary investigation into allegations including abuse of power by Chief Justice Cho Hee-de. Floor spokesperson Jeon Eun-su said at a briefing that ‘opinion converged that a parliamentary investigation should be held’, and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that when asked whether the goal is to put it to a plenary session on Nov. 5, he answered ‘That’s right’. According to Dong-A Ilbo, floor leader Han Byung-do said at that morning’s meeting that the controversy over political interference and the allegations of abuse of power surrounding the head of the judiciary must be cleared up, and that it must be determined whether the chief justice fulfilled the duty to defend the Constitution during the insurrection and how the chief justice came to refuse the Cheong Wa Dae’s request for a re-recommendation of a Supreme Court justice candidate. Dong-A Ilbo reported that Rep. Kim Hyun-jung said the caucus position is to collect evidence on the controversy over business promotion expenses and other matters, and that ‘no one talked about impeaching’.

Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the ruling party and Chief Justice Cho have been in conflict over matters including the reversal and remand in President Lee Jae-myung’s trial and the re-recommendation of a Supreme Court justice, and that Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s recent acquittal at the second trial acted as the ‘trigger’ for the push for a parliamentary investigation. Dong-A Ilbo reported that People Power Party chief spokesperson Park Chung-kwon said in a commentary that ‘refusal to accept a ruling has been disguised as a parliamentary investigation’, demanded an immediate end to ‘killing Cho Hee-de’, and criticized the push as a disguised tactic for impeachment. Yonhap reported that the People Power Party defined the parliamentary investigation as ‘grounds for impeaching President Lee Jae-myung and the ruling party’ and said it would mount national resistance. Yonhap reported that People Power Party floor leader Jeong Jeom-sik said a parliamentary investigation into the ‘3+1 fabrication special counsel’ should come before the special counsel on dropping prosecutions. Dong-A Ilbo noted that six days earlier a Democratic Party spokesperson had called the parliamentary investigation ‘a procedure to confirm concrete grounds for impeachment’. In a separate article, Kyunghyang Shinmun ran the headline ‘Successive acquittals in the Myung Tae-kyun gate brought on the Cho Hee-de parliamentary investigation’. SBS reported that the Democratic Party aims to pass it at the plenary session on the 5th of next month.

Supreme Court upholds 3-year prison term for ex-Marine 1st Division commander Lim Seong-geun… 3 years 3 months after the death on duty

Dong-A Ilbo reported that Supreme Court Division 1 (presiding Justice Shin Suk-hee) on the 27th upheld the lower court ruling sentencing Lim Seong-geun, former commander of the Marine Corps 1st Division, to 3 years in prison on charges including professional negligence resulting in death or injury. Yonhap and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the ruling came 3 years and 3 months after the death on duty of Cpl. Chae Su-geun. According to Dong-A Ilbo, Lim was accused of ordering a search for missing persons in Yecheon County, North Gyeongsang, in July 2023 without issuing safety equipment, causing Cpl. Chae to be swept away by a rapid current and die. Dong-A Ilbo reported that at the time of the accident a fragmentary order was issued transferring operational control to the Army. Kyunghyang Shinmun called Lim ‘No. 1 target of the Chae special counsel investigation’ in its headline. Yonhap reported that the Marine Corps special counsel said of the finalized conviction, in effect, that it ‘left trust that one can send a child to the military and feel safe’, and YTN reported in a headline that the Democratic Party assessed the sentence as ‘a sentence at odds with judicial justice’.

KOSPI closes up 3.09% at 5,638.34… buy-side sidecar triggered during the session

Korea Economic Daily reported that the KOSPI closed up more than 3% on the 27th, describing in its headline a rally that set off the ‘sidecar’. According to Electronic Times, the Korea Exchange triggered a buy-side sidecar on the KOSPI market at 10:13 a.m., with the benchmark mini KOSPI 200 futures at 893.02, up 5.05% from the reference price. Electronic Times reported it was the 26th buy-side sidecar this year, and Dong-A Ilbo reported it was the 53rd sidecar this year on the KOSPI market. Electronic Times explained that this sidecar is triggered when the most heavily traded KOSPI 200 futures contract of the previous trading day stays at least 5% above its reference price for one minute. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the Korea Exchange announced the trigger at 10:13:53. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix up 5-7% during the session, the news of Anthropic’s push for an initial public offering is interpreted to have worked as a positive for domestic chip stocks. By market tally, the KOSPI closed at 5,638.34, up 3.09% from the previous trading day.

Korean economy grew 1.0% on quarter in Q3… chips and facilities investment led the way

Electronic Times reported that the Bank of Korea said on the 27th that the advance estimate of Q3 real GDP growth came in at 1.0% from the previous quarter. Dong-A Ilbo reported that the figure beat by 0.7 percentage points the 0.3% Q3 (July-September) forecast the Bank of Korea gave in August, and is up 3.4% from a year earlier. Yonhap reported that the economy achieved ‘surprise growth’ thanks to rising chip exports and facilities investment. Electronic Times reported that, amid the chip boom, government consumption and facilities investment drove growth, that gross domestic income (GDI), which shows real purchasing power, rose by the largest margin in about 49 years, and that private consumption fell back. Yonhap ran a headline saying that even if Q4 growth is 0%, annual growth reaches 3.6%, and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that annual growth is expected to exceed the Bank of Korea’s earlier forecast of 3.3% and reach the high-3% range.

Supreme Court hearing on Kim Keon-hee’s additional detention warrant ends after 45 minutes… whether it will be issued undecided

Yonhap and Korea Economic Daily flashed that the Supreme Court on the 27th finished its hearing on the additional detention warrant for Kim Keon-hee in 45 minutes. According to Kyunghyang Shinmun, Supreme Court Division 2 (presiding Justice Oh Kyung-mi) began the hearing, related to charges of influence peddling under the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes, at 2:30 p.m. that day in Courtroom 1 of the Supreme Court in Seocho District, Seoul, and Kim said she ‘will calmly comply with the hearing procedure’. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that her current detention period expires on the 31st and the result could come as early as that evening. Yonhap reported that, four days before the detention expires at the end of this month, she stands at a fork between extended detention and release.

KBS News reported in a headline that Kim’s side takes the position that ‘the additional detention is a separate-case detention’. Yonhap reported that the Democratic Party said ‘Kim Keon-hee’s detention must continue’ and is watching the court’s decision.
