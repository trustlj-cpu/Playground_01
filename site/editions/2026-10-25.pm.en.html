Typhoon ‘Champi’ drops 295 mm of rain on Jeju’s Mount Halla… baseball playoff Game 1 cancelled, to resume on the 26th

Yonhap reported on the 25th that Typhoon No. 30, ‘Champi’, brought a string of wind and rain damage to Jeju, and that Game 1 of the KIA-Samsung playoff, due to be played in Daegu, was cancelled and will be played again on the 26th.

Yonhap and Dong-A Ilbo reported on the 25th that rain continued in Jeju for a third day under the influence of Typhoon No. 30, ‘Champi’. According to Dong-A Ilbo, the typhoon had weakened from intensity 2 to intensity 1 that morning and was moving north at sea about 360 km south-southwest of Seogwipo, Jeju; as of 7:30 a.m., a typhoon warning was in effect for the outer open sea south of Jeju Island, and a heavy rain advisory for the mountains and mid-mountain areas. A strong wind advisory was in effect across the province except northern and western Jeju City, and a high wave warning over the seas. Yonhap reported that 295 mm of rain fell on Mount Halla, and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that 8 cases of wind and heavy-rain damage were logged from the 23rd through that day, with a report coming in around 6:15 a.m. on the 25th that an underground pump room in Seogwipo had flooded. Yonhap reported that air and sea routes were also disrupted.

Yonhap and Dong-A Ilbo reported that at 6 p.m. on the 24th the Ministry of the Interior and Safety raised its crisis alert for storm and flood damage in Jeju Island and the southern coast from ‘attention’ to ‘caution’. Dong-A Ilbo reported that the step followed preliminary warnings issued around 4 p.m. the same day for the outer open sea south of Jeju Island and the outer open sea of the eastern South Sea, and that rain of up to 100 mm or more with strong winds was forecast for Jeju and the southern coast. SBS reported that the typhoon would come closest to Korea overnight on the 25th before heading to Japan, with up to 120 mm of rain in Jeju. Yonhap reported that as a result Game 1 of the KIA-Samsung pro baseball playoff was cancelled and the game will resume on the 26th.

Short-term jobs at 31-year low… temporary and daily workers in Q3 at 5,727,000, down 95,000 in a year

Chosun Ilbo reported on the 25th, citing the National Data Office’s Economically Active Population Survey, that temporary and daily workers numbered 5,727,000 in the third quarter this year, down 95,000 from a year earlier. Chosun Ilbo said this is the lowest for a third quarter in 31 years, since 1995 (5,451,000). Yonhap reported the same news under the headline ‘4050 shock as construction, domestic demand slump’, and SBS also said the number of temporary and daily workers fell to its lowest for the period in 31 years. Dong-A Ilbo reported that weakness in domestic demand industries such as the construction industry and lodging and restaurants led to a shrinking of short-term jobs. Dong-A Ilbo said Lee Sang-yong (34), who quit a small company last year and, after his unemployment benefits were cut off, works as a day laborer at construction sites, said, “The construction market is bad, so it’s not easy to find work.” Dong-A Ilbo also covered employment among people in their 40s and 50s, who support their households. The figure for daily workers alone among Q3 temporary and daily workers, and the size of the decline by industry and age group, do not appear in the reports we checked.

Politics · PartiesPress ✓ 4 outlets · full text of Cho Kuk’s remarks and follow-up moves inside the party unconfirmed

Jang Dong-hyeok on day 425 as People Power Party’s ‘longest-serving leader’… Cho Kuk warns of ‘election rout’, ruling party calls him ‘master of hanging on’

Dong-A Ilbo reported that People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyeok, on his 425th day in office on the 25th, became the longest-serving leader since the party’s launch in 2020. Dong-A Ilbo said the pro-leadership camp judged that “we built a party that can fight when conservatism has collapsed,” while some in the party, including the non-leadership camp, point out that a course of looking only to hard-line supporters means it fails to reap the benefit even from the disarray of the government and ruling party. Yonhap and SBS reported that the Democratic Party mocked Leader Jang’s record as “master of hanging on,” and according to SBS, Chief Spokesperson Park Sung-joon said, “Such political skill is rare.”

Yonhap reported that Rebuilding Korea Party’s Cho Kuk, head of the Innovation Policy Institute, said on the 25th that the Democratic Party, amid power struggles such as the dispute over merging with the Rebuilding Korea Party and the party convention leadership race, had missed the right moment for judicial reform and would be routed in the general election at this rate. Yonhap presented the remarks as Cho’s ‘wider stride’ after Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s acquittal. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that after Mayor Oh was acquitted in the appellate trial of a Political Funds Act violation case, the contest for leadership of the conservative camp became a three-way race among Oh Se-hoon, Jang Dong-hyeok and independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon.

Fair Trade Commission chair Joo Byung-ki: “Penalty surcharge for refusing an investigation to be raised to 1% of total revenue”… remark amid Coupang, Hanwha controversy

Yonhap and SBS reported that Fair Trade Commission Chair Joo Byung-ki said on the 25th that the penalty surcharge for companies that refuse investigations should be strengthened to about ‘1% of total revenue’. According to Yonhap, Chair Joo said in effect that a penalty surcharge is imposed when an investigation is refused, and that it is “not a slap on the wrist but 1% of total revenue.” Kyunghyang Shinmun said the remark came amid the controversy over Coupang and Hanwha’s refusal of investigations, and that Chair Joo said, “Expanding the penalty surcharge to 1% of revenue is necessary.”

KBS News reported that Chair Joo said strengthening the surcharge for refusing investigations to 1% of revenue would be efficient. In a separate report, KBS News said Chair Joo stated that the commission is conducting an internal inspection into the alleged leak of information about the Hanwha on-site inspection. The timing and procedure of a system revision do not appear in the reports we checked.

Economy · Real estatePress ✓ 4 outlets · reference date for the rise in loans unconfirmed

Seoul apartment prices up 0.61% in October, smallest rise this year; 5 major banks’ household loans up 2.6 trillion won this month

Yonhap and SBS reported that in statistics released by KB Real Estate on the 25th, Seoul apartment prices rose 0.61% in October, continuing their climb but at the smallest pace this year. KBS News carried the same content under the headline ‘Rise at year’s low’. All three outlets led their headlines with the 0.61% rise and the year’s lowest rate of increase.

SBS reported that as home prices keep rising in the Seoul metropolitan area, centered on outer Seoul and southern Gyeonggi, household loans are again surging at commercial banks. Yonhap reported that the 5 major banks’ household loans rose 2.6 trillion won this month and bank deposits rose 11 trillion won, and Hankyoreh also reported that the 5 major banks’ household loans were rising again, up 2.6 trillion won this month.
