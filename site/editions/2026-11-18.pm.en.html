President Lee and President Xi hold 35-minute summit in Shenzhen… Cheong Wa Dae: “Xi backs efforts to ease tensions, including US–North Korea dialogue”

The Korean and Chinese leaders sat down for the third time in Shenzhen, China, host of the APEC summit, and Cheong Wa Dae relayed Xi’s expression of support for efforts to ease tensions on the Korean Peninsula.

President Lee Jae-myung and Chinese President Xi Jinping held a summit in Shenzhen, China, on the 18th. The meeting came on the occasion of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen; according to Cheong Wa Dae, it ran for 35 minutes from 11:05 a.m. at Qilin Mountain Villa with simultaneous interpretation. National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac said in a written briefing that the two leaders discussed ways to develop Korea–China relations beyond a ‘full restoration’ into a more mature relationship. On the situation on the Korean Peninsula, Lee mentioned easing tensions between the two Koreas and realizing peaceful coexistence, and Xi voiced support for various efforts to ease tensions on the Korean Peninsula, including US–North Korea dialogue. This was the third meeting between the two leaders.

On the economy, Lee stressed mutually beneficial economic cooperation, and Xi stressed supply chain cooperation grounded in multilateralism in trade. Next year marks the 35th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic ties between Korea and China. Reports that Lee said the two sides should work together toward a fundamental resolution of the Yellow Sea issue, that Xi said the two countries should uphold strategic autonomy, and that the leaders agreed to widen cooperation on the back of concluding free trade agreement (FTA) services and investment negotiations came from only one breaking-news report and must be confirmed against the full text of the Cheong Wa Dae briefing. The specific agenda on the Yellow Sea issue, whether anything was agreed and the scope of wider cooperation are not known. Because the summit took place as the leaders of the United States, China and Russia gather in one place on the same APEC occasion, whether follow-up messages on the Korean Peninsula come remains a point of interest.

Daegu Mayor Choo Kyung-ho acquitted at first instance of ‘obstructing vote to lift martial law’… special counsel had sought 20 years in prison

The Seoul Central District Court on the 18th acquitted Daegu Mayor Choo Kyung-ho at first instance. Choo had been indicted on a charge of participation in key insurrection duties for obstructing People Power Party lawmakers’ vote to lift martial law at the time of the Dec. 3 emergency martial law. The special counsel’s sentencing request was 20 years in prison. The court was reported to have found that participation in the insurrection was not proven and that there was also insufficient proof that Yoon had made a request. Some reports also said a guilty verdict could have cost the mayoral post. Mayor Choo said after the ruling that every effort would go into Daegu city administration. People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk said ‘this is what a fabricated indictment looks like’, and there were reports that the People Power Party is considering filing a complaint against the special counsel for the crime of distorting the law. Han Dong-hoon said it was the natural result of the special counsel’s unreasonable investigation and indictment, and Lee Jun-seok was reported to have criticized it as politics that denies the constitution. On the other side, Jung Chung-rae argued that Cho Hee-de should be impeached, and Kim Min-seok is said to have reacted by calling it ‘absurd’. Whether the special counsel will appeal has not been confirmed.

Politics · Election investigation✓ 4 outlets · identities of the 8 and the warrant hearing schedule unconfirmed

Election commission special counsel seeks arrest warrants for 8 including staff… charges of ‘falsely entering turnout, forging public documents’

The special counsel team led by Lee Tae-han said on the 18th that it had requested arrest warrants for eight people, including staff of the National Election Commission and other election commissions at each level. Assistant Special Counsel Kim Jin-woo gave a briefing at the special counsel’s office in Seocho District, Seoul. The charges are falsely entering turnout and forging or altering public documents such as voting records. The warrant request comes more than five months after the shortage of ballot papers in the June 3 local elections. A voting record is an official document that notes the handing out of ballot papers and how voting went at a polling station.

Whether they are detained will be decided after the court’s hearing on the warrants; the hearing schedule and the specific identities and ranks of the eight have not been confirmed. Nothing has been disclosed about at which polling stations or by what method turnout was falsely entered and voting records forged or altered. The election commission’s official position on this request has not been confirmed.

Nvidia interim CEO: “New supply contract with Samsung Electro-Mechanics for chip substrates”… cooperation widens to MLCC

Nvidia interim chief executive Colette Kress said on the 17th (US time) that the company had signed a new supply contract with Samsung Electro-Mechanics for semiconductor package substrates. A package substrate is a component on which a semiconductor chip is mounted and connected to a circuit board. After the substrate supply, the two companies agreed to widen cooperation to multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCC), and Samsung Electro-Mechanics is reported to be increasing its supply of substrates for next-generation graphics processing units (GPUs). An MLCC is a small component that briefly stores electricity and releases it to steady the current, and it is widely used in smartphones and servers.

On the 18th Samsung Electro-Mechanics’ share price rose more than 7%, and a rise in the 6% range was reported intraday. The contract size and supply start date have not been disclosed. The announcement came on the same day as the first earnings Nvidia released after Jensen Huang stepped down.

Society · CSAT✓ 4 outlets · details of the 100 million won reward unconfirmed

President Lee: “Will consider extending exam time if disaster alerts disturb test-takers during CSAT”… follow-up order a day before the CSAT

President Lee Jae-myung said on the 18th, a day before the CSAT, that the government would consider extending exam time by as much as a test-taker is disturbed if an emergency disaster alert rings during the exam. The government is also considering sending additional disaster alerts without notice during the exam to catch test-takers who did not hand in their phones. Lee also said there would be a blanket check of whether CSAT exam papers had been uploaded to AI and that cheating would be dealt with sternly. This follows up on measures announced on the 17th by Prime Minister Han Seong-sook.

The CSAT is held on the 19th, and about 550,000 test-takers are subject to the preliminary assembly on the 18th. Reports said Lee is pushing a 100 million won reward in relation to cheating, but details have not been confirmed. Na Kyung-won was reported to have criticized the measures as ‘telling test-takers to watch one another’, and the government’s position is to hold the exam as scheduled without postponing it. There were also reports that it had earlier come to light that the top passer of the tax accountant exam confessed to AI cheating.
