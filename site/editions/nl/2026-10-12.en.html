At least 139 migrants drowned off Djibouti, 84 missing. The boat was heading for Yemen, where war rages

A boat carrying migrants capsized off the coast of Djibouti, bound for Yemen. The United Nations speaks of at least 139 dead, among them at least fifteen children, and 84 missing. An earlier figure of 109 is out of date.

At least 139 people drowned when a boat carrying migrants capsized off the coast of Djibouti, on its way to Yemen. Another 84 people are missing. That is what the United Nations says, according to reports by Reuters, NRC, de Volkskrant and PBS, among others. Among the dead are at least fifteen children. The accident was reported on Sunday. An initial Reuters report still spoke of 109 dead; later NRC, de Volkskrant, PBS and Le Monde reported the higher figure of 139. According to the Indian news site Livemint, there were 305 people on board. We have not seen that number from a second source and therefore leave it out of the headline.

The migrants wanted to cross the Bab el-Mandeb strait, the narrow passage between the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula, on their way to the Gulf states (NRC). The strait, about thirty kilometers wide at its narrowest point, has made the news lately mainly because of the Houthi rebels, the paper writes. The route has long been considered one of the busiest migration routes in the world: people from East Africa seek work in the rich Gulf states via Yemen, often with smugglers and in unseaworthy boats. Anyone who arrives in Yemen ends up in a country that has been at war for years and where the Houthis control large areas. That makes help for survivors and the search for the missing especially difficult, although we have not yet seen anything about that in the reports.

Much is still unknown. The reports we saw name no cause of the accident, no nationalities of the victims and no number of survivors. It is also unclear where exactly off the coast the boat capsized. Because 84 people are missing, the death toll may still change. A link with the Houthi attacks on the airport in Riyadh, which dominated the news the same days, has not been reported. Dutch victims have not been reported either. The UN figure is an interim count, not the result of an investigation. Such counts are often revised in the days after a disaster, as already happened here, because often nobody knows exactly how many people were on board. The Indonesian site Detik writes that the boat was overloaded; that, too, is a single source. What the UN and the authorities of Djibouti report in the coming days about the search for the 84 missing will determine whether the death toll rises further.

Sport · Formula 1Press ✓ 9 outlets · results from NOS and NU.nl

Verstappen wins the Singapore Grand Prix for the first time, his second win in a row

Max Verstappen (29) won the Singapore Grand Prix for the first time in his career on Sunday (NOS, NU.nl, De Telegraaf, AD). It is his second win in a row; the previous race also went to him (NOS). On Saturday he had already won the sprint race. Red Bull team boss Laurent Mekies called him ‘even more magical than usual’ (De Telegraaf). Verstappen himself said he is not fighting for the title but going ‘all-in for success on the day’ (AD, Parool), and that he wants to enjoy the last races (NU.nl). NRC writes about Verstappen’s ‘sudden dominance’ and that Red Bull is ‘as supple as ever’; De Telegraaf points to the driver’s ‘staggering statistics’ since the summer break. A weekend with pole, sprint win and race win was ‘perfect’, said NOS.

In the championship Kimi Antonelli leads the standings. After Singapore he is on match point: at the United States Grand Prix in Austin, in two weeks, he can become world champion. To do so he must win and George Russell and Lewis Hamilton must disappoint (NU.nl). Hamilton climbed out of his car ill and skipped his media obligations; later, according to De Telegraaf, he apologized (AD, GPblog, Formule1.nl). According to Motorsport.com Verstappen is moving closer to Hamilton in the championship; we have not seen the exact standings confirmed.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 8 outlets · figures from Saudi authorities

Attack on Riyadh airport: at least twelve dead, more than three hundred injured. A new attack followed on Sunday

In Saturday’s attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh at least twelve people were killed and 309 injured, the Saudi authorities say (AD, Parool, NOS, NRC). The Houthis, the armed movement that controls large parts of Yemen, claimed the attack (FinancialJuice). The Saudi-led coalition calls it a war crime and promised to ‘hit back hard’ (NOS). Western leaders condemned the attack (NU.nl, NOS); UN chief Guterres said he was ‘deeply shocked’ (AD). It was the worst in a series: on Thursday, attacks on the same airport killed three Saudi civilians, among them a pilot, Al Jazeera and Euronews reported.

A new attack followed on Sunday. According to the coalition, a cruise missile was intercepted and destroyed (De Telegraaf); AD wrote that an unidentified projectile again hit a terminal. The Saudi aviation authority reported one dead and eleven injured (FinancialJuice), figures that a second source has not yet confirmed. The Houthis repeated their warning to travelers to avoid Saudi airports (Reuters). Oil executives, BP among them, stayed away from an energy conference in Riyadh (FT) and the Saudi stock exchange fell (Reuters). Crude oil futures (WTI) were trading around $92.5 a barrel on Sunday evening, a slight gain (market data, one source). NRC reports that the US is also considering getting drawn into the fighting.

Sport · FootballPress ✓ 7 outlets · results from NOS, NU.nl and AD

Liverpool lose 3-0 to Manchester City after red card for Gravenberch

Liverpool lost 3-0 to Manchester City at Anfield on Sunday (NOS, NU.nl, AD, De Telegraaf). It was 0-0 until after the break. Then Ryan Gravenberch, Liverpool’s Dutch midfielder, received two yellow cards and thus a red. Erling Haaland put City ahead soon afterwards, a second goal followed shortly after and later a third, which AD called ‘matchless’. City remain without a dropped point in the Premier League (AD). Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola defended Gravenberch (NU.nl) and, according to the BBC, said he was not happy with the refereeing; City manager Enzo Maresca praised his players after a ‘not easy’ afternoon (NU.nl, De Telegraaf). We have not seen how long Gravenberch will have to sit out; according to AD’s live reports, Liverpool had three chances in one minute in the first half but did not score.

The match was overshadowed by the uproar around City. It was the first game since the Premier League found the club guilty of breaching the financial rules (NOS, TNT Sports); AD and de Volkskrant speak of a ‘fraud verdict’ and a ‘financial scandal’. Liverpool fans jeered the City players at every touch of the ball (AD) and Anfield emptied before the end (NU.nl). We have not seen what punishment City will get or whether there will be an appeal. According to French media, City play Paris Saint-Germain in the Champions League on Wednesday.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 7 outlets · statements from Trump and Zelensky

Trump announces an ‘energy truce’ between Russia and Ukraine. Kyiv knows nothing of it; Russia attacks a power plant near Kharkiv

President Trump said on Truth Social on Sunday that Russia and Ukraine have agreed with immediate effect to stop attacking each other’s energy supplies (Reuters, BBC, NOS, AD, De Telegraaf). President Zelensky responded that it was ‘news to me’ and that Ukraine is waiting for details from Washington (Axios via FinancialJuice, Reuters, NOS). In a message on Telegram he backed ‘real and reliable’ forms of a truce. Ukraine will take part if Russia agrees too (Axios), and according to AD Zelensky is willing to stop attacks on Russian refineries if Putin also holds back. Moscow has not, as far as we saw, confirmed the truce.

Shortly after the announcement, a Russian attack hit a power plant near Kharkiv, Zelensky said (Reuters, Kyiv Independent, The Hill, De Telegraaf). That feeds the doubt. Trump also demanded that Ukraine stop attacking refineries; FD speaks of a ‘diesel deal’ between the US and Russia that hits Kyiv hard. What that deal involves has not been confirmed. Earlier on Sunday, according to Bloomberg, Trump said it is ‘time for a new president’ in Ukraine. Zelensky said he is prepared to organize elections if Russia agrees to a sixty-day truce (Reuters). Canadian prime minister Carney said an energy truce can help reduce the war, but that the diesel arrangement harms the cause of peace (Medicine Hat News, BNN Bloomberg). European leaders backed Zelensky; Commission President Von der Leyen supports his call for a truce (FinancialJuice).
