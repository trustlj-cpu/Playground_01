After the PHP outage, the NCSC reports the first signs of exploitation on Thursday, while half a million Dutch websites cannot install the update

Two critical vulnerabilities in PHP knocked out DigiD, MijnOverheid and the House of Representatives on Wednesday; on Thursday the National Cyber Security Centre saw the first signs of exploitation, NOS and the Volkskrant report.

On Wednesday, October 21, Dutch organizations took websites offline as a precaution because of two critical vulnerabilities in the programming language PHP, NOS, NU.nl and TPO reported. According to NOS, DigiD, MijnOverheid, the website of the House of Representatives and that of the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) were among those that went offline. TPO wrote that through the flaws an attacker can run their own code on a server without logging in. NOS and TPO report that PHP released an update the same afternoon, after which the sites gradually came back. On Thursday morning NRC wrote that the NCSC saw no signs of active exploitation yet, and around noon NOS reported that the government pages were back online and that no data had been leaked or stolen so far.

Later on Thursday the picture changed. NOS and the Volkskrant reported later that day that the NCSC sees the first signs of effective exploitation of the hole. NU.nl wrote that there were millions of exploitation attempts after the disclosure, and quotes the organization behind PHP warning that websites without the update remain vulnerable. De Telegraaf reported that half a million Dutch websites cannot install the crucial update and that Meld Misdaad Anoniem and Veilig Thuis were still unreachable. NRC, AD and De Telegraaf wrote that the organization behind PHP calls the delay a ‘structural failure’; NRC adds that it had known about a security hole for well over two years.

Gambling Authority imposes record fine of 37 million euros on gambling company EOD Code SRL, which is behind twelve illegal gambling sites

The Netherlands Gambling Authority (Kansspelautoriteit, Ksa) imposed a fine of 37 million euros on EOD Code SRL on Thursday, October 22, NOS, AD, De Telegraaf, NU.nl and the Volkskrant reported. NOS writes that it is the highest fine the regulator has ever handed out for illegal gambling offers. According to NL Times, the company is behind twelve gambling sites, including Instantcasino and Skyhills, and according to its own websites is registered in Costa Rica. The decision dates from October 6 and was made public on Thursday. Because EOD Code SRL did not respond to a request for figures, the Ksa estimated the revenue from the number of Google searches, NOS and NL Times write. That came to a gross gaming result of more than 108 million euros in the Netherlands for 2025. With the increases the fine would have been 292.6 million euros, but the legal limit of 10 percent of worldwide revenue pushed the amount down. AD headlined that visitors could pay with crypto; NL Times also mentions the lack of visible age verification. The company can object, but that does not suspend the fine, and it has six weeks to pay.

Heineken must pay 111.2 million euros in damages to Greek brewer over years of abuse of market power

The court in Amsterdam has ordered Heineken to pay damages of 111.2 million euros to the Greek brewer Macedonian Thrace Brewery (MTB), NOS, the Volkskrant, BNR, Het Parool and De Telegraaf reported. NOS writes that the amount consists of 43.3 million euros in lost profit and 67.9 million euros in interest. MTB, known for the beer Vergina, suffered damage for sixteen years because of the conduct of Heineken’s Greek subsidiary Athenian Brewery, according to NOS. The Volkskrant headlines about ‘foul play’ at the Greek brewery.

According to NOS, Heineken as parent company is liable together with Athenian Brewery, so MTB can claim the full amount from both. The abuse began in 1998 and consisted of exclusive agreements and discounts for shops and cafés that did not sell competing beer. The Greek competition authority fined Athenian Brewery 31.5 million euros for it, later reduced to 26.7 million euros. Heineken says it will appeal and argues that the calculation partly rests on assumptions about a hypothetical market. De Telegraaf quotes the Greek brewer: ‘Abuse of their market position simply continues’.

Domestic · Aviation✓ 6 outlets · when the court will take it up has not been reported

Environmental groups and local residents go to court against the new Schiphol decree, which allows 478,000 flight movements a year

Environmental groups and local residents are going to court against the new airport traffic decree for Schiphol, Trouw, NU.nl, AD, Het Parool and De Telegraaf reported on Thursday, October 22. TPO names Mobilisation for the Environment, Greenpeace Netherlands, Milieudefensie, Foundation S4R, SchipholWatch and the Foundation for the Right to Protection against Aircraft Nuisance as the filers. NU.nl quotes them on ‘illegal nuisance’ and Het Parool on ‘years of illegal use of Schiphol’.

According to TPO and the ANP, which Trouw publishes, the decree of minister Vincent Karremans (VVD) allows a maximum of 478,000 flight movements a year, of which at most 27,000 at night. It takes effect on November 1 and is the first new airport traffic decree in 18 years. The groups will go to the Council of State as soon as the decree is published; according to the ANP, that had not yet happened. They want the decree to be annulled. De Telegraaf headlines with Schiphol’s response: ‘Important that there will be certainty for local residents’.

Major fire in the historic city centre of Delft right next to the Nieuwe Kerk: ten homes evacuated, fire brigade gets the blaze under control

A major fire broke out in the centre of Delft on Thursday morning, October 22, right next to the Nieuwe Kerk, NOS, De Telegraaf, AD, NU.nl and Omroep West reported. According to NOS and De Telegraaf, ten homes have been evacuated. Omroep West names the Kerkstraat as the location of the fire. De Telegraaf first wrote about the danger of the fire spreading to neighbouring buildings and later that the fire was under control; AD reports the same.

AD writes that residents were taken in at a café. De Telegraaf quotes a resident: ‘The fire brigade came to get me, I am only wearing pyjamas’. In another report, De Telegraaf describes residents who had to leave their homes in a hurry: ‘First grabbed the cats, then our wallets’. NU.nl published a video of smoke pouring out of a café right next to the church. What caused the fire cannot be found in the coverage.
