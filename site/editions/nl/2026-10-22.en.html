PHP vulnerability takes millions of websites down, DigiD and hospitals offline too

After warnings about a critical flaw in the coding language, many sites went offline as a precaution on Wednesday; an update is available, but recovery will take time.

Several large websites were taken offline on Wednesday after warnings about vulnerabilities in the widely used coding language PHP, AD reported. In the headline of a live report, the paper wrote that sites around DigiD and several hospitals were also unreachable. According to AD, local Dutch authorities and blogs running on WordPress, among others, took (part of) their websites offline as a precaution. In another report, AD cited the warning that there is a ‘danger that sites will be taken over’. Cloudflare, a cybersecurity company, had already seen the first attacks aimed at the vulnerabilities in PHP, according to AD and De Telegraaf. In the course of the day AD spoke of major cyberattacks worldwide after crucial flaws in the coding language. Other live reports from Dutch media also mentioned the impact across the world, while administrators waited for a crucial update for the affected software.

Later on Wednesday AD reported that a security update is available for the affected websites, but that the problems will persist for a while. De Telegraaf wrote on Wednesday evening that the millions of websites that went dark as a precaution are slowly coming back online. According to the paper, PHP itself admitted fault after the problems and spoke of ‘a structural failure’. De Telegraaf put that statement in the headline of its report on the consequences of the outage for the internet. Which organizations exactly switched off their sites, and how long recovery takes per site, differs per administrator according to the reports. How many websites have actually been taken over has not been confirmed.
