Ukraine resumes attacks on Russian refineries, despite American pressure

After a three-day pause, President Zelensky has the army hit Russian energy targets again. He is angry with Washington over missing intelligence and a restricted Starlink connection. Russia reports an attack; little has been confirmed about the damage.

Ukraine is having its army attack Russian energy targets again. Because Russia did not abide by the energy truce, the pause in attacks is over, President Zelensky said in an interview with the Financial Times (NOS, AD, NU.nl, FD, Reuters). The pause lasted three days and followed American President Trump’s call for such a truce (NOS, Bloomberg). Moscow, meanwhile, kept attacking Ukrainian energy infrastructure and civilian targets, according to Bloomberg. Zelensky points out that Russia itself carried out attacks earlier (NU.nl); De Telegraaf headlines with his words ‘We’re done with it’. Russia reports an attack on a refinery (NU.nl). According to the regional governor, a fire broke out at the Ryazan refinery after a drone attack (Reuters). We have not seen confirmation of how much damage there was. Ukraine also says it hit six Russian Su-34 bombers at an airfield (De Telegraaf, NU.nl, AD). The accounts differ: one source speaks of six destroyed aircraft, an English-language report quotes Zelensky on four destroyed and two damaged. These are Ukrainian figures; we have not seen independent confirmation.

Tension with Washington is meanwhile rising; the NOS calls it the biggest conflict between Zelensky and Trump since their confrontation in the White House. De Telegraaf quotes Zelensky lamenting the loss of the U.S. as an ally, but saying that this is not Ukraine’s doing. In that same interview Zelensky said he was angry about the ‘dangerous’ halt to the sharing of American intelligence and the throttling of Starlink (FT, AD). On Wednesday the Ukrainian army noticed that access to the satellite network was restricted (NU.nl). Later the military part, Starshield, would be fully working again, Reuters reports on the basis of a source at the Ukrainian Ministry of Defense (NOS). Elon Musk points to the American government and to Trump (AD, BBC, CNBC). According to an English-language report, the White House denies that it restricted the service (one source). Musk also said that Zelensky must ‘go’ (AFP, Kyiv Post). On top of this comes the criticism of the diesel deal that Trump announced with Russia last Friday and that is meant to bring millions of barrels of diesel onto the world market (NOS). Prime Minister Jetten wants the deal thrown in the trash (NU.nl) and called on both Trump and Musk to keep helping Ukraine (AD). According to the Volkskrant, the deal covers less than a week of American consumption. The NOS wonders whether Russia can deliver the diesel at all now that the refineries are under fire again. The FT also reports that American negotiators in Miami proposed releasing part of the frozen Russian assets and that European officials rejected this (FT, Reuters). We have not seen a reaction from Trump to the resumption.

Albert Heijn is fully restocking its stores again, but delivers nothing through Sunday

Albert Heijn said on Thursday that regular deliveries of shelf-stable and fresh products to stores across the country have fully resumed (NOS, NU.nl, AD, FD, L1 Nieuws). On Thursday morning several branches still had empty shelves, especially for vegetables and fruit, although more was being stocked (NU.nl, AD). According to De Telegraaf, the problems began on Sunday. Later in the day came bad news for anyone ordering online: Albert Heijn will deliver no groceries through Sunday at least, and new orders cannot be placed (NOS, NU.nl, AD, De Telegraaf). Customers who had already paid will be compensated as soon as the systems work (NU.nl). Competitors profited in recent days: childcare staff did their shopping elsewhere (NU.nl) and online supermarket Picnic got busier (AD).

The company is bringing its systems back in order ‘step by step’ after what it calls a cybersecurity incident (AD, De Telegraaf). The NOS speaks of an (attempted) hack attack, after which the systems were taken offline as a precaution early this week. What exactly happened and who is behind it has not been confirmed. Margaret Versteden, recently appointed top executive of Ahold Delhaize Europe and Indonesia, is temporarily responsible for Albert Heijn (FD, AD). A researcher at Clingendael tells the AD of being surprised that there was no backup; that is one voice. According to NU.nl, the Netherlands is poorly prepared for a major cyber threat. We have not seen whether customer data were taken.

Picnic-PostNL cycling team commissions investigation after stories of a culture of fear

NOS Sport and the cycling platform In de Waaier published a major investigation into the Picnic-PostNL team on Thursday. According to the publication, a culture of fear has prevailed for years: riders and staff members feel unsafe and some left the team traumatized (NOS, AD, NU.nl, De Telegraaf). In total, 71 people involved speak out (NOS, WielerFlits). The team says it does not recognize itself in the signals, but is commissioning an independent, professional investigation into how the team culture is experienced (NOS, NU.nl, De Telegraaf). It announced this the same day, shortly after the publication. According to De Telegraaf, the signals have been reported in more places. Who will carry out that investigation and when the outcome will be known, we have not seen.

Sporting director Rudi Kemna reacted with shock (NOS), and the team management and the riders are shaken too (NU.nl, AD). Former riders such as Marcel Kittel and Tom Veelers recognize the stories (NOS), and Nicolas Roche says the management is ‘not suited to cycling’ (NOS). The cycling federation KNWU calls the signals worrying (AD). During the Eneco Tour the team reacted visibly shaken (AD). The AD writes that the team’s future is at stake; that is the paper’s interpretation. The allegations have not yet been tested by an independent investigation. Until then it remains a picture sketched by those involved, which the team does not recognize itself in.

Netherlands · PoliticsPress ✓ 6 outlets · support in the House still uncertain

Health care budget still without a majority after two days of debate

The House of Representatives debated the cabinet’s health care budget for two days, and there is no majority yet (NU.nl, NOS, De Telegraaf, FD). The opposition first wants to see the cabinet work out the planned cuts better, while time is starting to run short (NU.nl). NRC speaks of a multibillion cut and calls support still far off, even though health minister Hermans wants to sit down with an ‘open mind’. The postponement of the increase in the deductible was meant to remove the biggest hurdle, but from left to right there still seems to be insufficient support for the plans of Hermans (VVD) and Sterk (CDA), according to De Telegraaf.

The NOS reports that Hermans got many questions about how the chronically ill will be compensated for the disappearance of the healthcare deduction in 2028. Parties also clashed sharply over late abortion (Reformatorisch Dagblad), and the SGP warned the cabinet with a motion of disapproval over abortion policy (De Telegraaf). How much the cuts amount to exactly and which parties can be persuaded is not in the reports we saw. EenVandaag shows a chronically ill person who remains in uncertainty for longer, and the FD wonders whether this cabinet has enough support for its health spending at all. Whether and when the House votes, we have not seen.

Chip stocks sink deep on fears of an AI bubble and higher rates, but the AEX keeps its loss limited

On the Amsterdam stock exchange, chip stocks fell hard on Thursday. ASMI was the biggest loser during trading according to the FD, down 8.5 percent, Besi lost 6.1 percent and ASML 5.2 percent. According to De Telegraaf it was the heaviest loss for chip stocks in fifteen months. The AEX stood 1.1 percent lower in the morning, but the chip companies recovered somewhat as the day went on and the index closed 0.4 percent lower at 1,132.58 points (price data). That the loss stayed limited was because investors cutting risk ended up at defensive companies, according to De Telegraaf; KPN was mentioned as a safe haven. Fastned went down after disappointing quarterly figures (De Telegraaf).

The sell-off was global. The reports cite as reasons stronger American inflation and worries about an interest-rate hike (De Telegraaf, Guardian), and doubt whether profits from artificial intelligence are sustainable given overcapacity and heavy investment (De Telegraaf, FD). Debt worries at the AI cloud company CoreWeave also weighed on sentiment (Reuters, Bloomberg). In Asia, markets plunged (FT, Guardian). Bloomberg counts a loss in value of about $1 trillion for chip stocks; Reuters writes that the American chip sector risks losing $2 trillion. Those are different measures, so we do not add them up. At the same time TSMC surprised positively: the profit of the world’s largest chipmaker beat expectations (FT), and revenue rose 50 percent to a record (Tweakers, one source). We do not have a Wall Street close for Thursday; De Telegraaf reported a positive picture in the evening.
