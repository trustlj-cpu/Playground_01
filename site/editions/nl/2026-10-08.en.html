Peak-hour power costs more from 2029. The more you run on electricity, the more you pay

The ACM wants the grid tariff for households to depend on when and how much electricity you use. Six in ten households come out ahead. A large home with a heat pump and an electric car pays hundreds of euros more.

Regulator ACM wants households to pay a grid tariff from 2029 that depends on the time at which they use electricity. Power at peak hours becomes more expensive, power at off-peak hours cheaper. NOS, NU.nl, de Volkskrant and De Telegraaf reported the proposal on Thursday. It concerns only the fee for transporting electricity over the grid, roughly a third of the electricity bill; the supply price and taxes do not change. Today a household pays a fixed amount of around €360 a year to the grid operator. Under the proposal about a third stays fixed, around €120, and the other two thirds depend on how much electricity you use and when. There will be four price levels and five time blocks a day. In winter, electricity is most expensive between 4 p.m. and 11 p.m., in summer between 7 p.m. and midnight. These amounts are indications based on this year’s costs. Anyone with solar panels may actually benefit in summer: on sunny afternoons, using the grid becomes cheap.

The ACM expects about 60 percent of households to come out ahead. A small apartment pays €100 to €200 less per year. Those who run a lot on electricity come out worse: a detached house with a heat pump and an electric car pays €300 to €450 more per year, even if the residents shift their use as well as they can. De Volkskrant pointed out that it is precisely large homes that have already gone green that get the bill. That clashes with Wednesday’s cabinet plan to make the hybrid heat pump the standard from 2029. With the new tariffs the ACM wants to reduce grid congestion: the power grid fills up mainly at the moments when everyone cooks, charges and heats at once. Anyone who charges their car at night, or runs the washing machine during the day when the sun is shining, can save money. In recent years grid operators have had to make tens of thousands of businesses and new housing developments wait for a connection. According to the ACM, spreading out use can prevent some of those expensive expansions, and in time lower costs for everyone. Energy suppliers will have to build the new tariffs into their contracts; a smart meter is needed for that. It is still a proposal. The ACM will decide later, and the exact tariffs for 2029 will only be set at the end of 2028.

Defence · PoliticsPress ✓ 4 outlets

Yesilgöz: Russia is no longer just the aggressor, but ‘our enemy’

Defence Minister Yesilgöz will from now on call Russia ‘our enemy’ and no longer just ‘the aggressor’. De Telegraaf, de Volkskrant, NU.nl and NOS reported the remark. NU.nl wrote that the cabinet’s tone is hardening. According to NOS, the new wording does not mean the Netherlands is suddenly choosing a different approach.

The words come in a week full of signals. In Germany a former head of the intelligence service BND was arrested, in Britain a navy serviceman is suspected of spying for Russia, and two Latvians were detained after entering an air force base. European services have long spoken of hybrid attacks: sabotage, cyberattacks and drones that stay just below the threshold of war. The Public Prosecution Service (OM) also turned out on Thursday to have been the target of a ‘state actor’. Which country that was has not been disclosed. The question for the cabinet now is what the new tone means for defence spending, for protecting cables and networks, and for support for Ukraine.

Nobel Prize in Literature goes to Canadian poet and classicist Anne Carson

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature goes to the Canadian writer Anne Carson. She is a poet, essayist and translator, and also a classicist: she translated and adapted Greek poets and playwrights. NRC spoke of a daring body of work; Trouw called her sharp and critical, with an eye for human frailty.

Carson is known for books that mix genres: stories in verse, essays that read like poems. Her best-known work, Autobiography of Red, retells a Greek myth as a modern love story. She had been on lists of contenders for years. The Nobel Peace Prize follows on Friday.

AviationPress ✓ 3 outlets · number of cancelled flights not confirmed

KLM cancels flights: too many aircraft grounded for maintenance

KLM has to cancel flights because part of its fleet needs maintenance or repair. NOS, NU.nl and De Telegraaf reported it on Thursday. According to NOS, it is being done as a precaution. How many flights and which destinations are affected, we could not yet confirm in two sources.

According to a quote in De Telegraaf, there are no easy solutions. Worldwide, airlines are struggling with long waits for maintenance and parts, especially for engines. Travellers with a cancelled flight are entitled to rebooking or a refund, and sometimes to compensation.

Housing market cools: more choice for buyers, less overbidding. Stock market closes slightly higher

House hunters have more to choose from, and prices are rising more slowly. NRC, the FD, de Volkskrant and De Telegraaf reported new figures on the purchase market on Thursday. There are more homes for sale per house hunter, and buyers bid less often and not as far above the asking price. NRC wrote that the worst heat wave in the housing market is over. Against that stands the mortgage rate, which has risen along with the rate on government bonds.

On the Damrak, the AEX rose 0.31 percent to 1,121.20 points after a red start. Shell helped: the oil price rose by more than 4 percent after the Houthis attacked airports in Saudi Arabia. A barrel of Brent rose above $105. The euro held around $1.12.
