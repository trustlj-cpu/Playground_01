Marianne Vos (39) becomes world champion for the fifteenth time. Same day: Mollema says farewell and Seixas (20) wins Lombardy

Vos won the Gravel World Championships in Australia on Saturday, her second world title on unpaved terrain. In Italy, Bauke Mollema rode his last race as a pro; Frenchman Paul Seixas became the youngest winner ever of the Tour of Lombardy.

Marianne Vos won her fifteenth world title on Saturday. The 39-year-old rider became world champion in Australia at the Gravel World Championships, a race over unpaved roads, gravel and sand tracks. World championships in this discipline have existed since 2022; Vos won the world title in 2024 with a sprint against Lotte Kopecky. It is her second title in this discipline, after her win in 2024 in Leuven, Belgium (NRC, NOS). According to the Newcastle Herald, this year’s championships are held in Nannup and run through Sunday. After the race Vos told NOS that the title is ‘special’ after a ‘difficult year’; what made that year so difficult was not spelled out in the reports we saw. Back in 2024, NOS wrote that she held world titles in four cycling disciplines: road, cyclo-cross, track and gravel. That a 39-year-old rider stays at the top for so long made her a cycling icon in Saturday’s reports. Details of how the race unfolded, such as the distance and who finished second and third, we did not confirm in the reports we saw and therefore leave out.

On the same day, the last of the year’s five monuments was ridden in Italy. The Tour of Lombardy counts as one of the monuments, the biggest one-day races. Paul Seixas won solo and at 20 is the youngest winner ever (NU.nl, NOS, AD, Telegraaf). For the Frenchman it is his first monument (Wielerflits, AD). For the Netherlands the day belonged mostly to Bauke Mollema (39). He won Lombardy himself in 2019 and now, after almost two decades in the peloton (NRC), rode his last race as a pro; Wielerflits wrote that he suffered ‘more than ever’ in ‘his’ Lombardy. He crossed the line last, in tears, and said ‘Glad it’s over’ (NU.nl, NOS, Telegraaf). Remco Evenepoel finished off the podium; according to Wielerflits he said he has not found his top form since the Tour, a claim we did not see confirmed.

The start was less festive. Five minutes before the departure the peloton protested: ‘We do not accept deaths’ was the message (De Telegraaf, with reports in several outlets). According to NU.nl it was a statement against unsafe races. We have not yet seen the UCI, the international cycling union, or the race organizers respond on the substance. It ties in with the discussion about rider safety, on which the peloton clearly spoke out on Saturday. For anyone who wants to watch on Sunday: the Gravel World Championships go on, according to the Newcastle Herald through Sunday. Whether Dutch riders are also fighting for the title there, we have not seen confirmed.

Sport · Formula 1Press ✓ 9 outlets · results from NOS and NU.nl

Verstappen wins the sprint in Singapore and takes his 50th pole. On Sunday he wants to win ‘for real’

Max Verstappen won the first sprint race at this circuit in Singapore on Saturday, after chaos caused by heavy rain, which delayed the start (NU.nl). George Russell was the first of five retirements (Reuters, NOS, NU.nl, Telegraaf). Verstappen called the win ‘not a real win’: ‘I really want to win the Grand Prix’, and he found his start ‘disastrous again’ (NU.nl). Qualifying was then postponed and run later on Saturday (AD). In it he took the fiftieth pole position of his career, ahead of, among others, the Ferraris of Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton (Telegraaf, AD; French media).

He has never won the main race in Singapore and sees the start as the most important moment: ‘Painful when you lose the race there’ (NU.nl), ‘Start is the most important’ (NOS). After the pole, too, he named a clear point for improvement at Red Bull, according to the Telegraaf: ‘There is no excuse for this’. GPblog reports that after the sprint he moved closer to Leclerc in the championship; that is one source. In a big interview with De Telegraaf he talked about his anniversary, his daughter Lily and the change at Red Bull; about quitting he said: ‘I was really up against it’. The race starts on Sunday.

Sport · FootballPress ✓ 6 outlets · final scores from NOS and AD

Feyenoord win the big match against AZ and top the table. Ajax drop points at home again against NEC

Feyenoord won the big match against AZ at De Kuip on Saturday and are leaders of the Eredivisie for the first time in almost a year (NOS, NU.nl, AD, Telegraaf). Nacho Ferri opened the scoring after a mistake by Wouter Goes; left back Javi López doubled the lead (AD). Striking was the VAR: a penalty for AZ was overturned after a long check. AZ striker Mexx Meerdink spoke of ‘the wrong images’ to referee Danny Makkelie (Telegraaf, NOS, NU.nl). The KNVB has not commented on this, as far as we saw.

At Ajax it went wrong against NEC (1-1). Tolu headed Ajax ahead early; NEC then equalized through a penalty from Chery after a foul by Mokio (AD). Ajax dropped points at home for the fourth time and fall further behind Feyenoord and PSV. The return of Dušan Tadić, now at NEC, was warmly received with a banner; supporters’ group F-Side celebrated its fiftieth anniversary with fireworks (AD, Parool). FC Twente drew 1-1 at Fortuna Sittard after a goal from a corner by Robin Pröpper (NOS, AD). According to some sports sites, Ajax escaped a straight red card (Voetbalzone); opinions on that are divided.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 7 outlets · figures according to Saudi authorities

At least twelve dead and more than three hundred injured in attack on Riyadh airport. Saudi Arabia points to the Houthis

An attack on King Khalid International Airport in the Saudi capital Riyadh killed at least twelve people and injured more than three hundred on Saturday, the Saudi authorities say (AP, NOS, NU.nl, Telegraaf, FinancialJuice). The Saudi-led coalition military in Yemen holds the Houthis responsible, calls the attack a war crime and announced a ‘resolute’ response. The Houthis are the rebels who control large parts of Yemen. Flights were cancelled and the airport halted operations (Reuters, FT, NOS). UN chief Guterres condemned the attack, President Trump called it ‘a terrible thing’ and Secretary of State Rubio said an American was killed.

According to NU.nl, the US is considering intervening. It is an escalation: earlier this week attacks hit the airports of Riyadh and Abha, killing three according to the Saudi aviation authority (Khaleej Times, Gulf News). According to the coalition, a missile was intercepted near Al-Kharj and a projectile fell near Dammam airport (FinancialJuice). Major Saudi conferences are going ahead, according to the organizers (Reuters). What the attack means for the oil price, we have not yet seen: the markets were closed. That Dutch travelers or KLM were affected, we have not seen confirmed.

Markets · EconomyPress ✓ 3 outlets · Friday closing prices from price data

Markets closed: the AEX closed 0.87 percent higher on Friday. On Monday the attack on Riyadh will factor in

There is no stock trading on Sunday. The last prices are from Friday: the AEX closed at 1,130.95 points, 0.87 percent higher. On Wall Street the S&P 500 gained 0.59 percent to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq 0.64 percent to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83 percent to 51,654.95. The American ten-year yield came in at 5.244 percent, 1.3 basis points higher. American oil (WTI) closed at $91.85 a barrel, 0.39 percent higher, and gold rose 1.43 percent to $4,216.30 an ounce. The euro stood at around $1.1206, 0.09 percent lower.

The prices predate the attack on Riyadh airport, which took place on Saturday. On Monday traders will have to judge whether it affects the oil price and interest rates. Euronext Amsterdam reopens on Monday, October 12. The Nobel Prize in economics follows then, too. On Friday Reuters expected that, in the coming season of quarterly results, it would mainly be AI companies delivering profit growth in the US. We do not give a market forecast of our own.
