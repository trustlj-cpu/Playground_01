The US puts the entire International Criminal Court on its sanctions list, hours after the Nobel Prize for former judge Pillay

For the first time Washington is hitting the institution itself, not just its judges and staff. The cabinet in The Hague, the court’s host country, condemns the move but seeks dialogue. Russia asked this week for the extradition of the court’s president.

On Friday the United States put the International Criminal Court (ICC) on its sanctions list as an institution. Until now Washington had hit only individual judges, prosecutors and staff. Reuters was first to report the step; AP, NOS, NU.nl, de Volkskrant, The Guardian and CBS followed. According to CBS, Secretary of State Rubio wants to dismantle the court ‘brick by brick’ if it does not change. The court, founded in 2002, prosecutes individuals for war crimes, genocide and crimes against humanity; many countries are members, the US is not. According to NOS, his statement does leave room: the member states can negotiate a solution to the American objections. The timing is striking. The sanctions came hours after the Nobel Peace Prize was awarded to Navi Pillay, a South African jurist, former ICC judge and former UN High Commissioner for Human Rights. According to The Hill, the court ‘firmly’ rejects the sanctions and says it will continue its work (Reuters); president Tomoko Akane said in a video statement that she will not be intimidated (Mainichi, Asahi).

For the Netherlands this is no distant matter. The court sits in The Hague, which makes the Netherlands its host country. Prime Minister Jetten called the sanctions ‘deeply regrettable’ and wants to talk to the US (NU.nl, NOS). He is glad that, according to NOS, there are still six months to find a solution, and would not say which countermeasures the Netherlands is considering. The cabinet says it exerted heavy diplomatic pressure for months to prevent new sanctions. At the UN at the end of September Jetten still said ‘hands off the court’ (NOS). Professor De Hoon called Friday’s tone mild compared with those earlier words: those who stay merely diplomatic, he says, will see Trump keep going further. Outside the Netherlands the tone is sharper. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas calls protecting the court a European priority; UN human rights chief Volker Türk speaks of unacceptable measures. According to Mainichi, eight foreign ministers, including those of Japan, the United Kingdom, France and Germany, declared themselves against the sanctions. Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu, according to Mainichi, praised the sanctions as a defense against a threat to national sovereignty. Russia asked the Netherlands and other countries this week to extradite Akane (Japan Times, NHK, Asahi); we saw no Dutch response. What the measures mean in practice for payments and businesses around the court has not yet been confirmed. This weekend, watch for the European response and for whether the Netherlands comes up with concrete protection.

Russia is supplying diesel to the US and the world market, says Trump. Zelensky: an investment in the war

After a phone call with Putin, President Trump said on Friday that Russia is ‘immediately’ delivering 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the United States. According to his post on Truth Social, more will follow, up to three million tonnes. The Kremlin confirmed the call and said it was ready to supply the US and the world market (Le Monde). At the same time the US Treasury issued, through OFAC, a temporary general license for imports of Russian diesel (FinancialJuice). Reuters, AP, NOS, NU.nl and the FD carried the news.

President Zelensky called it an ‘investment in the war’, according to Reuters; to Axios he said the deal is ‘not fair’. Whether the price at the pump will fall is uncertain. Earlier in the day Reuters wrote that Trump’s measures had not yet lowered the diesel price. For the Netherlands it matters that Europe buys on the same world market. American oil (WTI) closed on Friday at $91.63 a barrel, almost 0.2 percent higher.

The Netherlands returns the Banjarmasin diamond and nineteen other objects to Indonesia

The Netherlands is returning twenty objects that came to the Netherlands in the colonial era. Eighteen come from the Wereldmuseum, two from the Rijksmuseum: the Banjarmasin diamond, weighing more than 38 carats, and the sword Sikin Panjang. A handwritten Koran of Teuku Umar is also part of the group. NOS, NU.nl, NRC and de Volkskrant carried the report. The transfer was signed in Amsterdam on Friday by Indonesia’s Minister of Culture, Fadli Zon, and the Dutch minister Letschert.

Letschert called the return a repair of historical injustice; in her view the objects should never have been in the Netherlands (Volkskrant). In doing so she follows the advice of the Colonial Collections Committee. The physical departure will take a while: according to a spokesperson, the pieces will go to Indonesia only early next year. What happens to the diamond afterwards and who receives it is not worked out in the reports we saw.

Sport · FootballPress ✓ 4 outlets · results from NOS and NU.nl

The Oranje women win 0-8 in Hungary and take a big step toward the World Cup. PSV win as the Eredivisie restarts

The Oranje women won the first leg of their World Cup 2027 play-offs in Hungary 0-8 on Friday. Victoria Pelova and Lineth Beerensteyn each scored a hat-trick; Esmee Brugts and Damaris Egurrola scored the other two goals (NOS, NU.nl, Volkskrant, Telegraaf). The second leg is on Tuesday in Tilburg. According to NOS, whoever wins the two-legged tie plays Slovenia or Turkey for a place at the World Cup.

In the Eredivisie, which resumed after the international break, PSV beat Heerenveen 2-0. According to Volkskrant and NU.nl, the team did not convince despite the win; Guus Til scored the first goal. UEFA punished Serbia on Friday for discrimination during matches against Oranje and Greece (NU.nl).

Markets · EconomyPress ✓ 3 outlets · AEX close from price data

The AEX closes 0.87 percent higher, and so does Wall Street. Interest rates and fuel costs remain the worry

The AEX closed on Friday at 1,130.95 points, 0.87 percent higher than on Thursday. During trading De Telegraaf spoke of a record high for the index and reported that ArcelorMittal rose sharply while ASML was under pressure; we have not seen a second source for that record. On Wall Street the S&P 500 closed 0.59 percent higher at 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq gained 0.64 percent to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83 percent to 51,654.95 (Bloomberg Television, closing prices). The euro stood at $1.1206 late on Friday evening. On Saturday Euronext Amsterdam is closed; trading resumes on Monday.

The worries have not gone away. The American ten-year yield ended at 5.244 percent. Pimco chief Ivascyn told the FT, according to Reuters, that the rate could rise to 6 percent for the first time since 2000. American consumer sentiment worsened further in October (Reuters), and Delta Air Lines cut its profit forecast because of a $6 billion fuel bill (Reuters). Gold rose 1.51 percent to $4,219.90 an ounce; American oil (WTI) came in at $91.63. Next week the earnings season begins: Reuters expects AI companies to deliver most of the profit growth in the US.
