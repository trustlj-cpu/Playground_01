Trump says Russia will release diesel to world markets after a call with Putin. Kyiv calls it 'an investment in war', and Brent still settles above $104

Washington licensed Russian diesel imports the same day. Oil barely moved, the FTSE 100 gained 1.06% and the Bank of England is still expected to raise rates.

President Donald Trump said on Friday that he had held a successful call with Russian President Vladimir Putin and that Russia would release diesel onto U.S. and global markets, according to Reuters, AP, the BBC, the Financial Times and CNBC. His post on Truth Social named 300,000 tonnes immediately, 500,000 in November and 1 million after that, with 3 million tonnes to follow 'soon', depending on the state of Russian refineries. Russia's deputy prime minister, Alexander Novak, said export restrictions on diesel were being lifted at once, although he put the 1 million tonne tranche in December. The Kremlin said the 90-minute call concentrated on Ukraine and touched on Iran, that Mr Putin would consider when to restart peace talks, and that both men were interested in attending the APEC summit in China in November. The U.S. Treasury's OFAC then issued a temporary general licence allowing Russian-origin diesel to be sold, delivered and imported, including into the United States, until 7 April 2027. AP described the move as relaxing pressure on Moscow to ease prices before the 3 November midterm elections. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy called it 'an investment in war', and Axios reported that Ukraine's government was surprised. Reuters noted that Mr Trump's earlier steps to lift diesel supply have not yet lowered prices, and sources told FinancialJuice that a Trump directive would push officials to use the Defense Production Act and to bypass state rules that block energy production.

Markets took it calmly. Brent settled at $104.72 a barrel, up 0.42%, and WTI at $91.63, up 0.15%, FinancialJuice reported. The bigger physical shock is Hurricane Isaias: the consultancy MMA put the U.S. Gulf oil shut-in at 1.5 million barrels a day, or 72% of output, up from 63% on Thursday. In London the FTSE 100 closed 1.06% higher at 10,552.05, recovering from Thursday's 10,441.60. An Irish Times market report tied the gain to a retreat in gilt yields. The exchange is closed today, so that is the last reading until Monday. Sterling ended near $1.3233. The ratings agency S&P Global affirmed the UK's AA rating with a stable outlook, raised its 2026 growth estimate to 1.3% and said it expects the Bank of England to raise rates by 0.25 percentage points in late 2026, FinancialJuice reported. On Wall Street the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow 0.83% to 51,654.95; Bloomberg said tech shares rebounded from Thursday's scare over OpenAI's revenue. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.244%, and Pimco's Ivascyn told the FT it could reach 6%, a level last seen in 2000. Gold gained 1.51% to $4,219.9.

Labour holds Sir Keir Starmer's old seat of Holborn and St Pancras, but its majority over the Greens is 3,364

Sagal Abdi-Wali, the Labour leader of Camden Council, won the by-election with 12,292 votes against 8,928 for the Green Party leader, Zack Polanski, ITV reported. Camden Council put turnout at 38.76%. Reform UK's Peter Newman took 1,612 votes, behind the Conservative, Ewan Cameron, on 1,922, and the Workers Party 314. Sir Keir won 48.9% in 2024, while Ms Abdi-Wali's share was about 45%. The Guardian said the result shows Labour starting to win back voters on the left, and asked whether the Greens are now at a crossroads after failing to take the seat.

UK · Northern IrelandPress ✓ 6 outlets · the police inquiry is at an early stage; no one has been charged

Northern Ireland Secretary bans the Drumcree parade. First Minister Michelle O'Neill is invited to a police interview over the protest

Chris Bryant, the Northern Ireland Secretary, announced on Friday that the Orange Order's Drumcree parade would not be allowed along the Garvaghy Road in Portadown, the BBC, Sky News, the Guardian and the Irish Times reported. The Parades Commission, the independent body that rules on contested marches, had allowed the walk with restrictions, but a large protest on the road stopped it. Mr Bryant said the circumstances had changed considerably and that the Orange Order and nationalist residents could not reach an accommodation. Reports say he acted after a request from the PSNI chief constable, Jon Boutcher, to review the ruling. The Taoiseach, Micheál Martin, said it would bring relief to the Garvaghy Road community, and the Orange Order had warned of 'serious consequences' if the march were stopped.

The BBC, Sky News and the Irish Times also reported that Northern Ireland's First Minister, Michelle O'Neill of Sinn Féin, received a police letter inviting her to an interview about her part in the protest. She said she would co-operate and that she was 'proud to stand with the residents'. The PSNI said it was writing to people 'reasonably suspected of committing offences' and did not name anyone. Unionist politicians have called for action against Sinn Féin figures who joined the protest.

World · LawPress ✓ 6 outlets · the full text of the UK statement was not checked

U.S. sanctions the International Criminal Court hours after a former ICC judge wins the Nobel Peace Prize. The UK and seven allies back the court

Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced sanctions on the International Criminal Court on Friday, hours after the Nobel committee named Navi Pillay, a South African former judge of the court, as the 2026 Peace Prize winner, Reuters, AP, the BBC, the FT and NBC News reported. Mr Rubio called it a 'rogue court' and said the United States would cut off transactions with it. Reuters reported that the measures go further than earlier ones and could penalise companies that provide services to the court. Reports tie the move to the court's arrest warrants for Israel's prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, and its former defence minister, Yoav Gallant.

An ICC prosecutor condemned the sanctions and said the court's work would continue. The foreign ministers of the United Kingdom, Canada, Denmark, France, Germany, Italy, Japan and the Netherlands issued a joint statement reaffirming their 'continued and unwavering support' for the court, according to the reports. Ms Pillay, a former UN human rights chief, also chaired the UN inquiry into the occupied Palestinian territory.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · Saudi-led coalition claims come from a single source and are attributed

Houthi strikes on Riyadh airport kill three. The EU aviation safety body widens its Saudi airspace warning and the UK condemns the escalation

Saudi authorities said three people were killed in Houthi-claimed attacks on Riyadh airport, Reuters and AP reported. Reuters added that the European Union Aviation Safety Agency widened its warning on Saudi airspace after the strikes. Lufthansa and Indian airlines had already suspended flights to Riyadh. At the UN Security Council the UK said it 'unequivocally condemns' the Houthis' dangerous escalation, according to the Foreign Office statement.

FinancialJuice reported that a Saudi-led coalition said it was carrying out a wide-ranging operation against Houthi forces and capabilities in Yemen, and that Mr Trump said he was not pleased with the situation in Saudi Arabia. Separately, Reuters and the Guardian reported that UAE prosecutors say a co-pilot with flydubai planned a suicide attack on a flight to Tel Aviv.
