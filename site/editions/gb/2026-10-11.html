UK · YorkshirePress ✓ 8 outlets · the man has not been named; the details of how he got in are still under investigation

Man dies after entering the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park. Police say the tiger was unhurt and there is no wider risk to the public

The park near Doncaster was evacuated on Saturday afternoon and stays shut today. Police have asked people not to share the footage that circulated online.

A man died on Saturday after entering the tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park, near Doncaster, according to the BBC, the Guardian, Reuters, the Evening Standard and ABC News. South Yorkshire Police said officers were called at 1.35pm to reports of a man entering the enclosure. The tiger then attacked him, the force said, and he was pronounced dead at the scene despite the efforts of medical staff. His family has been told and is being supported by specialist officers. He has not been named. The park said it was dealing with a 'serious incident' and that its emergency procedures were 'immediately activated'. Visitors were evacuated, and the attraction will stay closed on Sunday as well as Saturday.

The Guardian and the Evening Standard said videos posted online appeared to show a man standing inside the enclosure before the attack, and police urged people not to share distressing footage. The Mirror, the only outlet to report it so far, said the man asked a couple to take a photograph of him before he went in, and credited zookeepers with ending the attack. Police have not confirmed either detail. Early reports from the Mirror, the York Press and The Star spoke of 'shots fired' and a tarpaulin over the enclosure. Police have since said the tiger was not injured and that there is no wider risk to the public. Tigers are an endangered species, and British zoos keep them partly as part of international breeding programmes, so the animal's welfare after the incident will also be a question for the park and its vets.

The investigation will now try to establish how he reached the animal. Big-cat enclosures normally combine a perimeter fence with a second barrier and keeper-only access, so investigators are likely to examine every barrier, whether any was breached or climbed, and whether the man was alone. Zoos in Great Britain are licensed by their local council under the Zoo Licensing Act 1981 and must meet standards on barriers and staff training. A sudden death at a visitor attraction is normally looked into by the police and the Health and Safety Executive, the workplace safety regulator, and referred to a coroner, who opens an inquest. Fatal tiger attacks in Britain are rare. In 2013 a keeper was killed by a tiger at Hamerton Zoo Park in Cambridgeshire, but she was working inside a restricted area, not visiting as a member of the public. No date has been given for the reopening of the Yorkshire park.

UK · ProtestPress ✓ 5 outlets · the crowd size is disputed

Thousands march for Palestine through central London. Police make at least 20 arrests, six after the Battle of Britain memorial was climbed

The Metropolitan Police listed at least 20 arrests at the National March for Palestine on Saturday, ITV, the Evening Standard and the Telegraph reported. Six were for criminal damage after people climbed the Battle of Britain memorial on the Embankment, and seven for breaching conditions the force had imposed, two of them for refusing to remove face coverings. Two people were held for displaying placards supporting Palestine Action, a group the government has proscribed, and two under Section 5 of the Public Order Act, one racially aggravated after a woman allegedly made a Nazi salute. Others were held for assaulting an officer and for a threat to kill linked to an earlier incident. The Telegraph reported a clash with a smaller group of Israel supporters, whom police kept apart from the march. Marchers gathered on the Embankment from midday and walked to Whitehall for a rally; the organisers, the Palestine Coalition, include the Palestine Solidarity Campaign, Stop the War and CND. No official crowd figure has been published. National marches of this kind have been held regularly in London since the war in Gaza began in October 2023, and the government banned Palestine Action in 2025, which is why placards for it now lead to arrests. In Edinburgh, thousands marched from the Mound to the Scottish Parliament, the BBC reported.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 6 outlets · Mr Trump's quotes are as reported; the Miami talks agenda comes from one source

Trump says it is 'time for Ukraine to get a new president' after Zelenskiy attacks the Russian diesel deal. Mr Burnham reaffirms UK sanctions

President Donald Trump said on Saturday that it was time for Ukraine to get a new president, hours after President Volodymyr Zelenskiy called his decision to take Russian diesel a 'weak decision' and a 'gift to Putin', the BBC, AP, Politico and Sky News reported. Mr Trump also said Mr Zelenskiy had better stop attacks on Russian oil refineries and that Ukraine should hold an election, FinancialJuice reported. Russian strikes continued: Reuters said seven people were killed in Zaporizhzhia.

In London, the government's readout on GOV.UK said Prime Minister Burnham spoke to Mr Zelenskiy, stressed that Russia must accept an energy ceasefire and stop strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure, and promised to intensify sanctions. The government said the two would meet in person in the coming days. Reuters reported that Germany is sticking to its Russia sanctions despite the diesel move, and that US, Ukrainian and European negotiators met in Miami; FinancialJuice, alone so far, said the talks covered post-war aid and a possible new security framework.

Sport · Premier LeaguePress ✓ 6 outlets · scores from BBC Sport and the Guardian

Chelsea thrash Bournemouth 5-1, Arsenal come from behind, and ten-man Spurs hold Manchester United. Liverpool host City today

Jordan Henderson scored twice on his home debut as Chelsea beat Bournemouth 5-1 at Stamford Bridge, leaving Bournemouth without a win this season, the BBC and the Guardian reported. At the Emirates, Bruno Guimarães scored the winner as Arsenal beat Leeds 2-1 after trailing; talkSPORT and the Evening Standard said a player was carried off in the warm-down. At Old Trafford, Rodrigo Bentancur was sent off for a headbutt on Luke Shaw, and Dominic Solanke equalised in stoppage time for Tottenham in a 1-1 draw. United's Michael Carrick said he was 'massively disappointed', the Guardian reporting six points from six games, the club's worst start since 1986. Brighton won 2-0 at Sunderland.

Today, Liverpool host Manchester City at Anfield at 4.30pm BST, live on Sky Sports. It is City's first match since the club were found guilty of breaking Premier League financial rules, a verdict they deny and are appealing, the Guardian said.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · the coalition's and Gulf shipping claims come from a single wire and are attributed

Houthi missile strike on Riyadh airport kills 12 and wounds more than 300, Saudi authorities say. Trump calls it 'a terrible thing'

Saudi authorities said 12 people were killed and more than 300 wounded in Saturday's strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, AP and the Guardian reported. Reuters said travellers were evacuated and airlines cancelled flights; the Saudi-led coalition blamed the Houthis, the Yemeni armed movement, called the attack a war crime and said it had shot down a ballistic missile at al-Kharj. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said an American was among the dead, and UN Secretary-General António Guterres condemned the assault. Mr Trump called it 'a terrible thing' and said he would take action; the Mirror alone says he is weighing joining Saudi strikes. The UK embassy told British nationals to avoid the airport.

Shipping is also under strain. FinancialJuice, citing the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre, said a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz was hit by a projectile, while Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a tanker struck a mine. Markets are closed until Monday. On Friday WTI crude settled at $91.85 a barrel, up 0.39%, and gold rose 1.43% to $4,216.3. The S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54, and the 10-year Treasury yield ended at 5.244%.
