UK · CourtsPress ✓ 9 outlets · allegations as told in court, not findings; the plea comes from an earlier New York Times report

First accuser gives evidence at the Russell Brand trial. He is presumed innocent and none of it has yet been tested

The court heard on Thursday from the first woman who accuses him, a day after the Crown called him a 'ruthless' predator. Everything reported so far is one side's account.

The second day of Russell Brand's trial at a London court moved from the Crown's opening to the evidence of the first woman who accuses him. The BBC reported that she told the court the alleged attack left her afraid she might be pregnant. The Guardian and the Evening Standard reported her account that he told her it was 'just fun' as it happened, and the Standard's headline places the alleged incident after a Labour conference. The Telegraph and the Times reported that the court heard he asked her beforehand to photograph him in his underwear, and ABC in Australia said the court was shown photographs of him shirtless in a hotel room. Reuters reported that she told police she blamed herself, and the Standard carried her account that she felt silenced and could not fight back. Complainants in sexual offence cases have lifelong anonymity, so she is not named here.

On Wednesday the Crown described Mr Brand as a 'ruthless', 'calculated and manipulative' sexual predator, a charge carried by ITV News, the Telegraph, the Daily Mail and the Guardian. The Mail and the Dutch paper De Telegraaf reported that prosecutors said he pursued a goal of sleeping with 5,000 women, and the Mail alone reported that the six women he is alleged to have targeted did not know one another. On Thursday he arrived at court saying he felt 'most blessed', the Independent reported. He pleaded not guilty at an earlier stage, according to a New York Times report we cited on Thursday; none of the reports in our window repeated the plea or carried a response from the defence. An opening speech is a statement of what the Crown says it will prove. It is not evidence.

The case is the most widely reported British story of the past day, with at least nine outlets covering it, and it will return each morning while the trial runs. What to watch is how the defence tests the first woman's evidence in cross-examination, and what the other complainants say when their turn comes. We saw no court timetable and no word on whether the court sits today, so we give none. Reports are limited to a fair and accurate account of what is said in court, which means one side is heard before the other. Do not read a verdict into one day of evidence, in either direction. Mr Brand is presumed innocent until a jury decides otherwise.

UK · EnvironmentPress ✓ 7 outlets · the 'driest spell in 100 years' line is the Mirror's alone

Severe drought is declared in parts of England and the Government activates its plan to protect consumers

Severe drought was declared in six areas of England on Thursday, the Guardian reported, and in parts of southern England according to the BBC and Sky News. The Government said it had activated a plan to protect consumers, GOV.UK reported. The BBC ran a guide to the hosepipe bans now in force, and the Evening Standard said water supplies are 'under immense pressure'. The Independent tied the drought to a record-breaking hot summer, and the Mirror said it follows the driest spell in more than 100 years, a claim we found in that paper alone. The Mirror also published a list of the six counties, but the headlines from the other outlets did not name the areas, so check your own water company. Thames Water, which supplies London and the Thames Valley, has asked for a further £2.4bn to avoid going bust this year or in early 2027, the Guardian reported; no other outlet carried that figure in what we saw. The Guardian's Nils Pratley wrote that reform of the sector is not moving much faster. Separately, the Oxford Mail reported that a burst Thames Water pipe left homes in Oxfordshire with little or no supply.

UK · EconomyPress ✓ 9 outlets · Budget details are briefing only; the £9bn figure is the Evening Standard's alone

UK economy grew 0.1% in August ahead of Healey's first Budget, and the Chancellor signals a tax raid on banks

The economy grew by 0.1% in August, the BBC and the Financial Times reported, from figures the Office for National Statistics published at 7am on Thursday. Sky News called it surprise growth, stronger than first thought, and City AM credited a boost in production. City AM's morning live blog had expected the economy to shrink, and the Telegraph framed the same release as a slowdown before the Budget, so outlets differ in tone though not in the number. The Chancellor, John Healey, said during an IMF visit that the Budget must reflect a 'tough time', the Evening Standard reported, and promised to 'stay the path' on cutting the deficit, the FT reported. A Treasury release quoted him saying it is his job to protect the resilience of the economy in the face of international headwinds.

The FT reported that Mr Healey signalled a Budget raid on banks, the Guardian said he is poised to raise taxes on them, and the BBC said banks expect to be hit. No outlet we saw gave a rate or a sum from the Treasury. The Evening Standard carried a union call for a levy of at least £9bn; that figure is the Standard's alone. The lobbying has started. City AM and the Standard reported business chiefs warning against a £1bn rise in an 'office tax' on London, and City AM said Entain and Rank warn that tax rises threaten betting shops and jobs. The Mail reported a letter from 200 bosses asking for business rates to be fixed. The Standard published an explainer on a possible mansion tax; we saw no confirmation that one is planned. The Budget is due on 28 October, as reported earlier this week.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 9 outlets · Starlink and asset claims are as reported by the FT, the Guardian and Kyiv sources

Streeting takes Britain's case for Ukraine to Washington as Zelensky orders new refinery strikes and Musk points at the US over Starlink

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting urged the US to back Ukraine on a visit to Washington, the Guardian reported, after the Starlink row; GOV.UK published his speech to the Center for Strategic and International Studies. It follows Wednesday's London visit by President Zelensky, when Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Britain's support 'will not change', as the BBC reported. Mr Burnham later avoided saying that Mr Trump had betrayed Ukraine, the Evening Standard reported. Kyiv has said Starlink limits hurt its drones. Elon Musk suggested the US government was behind the restrictions, the Guardian and the Daily Mail reported. A defence ministry source told the Kyiv Independent that service has been restored; we found that in no other outlet.

Mr Zelensky said Ukraine will resume strikes on Russian oil refineries, the BBC and Reuters (citing the FT) reported, and Bloomberg said he ordered new strikes. Reuters separately reported a drone attack that set the Ryazan refinery on fire, according to the regional governor; its headline did not link the two. The FT reported that Washington floated unfreezing some Russian assets to restart peace talks and that European officials reject the idea, and Reuters repeated the report. The Kremlin said Russia has restarted diesel exports after a deal with Mr Trump, Reuters reported, and Italian outlets said the White House defended that deal to Giorgia Meloni. The FT also reported that EU leaders will urge Mr Zelensky to rein in his criticism of Mr Trump.

UK · Courts · Northern IrelandPress ✓ 6 outlets · sentence as reported by Reuters, the Guardian, the Mirror and the Evening Standard

Ex-DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson is jailed for 15 years for child sex offences

Jeffrey Donaldson, the former leader of the Democratic Unionist Party, was sentenced on Thursday to 15 years in prison for child sex offences, Reuters, the Guardian, the Mirror and the Evening Standard reported. The BBC had said earlier in the day that the offences include rape. The Standard reported that the judge described his 'catastrophic descent into disgrace' and that he showed no emotion as the sentence was passed. It also reported the DUP saying he 'deserves every day' of it. The Standard called the sentencing the completion of a spectacular fall from grace for the former MP.

The Standard published victims' statements describing a 'stolen childhood', and a separate account of the evidence that led to the conviction, which we do not repeat. The headlines we saw gave no minimum term to be served before release, and none mentioned an appeal, so we report neither. The victims cannot be named under the law on sexual offences, and nothing here identifies them. The sentence closes the criminal case; the political effect on the DUP, and on the wider unionist parties in Northern Ireland, is something the reports we saw did not address.
