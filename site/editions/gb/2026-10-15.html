UK · CourtsPress ✓ 5 outlets · these are the prosecution's allegations, not findings; the plea comes from the New York Times

Prosecutors tell the Russell Brand trial he was a 'ruthless, calculated and manipulative' sexual predator. He is presumed innocent and has pleaded not guilty

The Crown set out its case in open court on Wednesday. What has been reported so far is the prosecution's account; none of it has been tested.

Prosecutors told a London court on Wednesday that Russell Brand was a 'ruthless, calculated and manipulative' sexual predator, according to the Guardian, which led its coverage with those words. Reuters, the BBC and the Evening Standard carried the same charge, and the BBC's review of the morning papers lists it among the front-page stories. The comedian and actor first appeared in court on Monday, as we reported on Tuesday; the BBC's Wednesday headline treats the prosecution's opening as the start of the trial, so outlets differ on when it began. The New York Times reported after Monday's hearing that Mr Brand has pleaded not guilty to all the charges, which are for rape and sexual assault, and the Mirror has published the full list. The Evening Standard and the Mirror also ran explainers on the allegations and the charges, so the outline is easy to find. Under English law he is presumed innocent until a jury decides otherwise.

A prosecution opening is a statement of what the Crown says it will prove. It is not evidence, and nobody has yet questioned it. Every description of Mr Brand on this page is the prosecution's, and we report it as such. The Standard said he arrived at court looking 'concerned'. The Evening Standard reported that the court was told Mr Brand laughed at women he is accused of abusing, and that people around him did nothing during an alleged sexual assault at a radio station. We found those two details only in the Standard, so treat them as one outlet's account of the opening. Our sources gave no count of complainants and no timetable for the trial, and none carried a response from the defence, so we give neither.

The story is the most widely carried British item of the past day, with at least five outlets reporting it, and it will return every morning while the trial runs. For readers, the rules of the room matter more than the headlines. Coverage is limited to a fair and accurate account of what is said in court, so one side is heard first and the other later. Expect the prosecution to call its witnesses, and the defence to question them, before Mr Brand or his lawyers set out their own case. Complainants in sexual offence cases are entitled to lifelong anonymity, so you will not see them named here or in the reports we saw. Do not read a verdict into an opening speech, in either direction. What to watch is whether the court sits as planned today, and whether the judge announces any limits on reporting.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 10 outlets · the Starlink limits are Kyiv's account; Mr Musk says they come from the US, not his firm

Burnham tells Zelenskiy that Britain's support 'will not change'. Kyiv says it feels betrayed by Trump and that Starlink limits are hurting its drones

President Volodymyr Zelenskiy was in London on Wednesday. He had an audience with the King at Buckingham Palace and thanked him for his 'unwavering support', the Evening Standard and Politico reported, then met Prime Minister Andy Burnham at Downing Street, a meeting GOV.UK logged. 'We are behind you and that will not change,' Mr Burnham said, the BBC reported. Before the talks the Guardian and the Telegraph said Britain would speed up deliveries of anti-drone missiles, and the Daily Mail reported that Mr Burnham called Mr Trump's deal with Vladimir Putin a 'dark moment' for Ukraine. Mr Zelenskiy said he felt betrayed by Mr Trump, the Telegraph, the Guardian and the Mirror reported, and Reuters quoted him calling it 'painful' when Mr Trump chooses a different side. The Standard said the two leaders agreed that Russia has no interest in peace, and the BBC reported Mr Zelenskiy saying Russian strikes had left 600,000 people without power. The immediate grievance is Starlink. A presidential aide told Reuters that SpaceX had limited its operations in Ukraine, disrupting military missions, Al Jazeera reported Kyiv's accusation that SpaceX throttled terminals, and the Guardian said soldiers' access in combat was restricted. Mr Musk said any limit is a US decision, not his firm's, the BBC reported, and Reuters reported that Poland's Sikorski asked him whether he was playing politics with Starlink and that Europe has no quick fix. The Financial Times separately reported that Washington has floated unfreezing some Russian assets to restart peace talks, and that European officials reject the idea.

UK · EconomyPress ✓ 6 outlets · all of it is pre-Budget briefing; the £1bn figure is City AM's alone

Healey's Budget on 28 October is reported to include a tax crackdown on online sellers, cheaper hiring of young people and no extra borrowing. Business lobbies for relief

John Healey, the Chancellor, delivers his first Budget on 28 October, and the briefings are already out. The Guardian, City AM and the Mirror reported that he plans a crackdown on online marketplaces by closing VAT loopholes for online sellers; the Mirror named Amazon and eBay among the firms in scope. City AM put the prize at £1bn and said it would fund tax breaks for shops, a figure we found nowhere else. The Financial Times reported that the giveaways would be paid for without more borrowing. City AM also said Mr Healey is looking at cutting employers' cost of hiring NEETs. Separately, the Guardian and Sky News reported that the hospitality industry will offer up to 100,000 jobs to young people out of work.

The lobbying is loud. The chief executive of Kingfisher, which owns B&Q and Screwfix, said the company would pull its NEET support if taxes rise and asked for lower business rates, City AM reported. City AM said Mr Healey was told to cut employment taxes and energy bills, and Sky News said bookmakers made a last-ditch plea to avoid a new tax on them. The FT and City AM reported that Kemi Badenoch is warning against a possible tax raid on banks; whether one is planned is not confirmed in our sources. The BBC's Faisal Islam asked what the surprising praise for the UK economy says about the Budget.

UK · PoliticsPress ✓ 10 outlets · the remarks are as filmed and described by ITV News and others; Reform's response comes from one outlet

Reform UK councillors are filmed making racist remarks and the party opens probes. Burnham rules out a deal with the Greens over their 'Zionism is racism' motion

An undercover investigation by ITV News, titled 'Inside Reform', showed Reform UK councillors and candidates making extreme and racist comments on camera, according to ITV's listing, the BBC and the Guardian. The Evening Standard reported that the programme alleged a London councillor claimed King Charles was Muslim and made demeaning remarks about black children. The Mirror reported that Reform has launched investigations into politicians named in the film; the BBC said the party faces criticism. Reform's own response is known to us only through the Mirror, so we do not quote it further.

The Greens have a row of their own. Prime Minister Andy Burnham ruled out any deal with the party after it passed a motion at its Brighton conference saying 'Zionism is racism', the BBC, the Standard, the Independent and the Daily Mail reported. The Mail called the motion antisemitic, and the National reported an angry reply from the Greens' leader, Zack Polanski, to what it called an accusation of 'antisemitism' from the Prime Minister. The Financial Times said the party is split, with its deputy leader saying the vote must be 'respected'. HuffPost UK headlined Mr Polanski's reply 'deeply revealing'. The Daily Mail separately reported a second poll putting the Conservatives ahead of Reform; we saw no pollster or figures.

Sport · Champions LeaguePress ✓ 6 outlets · the BBC and Sky Sports report Martínez's statement differently

PSG beat Manchester City 4-2 on Maresca's first defeat. Barcola's hat-trick lifts Liverpool to 6-3, Villa score six in a record win and Martínez's outburst rumbles on at United

Manchester City lost 4-2 to Paris Saint-Germain on Wednesday night, Sky Sports reported, with Erling Haaland scoring twice. It was the first defeat of the season for manager Enzo Maresca, and Pep Guardiola watched from the ground, Sky and the BBC noted. ESPN said it was City's first home game since the Premier League found them guilty of breaking financial rules. The Guardian credited quick goals from Doué and Dembélé for the visitors' win, the BBC asked whether PSG are building the greatest dynasty in European football, and the Telegraph said City were taken apart in a breathless match. Elsewhere, Liverpool won 6-3 at LASK, with Bradley Barcola scoring his first hat-trick for the club, the BBC, Guardian and Telegraph reported; the Telegraph added that Liverpool conceded three cheap goals.

Aston Villa beat Fenerbahçe by six goals in a record win, with Jackson and Barkley scoring twice each, the Guardian and the BBC reported. Real Madrid won 4-3 at Roma after a late comeback, Football Italia and the BBC said. At Manchester United, Tuesday's 2-0 loss at Atlético Madrid became a row. Defender Lisandro Martínez criticised the club's hierarchy, and the Guardian said his anger at a senior executive, Jason Wilcox, was widely shared by his team-mates. He then issued a statement. The BBC's headline says he apologised; Sky Sports says the statement clarified his words but did not apologise. We cannot settle which wording is right from the headlines we saw, so we report both.
