UK · SecurityPress ✓ 10 outlets · the threat assessments are MI5's own; Iran's reaction rests on a single service

MI5's chief says Iran is turning to harder targets such as military sites and Russia is stepping up hybrid attacks. AI, he warns, could 'change the stakes'

Sir Ken McCallum called the suspected RAF Fairford plot 'not unique'. On Brexit he said Russia did try to interfere in 2016, but that the result 'is not in dispute'.

Sir Ken McCallum, the Director General of MI5, used the security service's annual threat speech on Tuesday to describe a country under pressure from several directions at once. Iran, he said, is trying to hit harder targets in Britain, such as military sites, Reuters and the Evening Standard reported, and the Standard added that the past year has brought more Iranian action against Jewish sites. He called the suspected plot against RAF Fairford, the Gloucestershire air base used by US forces, 'not unique', AP and the Standard reported, and The Hill described it as one part of a wider Iranian effort. Russia, Reuters said, is escalating its hybrid warfare, meaning sabotage, disinformation and pressure short of open war, as it lashes out at Ukraine's allies. The BBC's headline and Der Spiegel both said MI5 is now dealing with more state threats than it did in the Cold War.

The speech followed Mr Burnham's statement to MPs on Monday that the Fairford plot was 'almost certainly' directed by Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a charge Tehran dismissed as unfounded, according to our report yesterday. Sir Ken's 'not unique' widens the frame from one incident to a campaign. The reports we saw, however, gave no count of plots, arrests or sites targeted, so the scale of that campaign is not yet public.

Artificial intelligence ran through the speech. CNA reported that he warned AI could 'change the stakes' on bioweapons and worsen cyberattacks and other threats, and The Independent's headline said terrorists could use AI to go after the UK with drones and bioweapons. NBC News listed China and terrorist groups alongside Iran and Russia among the dangers he named. The Mirror's headline put the threats on a post-9/11 scale, a framing we saw only there. The outlets agree on the shape of the warning if not on its emphasis: hostile states first, with terrorism and technology close behind.

The speech also landed in an argument. On Sunday Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Russia had interfered in the 2016 EU referendum, a claim Nigel Farage has called a lie. Sir Ken said it is openly documented that Russia sought to interfere in that vote, the Standard reported, and the Guardian's headline called that confirmation. But he said the result 'is not in dispute', Sky News reported, and the BBC and the Standard said he warned against overstating, or amplifying, Russian propaganda. The Telegraph ran a comment piece calling this a rebuke to Mr Burnham; that is the writer's reading, not a finding. One detail rests on a single source: FinancialJuice, a market news service, reported that Iran summoned Britain's ambassador and demanded evidence of Iranian links to the Fairford incident. We found no second outlet, and none of the reports we saw said the underlying evidence had been published.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 10 outlets · 'no warning' is President Zelenskiy's account; casualty counts differ

Russian missiles hit a Kyiv power plant for a second night with no allied warning. Burnham hosts Zelenskiy at Downing Street today

Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv late on Tuesday, hitting the CHP-4 combined heat and power plant and a nine-storey block of flats, Kyiv Post reported. Counts of the dead differ: Kyiv Post said at least one person died and 13 were hurt, while Reuters and Le Monde put the dead at two, Le Monde adding about twenty injured. ABC News said at least 600,000 people in Ukraine were without power early on Wednesday, citing President Volodymyr Zelenskiy. He said, as Reuters, the Guardian, CBC and Al Jazeera reported, that Ukraine got no warning from its partners for the second night running; Le Monde noted that Kyiv relies on Western, chiefly American, intelligence to detect launches. Mr Trump said on Tuesday that the United States was still sharing intelligence with Ukraine, Reuters reported. Twelve US senators, six from each party, urged him to rescind his diesel deal with Russia, the New York Times and Kyiv Post reported. In London, Mr Burnham will receive Mr Zelenskiy at Downing Street today, GOV.UK said, and Kyiv Post and Le Figaro report that he will promise faster delivery of air-defence missiles, more military aid and tougher sanctions on Russia. Mr Zelenskiy and Kyiv's mayor, Vitali Klitschko, also clashed over preparations for winter, West-France and VnExpress reported, and Reuters said Mr Zelenskiy has proposed talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in the coming days.

World · VaticanPress ✓ 9 outlets · the Vatican is the primary source for the medical details

Pope Leo is diagnosed with lung cancer after surgery to remove part of his right lung. The Vatican says the operation went well

Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer, the Vatican confirmed on Tuesday, after an operation that day. Reuters had reported that he would enter hospital on Tuesday for surgery on a lung nodule, a small growth, which he had made public on Sunday. The Vatican said the operation was 'successfully completed', the Straits Times reported, and GMA News said it went as expected. Until Sunday the pontiff had been seen as fit, Handelsblatt noted. AP, Politico and the Evening Standard said surgeons removed part of the right lung; Mint and ThisDay specify the lower lobe. The pope is 71, and the Straits Times said it is his first hospital stay since he became pope on 8 May 2025.

Mint reported that further tests will decide the next steps in his treatment. The reports we saw did not say what type of lung cancer it is, whether it has spread, or when he might resume his duties. ThisDay reported that the Vatican cancelled his engagements, and GMA News and Remate said Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle will preside over the Mass in St Peter's Square on Wednesday in his place. The New York Times reported messages from world leaders and, in an analysis, said the diagnosis raises questions about his longevity while his plans for the Church are still taking shape.

Sport · FootballPress ✓ 8 outlets · the Rooney remark is from one outlet only

Martínez turns on Manchester United's hierarchy after a 2-0 defeat at Atlético. Arsenal beat Lille 3-0, and the football regulator says 94% of clubs lose money

Atlético Madrid beat Manchester United 2-0 in the Champions League on Tuesday, with Barrios scoring after 53 seconds, the Guardian reported, and United finishing with ten men, the Guardian and Vietnam's Dân trí said. A match roundup named Lookman among the scorers. Afterwards United defender Lisandro Martínez said he expected more from the club and criticised its hierarchy, the Guardian, Sky Sports and the BBC reported. Argentina's La Nación added that he pointed to a lack of signings and defended head coach Michael Carrick, and that Wayne Rooney called his remarks disrespectful, a detail we found only there. The Telegraph saw signs of fighting spirit in the defeat. At the Emirates, Arsenal beat Lille 3-0 with three goals in seven minutes, ESPN and Dân trí reported; Le Monde called it another bad night for Lille against English clubs.

Off the pitch, the Independent Football Regulator said in a report on Tuesday that English clubs are 'dangerously exposed' to financial risk, after finding that 94% of clubs in the top five divisions were operating at a loss, Reuters reported via the Straits Times. The BBC, the FT and Sky Sports covered the report. Its chair, David Kogan, said he would resign if he faced political pressure over how to respond to Manchester City, the BBC and the Guardian reported. City were found guilty last week of breaking Premier League financial rules. Mr Kogan said he had faced 'absolutely no political pressure', the Guardian reported.

Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 8 outlets · no London or New York index closes were confirmed, so none are quoted

The IMF says the world economy 'could soon test the limits of resilience'. Chip shares slide, the Fed's Collins keeps a rate rise open and Bank of America is fined $1.2bn

Headlines on two outlets quoted the IMF at its annual meetings as saying the world economy 'could soon test the limits of resilience'. Reuters reported that the fund flagged risks from a correction in AI-related markets, war and debt, and cited an AI slowdown and spiking bond yields as looming dangers. In separate reports Reuters said the AI boom had lifted global trade, with China and India the main beneficiaries, and that the IMF cut its growth forecasts for Latin America and the Caribbean because of the energy shock. The fund has posted transcripts of its briefings on the Global Financial Stability Report and the World Economic Outlook.

The warning fitted Tuesday's trading. Chip shares extended their slide, Reuters said, on a report about China's memory chips and jitters over AI spending, and AP reported that South Korea's Kospi fell more than 5% in the sell-off. Wall Street ended Tuesday with small losses led by chipmakers, according to De Telegraaf, Bankier and Clarín. Bloomberg said US stocks recovered from their lows after Boston Federal Reserve president Susan Collins signalled that one more rate rise might be needed this year but, in its reading, not in 2027; Reuters reported her saying another hike is on the table before the end of 2026. Bank results were mixed: Goldman Sachs and Citi beat estimates, Wells Fargo's profit rose and JPMorgan posted a rare profit miss, Reuters said. We could not confirm closing levels for the FTSE 100, sterling or the S&P 500 from two sources, so the ears show a dash.

After the close, Bank of America said the US Treasury had fined it $1.2bn for failing to police suspicious transactions and breaching sanctions rules, the BBC, Reuters, CNN and Al Jazeera reported. Polish and Dutch reports put the unreported suspicious transactions at more than $5bn; the New York Times headline gave $1.8bn, a figure we could not reconcile with the others. The BBC said the bank inadequately flagged transactions linked to possible Chinese money laundering networks. In oil, Reuters said prices climbed as traders weighed Saudi supply risk and Russian diesel flows. It also reported loud booms near Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran; Bloomberg's Asia programme headline said the headquarters was hit in a Houthi attack, but we saw no confirmation of damage.
