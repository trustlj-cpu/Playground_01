UK · SecurityPress ✓ 9 outlets · the attribution is the government's assessment; Iran denies it

Burnham says Iran's Revolutionary Guards almost certainly directed the plot against RAF Fairford. Britain summons Iran's ambassador and adds sanctions

About 30 countries backed the finding in a joint statement. Iran calls the charge 'unfounded and malicious' and, in one report, a 'false flag'.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham told MPs on Monday that a suspected plot against RAF Fairford, the Gloucestershire air base used by US bombers, was 'highly likely' to have been coordinated in Iran and 'almost certainly' directed by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, or IRGC, the armed force that answers to Iran's supreme leader. Reuters, the Guardian, the Evening Standard, DW, SCMP and the Independent reported the statement, and the Guardian called it the firmest attribution Britain has made so far. The incident dates from the end of September, when arrests were made near the base, according to Zeit and Le Monde. Turkey's Habertürk reported that five people were held on suspicion of terrorism offences, a figure the other outlets did not give. DW reported that the scare led the United States to withdraw B-1 heavy bombers from the base.

The government followed the statement with action. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband summoned Iran's ambassador and said Britain would not tolerate such threats on its soil, the BBC and DW reported. GOV.UK announced measures against Iranian individuals, and the Financial Times and the Guardian reported new sanctions on Tehran and the IRGC, which are financial and travel restrictions on named people or bodies. Foreign ministers of about 30 countries, among them the United States, Canada, Australia, France, Germany and Spain, signed a joint statement that backed Britain's finding and warned Tehran that they would respond together to any attack, CBC, PBS and El País reported. Iran's government dismissed the charge as 'unfounded and malicious speculations', Haaretz reported, and Infobae reported that Tehran called it a 'false flag', meaning an act staged so that someone else takes the blame.

Mr Burnham's wording matters. An 'assessment' is an intelligence judgement, not a court finding, and the reports we saw did not say that the underlying evidence had been published or that anyone had yet been charged with acting for Iran. The Guardian noted on Sunday that Britain was only at the edge of the US and Israeli bombing of Iran, but that Tehran had made clear it wanted Britain to feel at risk. The charge arrives as Iran-backed Houthi rebels strike Saudi Arabia and the Iran war keeps fuel prices high. Fairford is one of the bases the US air force uses for long-range bombers, so a threat to it touches the alliance as well as British security. GOV.UK described the new measures as aimed at Iranian individuals rather than the Iranian state as a whole. The Home Secretary, Shabana Mahmood, also made a national security statement to MPs on Monday. Expect Iran's embassy, the opposition parties and Washington to respond today, and watch whether London names the individuals sanctioned and says what it would do if the finding stands.

UK · PoliticsPress ✓ 7 outlets · the Russia claim rests on the Prime Minister's word; no evidence was published

Burnham tells MPs the status quo with the EU is 'not enough' and points to a Brussels summit on 20 November. Tories and Farage attack his Brexit remarks

Prime Minister Andy Burnham told the House of Commons on Monday that standing still on relations with the European Union is not enough and that refusing a deeper partnership would be a 'dereliction of duty', the Guardian, the BBC, the Evening Standard and the Mirror reported. He vowed that the British public would be 'boss' in any talks, the Mirror reported, and called for 'serious dialogue' on the options and described the summit in Brussels, which GOV.UK and Turkey's Ekonomim say is set for 20 November, as a first step. ANSA and the FAZ said he wants agreements on food trade, energy and migration, including curbing illegal Channel crossings. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch said he was 'unpicking' the deal the UK signed with the EU, the BBC reported, and the Guardian reported that Tories called his approach 'dangerous and divisive'. Nigel Farage, who campaigned for Brexit, called Mr Burnham 'a liar' over his Sunday claim that Russia interfered in the 2016 referendum, the Evening Standard and HuffPost UK reported. The Washington Post said officials moved to make clear that the remarks did not mean the government rejects the result, and Foreign Office minister Stephen Doughty said Russia had failed to undermine British elections, the Mirror reported. No evidence for the interference claim was published in the reports we saw, and any deal needs the EU's agreement.

World · Russia and UkrainePress ✓ 8 outlets · Russia's export figure is a minister's claim; Moscow has not confirmed the 'energy ceasefire'

Trump defends his Russian diesel deal as lawmakers in both parties object. The Kremlin rejects Kyiv's 60-day ceasefire and Ukraine says the strikes go on

President Donald Trump defended his deal to let Russian diesel reach the market on Monday, as Republicans and Democrats in Congress pressed him to reverse course, Reuters and CNA reported. Reuters said US officials hope the arrangement can 'soften' Moscow on peace talks, and FinancialJuice quoted envoy Kushner calling it an effort to soften Russia. Russia's Deputy Prime Minister Novak said exports could reach up to 3.5 million tonnes a month by the end of the year, more than the volumes Mr Trump announced, Reuters and the Straits Times reported. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the deal is 'out of the question' for Europe, DW reported.

On the war itself, the Kremlin rejected President Zelenskiy's idea of a 60-day ceasefire and said it wants a comprehensive peace deal, Reuters reported. Moscow has not confirmed Mr Trump's 'energy ceasefire', announced on Sunday, and the BBC described it as tight-lipped. Ukraine accused Russia of breaking the truce with a strike on a power plant, AP, Al Jazeera and PBS reported, and Mr Zelenskiy said Russia is hitting fuel stores. Reuters reported that the EU adopted nearly 1,600 new sanctions listings against Russia. Oil swung on the news: Brent crude, the global benchmark, fell early on hopes of more supply, CNA reported, then rose after the Saudi airport attacks.

Sport · Premier LeaguePress ✓ 6 outlets · scorers from the Guardian, L'Équipe and NU.nl

Coventry beat Newcastle 4-1 for their first Premier League home win in 25 years. Burnham's Manchester City meetings are published

Coventry City beat Newcastle United 4-1 at home on Monday night for their first Premier League home win in 25 years, the BBC, RTÉ, the Guardian and Reuters reported. It was the newly promoted side's second win in a row and lifted them out of the relegation zone, Reuters said. Sidiki Cherif scored, L'Équipe and Cumhuriyet reported, and substitute Mfuni sealed the rout, the Guardian said; Newcastle's Sven Botman replied for the visitors, according to NU.nl. The Guardian credited manager Frank Lampard for balancing risk and reward, and Reuters noted that it was Newcastle's second defeat in six league games.

Manchester City's troubles reached the Prime Minister. The Greater Manchester Combined Authority said Mr Burnham met City chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak at an investment summit in 2021, when he was the region's mayor, Reuters and Al Jazeera reported, and the Evening Standard said further details of his meetings with the club were released. City were found guilty last week of breaking Premier League financial rules, and the Inquirer reported that they remain top with six wins from six. Mr Burnham had promised to publish his City meetings after Kemi Badenoch pressed him.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 7 outlets · casualty figures differ between early counts; the Brent price is from two outlets that differ slightly

Saudi Arabia probes how its air defences failed at Riyadh airport. Airlines cancel flights, the death toll is put at 13 and oil rises

Saudi authorities are investigating why air defences failed to stop the Houthi attacks on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, AP reported via PBS. CBS and CNA put the death toll from the first strike at 13, with hundreds injured; earlier counts said 12. Reuters reported that airlines cancelled flights to Saudi Arabia, and CNA said the airport extended its suspension of operations. The Houthis, an Iran-backed armed group that controls much of northern Yemen, claimed the attacks. Turkey, Pakistan, Egypt and Saudi Arabia condemned them, Reuters reported, and Iran offered to mediate between the Houthis and Riyadh, the Straits Times reported. Bloomberg said Saudi Arabia fended off fresh strikes, and the Guardian reported that Cristiano Ronaldo's Asian Champions League match in Riyadh was delayed by a missile alert.

Markets took it in stride on Wall Street. The S&P 500 fell 0.06% to 7,807.24 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.44% to 27,245.90 on Monday, according to Reuters via the Straits Times and Yahoo Finance data. Brent crude closed near $106 a barrel, up about $1.20, The Hill reported, and ABC Australia said it approached $107 overnight. Monday's FTSE 100 and sterling closes could not be confirmed from two sources, so the front-page boxes show a dash; Monday's Dow, WTI crude and gold closes were not confirmed either. Bloomberg's Asia wrap said a chipmaker sell-off weighed on sentiment before big US earnings and inflation data.
