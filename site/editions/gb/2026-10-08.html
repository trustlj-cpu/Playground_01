Burnham makes his first official visit to Berlin. Security talks with Merz, and a French minister says Britain may 'come all the way back' to the EU

The Prime Minister was greeted with military honours and agreed deeper cooperation on cybersecurity and sabotage. Talk of rejoining the EU came from Paris, not from Downing Street.

Andy Burnham, who took office on 20 July after Sir Keir Starmer resigned, was received with military honours in Berlin on Thursday at the start of his first official visit as prime minister, the BBC and Euronews reported. The BBC's preview said he would discuss security and EU ties with Chancellor Friedrich Merz. The talks were billed as security talks. Downing Street said the two countries will deepen cooperation on cybersecurity and on countering sabotage, sharing information and coordinating action to monitor and disrupt threats. The visit came after a drone attack on Leipzig/Halle airport in August, which Berlin has accused Russia of orchestrating. The leaders also marked the Kensington Treaty, signed by Mr Merz and Sir Keir Starmer last year, which covers security, the economy and freedom of movement and set up joint forums, including between intelligence services. Mr Burnham said Britain is open to Germany joining the GCAP fighter-jet programme; German officials did not publicly confirm they want to join, and Mr Merz did not mention it in the joint statement. There was no press conference. The statement to the press lasted about seven minutes. Government releases later in the day announced the first UK-Germany tech corridor and a £1 billion German investment to build 4,000 affordable homes across the UK.

The headline question was the European Union. The Financial Times described the visit as a bid to win Mr Merz's support for closer ties with the EU, and Sky News asked whether Berlin will help Britain rejoin. The loudest answer came from Paris. France's finance minister told the BBC, the Independent and The Times that Britain is welcome to "come all the way" back to the EU, and could do so "anytime". Neither Mr Burnham nor Mr Merz said that. Mr Merz said the two countries are, at the least, concluding a bilateral agreement, and no application has been made. Domestic politics is watching. The Conservatives have pledged to rip up the UK-EU food deal, which the FT said risks "huge uncertainty", and The Times has called his EU rethink a puzzle for the SNP. The Spectator said he looked ill at ease in Berlin. The government's own line is that the visit delivers jobs, homes and security. The FT reported separately that the government will press ahead with the Oxford-Cambridge corridor, another growth project, and that Germany is heading for its fastest growth since 2022, a reminder of what a closer partner looks like from London. The question the visit leaves is whether closer European ties can be sold to voters who are still divided on Brexit, and how far Mr Burnham is willing to go before he says so. For now the substance is security and money, not membership, and the words "rejoin" and "EU" were left to others.

World · DiplomacyPress ✓ 6 outlets · the two governments describe the outcome differently

Israel says Britain's Jerusalem consulate has stopped working. Miliband says the building stays open as the 'UK Mission'

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar announced on Thursday that the British consulate in East Jerusalem has "ceased to function as a consulate". He said the Consul General and 19 other diplomats are ending their assignments and leaving Israel, and that a task force of no more than seven diplomats may keep working in the building in a "very limited" role. The Times reported that Israel blamed "hostile rhetoric". He tied the move to British sanctions on Israeli settlers in the West Bank: "the Labour Government's actions have led us here." A day earlier an Israeli embassy spokesperson had told Sky News that UK-Israel relations "have seen better days". The Times, Sky News, the BBC and the Independent all reported the announcement. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband gave a different account. After discussions with the Israeli government, he said, the building "will not be closing" and will be known as the UK Mission, Jerusalem from Thursday 8 October. He said the consulate has been there for nearly 200 years and that Britain will "build back up" its presence. London said it would not retaliate for now, though earlier reports said it had warned it could expel Israeli diplomats. Sky News summed up the confusion in a headline: is the British consulate in Jerusalem shut or not? Both governments said they will keep talking, and each will be able to raise the issues that matter to it, according to the reports.

A tanker is hit off Qatar and oil tops $100 again. Gilt yields rise and the FTSE 100 ends 0.16% lower. The Budget is 20 days away

A tanker was hit by several projectiles about 51 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal off Qatar at around 19:00 GMT on Wednesday, UKMTO, the Royal Navy-run maritime reporting service, said, and casualties were reported. The BBC and the Guardian both reported the attack, and the Guardian added that Iran says a route through the Strait of Hormuz will be "closed". Brent rose above $100. Gilts sold off: the 10-year yield rose about 6 basis points to 5.44%, and the Guardian reported that medium-term borrowing costs for the UK government hit a 19-year high.

The FTSE 100 closed 0.16% lower at 10,441.60. Sterling ended at about $1.3226, up 0.09%. Sky News reported that UK gas storage is near record lows and asked what happens if it runs out. Chancellor John Healey presents his Budget on 28 October. The Times set out predictions for it, and the FT reported that higher mortgage rates are inflicting "pain" on the UK housing market.

Security · DefencePress ✓ 6 outlets · charges are allegations, not findings

A Royal Navy sailor is charged with spying for a foreign power. A seventh man is bailed over Fairford, and two Latvians are held at an RAF base

Teddy Young, 24, of Bedfordshire, a Royal Navy service member, was charged with two offences under the National Security Act 2023, the BBC, The Times, the Independent and the Guardian reported: obtaining protected information that could benefit a foreign power, and preparing to disclose it. The alleged offences are said to have taken place between 26 November 2024 and 21 May 2025. He was remanded in custody to appear at Westminster Magistrates' Court. Police have not named the country concerned and have said the case is not linked to their Fairford investigation. The Royal Navy said it would be inappropriate to comment while proceedings are ongoing.

In the Fairford case, the BBC and Sky News reported that a seventh man has been bailed; seven people have now been arrested and none has been reported charged. Separately, two Latvian nationals were arrested on suspicion of trespass at an RAF base in Cambridgeshire used by the US military, the BBC, Sky News and the Guardian reported. No link to Fairford has been stated.

Law · Royal familyPress ✓ 5 outlets · court ruling; no finding on the underlying allegations

Court rules the warrants used to search Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor's properties were unlawful

Thames Valley Police accepted in the High Court on Thursday that the search warrants it used on two properties linked to Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor were unlawful, the Guardian and the BBC reported. The warrants, granted by an Old Bailey judge on 18 February, were quashed by agreement before Mr Justice Hilliard, because the legislation under which they were issued had been incorrectly applied. Mr Mountbatten-Windsor, who did not attend, was arrested in February on suspicion of misconduct in public office, relating to his time as a trade envoy and links to Jeffrey Epstein. He denies wrongdoing.

The judge stressed that quashing the warrants "does not bring an investigation to an end, or mean that the whole of the investigation was unlawful". Thames Valley Police said the agreement concerns the legal basis for the warrants and is not a judgement on the wider case, and that it is working to find a legal route to keep the material seized. Other reports that same day said the former prince could face a witness summons in a £40 million London fraud trial, the FT reported.
