Burnham defends Jackdaw gasfield approval as attention turns to Rosebank

Gas from Jackdaw is due to flow within weeks, while campaigners condemn the approval and a Rosebank rejection is reported as likely.

The Evening Standard reported on Wednesday that attention was moving to Rosebank after Prime Minister Andy Burnham defended his approval of the Jackdaw gasfield in the North Sea. The paper said gas from the Jackdaw field would start flowing within weeks. The Guardian reported that climate campaigners had condemned the approval as ‘maddening’. Critics told the paper that the move would jeopardise climate targets, fail to cut the cost of living and distract from the need to decarbonise. The Guardian also carried a cartoon by Ben Jennings on Mr Burnham’s decision to approve the Jackdaw North Sea gasfield, and the Evening Standard’s report set the defence of Jackdaw directly beside the question of Rosebank.

In a separate Guardian exclusive published on Wednesday, officials told the paper that the energy secretary, Miatta Fahnbulleh, was minded not to give approval to the Rosebank oilfield. The paper described that as a victory for climate campaigners. Rosebank is an oilfield and Jackdaw a gasfield, and the two Guardian reports appeared within a few hours of each other on 21 October. The Guardian’s account of the Rosebank position rests on what officials said to the paper. The reports include no formal Rosebank decision and no on-the-record statement from the energy secretary confirming the lean. The Evening Standard said only that attention was shifting to Rosebank, while the Guardian’s reaction story said campaigners had condemned Jackdaw even as the paper reported the Rosebank lean.

Burnham suggests £108-a-year gas standing charges could be removed

The Mirror reported that during Prime Minister's Questions on Wednesday Andy Burnham suggested the £108-a-year standing charges on gas bills could be removed. The paper said he vowed to do more to bring down household bills. The Financial Times reported that Mr Burnham plans to cut standing charges from household gas bills. The £108 figure is the annual total of the daily fixed fee on gas bills as the Mirror presented it. The FT quoted experts who said energy providers would probably recover the loss of the daily fixed fee through the unit rate, the per-unit price customers pay for the gas they use. Neither report set out a start date or a legal mechanism. The Mirror described the remark as a suggestion made in the Commons rather than a confirmed policy, while the FT framed it as a plan.

Met police to interview Jeffrey Donaldson over alleged rape in parliament

The Guardian reported on Wednesday that the Metropolitan Police is investigating an allegation that Jeffrey Donaldson, the former DUP leader, raped a woman in 2016, after a referral from the PSNI. The BBC reported that the Met issued a statement confirming a 63-year-old man will be interviewed over an alleged rape inside the Houses of Parliament. The Met did not name him, while the BBC’s headline named Mr Donaldson as the person to be interviewed.

The PSNI is the Police Service of Northern Ireland, which passed the allegation to the Met, according to the Guardian. The Guardian said the allegation concerns a woman who was raped in 2016. No charge is reported by either outlet, and no response from Mr Donaldson appears in the reports.

Meta fined $25bn, the largest penalty yet against Facebook’s owner

The BBC reported on Wednesday that Meta was fined $25bn for ‘lies’ over user data privacy. The BBC said it is the largest fine to date against Facebook’s owner, larger than an $18bn deal with California. The summaries do not say which body imposed the fine or whether Meta will pay it in full.

Reuters reported early on Thursday that Meta had struck a deal to keep Zuckerberg in California after wealth tax concerns. Bloomberg reported that United Airlines chief executive Scott Kirby addressed his thoughts on a US wealth tax and defended his support for one. Reuters and Bloomberg did not link the Meta deal to the fine.
