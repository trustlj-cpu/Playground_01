Reform UK suspends aspiring MP Jason Powell after ITV's undercover film of its councillors and candidates

ITV's year-long investigation, scheduled for full broadcast on Sunday, shows Reform figures using racist and violent language, and the party says everyone identified has been suspended or has left.

ITV Exposure, working with Hardcash Productions, spent a year filming Reform UK councillors and candidates in England and Wales, UK Fact Check reports, and says the footage shows them using racist, Islamophobic and sexist language. UK Fact Check says the full documentary, Undercover: A Year in Reform, was scheduled for broadcast on Sunday 18 October. On Saturday ITV News ran a report headlined 'Inside Reform' about an aspiring Reform MP who calls for 'concentration camps' for migrants. Yahoo News and Brit Brief identify him as Jason Powell, an activist who has applied to become a parliamentary candidate. Both outlets report that he was filmed saying 'You cannot be Muslim and British, it's like oil and water', and that he said illegal migrants who cannot be returned to their home countries should be held in 'concentration camps'. The Guardian reported on Sunday that he has been suspended, and Brit Brief says Reform suspended him from party membership while an internal investigation takes place.

Other remarks from the film have been reported by different outlets. The Mirror reports that it shows a Reform councillor saying he wants to 'machine gun' perpetrators of grooming gang crimes. UK Fact Check also reports that Alan Feeney resigned his council seats, and that Dean Miller said he is disabled and was heavily medicated when he was recorded. Reform's response, as UK Fact Check quotes it, is 'In some cases, there have been suspensions and due process must be followed'. The same outlet reports that the party says it has improved and prioritised its vetting, and that from time to time 'a handful of individuals who do not share our values will initially make it through vetting'. According to UK Fact Check, Reform has confirmed that all the councillors identified in the film have either been suspended or have left the party.

Zack Polanski says he will not try to overturn the Green Party's 'Zionism is racism' motion but will amend it

Zack Polanski, the Green Party leader, said on BBC One's Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg on 18 October, 'I'm not going to try to overturn it', in reference to the 'Zionism is racism' motion passed at the party conference in Brighton on 4 October. GB News and the Express & Star report that the motion passed by 1,022 votes to 813. Mr Polanski did not vote on it, and he has said he would not have voted for it unless it had been amended. The Jewish Chronicle reports that he described the policy as not controversial. The BBC reports that he said he wants to amend it at the first opportunity and that the motion stands until 2028. GB News and the Evening Standard report that his poll rating has fallen to its worst as Green leader.

Daniel Dubois stops Fabio Wardley in the second round of their rematch to retain the WBO heavyweight title

Daniel Dubois retained the WBO heavyweight title on Saturday 17 October with a second-round stoppage of Fabio Wardley at the O2 Arena in London, Sky Sports, NBC News and Yahoo Sports report. Bleacher Report lists the time as 2 minutes 21 seconds of round two, and NBC News says the fight ended with 39 seconds of the round left. Yahoo Sports reports that Dubois knocked Wardley down early in the first round with a right hand. NBC News says the referee ended the fight, while FightMag reports that Wardley's corner stopped it. NBC News and FightMag give Dubois 24 wins and three defeats. Sky Sports and CBS Sports report that Dubois won the title in May at Co-op Live in Manchester, stopping Wardley in the 11th round. The Guardian reports that Dubois named Filip Hrgovic as a target after the fight and quotes him saying 'my time, my era'.

Nottingham trust dismisses four more staff over attack victims' records as the health secretary calls access a 'disgrace'

Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust has dismissed four more staff for inappropriately accessing the records of victims of the 2023 Nottingham attacks, taking the total to 15, up from 11 in May, Brit Brief, Time News and DataBreaches.net report. Brit Brief reports that the trust has also issued 15 final written warnings and two first written warnings, and that the records were those of Barnaby Webber, Grace O'Malley-Kumar and Ian Coates. Separately, Yahoo News UK, Yorkshire Live and Unilad report that two Yorkshire Ambulance Service staff are believed to have accessed or tried to access the records of William Chapman, who died at Yorkshire Wildlife Park on 10 October, without a legitimate reason. Yvette Cooper, the health secretary, called the access a 'total disgrace' on Sky News and the BBC reports her calling NHS data breaches a 'disgrace'. She said staff should be sacked if breaches are confirmed and suspended while an investigation takes place. Brit Brief reports that the trust is introducing a system to detect possible breaches, and that it has told the Information Commissioner's Office and Nottinghamshire Police.

Nottinghamshire Police officer shot in the foot on Angel Row after approaching two people in the city centre

A Nottinghamshire Police officer was shot in the foot in Nottingham city centre shortly before 04:50 on Sunday 18 October after he approached two people on Angel Row, Nottinghamshire Police, the BBC and the Guardian report. The BBC reports that the officer is being treated in hospital for a foot wound, and Brit Brief reports that his condition is stable. GB News and Brit Brief report that a man and a woman left the scene and have not been traced. Police say a firearm has been recovered. Assistant Chief Constable Claire Rukas called it a shocking incident and a sobering reminder of the dangers officers face, and appealed for anyone with information to come forward, and Police say there is no information to suggest a wider threat to the public. Angel Row remained closed on Sunday.
