Immigration agents arrest 244 foreigners in Zamboanga Sibugay, the biggest offshore-gaming raid of 2026

Raids on two sites on Friday netted mostly Chinese nationals, plus Malaysians, Indonesians and Cambodians. Authorities seized about 3,000 mobile phones, and trafficking checks are under way.

The Bureau of Immigration said it arrested 244 foreign nationals in raids on Friday, October 2, in Siay and Alicia towns in Zamboanga Sibugay, according to reports by The Star of Malaysia and the Daily Tribune. At the Siay site, 113 people were taken in, among them 110 Chinese nationals, two Cambodians and one Hong Kong national. The Alicia site yielded 131 more, mostly Chinese but also Malaysian, Indonesian and Myanmar nationals. The Tribune noted that nationalities were still being verified, and outlets differ by one or two on the Siay breakdown.

The operation was run by immigration agents together with the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) and the Armed Forces of the Philippines. Officials said they recovered about 3,000 mobile phones, 78 computer monitors, laptops and SIM cards from a compound in Siay, the kind of equipment used in online scam operations. Those arrested face immigration charges, such as working without proper visas, and investigators are looking into possible human trafficking for some of them.

The raid came almost two years after the government ordered a ban on Philippine offshore gaming operators, known as POGOs, and months after the Anti-POGO Act of 2025 was passed. DailyDrop has not seen any official statement on who owned the compound or whether local officials are being investigated, so those questions remain open. The fact that a site this large was still running is what makes the number notable.

PSEi slides for a fifth straight day to 5,629.03 as factory activity shrinks; inflation data due Tuesday

The Philippine Stock Exchange index, the PSEi, closed at 5,629.03 on Friday, October 2, down 0.44 points or 0.01 percent, its fifth straight decline, according to the Inquirer and the Daily Tribune. Net foreign outflows were about ₱1.25 billion on turnover of ₱5.56 billion, the Inquirer reported. Analysts blamed high oil prices, elevated Treasury yields and a weak peso.

The peso closed at ₱62.535 per dollar, stronger by 24 centavos or 0.38 percent, the Tribune reported. DailyDrop has only that one outlet for the peso close. A separate report said the S&P Global manufacturing PMI fell to 49.6 in September from 54.9 in August, a contraction after five months of growth, with output falling for the first time in nine months (GMA News, Philstar). Investors are waiting for September inflation on Tuesday.

Senate impeachment court has set no verdict date for Sara Duterte, says Pangilinan; hearings resume this week

Senator Kiko Pangilinan said on Saturday, October 3, that the Senate impeachment court has not discussed or decided a timetable for a verdict in the trial of Vice President Sara Duterte, the Daily Tribune reported. A December 16 date that had been floated was not an official schedule of the court but an assessment by its spokesperson, he said.

The trial opened on July 6 and was set for 92 trial days. It is now in the stage on alleged unexplained wealth. Based on the court calendar reported by the Tribune and GMA News, full-day hearings are planned for October 5 to 9 and 12 to 15, then a break from October 16 to November 8 so senators can work on the national budget. Conviction needs two-thirds of the senators able to take part. After the court voted 13-1 on September 23 to count only those senators, the threshold fell from 16 votes to 14, and challenges to that ruling are before the Supreme Court (Khaleej Times, Tribune). Duterte denies the charges.

GCash owner Mynt prices its IPO at ₱6.60 a share; public offer opens Tuesday, listing set for October 20

Mynt, the parent of the GCash mobile wallet, set the final price of its initial public offering at ₱6.60 per share, GMA News reported, citing a filing by affiliate Globe Telecom. The base offer is about 8.03 billion shares, or ₱53 billion, and could reach about ₱60.9 billion if the overallotment option is fully used. The company said institutional bookbuilding was oversubscribed several times.

Retail investors can buy during a public offer from October 6 to 12, and shares are expected to list on the main board of the Philippine Stock Exchange on October 20 under the ticker GCASH. The price is well below the ceiling of ₱10 that had been mentioned earlier. FinTech Futures reported a valuation of about US$7 billion; DailyDrop has seen that figure in only one outlet.

Iran says Strait of Hormuz stays shut until US meets its conditions; Philippines remains exposed on oil

Iran's chief negotiator, Parliament Speaker Mohammad-Bagher Qalibaf, said on Sunday, October 4, that the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until seven conditions based on the Islamabad memorandum of June are met, according to reports citing Iranian officials. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi had presented a seven-day plan on September 25 and said the clock would start once Washington accepts it, Al Jazeera reported.

The Philippines buys most of its crude from the Middle East, more than 97 percent in 2025, BusinessWorld reported, and has been under a national energy emergency since March 24. U.S. crude (WTI) closed Friday at $91.11 a barrel, down 1.90 percent, market data show. Pump prices change every Tuesday, so the next move will show whether the pressure is passing through to motorists.
