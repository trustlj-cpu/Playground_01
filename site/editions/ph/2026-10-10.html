Prosecution witness puts Sara Duterte's undeclared wealth at ₱817.87 million; defense promises its own expert

The forensic accountant said his estimate for 2022 to 2025 draws on a limited set of documents. Sen. Lacson says it must survive cross-examination before it counts.

Alexander Cabrera, a forensic accountant called by the House prosecution, told the Senate impeachment court on Day 37, Friday, October 9, that Vice President Sara Duterte and her family had about ₱817.87 million in assets and income from 2022 to 2025 that were not reflected in her statements of assets, liabilities and net worth (SALN), according to the Inquirer, the Daily Tribune and the Manila Times. He built the figure from bank records, SALNs and corporate filings, and described it as based on a limited set of documents.

The defense has said it will present its own expert to contest the figure, Philstar reported. Sen. Panfilo Lacson said the findings still have to withstand cross-examination and that he would not treat them as conclusive yet. Cabrera had been put forward on Thursday after another prosecution lawyer fell ill, which the defense called an "ultra-belated notice"; prosecutors said the defense had received his report earlier.

The testimony adds to Thursday's evidence from the Bureau of Internal Revenue that the couple's declared income was ₱85.34 million, and to earlier testimony on transactions flagged by the Anti-Money Laundering Council. Duterte denies the charges. The court has made no finding on any of the figures.

PSEi rebounds 1.61 percent to 5,700.99 as bargain hunters return; peso eases off its record low to ₱62.801

The PSEi gained 90.60 points, or 1.61 percent, to close at 5,700.99 on Friday, October 9, partly reversing Thursday's 2.2 percent fall, according to the Inquirer, the Daily Tribune and Philstar. Gainers outnumbered losers 120 to 57 and all six sector indices rose, led by mining and oil. Philstar described the move as a lift from an 11-month low.

The peso closed at ₱62.801 per dollar, 9.9 centavos stronger than the record-low ₱62.90 of Thursday, the Tribune reported. Ron Acoba of Trading Edge called it a technical rebound, said the broader outlook remains bearish and flagged 5,584 to 5,600 as the key support zone. Reported turnover figures differ: the Inquirer cited ₱4.73 billion excluding block sales and Philstar ₱7.82 billion. Local markets are closed on weekends and reopen Monday.

Marcos tells Singapore investors the Philippines is "terribly excited" about Pax Silica, despite protests at home

President Marcos told the Forbes Global CEO Conference in Singapore on Thursday, October 8, that the Philippines is "terribly excited" about Pax Silica, the U.S.-led effort on artificial intelligence and supply chain security, Philstar reported. He also said his overseas trips are a "big effort" to attract investment, according to the Manila Times. The trip began on October 7 and also covers an invitation from Prime Minister Lawrence Wong to the Singapore Grand Prix.

At home, plans for a high-tech site at New Clark City under Pax Silica have drawn protests over land, water and the displacement of Aeta communities, Gulf News and SCMP reported. A trade undersecretary said earlier that U.S. officials and businesses had raised questions after the protests but that investors were not pulling out. The Philippines chairs ASEAN this year, with a summit in Manila on November 15 to 17. The government has not published the terms of any commitments, and DailyDrop has not seen new signed deals from the Singapore trip.

Pump prices seen mixed again next week: diesel down about ₱2 a litre, gasoline up about ₱2.20

Industry estimates based on four days of trading in Singapore point to a second week of mixed pump price changes from Tuesday, October 13, according to GMA News, the Manila Bulletin, Philstar and the Manila Times. Diesel could fall by ₱1.80 to ₱2 per litre while gasoline could rise by ₱2 to ₱2.20. The Department of Energy confirms the final adjustment on Monday, October 12.

This week's change at the pump was a gasoline increase of ₱1.93, a diesel cut of ₱1.30 and a kerosene hike of ₱3.30. U.S. crude (WTI) settled on Friday at $91.85, up 0.39 percent. The forecast covers estimates only; the actual numbers may differ and oil companies set their own prices.

Houthi missile attacks on Saudi airports cut Riyadh flights; Philippine Airlines cancels Manila-Riyadh service

Houthi missile attacks on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and Abha airport on October 6 and 7 killed three people and injured 36, Saudi aviation authorities said, according to Aerocorner and other outlets; other reports give lower figures for Riyadh alone. Cirium data cited by Khaleej Times showed 169 of 378 scheduled departures from Riyadh cancelled or no longer flying. Lufthansa Group suspended flights to the Saudi capital through October 16, and Air India and IndiGo cut service.

Philippine Airlines cancelled its Manila-Riyadh-Manila flights on Thursday, October 8, Khaleej Times reported. Saudi Arabia is among the top destinations for Filipino workers. In September the Department of Migrant Workers said no Filipinos were affected by earlier blasts in Riyadh, but DailyDrop has not seen a statement on the October attacks. Debris from a missile interception also damaged a kindergarten and a medical complex in Riyadh on Thursday.
