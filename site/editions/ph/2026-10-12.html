Marcos says alleged China money to a Duterte family firm would be a national security threat “at the highest level” if proven; prosecutors rest their impeachment case today

The president, speaking in Singapore on Sunday, stopped short of accusing anyone. House prosecutors said they would end their presentation of evidence on Monday to speed up the long-running Senate trial.

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said on Sunday, October 11, that the alleged receipt of funds from China by a company linked to Vice President Sara Duterte could threaten national security “at the highest level” if proven true, calling it the “most disturbing aspect” of the case against her (GMA News, Manila Times). He spoke during a five-day working visit to Singapore and brushed aside claims from the Duterte camp of a smear campaign as “fake news” (Inquirer). The allegation surfaced at the Senate impeachment trial, where the Anti-Money Laundering Council testified that a food company whose incorporators include Duterte’s husband, lawyer Manases “Mans” Carpio, received about ₱319 million in remittances from China and Hong Kong, Reuters and the Daily Tribune reported earlier this month. The National Security Council said it is reviewing the material. Duterte denies wrongdoing. Her lawyers drew an admission that no document shows the Chinese government was involved with the senders, while Sen. Risa Hontiveros said at least two were government-linked (Reuters). DailyDrop has not verified the remittance figure itself. The Senate, sitting as the impeachment court, is in its 14th week of hearing the case; a conviction would remove her from office and could bar her from holding public office again.

Pressure on the Duterte household continued through the weekend. Fishermen from the West Philippine Sea asked her whether “China money” had bought her silence on the sea dispute (GMA News). Carpio said credit-card purchases shown at Friday’s hearing, including nearly ₱600,000 spent at Nike stores in Davao City and Kuala Lumpur according to a forensic analyst’s findings, were paid from his own money, not public funds, and told reporters he feels like “collateral damage” in his wife’s trial (GMA News, Inquirer). In the Senate, the prosecution said it would rest its case on Monday (Philstar). Manila Rep. Joel Chua, a prosecutor, dared the defence to file a motion to dismiss if it thinks the case is flawed, while Sen. Robin Padilla said he and allied senators may skip the trial over what he called a distortion of the Constitution, though he said no such decision had been made (Inquirer, GMA News). Sen. Francis Pangilinan said dropping the bribery charge could speed up the trial, and prosecutors are weighing whether to refer the bribery allegations to the Department of Justice and the Ombudsman (Manila Times). Separately, the Department of Justice said it is up to a Quezon City court whether to proceed with Duterte’s arraignment on three counts of grave threats (Manila Times). Whether the China-money question leads to any inquiry outside the trial is not yet decided; the National Security Council review is the only step reported so far.

Security · West Philippine SeaPress ✓ 4 outlets

Marcos warns of a “new phase” of escalation after China blocked a medical evacuation at Ayungin Shoal; militia vessel and 22 sampans intrude off Palawan

Speaking in Singapore on Sunday, President Marcos said China’s obstruction of a medical evacuation for a sick Filipino serviceman at Ayungin Shoal, also called Second Thomas Shoal, appeared to signal a “new phase” of escalation in the West Philippine Sea, the Philippine name for the parts of the South China Sea inside its exclusive economic zone (Inquirer, Manila Times, Philstar). He questioned Beijing’s insistence that Philippine vessels seek permission to operate there and said Manila would defy China’s growing “requirements.” Philstar reported that the Chinese coast guard blocked the evacuation days earlier. Ayungin is where the Philippine Navy has kept the grounded BRP Sierra Madre as a small outpost since 1999, resupplied by sea, so access to the shoal has been a flashpoint for years. Marcos is on a five-day working visit to Singapore. DailyDrop has not seen the serviceman’s condition or China’s account in the reports reviewed.

Separately, the National Task Force for the West Philippine Sea condemned the intrusion of a Chinese maritime militia vessel and 22 sampans, small wooden boats, into the territorial sea off Kota Island in Palawan, where they allegedly fished without authority and dumped waste at sea (Philstar). On Saturday, a suspected trail from a Chinese Long March-12 rocket launch was seen over Puerto Princesa, and authorities warned people not to approach possible debris (Inquirer, Cebu Daily News). The reports do not link the incidents to each other.

Government · CabinetPress ✓ 4 outlets

Marcos confirms he accepted DICT Secretary Aguda’s resignation after earlier denials; Lino Cayetano to replace Dave Gomez as communications chief

President Marcos confirmed on Sunday that he had accepted the resignation of Information and Communications Technology Secretary Henry Aguda, but said Malacañang held back the announcement while it looked for a replacement (Inquirer, GMA News, ABS-CBN). No successor has been named. Palace officials had said earlier in the week that Aguda was still working, and DailyDrop carried the resignation report as unconfirmed on Sunday; it is now confirmed. A broadcaster’s social-media summary tied the exit to the Ombudsman’s probe of alleged “ghost” connectivity projects, but DailyDrop has not seen Marcos’s own stated reason in full.

Marcos also named Lino Cayetano, general manager of the state broadcaster People’s Television Network (PTV) and a former Taguig mayor, to lead the Presidential Communications Office, replacing Dave Gomez, whose appointment was bypassed by the Commission on Appointments (Manila Times, GMA News, Philstar). The change will be formalised when Marcos returns from Singapore. Rappler counted Cayetano as Marcos’s sixth communications chief. Watch for the DICT successor and for whether the Ombudsman’s probe widens.

Houthi strike on Riyadh’s main airport kills 12 and wounds more than 300; Filipinos among the injured, DMW says

Saudi authorities said a missile and drone attack on Saturday, October 10, on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 12 people and wounded 309 (AP, BBC, Straits Times). The Straits Times counted it as the third strike in a week. The Houthis, the Yemeni armed group, claimed responsibility (Guardian) and on Sunday warned airlines, travellers and airport workers to stay away from Saudi airports (Reuters via Straits Times). The Saudi-led coalition said an unidentified projectile struck Terminal 4 on Sunday and that another missile was intercepted near the airport, and it vowed retaliation (Guardian, Inquirer, Bloomberg). The UN Secretary-General and EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas condemned the attack.

The Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) said Filipinos were hurt: the count rose from two to four through Sunday, with three in intensive care (Inquirer, Philstar, Manila Times). Saudi Arabia is one of the largest host countries for overseas Filipino workers. Saudi stocks slid after the attack (Reuters) and oil executives withdrew from a Riyadh energy congress, the Financial Times reported. Lufthansa Group had already suspended Riyadh flights through October 16.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 6 outlets · announced by Trump; Moscow had not confirmed

Trump announces a Russia-Ukraine “energy ceasefire” on Truth Social; Zelensky says it was news to him but backs it

President Donald Trump said on Sunday, October 11, on his Truth Social platform that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to halt strikes on each other’s energy infrastructure, “effective immediately” (Reuters, BBC, GMA News, Tagesschau). The post came hours after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky pushed back against US pressure for Kyiv to stop unilaterally its attacks on Russian oil refineries (GMA News, Straits Times). Zelensky told Axios the announcement was “news to me,” then said Ukraine supports an energy ceasefire if Russia complies and awaits details from Washington (Ukrainska Pravda, Global News). No Russian confirmation or terms had been reported by early Monday (BBC, PM News), and the Kyiv Independent reported that Russia struck a power plant in Kharkiv shortly after the announcement. The Financial Times reported that Washington had lobbied European capitals and Kyiv to stop the refinery attacks.

Trump also urged Ukraine to choose a new president. Zelensky replied that Ukrainians decide that, and that he would organise elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire (Straits Times, Le Monde). Germany and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen backed him (Straits Times, ANSA). For Filipinos the link is fuel: WTI crude, the US oil benchmark, closed Friday at $91.85, up 0.39 percent, and gold at $4,216.3. A lasting stop to refinery strikes could ease oil prices, while a failed truce would not, and Asian markets reopen on Monday. DailyDrop has seen no price reaction yet.
