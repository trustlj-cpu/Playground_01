Peso breaches ₱63 to the dollar for the first time, a record low, even as the dollar eases from an 18-month high

The peso touched an intraday low of 63.06 on Thursday morning, the Inquirer reported. Philstar tied the slide to renewed Middle East tension. DailyDrop has not yet seen the closing rate.

The Philippine peso fell past ₱63 to the US dollar for the first time on Thursday, October 15, setting a fresh record low (Inquirer, Philstar, Bloomberg). Citing data from the Bankers Association of the Philippines, the Inquirer reported an intraday low of 63.06 in morning trading. For scale, the last close DailyDrop confirmed, on Friday, October 9, was ₱62.801. The fall came even though the dollar itself had retreated from an 18-month high after US inflation data came in hotter than expected (Inquirer, Reuters). Philstar linked the peso’s drop to renewed tension in the Middle East, and a Manila Bulletin headline said intervention by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), the central bank, may no longer be enough to stem the fall. DailyDrop has not seen Thursday’s closing rate or any BSP statement on the move, so the ear above shows a dash.

The pressure is global. US consumer prices accelerated in September on costlier gasoline, with core inflation firmer, and US producer prices posted their largest increase in four and a half years (Reuters, Bloomberg). Reuters reported that Federal Reserve officials tempered bets on a rate hike, with the Fed’s Daly saying hikes are likely needed but not urgently. Technology shares extended their slide on worries over debt tied to artificial intelligence: Reuters said US chip stocks were set to lose about $2 trillion in value and CoreWeave’s shares and bonds fell further, while Asian markets sold off on Thursday (Reuters, Bloomberg). The IMF warned of slowing Asian growth (Reuters), and Philstar’s business pages reported local stocks plunged anew as the IMF flagged growth risk; DailyDrop has no confirmed PSEi close. Oil is the other channel: Reuters reported that oil shipping costs have spiked, lifting prices from pump to grocery, and that the IEA is speeding up its oil stock release. The Philippines buys its oil abroad in dollars, so a weaker peso and a turbulent Gulf pull in the same direction, as the Saudi story below shows. A weaker peso makes imported fuel and food costlier in pesos. Senate President Sherwin Gatchalian flagged an upcoming power-rate increase, with Meralco’s October rate at ₱15.0615 per kilowatt-hour (Inquirer). Remittances, which gain in peso terms when the currency falls, grew only 1.9% in August to $3.035 billion, a three-month low (BusinessWorld).

Politics · ImpeachmentPress ✓ 5 outlets

Prosecutors drop three of four impeachment articles against Sara Duterte; Senate leaders deny budget-pressure claim

House prosecutors withdrew three of the four articles of impeachment against Vice President Sara Duterte on Wednesday, October 14, saying they had seen her defence team plan to stall the trial (Inquirer, Manila Times, GMA News). Malacañang said it respects the prosecution’s “strategy” (Manila Times) and that there is no talk of replacing the Vice President (GMA News). One prosecutor said evidence from the withdrawn articles may be used in other complaints (GMA News). Duterte’s camp claimed ₱500 million was wasted on the dropped charges (BusinessWorld). DailyDrop has not seen which article remains or the date of the next session.

Duterte said on Wednesday that the 2027 budget could be used to pressure senator-judges and reward lawmakers who voted to impeach her (Inquirer). On Thursday Senate President Gatchalian and Senators Erwin Tulfo and Panfilo Lacson rejected that (Inquirer, GMA News). Lacson said slightly more than ₱14 million has been spent on the trial and ₱11 million remains, more than enough (Manila Times, GMA News, Manila Bulletin). A Pahayag survey found 63% see the trial as an unreasonable use of public funds, and Manila Times and BusinessWorld reported that about 44%, or four in 10, expect a conviction.

Rice and corn output seen falling more than 10% in the third quarter as the government readies for a “super” El Niño

The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) estimates that a strong El Niño and unfavourable weather threaten to cut palay (unmilled rice) and corn output by more than 10% in the third quarter of 2026 (Inquirer, Philstar). Philstar reported that agricultural damage this year has reached ₱6.8 billion. The Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) said water treated with filtration kits is safe to drink and that it will roll out the kits next week (Inquirer, Manila Times, Philstar). President Marcos told the Department of Transportation on Thursday to clear bottlenecks that slow the movement of essential goods (Inquirer), and has ordered tighter food, water and energy security measures (Inquirer).

Power supply is already tight. The Visayas grid went on yellow alert on Thursday, its first in more than two weeks, because two large coal plants were out and imports from Mindanao were limited, with 645.6 megawatts unavailable (Inquirer, GMA News). Separately, the Environment department recorded hazardous haze in eight Philippine areas (Inquirer), and thick haze from fires in Indonesia closed schools in Timor-Leste (GMA News, Inquirer). A UN biodiversity expert warned the El Niño is a “terrifying” threat to coral reefs (Inquirer, citing AFP). For households the effects arrive as food prices, water supply and power bills. DailyDrop has not seen an official forecast of how long the El Niño will last.

Economy · SurveysPress ✓ 4 outlets

Hunger climbs to 22.6% in SWS survey as Pulse Asia finds inflation is Filipinos’ top concern

The share of Filipino families reporting involuntary hunger rose to 22.6% in September from 20.3% in June, the Social Weather Stations (SWS) survey released on Wednesday showed (Inquirer, Philstar). The Inquirer said the rise reflects sharply higher living costs. Pulse Asia’s September 2026 Ulat ng Bayan survey, conducted from September 17 to 24 through face-to-face interviews, found that Filipinos are most concerned about controlling inflation, followed by fighting graft and corruption (Inquirer, Manila Times, BusinessWorld). DailyDrop has not seen the surveys’ margins of error.

On approval, Pulse Asia found President Marcos’s trust and approval ratings edged up while Vice President Sara Duterte’s declined, though she still holds a slim majority of approval and trust (GMA News, Inquirer, Philstar). Nearly half of respondents disapprove of Marcos’s performance (Inquirer). Taken with the weaker peso, the surveys point at the same pressure: prices. Check what your household spends on rice, fuel and power; those are the items the El Niño and the dollar push first. A separate survey reported by the Manila Times found nearly half of Filipinos want the government to pay system loss costs. The SWS and Pulse Asia releases came within a day of each other, but they use different methods, so DailyDrop does not compare their percentages.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · attack claims attributed to their sources

Houthi attacks reach Aden and Saudi targets, Hormuz shipping attacks hit a wartime high, and seven injured Filipinos are to be sent home

The Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) will provide financial and medical help, including repatriation, to seven Filipino workers injured in attacks attributed to the Houthis in Saudi Arabia, Secretary Hans Leo Cacdac said in a radio interview (Inquirer). An earlier DMW count was four injured. The Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis tried to attack dozens of Saudi facilities (Reuters), and officials said the group fired missiles at the presidential palace complex in Yemen’s Aden; the prime minister was unharmed (Reuters, FinancialJuice). The Houthis said the Aden strike hit a Saudi military operations headquarters (FinancialJuice). Reuters also reported that the EU and Gulf states postponed a summit due in Saudi Arabia.

Attacks in the Strait of Hormuz reached their highest level since the war with Iran began, according to security data (GMA News), and BusinessWorld reported two ships sank after being hit by projectiles near the strait; Reuters, citing sources, said a container ship sank. The International Energy Agency (IEA) said its total oil stock release will exceed 400 million barrels, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel (Reuters). Reuters also reported that oil shipping costs have spiked, lifting prices from pump to grocery. Oil prices were volatile as investors weighed supply and the Middle East (Reuters, Bloomberg Television). For Filipinos the links are workers in the Gulf, pump prices and the peso. DailyDrop has not seen details of where the seven were injured.
