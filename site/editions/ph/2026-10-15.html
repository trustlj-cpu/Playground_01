Politics · ImpeachmentPress ✓ 6 outlets · poll figures belong to the survey firms

Senate impeachment court lets prosecutors drop three of four articles against Sara Duterte; the House team calls the unexplained-wealth charge “a risk worth taking,” and she says she will “never resign”

The court agreed to the narrowing on Tuesday, then adjourned the trial until November 9. Malacañang said it respects the prosecution’s “strategy” and will not interfere if she stays in office.

The Senate, sitting as the impeachment court, on Tuesday, October 13, the 39th day of the trial, allowed the House prosecution panel to withdraw three of the four articles of impeachment against Vice President Sara Duterte. The allegation of unexplained wealth and nondisclosure of assets is now the only basis for the trial (Inquirer, GMA News, Rappler). The day was meant to be spent scheduling the remaining proceedings; instead the prosecution made a surprise request to focus on Article 2, and Rappler asked how Presiding Officer Francis “Chiz” Escudero could rule so fast on it (GMA News, Rappler). The court then adjourned until November 9, with several full-day hearings scheduled through that month (Manila Times). On Wednesday, Manila Rep. Joel Chua and another prosecutor said the three articles were dropped because they believed the defence would only try to delay the trial, and the panel said resting its case on one article is “a risk worth taking” (Inquirer, GMA News). A private prosecutor added that testimony already given on the withdrawn articles may still be used in the complaints pending against Duterte before the Ombudsman (GMA News).

Duterte’s camp said the withdrawal admits that the allegations “lacked basis,” and her spokesperson said it denied her the chance to clear her name (GMA News, Philstar). The camp also said about ₱500 million was wasted on the dropped charges; Sen. Panfilo Lacson called the figure misleading and said it reflects the Senate’s regular operating expenses rather than the cost of the trial (BusinessWorld, Inquirer, GMA News). Duterte herself said, “I will not resign. I will never resign” (Rappler, Philstar). Malacañang replied that nobody will interfere with her decision to stay and that it respects the prosecution’s strategy (GMA News, Manila Times). Surveys give mixed readings. Pulse Asia’s September poll of 2,400 adults found 51 percent want her to testify in person (GMA News, Inquirer, Philstar), while a PUBLiCUS Asia poll taken October 1 to 4 found 44 percent of registered voters expect a conviction and 31 percent an acquittal (Manila Times, BusinessWorld). An SWS survey commissioned by the Stratbase Group put her trust rating at 56 percent, against 36 percent for President Marcos (Manila Times, Inquirer, Philstar). The head of the NCRPO told Metro Manila police to stay alert for rallies (Inquirer, Manila Bulletin). Whether the dropped articles can be revived is unsettled: one report quotes Escudero as saying senators can challenge the withdrawal, another quotes the court’s spokesperson as saying two cannot be refiled (Panay News, Politiko). A conviction would remove Duterte from office; DailyDrop has not seen a date for a vote.

World · DiplomacyPress ✓ 6 outlets · programme not yet released

Palace confirms Trump will attend the ASEAN Summit in Manila in November, his first visit since 2017

Malacañang confirmed on Wednesday that US President Donald Trump will come to Manila for the 49th ASEAN Summit next month (Inquirer, GMA News, BusinessWorld, Philstar). Press Officer Claire Castro said, “It is confirmed,” but gave no details of the visit (Inquirer). The Manila Times reported that United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres will attend too and gave the summit dates as November 16 to 18. Philstar noted that it will be Trump’s first trip to the Philippines since 2017, the last time the country hosted the summit. BusinessWorld linked the announcement to continuing Philippine–US talks on an agreement for a multibillion-dollar technology hub in New Clark City.

A confirmation is not yet a programme. The reports reviewed do not say where Trump will stay, whether a meeting with President Marcos is planned, or what he will discuss, and DailyDrop has not seen a statement from Washington. Expect road closures and tighter security in the capital around the summit dates, though no routes have been announced. For the Philippines, the visit puts a US president in the room as host of a regional meeting, which makes the technology-hub talks and any trade or defence announcements worth watching in the weeks before it.

Weather · FoodPress ✓ 6 outlets · figures from NDRRMC and PSA

El Niño: 109 towns and cities declare a state of calamity as the government prepares water filters and warns of lower rice output

The NDRRMC said on Wednesday that 109 cities and municipalities have declared a state of calamity because of El Niño (GMA News). In its situation report as of October 14, it counted more than 451,000 people affected, including over 215,000 farmers and fisherfolk and 121,185 families (Inquirer). President Marcos convened government agencies at Malacañang on Tuesday and ordered tighter measures on food, water and energy security (Inquirer). After he urged Filipinos to collect rainwater in buckets, the DSWD, led by Secretary Rex Gatchalian, said it will distribute household water filtration kits and set up community water depots; the Inquirer reported the rollout is set for next week (Manila Times, BusinessWorld, Inquirer).

The harvest is the next worry. The Philippine Statistics Authority projects palay output to fall 17 percent in the third quarter (Philstar), and the Inquirer reported that palay and corn together could drop by more than 10 percent. Chinabank Research said inflation could pass 8 percent in the fourth quarter if a severe El Niño materialises (BusinessWorld); the BSP has not published a matching forecast in the reports reviewed. Separately, the Department of Agriculture cancelled a ₱700-million rice shipment from Vietnam, and Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. denied graft claims over the decision (Philstar, Inquirer, GMA News). PAGASA expects rain in parts of the country on Thursday, but that is not a sign the dry spell has ended (Inquirer, Philstar).

World · FaithPress ✓ 8 outlets · medical details from Vatican bulletins

Pope Leo XIV has lung adenocarcinoma, the Vatican says; he is walking and eating after surgery, and Cardinal Tagle takes his place at Wednesday’s Mass

Pope Leo XIV, 71, underwent a 90-minute operation in Rome on Tuesday in which doctors removed the lower lobe of his right lung, and tests confirmed lung adenocarcinoma, the Vatican said (Reuters, AP via Inquirer, BBC, GMA News). Adenocarcinoma is the most common form of lung cancer and, as AP reported, is very treatable if caught early, as the pope’s tumour appears to have been (Inquirer, BusinessWorld). The Holy See Press Office said his first night was calm and, on Wednesday, that his recovery is “progressing normally” based on test results; doctors said he is out of bed, walking and eating (GMA News, Inquirer, Guardian, BBC).

Filipino Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle was to preside over Wednesday’s Mass at St. Peter’s Square, at 4 p.m. Philippine time, in place of the pope (Manila Times, Philstar, GMA News). Cardinal Pablo Virgilio David asked Filipino Catholics to send the pope a “hug” through prayer (GMA News). The Philippines has the largest Catholic population in Asia, so the news was widely reported here. Rappler quoted experts who said the size of the tumour and any spread to lymph nodes still need to be reported before the outlook can be judged. DailyDrop has not seen the cancer’s stage or a treatment plan, and the Vatican has not said when the pope will leave hospital.

World · Energy · MarketsPress ✓ 7 outlets · oil settlement prices from one data service

Houthi missile hits Saudi Aramco’s headquarters and Iran steps up ship attacks near Hormuz; oil rises, and US inflation of 3.7 percent revives talk of a Fed rate hike

A ballistic missile hit the headquarters complex of Saudi Aramco in Dhahran, a company source told Reuters, in reports that reached Manila early on Wednesday (GMA News, Reuters, Bloomberg Television). Reuters later cited sources describing extensive damage, while BusinessWorld reported a limited fire, and the Houthis vowed more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities (Reuters, BusinessWorld). In Riyadh, Saudi Civil Defense said debris from two intercepted missiles injured four people and slightly damaged 15 homes (GMA News), and AP reported residents heard new explosions. In the Gulf, security data showed at least ten vessels attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz since Sunday, the highest since the war began (GMA News). A container ship sank after being hit by a projectile and a second vessel was reportedly sinking (Reuters, Guardian). Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks on shipping before the US vote, officials said (Reuters via GMA News, BusinessWorld, Rappler). The DMW said it is assisting a Filipino seafarer hurt in a drone attack in the Persian Gulf (Manila Times).

Oil rose on the Saudi supply risk and the Russian diesel flow (Reuters, Bloomberg). FinancialJuice, a market-data service, put West Texas Intermediate for November at $95.09 a barrel, up 1.01 percent, and Brent at $106.06; DailyDrop has not matched those figures with a second source. In the United States, consumer prices rose 3.7 percent in September on costlier gasoline, with core CPI firmer (Reuters, FT). Bloomberg reported that stocks and bonds fell as traders raised bets on a Fed rate hike; the Fed’s Collins said another hike is on the table before the end of 2026, while the Fed’s Bowman said there is time to “wait and see” (Reuters). Chip stocks tumbled and the S&P 500 ended its longest spell of calm since 2020 (FT, Bloomberg). For Filipinos the links are pump prices, the peso and the BSP; the reports reviewed carry no pump-price forecast for next week.
