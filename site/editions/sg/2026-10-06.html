Eight public officers referred to the police over homes bought near future MRT stations. A referral is not a finding of guilt, Chan Chun Sing says

The Public Service Division reviewed 191 officers in rail-planning agencies who bought property between 2007 and 2014 near sites later announced as stations. The review began after a US working paper claimed evidence of informed trading.

Coordinating Minister for Public Services Chan Chun Sing told Parliament on Tuesday that eight public officers have been referred to the police over property purchases near MRT stations that had not yet been announced. The Business Times, the Straits Times, the South China Morning Post, The Star and Malay Mail all reported the statement. The eight were picked out of a review by the Public Service Division of 191 officers who worked in agencies involved in rail planning and who bought property between 2007 and 2014 near locations that were later announced as stations.

Mr Chan was careful about what the referral means. It is not a finding of guilt, he said: the police will look at the facts independently, and due process will be followed. According to the reports, cases were flagged either because of the particular circumstances of a purchase, or because there was not enough information to rule them out. The review weighed each officer's role at the time, whether the officer had access to relevant information that was not public, and the timing of the purchase. The period was extended to 2014 as a precaution, because new station locations kept being announced after 2011.

The review was set off by a working paper from the US National Bureau of Economic Research, which claimed to find clear evidence of informed trading by civil servants near future stations. Working papers of this kind are not peer-reviewed. The government had said before Tuesday's sitting that any wrongdoing would be referred to the authorities. Mr Chan's statement restated the principle at stake: public officers must never use information gained in their official duties to advance their private interests.

The case touches a sensitive nerve. MRT announcements move property prices, and land-use planning in Singapore depends on the public trusting that those who draw the lines do not profit from them. Reports differ slightly on whether the review is closed: one says further checks are continuing, another that the government has not ruled out a wider review. What happens next is in the hands of the police. Until they report, the eight officers remain unnamed and no one has been charged. DailyDrop will report charges, if any, and whether the review is extended beyond 2014.

Two questions will follow the case. The first is scope. The review covered officers in rail-planning agencies, but station plans pass through many hands, from land agencies to contractors, and MPs may ask whether anyone outside the public service was checked. The second is method. The NBER paper is a statistical claim about patterns across many purchases; the police inquiry will test individual cases. A gap between the two is possible either way, and the government will need to explain its findings plainly if it wants the matter closed.

A 200ha site on Pulau Tekong could become Singapore's next energy hub, with room for an LNG terminal

The Energy Market Authority has launched a tender for a consultant to study whether part of Pulau Tekong can host an energy infrastructure cluster, CNA, the Business Times and the Straits Times reported on Tuesday. The site is about 200ha, which CNA put at 40 per cent of the size of Sentosa.

The Straits Times said the cluster could include a third LNG terminal and facilities for electricity imports. The Business Times said it could also house hydrogen-ready gas-fired power plants and other low-carbon technologies. The consultant is expected to be appointed by mid-2027, and the study is estimated to take about two years.

The reason is demand. Data centres, electrification and a growing population all need more power, and the Business Times cited EMA projections of peak demand growing by 2.4 to 4.8 per cent a year over the next decade. Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said in August that about 500ha of Pulau Tekong would be freed for civilian use once the SAF's needs were met. This is the first concrete idea for part of that land. Nothing has been decided: the study comes first, and it will take until about 2029.

'Not the time to go soft': Shanmugam signals new laws to ensure deterrent sentences for scam mules

Home Affairs Minister K Shanmugam signalled on Tuesday that new laws will be introduced to ensure deterrent sentences for all scam mules, the Straits Times and CNA reported. Scam mules are people who let criminals use their bank accounts or phone lines to move the proceeds of scams.

He rejected calls for leniency towards young offenders, telling the House to 'spare a thought for elderly victims', CNA reported. The details of the new laws, including minimum penalties, have not yet been published. The message to anyone tempted to rent out a bank account for quick cash is clear: the government intends that it ends with jail.

ParliamentPress ✓ 2 outlets

Parliament approves higher salaries for the President, the Speaker and Deputy Speakers

Parliament approved increases to the salaries of the President, the Speaker of Parliament and the Deputy Speakers on Tuesday, the Straits Times and the Business Times reported. The Straits Times also reported that, from 2016 to 2025, the performance bonus for political office-holders ranged from 2.5 to 6 months.

Ministerial and presidential pay is pegged to private-sector benchmarks in Singapore, and every change draws debate over whether the formula is right. The reports did not give the full new figures in the headlines we checked, so we leave the amounts out.

EnvironmentPress ✓ 2 outlets · readings from NEA as reported

Haze holds: PSI unhealthy across most of the island as PM2.5 rises in four regions

The haze did not lift on Tuesday. The Straits Times reported the 24-hour PSI unhealthy across the entire island early in the day, and CNA said PM2.5 levels were elevated in four regions. At 6am, readings were reported at 107 in the north, 112 in the east, 117 in the west and 130 in the central region, with only the south in the moderate range.

The winds are the problem. As long as they blow from the south-east, smoke from fires in Sumatra keeps reaching Singapore. For outdoor workers and schools, it is now a second unhealthy day in a row, and families are planning around the morning reading.
