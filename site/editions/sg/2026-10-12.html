Haze stays unhealthy as subsidised treatment for haze-related illnesses starts today

NEA expected the 24-hour PSI to remain in the unhealthy to high unhealthy range on Sunday. From today, eligible Singaporeans pay no more than S$10 at GP clinics and polyclinics.

Air quality across Singapore stayed in the unhealthy range on Sunday, though it improved slightly, CNA and the Straits Times reported. The National Environment Agency (NEA) expected the 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) to sit between "unhealthy" and "high unhealthy", CNA said, and the Straits Times put one-hour readings of PM2.5, the fine particles that reach deep into the lungs, at 56 to 108, in the elevated band. The PSI bands matter: 101 to 200 is unhealthy, 201 to 300 very unhealthy and above 300 hazardous. DailyDrop has not seen the full hourly readings for Sunday night, so check NEA directly before you plan the day.

The health measures start today. Health Minister Ong Ye Kung said a scheme providing subsidies for haze-related conditions begins on 12 October, the Straits Times reported, and the Ministry of Health (MOH) said eligible Singaporeans will pay no more than S$10 for treatment at GP clinics and polyclinics, CNA reported. The Straits Times said the qualifying conditions include allergic rhinitis and asthma. DailyDrop has not seen the full list of conditions, how "eligible" is defined, or whether the S$10 cap covers medicines, so ask the clinic before you go.

Schools have a plan if the air gets worse. Education Minister Desmond Lee said the Ministry of Education (MOE) will consider closing schools, and may postpone national examinations, if the next-day 24-hour PSI forecast exceeds 300, The Independent Singapore reported. Until then, lessons and examinations continue in enclosed venues with air purifiers. That is a single-outlet account, so confirm with your school. As DailyDrop reads it, the 300 line is the point at which the rules change, and the forecast, not the hourly number, is what triggers it.

The smoke is crossing borders. Firefly, the Malaysian low-cost carrier, cancelled all flights between Subang and Seletar airports through today, CNA and the Straits Times reported, moving passengers to Malaysia Airlines. Johor extended its school closures to 16 October, having begun on 8 October (CNA, Straits Times), while schools in Melaka, parts of Perak and Kuala Lumpur reopen today as air quality improves, the Straits Times said. The Formula 1 weekend went ahead in the murk and drew a near-record crowd, Bloomberg reported (see the Grand Prix story below). The Singapore stock market reopens today after the weekend; the Straits Times Index ended Friday at 5,401.89, down 0.20 per cent. Watch the NEA forecast for tomorrow, and whether the wind changes.

Singapore · SportPress ✓ 5 outlets · result and attendance as reported by the Straits Times and CNA

Verstappen wins the Singapore Grand Prix at his tenth attempt, and Antonelli moves close to the title

Max Verstappen won the Singapore Grand Prix at Marina Bay on Sunday night, his first win here at the tenth attempt, after also winning Saturday's sprint, the Straits Times and CNA reported. Ferrari's Charles Leclerc finished second and Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli third, Formula 1's website said. Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur said Lewis Hamilton's missed pit-lane stop cost the team a double podium, the Straits Times reported. Sky Sports said Hamilton apologised for the strategy.

The result keeps the title race moving. The Straits Times said Antonelli could seal his first world championship at the next race in the United States on 25 October. DailyDrop has not seen the points gap, so treat "close" as the Straits Times's word. Off the track, the Straits Times reported attendance of 301,294 over the weekend, the second highest on record behind the 302,000 set in 2022, and Bloomberg called it a near-record turnout despite the haze. The point for readers is that the sport went ahead in unhealthy air and the crowds still came; the next test is whether organisers and NEA review the rules for outdoor events if the haze lasts.

Trump announces an "energy ceasefire" between Russia and Ukraine; Kyiv says it supports it but awaits details

President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social on Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an "energy ceasefire", urging both sides not to break it, Reuters, the BBC, CNA and the Straits Times reported. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said on Telegram that he had spoken to Mark Carney, Alex Stubb and Mark Rutte and that Ukraine supports the ceasefire, though The Week quoted him calling the announcement "news to me" and FinancialJuice said Kyiv awaits details from Washington. Mr Zelenskiy said Ukraine will stop hitting Russian refineries if Moscow halts its attacks on Ukrainian energy, Reuters reported.

DailyDrop has not seen a Kremlin statement. Mr Zelenskiy also told ABC he would organise elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire (Reuters), after Mr Trump called for his removal; Germany's foreign minister, Johann Wadephul, restated Berlin's support for him (Reuters, Straits Times). Separately, Republican Senator Thom Tillis said Mr Trump's diesel supply deal with Russia may be illegal, adding to bipartisan criticism (Reuters, Straits Times).

Riyadh airport attack kills 12 and wounds 309; the Houthis claim it and warn airlines off Saudi airports

Saudi civil aviation authorities said at least 12 people were killed and 309 wounded in a Saturday attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, AP and CNA reported. The Straits Times said it was the third strike in a week, and Yemen's Houthis claimed responsibility and renewed a warning to airlines, travellers and airport workers to stay away from Saudi airports. AP reported that the closure of the airport was extended, and Reuters said Saudi stocks slid.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attack, and Singapore's Zaqy Mohamad said targeting civilians and civilian infrastructure "cannot be justified" (Straits Times). The Financial Times reported that BP and other oil executives skipped the World Petroleum Council's energy congress in Riyadh. A Houthi claim of strikes on Dammam airport and Aramco facilities had no immediate Saudi confirmation. WTI closed Friday at US$91.85.

Pope Leo says he will have surgery this week to remove a small growth from his lung

Pope Leo XIV, 71, said on Sunday that doctors found a small nodule on his lung during a recent health check and that he will have surgery in the first days of this week to remove it, Reuters, the BBC, the Guardian, CNA and the Straits Times reported. The BBC and the Guardian quoted him saying it was identified "at a very early stage".

DailyDrop has not seen the hospital, the surgeon's name or the expected recovery time, and nothing has been announced about changes to his engagements. A nodule is a small lump of tissue; it is not always cancer, and the Pope did not say what this one is.
