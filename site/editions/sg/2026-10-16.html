Singapore weighs legal action against the firms behind the haze fires; Malaysia says it has no plans to

Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said Singapore will act under its Transboundary Haze Pollution Act where warranted. Indonesia has agreed to accept foreign firefighting help, while Kuala Lumpur prefers talks to legal steps.

Singapore moved from coping with the haze to asking who is responsible. Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said on Thursday that the government is looking into which companies are behind the fires that cause the haze, and that it may take legal action, the Straits Times, CNA and the Business Times reported. The Straits Times said he added that, where warranted, Singapore will act under its Transboundary Haze Pollution Act. CNA said Singapore is also exploring joint action with other countries. The South China Morning Post and Free Malaysia Today carried the same line. DailyDrop has not seen Mr Wong name a company.

Malaysia took a different line. Foreign Minister Mohamad Hasan said Malaysia has no plans to take legal action or send a diplomatic note over the haze, and that talks between leaders and through regional channels are the preferred route (Straits Times). The Straits Times also reported that frustration is building across the Causeway, with calls to hold polluters to account and school closures that families cannot predict. Malaysia has raised the bar: schools now close where the Air Pollutant Index (API) is above 200, and outdoor activities stop once readings reach 100 (CNA). Schools in Kuala Lumpur are due to open today with outdoor activities suspended (Straits Times).

Indonesia, where the fires burn, gave some ground. Foreign Minister Arrmanatha Nasir said President Prabowo Subianto has approved foreign help with firefighting, CNA reported, without saying which countries had offered or in what form. The Straits Times said the decision followed mixed signals from officials after offers from Malaysia and Singapore, and the Business Times said the National Disaster Mitigation Agency is handling the technical talks. That agency has a new head: Mr Prabowo replaced its chief a day after the chief’s brother was among five people held over alleged misuse of disaster funds (Straits Times, CNA, Business Times). Indonesia blames degraded peatland and promises restoration (Reuters, Straits Times), and will trial dry ice over South Sumatra to disperse smoke (CNA, The Guardian). Timor-Leste has shut schools (CNA, Straits Times).

At home the air stayed poor. The 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) in the west was 207 at 8am, in the very unhealthy range (CNA), and by 5pm the hourly PM2.5 reading was in the high range of 151 to 250 across the whole island (Straits Times). ActiveSG closed outdoor facilities in the central, southern and western regions, while the north and east stayed open (Straits Times). Free N95 masks went out, with long queues and stock shortages for some people and little fuss for others (CNA), though the Straits Times said the haze centres themselves saw few visitors. Organisers of this weekend’s BigBang concerts issued a haze advisory (CNA, Straits Times). Greenpeace named two Singapore-listed palm oil firms in a report on fires on Indonesian plantations, and First Resources disputes that the fires began on its land (Straits Times, Business Times). Today’s question is whether the reading in the west falls below the very unhealthy range.

Energy · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · release totals partly from a single feed

The IEA speeds up its emergency oil release as Houthi attacks widen and Hormuz sees its most attacks of the war

The International Energy Agency (IEA) said its members agreed to complete the release of the 100 million barrels still outstanding from the emergency action launched in March, Bloomberg and Reuters reported. Reuters said the total release will exceed 400 million barrels and that more than 20 million barrels of diesel are to be released as soon as possible. FinancialJuice, a single feed, put the amount released so far at about 330 million barrels. The decision came as the Houthis, who are aligned with Iran, widened their attacks. They fired missiles at the presidential palace complex in Aden on Thursday, killing two security guards, officials told Reuters, and the BBC said the prime minister and other ministers were unhurt. The Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis had tried to attack dozens of Saudi airports, ports and oil facilities (Reuters, Straits Times).

At sea, attacks in and near the Strait of Hormuz are at their highest since the Iran war began, with at least ten vessels hit since 11 October (Straits Times, citing security data). Reuters said a container ship hit by a projectile sank. The knock-on reaches travel: the EU and Gulf states postponed a summit due in Saudi Arabia (Reuters, Straits Times), JAL and ANA are raising fuel surcharges (Straits Times), and Lufthansa told jets to carry extra fuel into Frankfurt (Reuters). Oil eased in Asian trading as investors weighed supply (Reuters, CNA).

Markets · TechnologyPress ✓ 6 outlets · STI level from SGX market data

Chip stocks slide again even as TSMC reports a record profit; the STI closes down 0.37 per cent

TSMC said net profit for July to September rose to NT$763.1 billion (S$30.23 billion), a record for a third quarter, the Straits Times reported. The Financial Times said it beat estimates, and Reuters had expected a sixth straight quarter of record profit. Bloomberg said the outlook failed to ease fears of an AI bubble. Breakingthenews carried a company line that demand significantly exceeds supply capacity; DailyDrop has seen that from one outlet only.

Chip shares fell regardless. South Korea’s KOSPI triggered a circuit breaker and closed at a six-month low, with Seoul and Tokyo bearing the brunt of Asia’s selling (CNA). In Singapore, the Business Times said AEM Holdings, CSE Global, UMS Integration and Frencken each fell more than 6 per cent on Thursday, and the Straits Times Index (STI) closed down 0.37 per cent at 5,403.5. The worry is shifting to the debt behind the AI build-out: CoreWeave’s shares and bonds slid further (Reuters, CNA), and Reuters said US chip stocks were on course to wipe out US$2 trillion after the latest sell-off. Wednesday’s last completed US session ended with the Nasdaq down 1.88 per cent at 26,692.87 (Straits Times). Thursday’s US session ends after this edition. SGX starts trading five new depository receipts, including TSMC and SK Hynix, today (Business Times).

Economy · United StatesPress ✓ 5 outlets · figures as reported by Bloomberg and the Straits Times

US producer prices jump the most since 2022, yet the dollar eases and gold rebounds as Fed hike bets cool

US producer prices rose in September at the fastest pace in more than four years as higher energy costs worked through the economy, Bloomberg reported. The producer price index rose 1.3 per cent from August, the most since March 2022, and 6.3 per cent from a year earlier (Bloomberg, Straits Times). Reuters called it the largest increase in four and a half years. Retail sales beat expectations in September on higher petrol prices (Reuters).

Markets read the data less harshly than they had read Wednesday’s consumer prices. The dollar eased from an 18-month high as Treasury yields fell and bets on a Fed rate rise cooled (Reuters, CNA). Reuters, in an exclusive on Wednesday, quoted the Fed’s Daly as saying rate rises are likely to be needed, but not urgently. Spot gold rebounded 1.1 per cent to US$4,149 an ounce after a one-week low (Straits Times). Bloomberg said stocks fell while yields plunged, as CoreWeave’s troubles weighed on tech. For Singapore, the Business Times said low rates may weigh on the Singdollar after the MAS policy hold.

Transport · SingaporePress ✓ 2 outlets · figures from the Land Transport Authority as reported

MRT reliability hits another record in September, and the Bukit Panjang LRT renewal stays on track for year-end

The MRT network’s mean kilometres between failures rose to 3.38 million train-km in September, above the previous record of 2.88 million set in August, CNA reported. The Straits Times said it was the first time the measure had passed three million. Both outlets carried the Land Transport Authority’s (LTA) figures.

The renewal of the Bukit Panjang LRT, Singapore’s oldest LRT line, is due to be finished by the end of 2026, with the work to replace the power rails 98 per cent done, the Straits Times said. CNA noted that the programme began in 2018, and the LTA said the upgrades have lifted reliability indicators for the network (Straits Times). Elsewhere on the roads, six passengers were injured when three public buses collided in Yishun (CNA, Straits Times); MS News said the bus captain is assisting investigations. DailyDrop has not seen a cause given.
