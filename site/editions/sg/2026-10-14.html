Haze stays unhealthy across Singapore, but the Health Ministry sees only a small rise in clinic visits

The 24-hour PSI stayed unhealthy in every region on Tuesday morning. Sixteen flights were diverted from Seletar in a week, and Malaysia closes more schools today.

Air quality stayed in the unhealthy range on Tuesday. The Straits Times reported on Tuesday morning that the 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) remained unhealthy in all regions of Singapore, and AsiaOne reported that the PSI had been rising overnight as fine-particle readings climbed. At 6am the one-hour PM2.5 reading, which measures particles small enough to reach deep into the lungs, was in the elevated band in four of the five regions (Straits Times); by about 8am CNA reported it was elevated everywhere except the north. NEA, the National Environment Agency, publishes both readings. DailyDrop has not seen the evening figures, so check NEA directly before you plan the day.

There is no sign yet of a health surge. The Ministry of Health (MOH) said there was a small uptick in visits to polyclinics and emergency rooms, and that it was not aware of any deaths or haze-related cases sent to intensive care, the Straits Times reported. Demand for protection is rising: a retailer told the Straits Times that sales of air purifiers and air conditioners picked up in recent weeks and jumped from 9 October, while stocks of N95 masks are sufficient. A separate Straits Times piece explains why switching on an air conditioner alone will not keep haze particles out of indoor air.

The smoke is also reaching the runways. The Civil Aviation Authority of Singapore (CAAS) and Changi Airport Group said 16 flights were diverted from Seletar Airport between 5 and 11 October because of low visibility; Changi and Seletar remain open, and further measures will follow if the haze worsens (CNA, Straits Times). Malaysia’s Firefly extended its cancellation of Seletar–Subang flights, calling it a “proactive safety measure” (CNA). Across the border, schools in Johor, Melaka, Negeri Sembilan and several other districts are closed today (Straits Times).

The argument is turning to the source. Malaysia’s foreign minister, Mohamad Hasan, said his country will raise transboundary haze at an ASEAN meeting because the bloc’s disaster mechanism does not class haze as a natural disaster (CNA). In Indonesia, President Prabowo Subianto has ordered tougher action on the fires, and foreign offers of help were not discussed, the Straits Times said; CNA reported that poor visibility and wild animals are hampering firefighters. MS News carried an Indonesian official’s remark that “when there is no haze, we send oxygen”, and the Straits Times ran a headline saying Malaysian and Singaporean firms are under investigation. DailyDrop has not seen the details of that inquiry or any company named.

Singapore tells citizens to defer all travel to Saudi Arabia as Houthi fire reaches Aramco’s headquarters, sources say

Singapore’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA) advised Singaporeans on Tuesday to defer all travel to Saudi Arabia because of heightened security risks (CNA, Straits Times). Those already there should avoid King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and other high-risk areas until further notice, and avoid non-essential travel within the kingdom, CNA quoted the MFA as saying. The advice follows the Houthi strikes on the airport on Saturday: Reuters, via the Straits Times, put the toll at at least 18 dead and more than 300 wounded, while CNA’s latest count was 14 dead, so the figure depends on the report. The airport stays shut this week.

The war widened on Tuesday. The Saudi-led coalition told civilians to leave a Yemeni oil site in Sanaa and then struck oil depots on the capital’s outskirts, Reuters, the Guardian and the BBC reported, and the BBC said the Houthis vowed to retaliate against Riyadh and Dammam. Later Reuters reported loud booms near Saudi Aramco’s headquarters in Dhahran; Bloomberg, citing people with knowledge, said the headquarters was hit in a missile attack that started a fire in part of the complex. DailyDrop has not seen a Saudi or Aramco statement. Brent settled at US$105.69, down 0.9%, according to FinancialJuice (single source).

Public hospitals and clinics recover after a disruption of about nine hours that hit call lines and systems

Public healthcare institutions reported system disruptions on Tuesday. CNA said call-centre lines across NHG Health institutions, including four hospitals, were unavailable, and that the National University Hospital (NUH) and Singapore General Hospital (SGH) said separately that they were working to resume normal operations. The four NHG Health hospitals are Tan Tock Seng Hospital, Khoo Teck Puat Hospital, Woodlands Hospital and Yishun Community Hospital (CNA). NHG Health said on Facebook it was working to restore access (Straits Times). The Business Times and the Straits Times told patients to expect longer waits, and AsiaOne said institutions under NHG Health, NUHS and SingHealth were hit by digital disruptions.

By evening the systems were back. AsiaOne reported that public healthcare services were fully restored after the network disruption, and CNA described the clusters as recovering from a nine-hour disruption. Across seven institutions it visited, most patients said they had few or no problems, though signs warned of possible longer waits. The reports DailyDrop has seen do not say what caused the outage or whether any patient data was affected, so both remain open questions.

Singapore refreshes its cyber strategy to use “best available” AI, as Shanmugam presses tech firms for full transparency

Singapore unveiled an updated national cybersecurity strategy, CNA and the Straits Times reported on Tuesday. Senior Minister and Coordinating Minister for National Security K Shanmugam said Singapore will try to act earlier, at national scale and at the speed of malicious attackers, and CNA said it will tap the “best available” AI models for cyber defence. The Straits Times said the refresh includes a new national tool to block malware and phishing sites, and a second report said professional standards are being developed as cyber defenders’ roles change, with business judgement and AI proficiency gaining weight. OpenGov Asia and GovInsider also covered the strategy, which the Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) has posted on its site.

Mr Shanmugam also said Singapore has asked major technology firms for full transparency and quicker information-sharing about AI risks, after a hack of the Australian government, the Straits Times reported. The companies have generally shown “an attitude of cooperation”, he said, but it is “a work in progress” (CNA, Straits Times). The Straits Times also quoted a UN special envoy saying minimum safety standards and independent testing are a must to build trust in AI. DailyDrop has not seen the details of the Australian hack or any timetable for the transparency request.

Singapore shares edge up 0.3% as a chip rout hits the region; the IMF says AI spending is what keeps world growth above 3%

Singapore shares ended Tuesday 0.3% higher, with Jardine Matheson and the banks gaining, the Business Times reported; Yahoo Finance Singapore said the market held up while regional markets fell. The region had been hit by a rout in chip stocks. Bloomberg wrote of cracks across the AI trade as memory-chip stocks tumbled amid China’s CXMT claiming a breakthrough in a memory design, and CNBC said Micron, SoftBank and ASML slid as the Wall Street chip rout spread. Jardine raised its 2026 earnings guidance to as high as US$5.65 a share and is buying the remaining half of Gammon Construction for US$330 million (Business Times). Wall Street itself closed slightly lower on Tuesday, with tech shares weak and the Dow ending a three-day rise (Telegraaf, Het Financieele Dagblad, Zonebourse). DailyDrop has not confirmed index levels, so none is shown.

The backdrop is a world economy leaning on AI. The IMF said AI investment is propping up global growth, which it forecasts at 3.1% this year and 3.4% in 2027, but that war in the Middle East and rising debt threaten the outlook (Reuters, CNA, Straits Times). Separately a senior IMF official called surging government bond yields the biggest emerging risk to the financial system (Bloomberg), and Reuters reported the fund citing an AI slowdown and spiking yields as risks. Closer to home, Bloomberg and The Edge Singapore asked whether a sell-off in Singapore’s banks tests the city-state’s safe-haven status, the chief executive of Southeast Asia’s largest bank argued on CNBC that “boring is not bad”, and brokerage CGSI upgraded DBS to “add” with a S$79.50 target price (Business Times). AirTrunk, the Blackstone-backed data-centre operator, delayed a Singapore Reit listing amid the AI stock sell-off, sources told the Business Times and the Wall Street Journal.
