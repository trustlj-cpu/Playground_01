Haze pushes air quality into the unhealthy range across Singapore. NEA expects no quick relief on Tuesday

The 24-hour PSI crossed 101 in every region on Monday, a month after the first unhealthy readings since 2023. Smoke from fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan is drifting in on south-easterly winds.

Singapore spent Monday under a grey sky. The Straits Times reported that air quality fell back into the unhealthy range overnight, and by the afternoon both CNA and the Straits Times said the 24-hour PSI was in the unhealthy band in all regions of the island. The PSI is a rolling 24-hour average of six pollutants, and readings from 101 to 200 count as unhealthy. On Sunday morning only the central region had crossed that line, at 102. The spread to every region on Monday marks the worst day of the current episode so far.

The National Environment Agency forecast that Tuesday's 24-hour PSI would sit between the high moderate and mid unhealthy range, because winds were expected to keep blowing from the south-east or east-south-east. That direction carries smoke from land and forest fires in southern Sumatra and Kalimantan. Regional weather forecasters have warned of strong El Niño conditions and below-normal rainfall over much of South-east Asia from September to November, which keeps peatland dry and makes fires harder to put out.

This is not a short spell. Singapore first slipped into the unhealthy range on 4 September, for the first time since 2023, and the haze has come and gone since then. CNA published an explainer on Monday on the billions of dollars the region has lost to past haze episodes, and on why prevention efforts across South-east Asia keep falling short. The same pattern is showing up elsewhere: the Straits Times reported that Myanmar has warned of power cuts linked to El Niño.

For most people the practical question is simple: whether to go outside. NEA's standing advice for the unhealthy range is for healthy people to cut down on prolonged or strenuous outdoor exercise, and for the elderly, pregnant women and children to keep such activity to a minimum. People with chronic lung or heart conditions are told to avoid it. The timing is awkward for the city's busiest sporting week: the Formula One race weekend arrives later this week, and outdoor fan events are already under way. Organisers have not announced any changes. DailyDrop will watch the morning PSI and NEA's three-day outlook.

Some things remain unclear. We could not confirm an official count of fire hotspots in Indonesia for Monday, and figures circulating online appear to come from earlier haze years, so we have left them out. Nor is there yet an estimate of what this episode is costing Singapore. What is clear is the pattern of the past month: readings rise when the wind turns south-easterly and ease when it shifts. That makes the wind forecast, more than any single day's reading, the best guide to the week ahead. Until the inter-monsoon rains arrive in earnest, hazy days are likely to recur.

Seven workplace deaths prompt MOM to call a safety time-out in construction, transport and storage

The Ministry of Manpower on Monday urged companies in construction, transportation and storage to hold a voluntary safety time-out after seven workplace deaths, CNA and the Straits Times reported. A safety time-out means pausing work to review risks and procedures before resuming. MOM also said it would tighten enforcement in those sectors.

The ministry's call is voluntary, so its reach depends on how many firms take part. Enforcement is the sharper tool: more inspections, and penalties where firms fall short. Both outlets placed the seven deaths in a short recent period; MOM has not published a breakdown by sector in what we have seen.

For workers, the call is a chance to raise unsafe practices on site. For companies, a stop-work order or fine costs more than a planned pause.

The three sectors named share a profile: tight schedules, heavy machinery, work at height or around moving vehicles, and many workers on short contracts or employed by subcontractors. That is where accidents tend to cluster when projects run late. MOM's message is that a day lost to a pause is cheaper than a life lost to a shortcut. Whether firms listen will show in the next set of workplace safety statistics, and in whether the run of deaths stops.

Parliament will take up claims that civil servants bought homes near MRT stations before they were announced

The Straits Times, the Business Times and CNA reported on Monday that questions about a study on civil servants' property purchases near planned MRT stations will be raised when Parliament sits on Tuesday, 6 October. The study claims that public officers bought homes near stations that had not yet been announced in disproportionate numbers.

A study is not a finding. The government has said any wrongdoing would be referred to the authorities, and the question in the House is what the review has found so far. If public officers used information from their work to buy property, that would breach the most basic rule of public service. If the pattern has an innocent explanation, the government will want to say so clearly, because trust in land-use planning depends on it.

Economy · TechnologyPress ✓ 2 outlets · figures attributed to the official report

Singapore's digital economy grows to S$144.1 billion as more firms adopt AI

The digital economy reached S$144.1 billion, CNA reported on Monday, as adoption of artificial intelligence by businesses accelerated. The Straits Times said AI adoption among firms rose and that the digital economy now accounts for a larger share of GDP.

The figure covers both technology companies and the digital activity of firms in other industries, such as banks and retailers. It matters for jobs: the more of the economy runs on software and data, the more workers in ordinary firms need digital skills, not only those in technology companies. The reports did not say how much of the gain came from AI itself.

ConsumerPress ✓ 3 outlets · loss figures attributed to CASE

CASE calls for a law to protect prepaid packages as consumer losses climb past S$3.7 million

The Consumers Association of Singapore (CASE) called on Monday for mandatory safeguards for people who buy prepaid packages, after a jump in losses when businesses closed. The Business Times reported that consumer losses linked to True Group had reached S$3.78 million, and Mothership put losses from fitness closures at S$3.82 million. The Straits Times also reported CASE's call.

Prepaid packages, from gym memberships to beauty treatments, leave customers exposed when a business shuts: the money is spent and the service is never delivered. CASE wants the rules to require protection such as escrow or insurance, so that customers can recover at least part of what they paid. Until then, the cheapest safeguard is to buy shorter packages and pay by credit card where possible, so that a chargeback is an option.
