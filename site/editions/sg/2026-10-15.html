Haze reaches the “very high” band in western Singapore for the first time this season, and N95 masks go out early

The one-hour PM2.5 reading in the west rose from 195 at 1pm to 284 at 2pm on Wednesday, which CNA says is the highest hourly level since 2015. Community clubs began handing out masks ahead of schedule.

Singapore’s air worsened sharply on Wednesday afternoon. The one-hour PM2.5 reading in the west rose from 195 at 1pm, in the high band, to 284 at 2pm, which put it in the very high band for the first time this season, CNA reported. A later CNA report gave the 2pm peak as 288. At 2pm the south read 159, the east 195 and the central region 206, all in the high band. AsiaOne said the 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) in the west reached 203, in the very unhealthy range of 201 to 300 and a first for 2026, and Bloomberg’s headline said Singapore’s haze had hit its highest level as Indonesian fires worsen. PM2.5 means fine particles small enough to reach deep into the lungs. DailyDrop has not seen the evening or overnight readings.

The government moved its measures forward, and community clubs began handing out N95 masks earlier than planned (CNA). Eligible people are Pioneer and Merdeka Generation members, pregnant women, children aged seven to 16 and people with medical conditions, each receiving one pair on showing an NRIC (Business Times, MS News). The authorities also said haze shelters are to open (Straits Times, Business Times), and some outdoor, non-air-conditioned facilities in three regions were shut temporarily (CNA, AsiaOne). The Ministry of Education told schools to adjust their activities to the hourly PM2.5 reading, and some suspended outdoor play (Straits Times). Earlier on Wednesday, CNA said attendance at pools and stadiums had fallen 12 per cent as the haze curbed outdoor exercise, and the Straits Times reported rising demand for air-conditioner servicing.

Attention turned to the source. Indonesia named more than 200 people and nine firms as suspects in the forest fires and hopes to put the blazes out within a month, CNA reported, and it is assessing offers of foreign firefighting help. Malaysia said the two countries have for the first time begun talks on working together against fires in Kalimantan and Sumatra (CNA). On legal action, Grace Fu said Singapore is collecting and reviewing data and will “keep its options open” on prosecuting companies, that its offer still stands and that Indonesia has given no update so far (Business Times; CNA and the Straits Times carried the same remarks). DailyDrop has not seen NEA or any other Singapore agency name a company.

Across the Causeway, schools in Selangor, Negeri Sembilan, Melaka and Pahang were closed on Wednesday and are due to reopen today (Straits Times), while Firefly has extended its Seletar–Subang flight cancellations to 16 October (Straits Times). Timor-Leste has closed schools too (Reuters, Malay Mail), and the South China Morning Post looked at how the haze is testing Singapore’s events economy. Today’s question is whether the reading in the west falls back below the very high band, and that depends on the wind and rain as much as on any measure taken here. NEA publishes the hourly figures.

Two Singapore-registered ships are struck in the Strait of Hormuz; one sinks and all crew are safe

Two container ships registered in Singapore were struck in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, and one of them sank, the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA) said, as reported by CNA, the Straits Times, Reuters and the Business Times. All crew on both vessels are safe and no injuries were reported. The Business Times put the crew at 43, none of them Singaporean. MS News quoted the MPA as calling the attacks “unprovoked, unjustifiable” and a breach of international law. Reuters, citing sources, said the ship that sank was hit by a projectile, and Bloomberg reported that the strike started a fire and the crew were evacuated.

Security data show attacks near the strait at their highest since the Iran war began, with at least ten vessels hit since 11 October (Straits Times). Reuters also cited officials who say Iran is shifting tactics ahead of the US vote. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards, through state media, said a container ship sank after hitting a naval mine; DailyDrop has seen that only through FinancialJuice and has not seen it tied to these vessels. Bloomberg reported Brent above US$106, and a UN official told the Straits Times that failure to agree a Hormuz fertiliser plan within weeks could cause a food crisis in parts of Asia and Africa. DailyDrop has not seen the ships’ names or owners, or whether the MPA will change its guidance to Singapore-registered ships.

Economy · SingaporePress ✓ 7 outlets · figures as reported by each outlet

MAS holds policy unchanged and trims its inflation view as the economy grows 5.4 per cent in the third quarter

Singapore’s economy grew 5.4 per cent in the third quarter, beating forecasts, the Straits Times, Business Times, CNBC and Nikkei Asia reported. On a seasonally adjusted basis it expanded 1.1 per cent from the previous quarter (Straits Times). CNBC said growth softened from a four-year high, and the Business Times and Nikkei tied the strength to electronics and AI-driven demand. Shortly afterwards the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) kept its policy unchanged after tightening twice this year (CNA). MAS uses the exchange rate as its main tool, so a hold means it left the pace at which the Singdollar may appreciate as it was (Straits Times).

The hold surprised some. The Edge Singapore said MAS unexpectedly held, and MUFG called it a surprise that signals an extended pause (FXStreet). MAS narrowed its forecast for core inflation to 1.5 to 2 per cent from 1.5 to 2.5 per cent, while warning that inflation is elevated (CNA, Straits Times). It said AI-led growth should continue into 2027, though the Business Times reported that the boost is expected to moderate. Singapore shares edged lower as investors digested the hold and the stronger GDP figures (Business Times), and DBS cut SGX to “hold” with a S$20 target price, citing cooling securities turnover (Business Times). DailyDrop has not seen MAS’s full-year growth forecast, so check the statement itself.

Hot US inflation hits Wall Street: the Nasdaq falls 1.88 per cent as traders bet on a Fed rate rise

Wall Street fell on Wednesday after the US inflation report came in hot. Consumer prices accelerated in September on costlier petrol, and core inflation, which leaves out food and energy, was firmer, Reuters reported via the Straits Times. The Financial Times put the annual rate at 3.7 per cent and tied the rebound in fuel costs to the Iran war; Upper Michigan’s Source, a US regional outlet, gave the same figure. Bloomberg called it the fastest rise in inflation since April and said Treasuries slumped as traders raised bets on a Fed rate rise later this month and next year. CNA headlined that a Fed hike this month is back on the table.

The Nasdaq fell 1.88 per cent to 26,692.87, its biggest daily drop since 23 July, the Straits Times reported. Bloomberg said the S&P 500 ended its longest spell of calm since 2020 as the AI trade sank, and the FT said Nvidia, Oracle and CoreWeave fell as inflation and AI debt fears unsettled investors. ASML offered a counterweight, nudging up its outlook as AI demand outruns what it can supply (CNA, Bloomberg). The Straits Times added that US mortgage rates reached 7.5 per cent. DailyDrop has not confirmed the S&P 500 or Dow closing levels from two outlets, and the Nasdaq figures rest on the Straits Times alone.

Middle East · Singapore travelPress ✓ 4 outlets · damage reports rest on unnamed sources

Houthi strike badly damages Aramco’s headquarters; MUIS advises Singaporeans to consider postponing Umrah

Saudi Aramco’s headquarters in Dhahran suffered extensive damage in an attack on Tuesday, with a fire spreading across several floors, three people with direct knowledge told Reuters, which the Straits Times carried on Wednesday. Bloomberg said Iran-backed Houthi forces fired missiles and drones at several Saudi cities, and the Houthis later vowed to keep attacking Saudi airports and oil facilities (Reuters, Straits Times). The Straits Times reported that pilgrims in the kingdom were caught up in the strikes, with some changing their plans. President Donald Trump said the Houthis were trying to push up oil prices before the US election (Straits Times). Bloomberg reported Brent above US$106 as hostilities spread from Russia to the Middle East.

For Singaporeans the practical advice came on Wednesday. The Islamic Religious Council of Singapore (MUIS) advised them to consider postponing Umrah and those already in Saudi Arabia to stay calm and follow local authorities and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA) (CNA, Straits Times). The Straits Times said MUIS is talking to Umrah travel agents about postponing or rescheduling, and reported that the MFA advised Singaporeans to defer travel to Saudi Arabia because of heightened security risks. The Six Kings Slam tennis tournament in Saudi Arabia has been postponed to December (Straits Times, CNA). DailyDrop has not seen Aramco say how long repairs might take or whether output is affected.
