President Lee taps tax service head Im Kwang-hyun as new chief of staff, 23 days after Kang Hoon-sik's resignation

The Blue House cited administrative experience and political sense. The People Power Party criticized the pick, saying his brother-in-law was President Lee's lawyer

President Lee Jae-myung on the 14th appointed National Tax Service commissioner Im Kwang-hyun as the new presidential chief of staff (Yonhap, Chosun Ilbo, Dong-A Ilbo, Kyunghyang Shinmun). He succeeds Chief of Staff Kang Hoon-sik, who announced his resignation on the 21st of last month, and the appointment came 23 days after that (Chosun Ilbo, Dong-A Ilbo). Kang himself announced the successor at an afternoon briefing in the Chunchugwan press room (Dong-A Ilbo, ZDNet Korea). The Blue House introduced Im as a politician from an economic-bureaucrat background who combines administrative experience with political sense, having served as a 22nd National Assembly member and as National Tax Service commissioner (Chosun Ilbo, SBS). It is also notable that, after policy chief Ha Jun-kyung was appointed earlier this month, an economic expert now holds the chief of staff post too (ZDNet Korea single report). President Lee wrote on X, “I trust and expect him to do as well as Hoon-sik hyung, no, even better” (Dong-A Ilbo, Electronic Times), and Im said, “Just as people's days are desperate, the Blue House's days must be desperate too” (Kyunghyang Shinmun, Korea Economic Daily).

Reactions were mixed. The Democratic Party welcomed him as the right person to speed up results in governing (Yonhap). The People Power Party criticized the pick right after it was announced as ‘the definitive payback appointment for the Daejang-dong family’ (Chosun Ilbo, Chosun Biz). Party leader Jang Dong-hyeok pointed out that Im's brother-in-law, lawyer Lee Seung-yeop, had defended President Lee in the Daejang-dong, Public Official Election Act, subornation of perjury and illegal remittances to North Korea cases (Chosun Ilbo). Chosun Ilbo also reported that Im's wife, Lee Kyung-eun, was appointed consul general in Houston under this government. People Power Party floor leader Jeong Jeom-sik asked whether a figurehead for the Seongnam-Gyeonggi line had been put forward (Dong-A Ilbo) and demanded that President Lee also say whether he accepted Kang's resignation (Electronic Times). These assessments are the opposition's claims, not confirmed facts. Electronic Times read the pick as a performance-based appointment of someone who delivered results in collecting large tax arrears, while Yonhap wrote that it was a ‘surprise card’ after three weeks of deliberation, so all the rumored names missed. Today, Im's first message and whether the Blue House staff will be reshuffled further are worth watching.

Markets · StocksPress ✓ 8 outlets · close per exchange tally

KOSPI falls for a sixth straight session to 6,008.81, failing to rebound after the 5% plunge on the 13th

The KOSPI closed down 0.44% at 6,008.81 on the 14th, falling for a sixth straight session and just barely holding the 6,000 level (Yonhap, YTN, Electronic Times, Chosun Ilbo, Kyunghyang Shinmun). The Kosdaq fell 1.57% to 823.88 (Yonhap, Electronic Times). Rebound buying after the 5.46% plunge the day before lifted the index as much as 2% early in the session, but foreign selling erased the gains, and in the afternoon it dipped below the 6,000 level (Yonhap, Chosun Ilbo, Korea Economic Daily). Foreigners posted net selling for a seventh straight session (Kyunghyang Shinmun). As the backdrop to the plunge on the 13th, Dong-A Ilbo cited Chinese memory maker CXMT's plan to launch next-generation server memory, and SBS cited fear of Chinese chips catching up, while Korea Economic Daily pointed to worries over ASML earnings as the reason the rebound failed on the 14th. The amount of forced liquidation on the 13th was 59.35 billion won, the most since late July (Chosun Ilbo, Yonhap). Samsung Electronics was flat at 232,000 won, and SK hynix fell 1.66% to 1,419,000 won (Electronic Times). U.S. September consumer prices rose 3.7% from a year earlier, above expectations (Yonhap). The release came after the Korean market had closed, so it was not reflected in trading on the 14th. On the 13th, New York stocks all fell on mixed bank earnings and weak chips, with the Dow down 0.35% at 51,670.10 (Chosun Ilbo, Newsis).

Economy · FinancePress ✓ 9 outlets · count per financial authorities, may rise

16 financial firms hit by AI hacking, 100,000 records of personal data leaked. Authorities: “The number could rise”

On the 14th the financial authorities held a second emergency response meeting chaired by Financial Services Commission secretary general Shin Jin-chang and said 16 financial firms across all sectors had been confirmed breached in hacking presumed to have used artificial intelligence (AI): banks, savings banks, mutual finance, specialty credit, electronic finance, P2P lenders and virtual asset service providers (Yonhap, Dong-A Ilbo, Chosun Ilbo). About 100,000 records of personal information leaked (Korea Economic Daily, SBS, Dong-A Ilbo). The banks named were Shinhan, KB Kookmin, Hana and Busan (Dong-A Ilbo). Authorities said financial firms are checking for breaches based on shared internet addresses (IPs), so the number of affected firms could grow (Dong-A Ilbo). Dong-A Ilbo reported that authorities believe information that could let money be taken out, such as account or card numbers, was not leaked. Boannews, by contrast, reported that the information leaked from Saemaul Geumgo included account information (single report).

An emergency check by the Financial Services Commission of 555 financial firms found lax security management at 130 (Electronic Times). Kyunghyang Shinmun put it as about one in four. Authorities will strengthen CEOs' responsibility for security and raise vulnerability checks of large financial firms to at least twice a year (Chosun Ilbo). According to SBS, information on some 400 customers at Saemaul Geumgo and some 180 at OK Savings Bank was leaked. The Saemaul Geumgo central federation posted an apology alongside a customer-appreciation event banner before switching to a standalone notice (Electronic Times). Woori Bank was attacked but avoided a leak, which Dong-A Ilbo credited to constant checks of even non-critical systems with AI mock-hacking tools (single report).

Politics · JusticePress ✓ 12 outlets · first-instance ruling, appeal remains

Kang Sun-woo, in the ‘100 million won nomination bribe’ case, gets 2 years 6 months at first instance and is detained in court. Former city councillor Kim Kyung gets 1 year

Presiding Judge Lee Chun-geun of Criminal Single Division 1, Seoul Central District Court, on the 14th sentenced independent lawmaker Kang Sun-woo, charged with violating the Political Funds Act, to 2 years 6 months in prison and a forfeiture of 100 million won (Dong-A Ilbo, Kyunghyang Shinmun, SBS). She was accused of taking 100 million won in exchange for a city council candidate nomination. Kim Kyung, the former Seoul city councillor who handed over the money, got 1 year in prison, and Kang's former aide, who arranged their meeting, got 8 months with a suspended sentence (Dong-A Ilbo). The court said the offense harmed the fairness of elections and seriously damaged public trust, so severe punishment was needed (Dong-A Ilbo). Prosecutors had sought 4 years in prison (Yonhap).

The two, who had been released on bail shortly before their detention period was to expire, were detained in court again right after sentencing (Dong-A Ilbo, SBS). According to Kyunghyang Shinmun, the court found all charges guilty and rejected the claim that it was the aide's deviation. Kang claimed she never received any money (Kyunghyang Shinmun). Because this is a first-instance ruling, an appeal and higher-court review remain, and according to Dong-A Ilbo, if the sentence is finalized at the Supreme Court, Kang loses her seat.

Industry · BusinessPress ✓ 8 outlets · won conversions differ by report

Hyundai Motor Group to invest $15.5 billion in India through 2032. Chairman Chung Eui-sun meets Prime Minister Modi

Hyundai Motor Group Chairman Chung Eui-sun met Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi at the prime minister's residence in New Delhi on the 13th (local time) and said the group would invest about $15.5 billion in India over seven years starting this year (Dong-A Ilbo, Yonhap, Chosun Ilbo, Electronic Times). That is around 20.8 trillion won (Dong-A Ilbo, YTN, SBS). Chosun Ilbo converted it to 20.7 trillion won. The money will go to new plants, R&D facilities, the local parts supply chain and future technology (Electronic Times, Chosun Ilbo), and the group will set up a dedicated investment entity in India (Yonhap, Electronic Times). Chosun Ilbo read it as a plan to build India into an export base for emerging markets such as the Middle East and Africa.

Reports differ on production scale in India. Dong-A Ilbo reported that Chung said operating the Pune plant had secured an annual production system of 1.4 million units, while Yonhap used 1 million and 1.5 million units a year in different headlines. Which figure is right, and whether they use the same basis, has to be checked against Hyundai Motor Group's official material. Yonhap reported that the investment would be an opportunity for domestic parts makers too. Chung visited India on the sixth anniversary of taking office (Yonhap).
