NEC special counsel to conduct voluntary inspection of 2.47 million local-election ballots at Olympic Park on the 28th… ‘zero detention warrant requests’ in 50 days since launch

Dong-A Ilbo reported that the NEC special counsel said in a notice on the 26th that from 9 a.m. on the 28th it would conduct a voluntary inspection of about 2.47 million Songpa District ballots and CCTV footage kept at the Olympic Park Handball Stadium.

Dong-A Ilbo reported that the NEC special counsel (Special Counsel Lee Tae-han), which is investigating allegations surrounding the National Election Commission, said in a notice to the press on the 26th that from 9 a.m. on the 28th it would conduct a voluntary inspection of items including about 2.47 million Songpa District ballots from the 6·3 local elections, kept at the Olympic Park Handball Stadium in Songpa District, Seoul, and on-site CCTV footage. It will proceed with the cooperation of related agencies such as the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency and the National Election Commission; the 29th had initially been floated as the likely date, but after consultations among the agencies the 28th was set, Dong-A Ilbo reported. Kyunghyang Shinmun carried the inspection under the headline ‘Equipment and documents check’, and Yonhap put ‘Verifying additions, subtractions and forgery’ in its headline. SBS reported that 2.47 million ballots and CCTV footage would be inspected.

Yonhap reported that Acting Chair Wi Cheol-hwan of the National Election Commission said on the 26th that after the special counsel’s on-site inspection at Olympic Park the ballots would be transferred and the commission had been asked to assist with the ballot count. The same day, Yonhap ran an article saying that the special counsel team, which has been investigating matters such as the ballot-paper shortage in the 6·3 local elections, marked 50 days since its launch but that its investigation was slow, with ‘zero detention warrant requests’ and the like. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the special counsel is showing impatience over a lack of staff and time, with a suspect under investigation having died and an open dispute with the court over a search-and-seizure warrant.

Lula wins fourth term as Brazil’s president… Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro “rejects result”, Electoral Court says “no effect on result”

Yonhap reported that in Brazil’s presidential runoff on the 25th (local time), incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (80) won a fourth term overall and stopped the ‘Blue Tide’, the right-wing wave in South America. SBS also reported that President Lula won a fourth term for the first time. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the close of voting was extended by 2 hours, from the scheduled 5 p.m. to 7 p.m. Chosun Ilbo, citing the Associated Press and others, reported that the defeated Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro said, “I cannot accept the result of the vote” and “I will ask for this election to be annulled.” Yonhap ran an article headlined that the Electoral Court said the result was ‘not affected’, and Korea Economic Daily put in its headline that a Brazil ETF fell 19% in overnight trading on Lula’s re-election.

KOSPI falls 1.46% to close at 5,469.47, lowest since April… Kosdaq down 3.26% at 727.21

Electronic Times, citing Korea Exchange data, reported that on the 26th the KOSPI closed at 5,469.47, down 1.46% from the previous trading day. It was the lowest close since the 5,450.33 recorded on April 6, and the paper explained that the index gave up the 5,500 level ahead of the US Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting and major corporate earnings releases. The same paper reported that the Kosdaq closed down 3.26% at 727.21, its lowest close since July 31, and Yonhap also reported that the Kosdaq ended the session down 24.48 points (3.26%) at 727.21.

Seoul Shinmun carried ‘Foreigners net sell 695.5 billion won’ as its headline, and Ajunews put the collapse of the 5,500 level amid foreign selling and AI investment worries in its headline. Yonhap reported that with the Kosdaq down 38% from its peak, SK Securities analyzed that it is ‘building a floor’. Korea Economic Daily looked at whether the Kosdaq rebound formula, that stocks bottom when ‘debt-funded investing’ plunges, will work again this time.

Fallout from Oh Se-hoon acquittal… Kim Min-seok: “concern over politicization of the courts”, Choi Min-hee: “introduce AI judges”, National Court Administration chief: “excessive denigration of the judiciary”

Electronic Times reported that Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok said at the Supreme Council meeting on the 26th that he was concerned about the ‘politicization of the courts’ over the appellate trial acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon in his Political Funds Act case. According to the same paper, Kim pointed out that the court recognized that the opinion polls were conducted and that a close associate-sponsor paid for them but judged that Oh’s involvement had not been proven, and the Democratic Party said it would hold people responsible for the Myung Tae-kyun-related allegations and for the allegations of illegality in Seoul city administration. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that some in the party leadership even argued ‘let’s introduce AI judges’, and Dong-A Ilbo carried Rep. Choi Min-hee’s ‘Let’s introduce AI judges’ remark as its headline.

Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that National Court Administration chief Noh Kyung-pil, at the National Assembly Legislation and Judiciary Committee’s comprehensive audit on the 26th, answered, in response to some ruling-party lawmakers citing the appellate acquittal of Mayor Oh and the like, that it was not appropriate to say what the Cho Hee-de judiciary had done. Dong-A Ilbo reported that when Rep. Kim Yong-min argued that ‘all the judiciary’s power is concentrated in the hands of the Chief Justice alone’, Noh rebutted that this was ‘excessive denigration of our judiciary, which has reached world-class standards’. Yonhap ran an article headlined with the ruling-party claim that ‘Cho Hee-de impeachment is the answer’ and the People Power Party’s retort, ‘Are they in their right mind?’

Special counsel seeks 5 years for ex-vice minister Kim O-jin over ‘Yoon government residence relocation favors’… 5 years for ex-administrative officer Hwang, 3 for 21Gram CEO

Dong-A Ilbo reported that the Kim Keon-hee special counsel team (Special Counsel Min Joong-ki) on the 26th asked for 5 years in prison for Kim O-jin, former First Vice Minister of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, who was indicted over allegations of favors in the relocation of the presidential residence under the Yoon Suk Yeol government, at a final hearing before Criminal Division 24 of the Seoul Central District Court (presiding judge Lee Young-sun). The charges include violation of the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes (fraud). According to Dong-A Ilbo, 5 years in prison was sought for Hwang, a former administrative officer at the Presidential Secretariat who was indicted with him, and 3 years for Kim Tae-young, CEO of 21Gram, the residence construction contractor.

Hankook Ilbo reported the same sentence requests over charges of handing the residence construction to an unqualified firm and defrauding 1.6 billion won, and Ajunews reported under the headline ‘Led name-lending and sham contracts for residence construction’. Daejeon Ilbo reported that Kim was separately indicted on charges of illegally diverting budget funds to pay for residence construction costs and on the 16th was handed a sentence request of 3 years 6 months in prison. Yonhap and Korea Economic Daily flashed the news of the sentence requests.
