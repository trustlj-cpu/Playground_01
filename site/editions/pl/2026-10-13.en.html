Two Turkish nationals detained in Germany for arson at a Starlink station; Warsaw points to Russia

The head of the Interior Ministry speaks of subversion on behalf of foreign intelligence, the minister-coordinator of the special services of a Russian order. No evidence has been shown publicly, and the men have not yet heard Polish charges.

On Monday, October 12, German police, at the request of the Polish services, detained two Turkish nationals suspected of arson at a Starlink ground base station; the fire broke out overnight from September 23 to 24 in Wola Krobowska in Mazovia (Polsat News, WP, Bankier). The news was announced by the head of the Interior Ministry (MSWiA), Marcin Kierwiński, who called the arson an act of sabotage and subversion; the suspects are to hear charges of acting on behalf of foreign intelligence (Interia, WP). They are to be handed over to Poland under the European Arrest Warrant, the EU fast-track procedure for surrendering suspects between states (Rzeczpospolita). The detention was also confirmed by Prime Minister Donald Tusk (Onet, Reuters, DW).

The minister-coordinator of the special services, Tomasz Siemoniak, said that prosecutors have no doubt the perpetrators acted on the orders of foreign services, and that he sees no one other than Russia who would want such equipment destroyed (TVN24, Onet). Reuters writes that Poland blames Russia for the fire. The evidence is to be presented to a court. DailyDrop has not seen the case file or the charges and does not know who is said to have ordered the arson or how the suspects were connected to that person; in the materials we reviewed there is also no comment from Moscow or Ankara.

Russian hybrid war, that is, the combination of sabotage, disinformation and cyberattacks below the threshold of open conflict, was the subject of warnings on the day of the detentions. Tusk said at the opening of the Warsaw Security Forum that anyone who suggests Ukraine is to blame for the war gives Putin an “extraordinarily valuable gift” (Rzeczpospolita, Bankier). Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski warned in an interview with the Financial Times that NATO has no clear procedures for a hybrid attack in which people would die (Polsat News, WP). The German interior minister spoke of a “shadow war” going beyond the threats of the Cold War (Reuters), and the head of German counterintelligence of possible further provocations (Interia, Gazeta). In Warsaw, the trial of four people accused of arson on Russian orders began, including the fire on Marywilska Street (Notes from Poland, Bankier). The head of Czech intelligence warned that Russia may reach for riskier operations in NATO countries (Reuters), and NATO’s Supreme Allied Commander Europe, Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, assured a forum in Warsaw that the alliance will block an initial attack and, if necessary, break the adversary’s forces (Onet, TVN24). Tusk added that Poland is preparing for various scenarios (Polsat News).

The detentions were independently confirmed by German media, including “Tagesschau,” “Spiegel” and “Zeit,” which write of a possible Russian trail. In the materials we reviewed there is no information on whether the men will consent to extradition or when they will appear before a German court. Ground stations link Starlink satellites to the network, so a fire at one does not have to mean an interruption of service; DailyDrop found no data on the effects for users.

Three questions remain for today: whether a German court will agree to surrender both men and when they will reach Poland, what charges they will hear from Polish prosecutors, and whether investigators will show evidence of an outside order. On the person who commissioned the arson we have so far only ministers’ declarations, not evidentiary material.

Suspect in plot to assassinate Tusk is Krzysztof N., a 66-year-old from Wrocław; charges not yet known

On Monday new details emerged in the case of the man detained after a warning about an assassination of Prime Minister Tusk and Deputy Prime Minister Kosiniak-Kamysz. According to Polsat News and Onet he is 66 (WP still writes of “about 60,” as Minister Kierwiński did earlier), and “Gazeta Wyborcza” describes a Wrocław man, Krzysztof N. He was taken to Warsaw: WP writes of the Warsaw Metropolitan Police Headquarters, and Polsat News says that because of his health the transport required a doctor’s consent. Investigators are securing evidence in his home and analyzing data storage devices, and a spokesman for the Warsaw Regional Prosecutor’s Office called the case very serious (WP, Polsat News). Onet writes that the man “was looking for volunteers,” and “Rzeczpospolita” that radical followers were said to be gathering around him, planning to seize power; each of these claims was reported by one outlet.

DailyDrop has not seen charges, a court decision or evidence and does not know whether the detainee led a group or had the means for an attack. Detention lasts up to 48 hours, after which a prosecutor must send a motion for pretrial detention to a court or release the suspect, so a decision can be expected today. Onet writes that the presidential minister Marcin Przydacz called the case a “political show,” while the government considers the warning credible.

Poland · SchoolsPress ✓ 8 outlets · figures per police

Police: more than 135 detained and nearly 80,000 schools checked; Tusk admits the government is “oversensitive”

On Monday, October 12, about 20,000 police officers continued patrols at and around schools, and 18 more people were detained during the day, Tusk announced after a briefing with ministers and heads of the services (Rzeczpospolita, Interia, Reuters). According to police, cited by Bankier.pl, 135 people have been detained since the start of the operation and nearly 80,000 institutions checked. We do not know what these people were detained for, how many of them are minors, or how many reports of threats the checks concerned; the figures come from police and were not independently verified. Asked about a permanent police presence, the prime minister answered: “Yes, we are oversensitive” (WP).

Deputy Prime Minister and Digital Affairs Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski has called an extraordinary meeting of digital platforms for Friday (Polsat News, WP). Sejm Marshal Włodzimierz Czarzasty announced fast tracks for bills on young people’s safety online (TVN24) and appealed to President Karol Nawrocki, pointing to children and online hate (WP, Onet, Rzeczpospolita). Tusk named games, including Minecraft, among the phenomena of growing violence; the gaming community is protesting (WP). Biedronka, Smyk and Dealz are withdrawing Halloween accessories imitating dangerous tools (Polsat News, Onet). On Tuesday is the funeral of a 37-year-old school employee from Leszczydół-Nowiny, who died after an attack by a 15-year-old former student (WP, Onet).

World · UkrainePress ✓ 7 outlets · truce not confirmed by the parties

Kremlin rejects 60-day ceasefire, and Trump on an “energy truce”: they agreed because he told them to

Asked on Monday about the “energy truce,” Donald Trump answered that both sides had agreed because he told them to; WP notes that neither side has confirmed talks, and BBC that Moscow is silent. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov rejected the 60-day ceasefire proposed by Zelensky, which was meant to allow elections in Ukraine, and said that Moscow wants a comprehensive peace; according to Reuters, Putin would be ready to halt attacks on the day of Ukraine’s elections (Polsat News, Bankier). Kyiv accuses Russia of violating the truce because strikes on energy continue (Reuters, AP). Zelensky told Fox News that Ukraine halted attacks after Trump’s post and that Moscow ignored the appeal (WP). In the evening a drone hit a high-rise in Kyiv (WP, one outlet).

In the background is the deal on Russian diesel. Trump defended it after criticism from politicians of both parties and wrote that the EU paid Russia $250 billion for energy (Reuters, TVN24, Bankier); that is his claim, which we have not checked. According to Reuters’ sources, the US hopes the deal will “soften” Moscow ahead of peace talks. In Brussels, EU foreign ministers unanimously adopted a record sanctions package covering more than 1,600 people and entities (Bankier, Money.pl, WP; Reuters counts nearly 1,600 entries). Tusk said that nothing indicates Russia’s readiness and demanded guarantees from the Kremlin and the US (TVN24); Sikorski said on Bloomberg that pressure must be put on the aggressor, not on the victim (Onet, Interia).

Poland · StatePress ✓ 7 outlets · expert opinions, not a verdict

Pegasus: ABW experts — CBA did not have full control, and data left Poland; Sejm to take up State Tribunal motion for Ziobro

The National Prosecutor’s Office released on Monday the opinions of experts of the Internal Security Agency on the Pegasus system, that is, spyware from an Israeli manufacturer. They show that the system made it possible to obtain a broad range of data from phones without the knowledge or consent of their users, that the Central Anticorruption Bureau did not have full control over it, and that data left Poland (TVN24, WP, Interia). National Prosecutor’s Office spokesman Przemysław Nowak said the manufacturer could have gained access to the infrastructure where materials from operational surveillance were stored (Bankier); prosecutors cited by Notes from Poland say the Israeli manufacturer had access to the data obtained. The head of the Sejm investigative committee, Magdalena Sroka, added that the system operated without the required ICT security accreditation (Bankier).

In a recording on X, Tusk said that the people responsible for justice put tens of thousands of sensitive pieces of Poles’ data into the hands of a foreign state, Israel, and spoke of a “state crime” (Rzeczpospolita, WP); according to Onet, Poland paid more than PLN 30 million for the system. Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek judged that “this was not a fight against crime” but an endangering of state security (Onet). The Sejm Committee on Constitutional Accountability will take up a supplemented motion to send former justice minister Zbigniew Ziobro before the State Tribunal; it concerns, among other things, Pegasus and the Justice Fund (WP, Bankier, Rzeczpospolita). The motion still has to pass through the committee and the Sejm. DailyDrop did not find Ziobro’s position in the materials reviewed, and the experts’ opinions do not settle the guilt of any person.
