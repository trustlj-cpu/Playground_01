Poland · SecurityPress ✓ 8 outlets · motive and charges unknown

Man of about 60 detained after threatening to assassinate Prime Minister Tusk and Defense Minister Kosiniak-Kamysz

The services tracked him down thanks to an informant and an analysis of his online activity. The government calls the case “deadly serious,” the opposition a political show.

On the morning of Sunday, October 11, the services reported a warning about a possible assassination of Prime Minister Donald Tusk and Deputy Prime Minister and Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz. Jacek Dobrzyński, spokesman for the special services coordinator, assured that the case is being treated “deadly seriously,” and TVN24 cites an assessment that the likelihood of the threats being carried out is high. The report arrived overnight (TVN24). Rzeczpospolita established that a message containing threats and plans to kill both politicians reached a publicly available e-mail address of the State Protection Service’s chancellery, and that its author was not the planned assassin but knew his plans; the paper sees no link to the attacks at schools. The authorities judged the risk to be very high (WP). A spokesman for the Interior Ministry (MSWiA) announced an urgent meeting of the services, which was held at the Counter-Terrorism Center of the Internal Security Agency (TVN24, Rzeczpospolita). Reuters and AP reported the news around noon, followed by BBC and DW, and within hours Spiegel, Le Monde and Repubblica, among others, had covered the case; BBC wrote that police had received credible information about possible attacks. According to Wirtualna Polska, the Czech government declared its readiness to help. An expert cited by WP appealed for calm, because the aim of such threats is often to cause fear, and an expert on Polsat News expressed hope that the case would not become an element of political games.

In the afternoon, Minister of Interior and Administration Marcin Kierwiński wrote on social media that the man who had threatened the prime minister and planned an attack had been detained; Tusk thanked the services for their quick and decisive action (Polsat News, Interia). On TVP Info, Kierwiński said the man is about 60 years old and was detained in Lower Silesia, and that the services got onto his trail thanks to an informant and an analysis of online content. According to unofficial findings by Polsat News, he was detained in Wrocław. Detention lasts, as a rule, up to 48 hours; within that time a prosecutor must file a motion for pretrial detention with a court or release the detainee. DailyDrop has not seen any charges, does not know the man’s identity or motive, and does not know whether the detainee is the author of the e-mail or the person whose plans it described, or whether he had real means of carrying out the threats. Threatening an assassination is not the same as preparing one: only charges and evidence will show whether the man really planned an attack or merely announced one.

On Sunday Tusk came to the Chancellery of the Prime Minister on foot, which journalists contrasted with the alert; Dobrzyński replied that the prime minister had full protection (TVN24, WP). According to Onet, politicians of PiS and Confederation accused the authorities of sowing panic and staging a political show, and former president Andrzej Duda said on Polsat News that announcing the case “looks like covering up” the prime minister’s problems with school safety. Former police officers cited by Interia said the signal worries them. WP’s interlocutors in the services, however, were skeptical about the scale of the commotion. DailyDrop found no official information about the involvement of a foreign state and offers no conjecture about the motive.

Three questions remain for today: whether prosecutors will bring charges of criminal threats or of something more serious, and whether the court will order detention; how the threat reached the address of the State Protection Service; and whether the man acted alone. The case will be decided on Monday or Tuesday, because detention cannot last longer than 48 hours without a court decision.

Poland · SchoolsPress ✓ 5 outlets · figures per police

After knife attacks, 10,000 police officers at schools and 117 detained; 21-year-old woman from Praga held in custody for three months

After an urgent briefing of the services, Prime Minister Donald Tusk announced that on Sunday, October 11, there would be 10,000 police officers at and around schools, and that checks would continue in the following days, including Monday (Interia, Polsat News, TVN24). The Farmer.pl site summarizes his announcement as “zero compromises,” and the capital’s police spokesman appealed to parents (TVN24). According to Wprost and the Lublin112 site, 117 people were detained; Swiss SRF, citing police, writes of checks at 67,000 schools. We do not know what these people were detained for or how many of them are minors; police also did not say how many of the checks concerned specific reports of threats.

Tusk judged that the perpetrators are often young people acting spontaneously under the influence of online activity, and pointed to popular games with increasingly visible violence (Polsat News, WP). A court ordered three months’ detention for a 21-year-old woman who on Saturday wounded three women with a knife in Warsaw’s Praga district; she was charged with five counts, including attempted murder of three people (Polsat News, Interia). Rzeczpospolita and Wprost also describe a dispute between Tusk and President Karol Nawrocki over rules blocking illegal content online, and Onet writes about the behind-the-scenes of this “war” (one outlet). The figures on detentions and checks come from police and were not independently verified.

Poland · ElectionsPress ✓ 6 outlets · official result not yet known

Gibała beats the ruling coalition’s candidate in Kraków: about 61% according to exit poll

In the second round of the early election for mayor of Kraków on Sunday, October 11, Łukasz Gibała won — a nonpartisan businessman, councillor and former MP, founder of the Kraków for Residents association. According to the Ipsos exit poll for TVP, TVN and Polsat he received about 61% of the vote, and according to the Ipsos late poll 62.3% versus 37.7% for Senator Monika Piątkowska, the candidate of KO and PSL (WP, TVN24). TVN24 wrote of Gibała’s victory on the basis of data from the National Electoral Commission (PKW) after more than 90% of votes had been counted. DailyDrop does not yet know the final, official result or the turnout.

According to Ipsos, the result was decided mainly by voters who in the first round backed PiS candidate Michał Drewnicki (Interia); Gibała won in every age group, most strongly among the young, while seniors more often chose Piątkowska (TVN24, WP). Piątkowska congratulated her rival and said she would “keep a close watch.” PiS politicians called the result a “defeat” for the ruling coalition, which Tusk and Kosiniak-Kamysz had supported in the campaign (TVN24, Rzeczpospolita). Interia recalls that this is a local election, not a mirror of nationwide polarization, but commentators of Rzeczpospolita and Politico read it as a signal to Tusk a year before the parliamentary elections. In his first speech Gibała said that “after 24 years the people of Kraków have taken back their city”; former Kraków mayor Jacek Majchrowski replied on TVN24 that Gibała “bought himself Kraków.”

World · UkraineUS president’s own post + 5 outlets · Russia has not confirmed

Trump announces “energy truce”; hours later Russia strikes a combined heat and power plant in Kharkiv

On Sunday, October 11, at about 4 p.m. Polish time, Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social that an “energy truce” in Russia’s war with Ukraine was in force with immediate effect and that both sides had agreed to it. Volodymyr Zelensky said he learned of it from the media; according to Axios and Reuters, Ukraine will halt attacks on Russian refineries if Russia stops striking Ukrainian energy, and is waiting for details from the American side. BBC noted that Moscow had not commented on the announcement.

That same evening, Russian drones and aerial bombs struck Ukrainian energy infrastructure, including the CHP-5 plant in Kharkiv (WP, Onet); Reuters writes of six people killed in two Ukrainian cities. Prime Minister Tusk spoke with Zelensky on Sunday and assured him of full support (Polsat News, Bankier). Zelensky added in an interview with ABC that he would organize elections if Russia agrees to a 60-day ceasefire (Reuters). In the background are also two contested threads: Financial Times writes that the US is threatening to halt weapons for Ukraine because of the attacks on refineries, and Senator Thom Tillis considers Trump’s deal on Russian diesel a possible violation of law (Reuters, USA Today).

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · figures per aviation authorities and BBC

Houthis hit Riyadh airport twice in two days: 12 dead, more than 300 injured; Saudi Arabia promises response

On Saturday, October 10, a missile attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 12 people and injured more than 300, according to aviation authorities cited by agencies and BBC; CBS calls it the deadliest on Saudi territory in more than a decade. On Sunday BBC described a second strike on the airport within two days, and the Houthis renewed their warning to airlines and travelers against using Saudi airports (Reuters). The airport was closed on Sunday, and Flightradar24 wrote of the closure being extended by another 24 hours.

Saudi Arabia promised a “decisive response” (France 24), and US Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attack and confirmed that an American is among the victims (Saudi Gazette). The Riyadh stock exchange fell after the attack (Reuters), and according to Financial Times oil company chiefs withdrew from an energy congress in the Saudi capital. The embassy of the United Kingdom warned its citizens against all but essential travel to Saudi Arabia (FinancialJuice), and India, Pakistan, the UN and the EU condemned the attack (Hindustan Times, Geo, Channels TV). For Polish drivers what matters most is oil: on Friday WTI cost $91.85, and the market’s Monday reaction to the second attack is unknown.
