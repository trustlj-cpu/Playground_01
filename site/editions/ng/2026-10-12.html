Marketers raise pump prices as Tinubu's N66 petrol discount becomes the first big argument of the 2027 campaign

The Presidency says the 30-day discount is not a return of the subsidy. Atiku Abubakar calls it a "political gimmick" and Peter Obi calls it reactive.

Nigeria's 30-day petrol discount, announced on Thursday, October 8 by Finance Minister Taiwo Oyedele, became the main political argument of the weekend. The discount takes N66 off each litre of petrol sold at NNPC stations, and motorists must pay through the NNPC Fuel App to get it, according to Legit and Leadership. NNPC says the cut is customer relief paid from its own retail margin, not a subsidy funded from the Federation Account, the pool of oil and tax revenue shared among the federal, state and local governments. Reports differ on the end date. Premium Times and Legit say NNPC extended the offer to October 31, while an earlier report in Megastar Magazine gave October 14. DailyDrop uses October 31, the later and more widely reported date, and has not seen NNPC's own notice. Vanguard reported that the federal government framed the 30 days as a discount for transporters at NNPC filling stations.

On Sunday the argument turned political. The All Progressives Congress (APC) national chairman, Yilwatda, said the discount does not mark a return to subsidy, Tribune reported. The Presidency, speaking through its media adviser Sunday Dare, dismissed the production subsidy proposed by former Vice President Atiku Abubakar as "a dangerous mathematical fantasy wrapped in political deceit", saying Nigeria lacks the crude oil volumes to fund it, Punch reported. Atiku had called the N66 cut a "political gimmick" and asked who authorised NNPC to bear the cost, according to Vanguard. Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), asked whether a one-month discount would reduce poverty, unemployment, insecurity and inequality, and recalled that the subsidy was removed on May 29, 2023 without adequate consultation, Vanguard reported. Governor Seyi Makinde of Oyo State asked Tinubu to publish the pricing template behind the discount, Premium Times said.

At the pump the relief is uneven. Vanguard reported that marketers raised petrol pump prices by up to N29 a litre in Abuja and Lagos days after the announcement. Leadership found earlier that some Abuja stations charged customers paying at the pump more than app users. Premium Times, in an explainer, said the two plans differ on who pays: NNPC carries the cost of the discount, while Atiku backs support for domestic refineries. The Presidency also pointed to President Donald Trump's October 5 executive order on diesel tax relief as a precedent, according to Tribune. Crude oil, which sets the cost of imported fuel, stayed high, with U.S. benchmark WTI closing at $91.85 a barrel on Friday. Civic and party voices have joined in. The Working People United urged transport operators and food distributors to cut fares and prices after the intervention, Vanguard said, and the Young Progressives Party candidate Peter Agada proposed a targeted fuel subsidy costing N3.2 trillion in its first year to cut fares. The test this week is whether independent stations follow NNPC's price.

Tinubu says World Bank report proves reforms are working and tells states to spend higher revenues prudently

President Bola Tinubu on Sunday welcomed the World Bank's October 2026 Nigeria Development Update, titled "Beyond the Federal Purse: How Higher Revenues Reshaped State Priorities", according to Daily Trust, Vanguard, Channels TV, Leadership and Tribune. He said the findings confirm that the difficult decision to remove the petrol subsidy stabilised the economy, Channels reported. Tribune quoted the report as saying that public debt is expected to fall from 40.0 percent of gross domestic product (GDP) in 2025 to 38.1 percent in 2026.

Tinubu urged state governments to spend their higher revenues on projects that improve living standards, health and education, Leadership said. BusinessDay reported that state revenues have surged and are funding an infrastructure boom. Vanguard said the president conceded that more must be done to turn macroeconomic gains into better living conditions and promised more relief. Tribune quoted him as saying he knew subsidy removal would bring hardship for many households. The report arrives as Nigeria's delegation joins the IMF and World Bank meetings in Bangkok, where debt, energy costs and artificial intelligence top the agenda, BusinessDay and Channels reported. Opposition candidates have not accepted the reform claim. Separately, the First Lady said Tinubu would take Nigeria from reforms to prosperity if re-elected, Vanguard reported, a sign of how the report feeds the campaign. DailyDrop has not seen the full report text, so the headline figures come from press quotes.

INEC publishes final candidate lists: 18 presidential contenders, but Pantami and Adebutu are missing from governorship lists

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) published its final lists of candidates on Saturday, October 10. Headlines in BusinessDay and Leadership put the number of presidential candidates at 18, with Tinubu, Atiku, Obi and Makinde among the leading names. BusinessDay said 4,408 candidates are contesting seats in the National Assembly. Leadership dated the presidential election to January 2027, which DailyDrop has not confirmed from a second outlet.

The governorship lists drew immediate protests. Leadership reported that Isa Ali Pantami, a former communications minister, is missing in Gombe and Ladi Adebutu in Ogun, both Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) candidates. The PDP said it is engaging INEC, Punch and Vanguard reported. Pantami's camp urged calm and noted that a court-ordered repeat primary on September 29 was monitored by INEC, Premium Times and Channels said. Tribune reported that four parties were left out in Bauchi, Vanguard that the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and NDC were excluded in Cross River, and Punch that the ADC lost eight candidates in Jigawa. The Social Democratic Party (SDP) also plans to engage INEC. No report seen gives INEC's reason for the omissions.

Houthi missile strike kills 12 at Riyadh airport; a second projectile hits the terminal a day later

A missile attack claimed by Yemen's Houthi rebels on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday, October 10 killed 12 people and injured 309, according to Premium Times and the Manila Times. Mint put the toll at 13 after an Indian national died of his injuries, which the Hindu also reported. On Sunday a projectile struck a passenger terminal, the Saudi-led coalition said, according to Philippine Daily Inquirer, and AFP journalists heard two blasts in the capital. Premium Times reported that Saudi Arabia vowed to retaliate, and BusinessDay and Vanguard said the airport's shutdown was extended.

For Nigerian readers the main link is travel and oil. Many Nigerians fly to Saudi Arabia for Umrah, the minor pilgrimage, and Saudi Gazette warned that the attacks threaten pilgrims and families passing through the airport. DailyDrop has seen no report of Nigerian casualties. Oil is the second link: Saudi Arabia is the world's largest crude exporter, and any threat to its infrastructure feeds into the price of crude that Nigeria sells and that sets the cost of imported fuel.

Trump announces a Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire"; Zelenskyy says Russian strikes on power plants continued

President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social on Sunday, October 11 that "effective immediately, there is an ENERGY CEASEFIRE in the War between Russia and Ukraine", adding that both parties had agreed and "do not break it". Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he was waiting for details but that Ukraine supports such an agreement, according to Straits Times. He added that Russia kept attacking, and Philippine Daily Inquirer reported that Russian strikes hit several Ukrainian power plants on Sunday. DailyDrop has not confirmed which plants were hit, and has not seen a Kremlin statement.

The announcement came two days after Trump unveiled a diesel supply deal with Moscow. Seven U.S. senators from both parties said on Sunday that the deal may be illegal, the Inquirer reported, and Republican Senator Thom Tillis said it could put American lives at risk, Straits Times said. Britain's prime minister and Canada's Mark Carney backed the energy truce, according to Punch and the Inquirer. Zelenskyy told ABC he would organise elections if Russia agrees to a 60-day ceasefire, CNA reported. For Nigeria the stakes are fuel: Presidency officials cited Trump's diesel order when defending the N66 cut, and any halt to attacks on energy sites bears on crude prices.
