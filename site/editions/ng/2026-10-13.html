Dangote extends refinery share offer by two weeks to October 27 to draw more African investors

The Securities and Exchange Commission approved the new date, Daily Trust reported. Reuters, citing an executive and an adviser, put the extension at 10 days.

Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemicals extended the closing date of its initial public offering (IPO), its first sale of shares to the public, to Tuesday, October 27, 2026. The company announced the change on Monday, according to Daily Trust, Punch, Channels TV and Vanguard. Daily Trust said the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), Nigeria's capital-market regulator, approved the extension. Earlier reports said the offer would close on Tuesday, October 13. Channels TV said the extra two weeks would support efforts to open the offer to investors in more African countries, and Punch said the aim is to attract more African investors. Bloomberg's headline described the move as a way to add more nations. Reuters put the extension at 10 days, which does not match the two weeks and the October 27 date in the Nigerian reports, so DailyDrop uses the date given by Daily Trust and Punch.

The offer is large by Nigerian standards. Earlier reports in Nairametrics, allAfrica and Intellinews said the refinery is selling 4.1 billion shares at N525 each, aiming to raise about N2.15 trillion, with the window opening on September 14. TheFact reported the size as N2.1 trillion. Bloomberg reported on October 7 that investors in East Africa were being offered almost 20 percent of the shares, in a deal that could raise as much as $300.4 million, and said the sale is set to be the largest IPO the continent has seen. Bloomberg describes the company as the owner of Africa's biggest crude-processing facility, and earlier reports said the shares are to be listed on the NGX. The minimum application was 10 shares, or N5,250, paid in full, and the company may take up to 30 percent more with regulatory approval. DailyDrop has not seen a final subscription total, a revised date for the allotment of shares, or any change to the N525 price or that 30 percent option in the reports available.

The stated reason is wider participation, not weak demand, and no outlet seen has published a subscription figure that would test either explanation. Investors in Nigeria can apply through licensed stockbrokers and banks until the new date. A sale of this size can pull cash out of bank deposits, treasury bills and other shares at the same time, and it comes while the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), the Lagos stock market, is under pressure. Nairametrics reported on Monday that Nigerian stocks shed N407 billion as banking shares were sold, a figure DailyDrop has seen in one outlet only. Aliko Dangote, Africa's richest man, has pitched the offer as an IPO for the people. The extra two weeks also give Nigerian savers more time to read the prospectus, the document that sets out the risks, before they commit money.

What to watch: the company's own notice and any SEC statement on the revised timetable, the subscription total, and the date when shares are allotted. Earlier reports expected allotment around November 11.

Court strikes out forgery charge against the alleged "fake agency" director-general as the government files a fresh one

Justice Mohammed Umar of the Federal High Court in Abuja on Monday struck out the eight-count criminal charge against Prince Adeniyi Adeyemi, according to ThisDay, Vanguard, Daily Trust and Channels TV. Adeyemi is the self-styled director-general of the Presidential Foreign Investment Promotion Council (PFIPC), a body the federal government says does not exist, Vanguard reported. He was being tried on charges of conspiracy and forgery alongside two others whose whereabouts were unknown, Channels said.

BBC Pidgin reported that the government had wanted to take the case to the High Court of the Federal Capital Territory so that it could start again from the beginning. A Channels TV headline said the federal government filed a fresh charge. DailyDrop has not seen the new charge sheet, the court it was filed in, or any statement from Adeyemi's lawyers. Striking out a charge ends the case in its present form without deciding guilt, and prosecutors can bring it again. Punch described the order as striking out the government's charges against the "fake" agency's head, and Daily Trust called the body a "phantom" agency. The government's description of the council as non-existent is its own position, as reported, and DailyDrop has not checked the council's registration. Readers who receive offers of jobs, contracts or investment access in the name of a government body should confirm them with the body itself.

Pantami gives INEC 72 hours to publish his name on the Gombe governorship list as parties protest omissions

Isa Ali Pantami, a former minister of communications and digital economy, gave the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) a three-day ultimatum on Monday to publish his name as the governorship candidate in Gombe State, according to Leadership, Daily Trust and Tribune. His lawyers called the omission from the final list unlawful, Daily Trust said, and Punch reported that lawyers for the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) sent INEC a separate three-day demand. Tribune described him as the PDP candidate. The list was published at the weekend, and DailyDrop has not seen INEC's reply to the ultimatum or any reason for the omission.

Other complaints followed. BusinessDay reported that parties and candidates left off the final lists of governorship and state assembly contestants are protesting. The Muslim Rights Concern (MURIC) criticised INEC over the omission of opposition politicians, Leadership said. Premium Times quoted an INEC official on duplicated candidate names across some parties: the official said the lists came from the commission's headquarters, which had been alerted and was yet to reply. On Monday INEC also denied a circulating notice that claimed problems with governorship candidates in six states, calling it fake in a post on X, Tribune reported.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 7 outlets · toll 12 to 14

Houthis claim a new missile attack on Riyadh airport as Saudi Arabia keeps it shut and oil rises

Yemen's Houthi rebels said they targeted Riyadh's international airport with a ballistic missile on Monday, after the Saudi-led coalition said it had shot down a missile over the capital, according to GMA News and FinancialJuice. Reuters, in the Straits Times, said Saudi Arabia faced a new barrage of incoming fire and that King Khalid International Airport would stay shut. Mint reported that the closure runs until October 17, while CNA said it would last this week. Cirium, an aviation analytics firm, expected about 60 percent of scheduled flights to be cancelled. Earlier reports counted 12 dead and 309 injured from Saturday's strike; CNA now puts the toll from the repeated attacks at 14 dead and more than 300 injured.

A Houthi official warned of a "swift, painful response" if the United States enters the war in Yemen, according to Vanguard and Le Figaro. Delta Air Lines postponed the start of Atlanta-Riyadh flights, The Hill reported, and Malaysia urged citizens to postpone Umrah trips, CNA said. Nigeria's Foreign Ministry condemned the attack as a "dangerous escalation", Leadership reported. Brent crude, the international oil benchmark, closed near $106 a barrel on Monday, about $1.20 above Friday, The Hill said; DailyDrop has seen that figure in one outlet. Oil matters here because Nigeria sells crude and buys much of its petrol abroad.

NLC and NUT call Wike's "no work, no pay" order illegal as the Abuja strike continues

The Federal Capital Territory (FCT) branches of the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC) and the Nigeria Union of Teachers (NUT) on Monday rejected an order by Minister Nyesom Wike that striking workers should not be paid, according to Punch, Vanguard, BusinessDay and Premium Times. Premium Times said Wike directed the area council chairpersons on Friday to enforce the policy. The unions called it unconstitutional and unenforceable, citing Section 40 of the 1999 Constitution, the Trade Unions Act and International Labour Organization (ILO) Conventions 87, 98 and 151, which Vanguard said Nigeria has ratified.

Punch reported separately that Wike approved a rescheduled promotion examination for 5,990 education officers who missed the earlier exercise because of disruption. ICIR reported that Wike called the strike politically motivated; DailyDrop saw that in one outlet. It has not seen the unions' full list of October demands or any statement on whether salaries were withheld. Wike also said on Monday that he will coordinate President Bola Tinubu's 2027 re-election campaign in the FCT and Rivers State, Leadership, Tribune and BusinessDay reported.
