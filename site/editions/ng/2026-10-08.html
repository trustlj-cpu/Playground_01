Security forces rescue the 20 abducted corps members in Imo; officials say they were unharmed

Police put the rescue at about 4:28 pm on Wednesday in Amakohia Ubi, Owerri West, after a week in captivity. Neither police nor the governor said how it was done or whether a ransom was paid.

The 20 prospective National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) members seized in Imo State on October 1 were rescued on Wednesday, October 7, according to Arise, Voice of Nigeria, Legit, ThisDay and The Nigeria Brief. Police said the rescue came at about 4:28 pm at Amakohia Ubi Village in Owerri West after what they called sustained, coordinated search-and-rescue operations. ThisDay reported that the military, the Department of State Services (DSS), police and other agencies took part, and that a civilian was also freed, per one report.

Governor Hope Uzodimma praised the agencies and said the corps members were freed unharmed, Arise reported. He added that, because lives were at stake, the government was cautious about releasing details, so how the camp was entered is not explained. Earlier reports had said the hideout was mined and that the gang demanded N50 million per victim, later N1 billion in total. None of the outlets says any money was paid. Reports differ on whether the captives were held six or seven days.

Police said efforts continue to arrest the suspects. DailyDrop has not seen any arrests announced.

Nigeria opens a 2026 licensing round for 40 oil and gas blocks and will require bidders to name their real owners

The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC) opened a licensing round on Wednesday, October 7, offering 40 oil and gas blocks on land, in shallow water and in deep water, according to Reuters (via Zawya), Arise, Legit, OE Digital and Nairametrics. The commission chief, Oritsemeyiwa Eyesan, announced it at the commission's fifth-anniversary event in Abuja after President Bola Tinubu's approval.

The round will require all bidding companies to disclose their beneficial owners, and will publish more of the scoring method and results, Reuters reported. Rio Times says the beneficial-owner rule follows advice from the Nigeria Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (NEITI). The block list, qualifying rules and procedure are to be posted on a licensing-round portal and the NUPRC website. Reuters also notes Nigeria is trying to restore about 788,000 barrels a day of shut-in output and to move offshore projects worth $30 billion to $50 billion toward final investment decisions.

Presidency adviser concedes poverty rose under Tinubu but blames the cost of reform

Presidential adviser Daniel Bwala said on Wednesday night, on Channels TV's Politics Today, that the Tinubu government does not deny that poverty has risen, and placed the increase within structural reforms that earlier presidents avoided, according to Legit and Arise. Bwala has also blamed Nigeria's large population and infrastructure gap for slow benefits.

Budget Office Director-General Tanimu Yakubu has argued that hardship cannot be traced to reforms and that a World Bank poverty figure included people who were above the line but vulnerable to shocks, Arise reported. A Guardian Nigeria editorial said millions more people have been pushed into poverty by the cost-of-living crisis. The first-hand words are a television interview, not a social-media post.

NLC begins an indefinite strike in the FCT over teachers' promotions

The Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC) council in the Federal Capital Territory began an indefinite strike on Wednesday, October 7, directing workers in public and private sectors to withdraw services, according to Voice of Nigeria, BusinessDay, 21st Century Chronicle and The Nigeria Lawyer. The seven-day ultimatum to the FCT Administration had expired. The core grievance is a vacancy clause that makes teachers' promotion depend on openings.

The union also wants teachers affected by a halted 2025 promotion exercise recalled to write exams, and wants the redeployment of directors and principals to classrooms reversed. The FCT branch of the Academic Staff Union of Secondary Schools (ASUSS) told its members to resume work, calling the action premature, NAN reported. Civil groups urged suspension. DailyDrop has not seen the FCT Administration's latest position.

NGX All-Share Index closes at 250,096.75; naira near N1,332 to the dollar

The Nigerian Exchange (NGX) All-Share Index closed at 250,096.75 on Wednesday, October 7, with market capitalisation near N162.39 trillion, Mansa Markets reported. The same source gave the change as down 0.07 percent, but that does not match its own Tuesday close of 249,865.25, which would make Wednesday a small gain. DailyDrop therefore shows the level and treats the change as unconfirmed, and no second source was found.

The naira traded around N1,331.77 per dollar on the official market (NFEM) according to Frontier Brief, and N1,332.90 according to Tech Economy. In New York on Wednesday, WTI closed at $88.28 (down 1.30 percent), the S&P 500 at 7,801.77 (down 0.22 percent) and gold at $4,140.7 (down 1.11 percent).
