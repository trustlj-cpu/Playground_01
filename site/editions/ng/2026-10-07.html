World Bank lifts Nigeria's 2026 growth forecast to 4.3 percent, reversing its April cut

The bank says steadier macro policy, firmer investor confidence and a slow recovery in private investment explain the upgrade, even with oil and energy prices lifted by the Iran war.

The World Bank raised its forecast for Nigeria's economic growth in 2026 to 4.3 percent from 4.1 percent, according to Nairametrics, Blueprint and The Guardian Nigeria, citing its October Africa economic update. It sees growth of 4.4 percent in each of 2027 and 2028. The April update had cut the 2026 forecast to 4.1 percent because of weak investment and structural constraints, Arise reported at the time, so the new number reverses that cut. One outlet, 21st Century Chronicle, headlined 4.4 percent, which matches the bank's January outlook, not the October update; DailyDrop uses 4.3 percent.

The bank credited improving macroeconomic stability, stronger investor confidence and a gradual recovery in private investment, the reports said. Blueprint noted that the upgrade came despite higher energy prices linked to the Iran war. A separate briefing, Frontier Brief, said the National Bureau of Statistics put second-quarter growth at 4.43 percent; DailyDrop has seen that figure in only one outlet. The bank lifted its regional forecast for Africa to 4.3 percent as well, according to one summary.

A forecast is a projection, not a measurement. Growth of 4.3 percent would be faster than population growth, but the bank has not said how living standards would change, and many households still face high food and fuel prices. Whether Nigerians feel the gain depends on prices, jobs and wages.

Air Force corrects Ondo crash toll to 25 and publishes the names as mourning continues

The Nigerian Air Force said 25 people died when its ATR 42, NAF 931, crashed near Igbokoda in Ondo State on Monday, October 5, five crew and 20 passengers, according to AP reports carried by the Canberra Times and ABC News, and Nairametrics. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo had earlier said 32 died; officials did not explain the gap. Nairametrics reported that the Air Force released the names of the 25 killed.

President Bola Tinubu ordered flags at half-mast during three days of national mourning that began on Tuesday. The cause has not been stated and an investigation is under way. DailyDrop has not seen results of any recovery of the flight recorders.

Four state assemblies approve the state police amendment; at least 24 of 36 must agree

The assemblies of Osun, Oyo, Rivers and Edo have approved the constitutional amendment to create state police, according to Legit, The Guardian Nigeria, Voice of Nigeria and Arise. The change needs approval by at least 24 of the 36 state houses of assembly before it goes to the president for assent. Osun was first, passing it unanimously in plenary.

The Rivers assembly voted 25 to 0 in favour, Arise reported. Oyo passed the Sixth Alteration Bill 2026 after clause-by-clause consideration, VON said. The Edo vote is listed by a Legit roundup with few details, so DailyDrop treats it as less certain. The National Assembly said only the State Police Services Bill had been sent to states, not the whole package.

Appeal court reserves judgment on El-Rufai's bail appeal, with no date set

The Court of Appeal in Kaduna reserved judgment on former Kaduna governor Nasir El-Rufai's appeal against the refusal of bail by the state High Court, Legit and Channels TV reported. A three-member panel led by Justice Onyekachi Otisi heard arguments from his lawyer, Paul Erokoro, a senior advocate, and the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC).

ICPC called the appeal unmeritorious and said the trial court had rejected three separate bail requests. El-Rufai's counsel argued the offences are bailable. Earlier reports describe other bail proceedings in other courts, and DailyDrop could not reconcile them from the sources seen.

NGX All-Share Index falls 0.32% to 249,865.25; naira near N1,331 to N1,333

The Nigerian Exchange (NGX) All-Share Index closed at 249,865.25 on Tuesday, October 6, down 0.32 percent, with market capitalisation at about N162.23 trillion, Mansa Markets reported. This is the only source found for the close, so it is not yet confirmed.

Parallel-market quotes were around N1,389 to N1,390. In New York on Tuesday, the S&P 500 closed at 7,818.93 (up 0.58 percent), WTI at $89.44 (up 0.01 percent) and gold at $4,187.1 (up 0.73 percent).
