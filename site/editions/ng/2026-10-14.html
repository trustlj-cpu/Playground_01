Dangote refinery reports $4.7bn profit for nine months as its share offer, open until October 27, is said to be four times subscribed

Punch, ThisDay, BusinessDay and Premium Times carried the results. The subscription claim comes from Aliko Dangote and the company, not from the regulator.

Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemicals reported revenue of $22.5 billion and a net profit of $4.7 billion for the nine months to September 30, 2026, according to Punch, ThisDay, BusinessDay and Premium Times. ThisDay labelled the figures unaudited and said revenue rose 144 percent, with earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) of $5.5 billion; the excerpt DailyDrop saw does not say which period the 144 percent is measured against. BusinessDay put cash reserves at $6.7 billion, and ThisDay said the plant had moved from $1.7 billion of net debt to $651 million of net cash. Premium Times reported that the company credited the improvement to higher refining throughput, better operational reliability and stronger refining margins. On those numbers profit was about 21 percent of revenue, a DailyDrop calculation and not a company figure.

The results arrive while the company's initial public offering (IPO), its first sale of shares to the public, is still open. The closing date has been moved to Tuesday, October 27. ThisDay carried a claim attributed to Aliko Dangote that the offer was already four times oversubscribed, and Punch reported subscriptions at four times the N2.15 trillion target, using the word “reportedly”. Premium Times quoted a company statement that the IPO is “going very well” and that the date would move so that more Africans could take part. The reports seen do not cite a subscription total from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) or the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), so every figure on demand is still the issuer's own.

The offer is also reaching public budgets. Daily Trust reported that Yobe State Governor Mai Mala Buni approved N230.62 million to buy shares for 43,928 vulnerable women across the state's 17 local government areas. That works out at N5,250 each, the minimum application noted in earlier reports; the division is DailyDrop's. Vanguard, in a single report, quoted Mr Dangote as saying the plant plans to lift capacity from 500,000 to 1.4 million barrels a day by early 2029. DailyDrop has not seen, in these reports, a statement of how the IPO proceeds would be used.

What to watch: the prospectus, the document that sets out the risks, and audited accounts before the October 27 close; the first official subscription figure; and the allotment date, when applicants learn how many shares they receive. A profit of this size is the company's case for the price. It is for investors to test it.

IMF lifts Nigeria's 2026 growth forecast to 4.2 percent but warns that dearer essentials will deepen poverty

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) now expects Nigeria's economy to grow 4.2 percent in 2026, up from 4.0 percent, according to BusinessDay, Nairametrics, Premium Times and ThisDay. ThisDay and Channels TV gave 4.3 percent for 2027. The Fund published its forecasts during the annual meetings it holds with the World Bank, this year in Bangkok. Premium Times quoted the report as saying growth is supported by better macroeconomic stability and favourable terms of trade, “though higher prices for essentials are expected to further aggravate poverty and food insecurity”. BusinessDay's headline says Nigeria still trails African peers; DailyDrop has not seen the comparison.

On policy, Channels TV and Vanguard reported that Fund officials urged targeted, temporary help for poor households, such as cash transfers. Vanguard quoted an official saying living costs are still very high and that the Fund is mindful the population is suffering. Channels TV separately reported advice that Nigeria can ease monetary policy as inflation improves but should stay guided by data. Two UN poverty counts also ran this week: Tribune reported 113 million Nigerians deprived of health, education or living standards, and Punch, citing UNDP, reported 17.9 million falling into multidimensional poverty between 2021 and 2024.

Politics · 2027Press ✓ 6 outlets · full list not seen

APC puts Wike, Adeleke and Soludo on Tinubu's campaign council; opposition parties trade blows over it

The All Progressives Congress (APC) published a revised presidential campaign council list that names Nyesom Wike, Ademola Adeleke and Chukwuma Soludo as campaign managers for the Federal Capital Territory, Osun and Anambra, ThisDay reported. Vanguard said the list draws in leading figures from the PDP, Accord and APGA despite strong push-back inside the party, and Daily Trust called it a “rainbow” council meant to widen the party's appeal. Punch, Premium Times and Channels TV said President Bola Tinubu is due to inaugurate the council today. Vanguard and Channels TV give the date of the presidential election as January 16, 2027.

Reaction split along party lines. Punch reported that the PDP, APGA and Accord backed the inclusion of their own chieftains. BusinessDay reported opposition parties insisting that the move will not save the APC. Accord's presidential candidate, Mr Olawepo-Hashim, called it “indiscipline, rascality” and said Mr Adeleke is not the party's leader and cannot compel Osun voters, Vanguard and Channels TV reported. Leadership found political scientists divided on what it means. DailyDrop has not seen the full list of names, or any statement from Mr Wike, Mr Adeleke or Mr Soludo on their roles.

World · Oil and fuelPress ✓ 3 outlets on the strike, 4 on the discount

Houthi missiles hit Aramco's headquarters and keep Brent above $105, while Nigeria's 30-day petrol discount draws fire

A Houthi missile attack on Tuesday hit Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran and started a fire in part of the complex, Bloomberg reported. Reuters had earlier reported loud booms near the site, citing sources, and FinancialJuice relayed a Saudi state news agency report, citing an energy ministry official, that the fire was limited and there were no casualties. The Financial Times and FinancialJuice reported coalition strikes on Houthi targets in Sanaa, including an oil facility. Reuters reported President Donald Trump saying the Houthis are trying to push up oil prices before the U.S. midterm elections. FinancialJuice put Brent crude at $105.69 a barrel at Tuesday's settlement, down 96 cents, a figure DailyDrop saw in one outlet; Reuters reported oil climbing again in Asian trade as traders weighed Saudi supply risk.

At home, the debate is about pump prices. Punch and Premium Times reported the information minister saying Mr Tinubu will not abandon Nigerians and that NNPC's 30-day discount is not a return to subsidy. The CPPE backed the relief as temporary but said its effect on households will be minor, TheCable and Punch reported. Leadership, quoting the rights group HURIWA, put the cut at N66 a litre against a pump price of N1,370. ThisDay reported Peter Obi, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate, saying the discount covers fewer than 4 percent of filling stations, a claim DailyDrop has not checked.

INEC gives parties until October 24 to challenge omissions from the governorship list as Pantami's supporters cry foul

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) said political parties that believe their governorship candidates were wrongly left off the final list for the 2027 elections have 14 days to notify it, which puts the deadline at October 24, according to Punch, Leadership, Vanguard, Channels TV and ThisDay. ThisDay and Leadership said the window comes from Section 32(2) of the Electoral Act, 2026. Leadership named Prof Isa Ali Pantami, Ladi Adebutu and Gwarzo among those affected; Punch named Pantami and Adebutu. Vanguard tied the deadline to a Supreme Court ruling, and Daily Trust said the court upheld INEC's revised timetable and affirmed parts of the Act, Sections 77(5) to 77(7).

Pressure continued. The Muslim Rights Concern (MURIC) called Prof Pantami's omission “impunity” and contempt of court, saying he emerged from a court-ordered fresh primary, Vanguard reported; DailyDrop saw that claim in one outlet. The Ogun APC campaign council denied any part in Mr Adebutu's omission, Punch reported, and Vanguard said a PDP assembly candidate in Ogun warned INEC not to “set Ogun on fire”. Channels TV said INEC explained the omissions; the reasons were not in the reports DailyDrop saw. Prof Pantami's own 72-hour ultimatum to INEC, covered yesterday, runs out around Thursday.
