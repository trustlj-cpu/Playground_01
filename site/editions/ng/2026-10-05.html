Police find the camp holding 20 abducted corps members in Imo, but say it is mined with explosives

Gunmen seized the prospective corps members from two buses on Independence Day. Families say the ransom demand ran to N50m a head, and officers are moving in with bulldozers and armoured vehicles.

Police in Imo State said they have located the hideout of the gunmen who abducted prospective National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) members on Thursday, October 1, but could not move in at once because the site is reportedly mined with improvised explosive devices (IEDs), according to The Guardian Nigeria, Blueprint and ThisDay. The victims were travelling in two 18-seater buses from Ibadan to orientation camps in Abia and Akwa Ibom when the vehicles were stopped near Umunoha in Mbaitoli, on the Owerri-Onitsha road. Reports put the number of corps members at 19 or 20; police first said the count was still being verified.

Imo Police Commissioner Audu Bosso said a drone helped locate the camp. Operatives then brought in bulldozers and armoured personnel carriers to clear a path, the outlets reported. Families told reporters that the kidnappers first asked for N50 million per victim, a total of about N950 million to N1 billion, and later gave a 24-hour ultimatum, according to Arise and Legit. The Punch front page, as summarised by TVC News, said the demand was cut from N50 million to N5 million per head. That reduction has not been confirmed by a second outlet, so DailyDrop treats it as unverified. One parent also told the press that the captives were being beaten; that account is not independently confirmed.

The abduction has revived worry over the safety of young people posted to camps across the country. A parent appealed to President Bola Tinubu to step in, ICIR reported. DailyDrop has not seen any official statement on who the gunmen are or whether any ransom has been paid. The next hours decide whether security forces can reach the captives without setting off the explosives.

Public servants end a three-day warning strike over fuel prices and pay, and threaten a total shutdown

The Joint National Public Service Negotiating Council (JNPSNC) staged a three-day warning strike from Friday, October 2, to Sunday, October 4, across federal, state and local government services, according to Legit, The Guardian Nigeria and Arise. The council said the government ignored a September 21 letter to the president. It wants petrol cut to N500 a litre, an immediate wage award, and talks on a new national minimum wage. The council puts current pump prices between N1,450 and N2,000 a litre; that is its own figure.

Council secretary Gbenga Olowoyo told Arise that a total strike would follow if demands are not met within one to two weeks. A Monday briefing by Frontier Brief quotes about 65 percent compliance, but that comes from one outlet. DailyDrop has not seen a government reply.

Iran says Strait of Hormuz stays shut until US meets its conditions

Iran's parliament speaker and chief negotiator, Mohammad-Bagher Qalibaf, said on Sunday, October 4, that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until seven conditions are met, reported Arab News, Oman Observer, BusinessDay South Africa and the Star-Advertiser. Oman Observer says the conditions include lifting the US naval blockade, unfreezing assets and halting military pressure. Other outlets did not list them.

Tehran's plan was presented at the United Nations in late September; it would reopen the waterway within seven days if the US lifts its blockade, waives oil sanctions and observes a ceasefire (Al Jazeera, LBC). The US replied through Qatari mediators. For Nigeria, an oil exporter and a petrol importer, higher crude helps government revenue but raises pump prices. WTI closed Friday at $91.11, down 1.90 percent. DailyDrop has not seen a US reply to Qalibaf.

US employers add just 29,000 jobs in September as unemployment rises to 4.2 percent

The US economy added 29,000 jobs in September, far below the roughly 90,000 economists expected, the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) said on Friday, October 2, according to UPI, Al Jazeera, Gulf Times and Straight Arrow News. Unemployment rose to 4.2 percent. Health care led with 17,000 jobs, while financial activities lost 7,000.

Earlier months were revised down: August from 162,000 to 133,000 and July from 21,000 to a loss of 10,000. Markets read the data as lowering the chance of a Federal Reserve rate rise this month. For Nigeria, a softer US economy can weaken the dollar and ease pressure on the naira, but it can also cut demand for oil. S&P 500 closed at 7,722.72 on Friday, up 0.73 percent.

NGX All-Share Index slips 0.16% to 250,808.27 on Friday; naira trades near N1,330

The Nigerian Exchange (NGX) All-Share Index closed at 250,808.27 on Friday, October 2, down 0.16 percent, with market capitalisation at about N162.84 trillion, according to Mansa Markets and Nairametrics, which also gave a fall of N261.92 billion in market capitalisation. Ten stocks gained and ten fell.

The naira traded around N1,328 to N1,332 to the dollar on the official market (NFEM), by differing counts from Tech Economy, WithinNigeria and others; parallel-market quotes were N1,365 to N1,390. In New York on Friday, WTI closed at $91.11 (down 1.90 percent), gold at $4,162.3 (down 1.00 percent), and the S&P 500 at 7,722.72 (up 0.73 percent).
