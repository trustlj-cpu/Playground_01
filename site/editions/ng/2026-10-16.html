Tinubu opens his 2027 campaign council, and Atiku and Shettima trade blows

The President told the expanded APC team to deliver polling units and promised that governance will not take a back seat; opponents say the size of the council shows fear.

President Bola Tinubu formally opened Nigeria’s 2027 election season on Wednesday, inaugurating the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign council at the Presidential Villa in Abuja and urging its members to deliver polling units. Channels Television, Premium Times and ThisDay all covered the event. Tinubu said governance would remain his priority and would not take a back seat to politics, and ThisDay quoted him as saying he is ready to renew his pact with Nigerians. Premium Times added that governors and lawmakers have been tasked with grassroots mobilisation for the re-election bid.

The opposition answered the same day. Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar faulted the expanded council and the use of the Presidential Villa for a party event, according to Premium Times, and told Vanguard that the enlarged team was “a confession of fear”. Several outlets also carried his remark that Tinubu is “ashamed to face the people”. Vice-President Kashim Shettima hit back, telling Atiku “You’re not Abraham Lincoln” and accusing him of disrespecting the President, while ThisDay said Shettima also took jabs at Obi. The African Democratic Congress (ADC) went further and accused the APC of coercing opposition governors into joining the campaign council. That is an allegation, and we found no reply from the APC in the reports we reviewed.

Inside the ruling party the message to state chiefs was blunt. Wike challenged APC governors to deliver their states for Tinubu in 2027, as BusinessDay and others reported, while Kaduna State Governor Uba Sani urged public office holders from his state to rally behind the President.

By Thursday the President was already managing expectations. Premium Times reported that Tinubu will skip some APC rallies because of the demands of governance, and Leadership quoted him saying he will not visit every state to campaign. On the other side, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde, whose presidential campaign promises to end hunger and poverty, told Punch that Nigerians can reject leaders after one term while criticising the Federal Government’s economic reforms. Channels also reported that INEC has explained its legal basis for certain exclusions and the procedure parties can use to challenge them; we could not confirm the details from a second outlet.

Akpabio says the Senate presidency is not vacant and defends his religion remarks

Senate President Godswill Akpabio spent Wednesday and Thursday defending both his seat and his words. Premium Times reported that senators declared the Senate presidency seat non-vacant and backed Akpabio, Deputy Senate President Barau and other principal officers. The same outlet carried a separate report that Akpabio courted Tinubu and warned Uzodimma over ambition. Daily Trust quoted him telling APC governors to stop eyeing “a position that is not available”, and said he declared that nobody can penetrate the National Assembly to destabilise Tinubu. On Thursday Akpabio also answered criticism of his recent comments on religion. Punch and Daily Trust both quoted him saying he has sponsored Muslim pilgrims for 25 years and has sent more than 5,000 people to Mecca. The reports do not spell out the original remark, so readers should check the earlier coverage before judging the row. What is clear is the timing: the defence came the day after the APC council was inaugurated, with the Senate leadership signalling that it expects to stay in place through the 2027 cycle.

Economy · Monetary policy✓ 2 outlets on the AFC post · IMF remarks from Premium Times only

Cardoso is named Africa Finance Corporation chairman, then defends the MPC’s independence

The Africa Finance Corporation (AFC) has appointed CBN Governor Olayemi Cardoso as its board chairman, according to Punch and ThisDay, whose report was datelined Bangkok. Premium Times separately reported that Cardoso outlined Nigeria’s approach to credible monetary policy at an IMF forum and explained how the MPC avoids “groupthink” and keeps its independence. Both are single-outlet accounts of his remarks, and we have not checked the full speeches.

The home debate over those policies continues. Punch quoted Finance Minister Taiwo Oyedele saying economic reforms must protect vulnerable Nigerians and highlighting social protection, while Premium Times quoted him saying Nigeria is lucky to have Tinubu as leader now. Makinde, now a rival for the presidency, argued that the reforms have hurt ordinary people. Expect the Central Bank’s credibility abroad and the cost of living at home to sit side by side in the campaign. We do not have a confirmed close for the naira or the NGX this morning, so the tape above shows a dash.

Security · Policy✓ 3 reports on state police · Tinubu’s order from Punch only

Tinubu orders security chiefs to rescue abductees as the state police bill is “90% ready”

Punch reported that President Tinubu has ordered security chiefs to rescue all abducted persons and make sure no terrorist or bandit camp survives. It is a single-outlet report, so the exact wording and any deadline remain unconfirmed. The test of any such order is what follows on the ground, and the reports give no timetable. Separately, Gbajabiamila said the national policing bill, which would create state police, is 90% ready, a figure that appeared in three reports but is his own estimate and not an independent measure.

Channels Television quoted the Defence Minister assuring voters of their safety in 2027 and saying Tinubu will inaugurate new military aircraft. ThisDay reported that the Nigerian Air Force (NAF) is moving to prevent air crashes after the Chief of the Air Staff ordered stronger flight safety oversight. Médecins Sans Frontières also reported treating more than 87,000 children in Katsina, a reminder of what insecurity and hunger do together in the north.

Business · Energy✓ Dangote IPO from Punch only · Kwankwaso from Channels only

Dangote IPO will favour small investors, and Kwankwaso floats a fuel subsidy

Aliko Dangote says investors applying with N50,000 or less will get priority in the Dangote Refinery initial public offering (IPO), Punch reported. That is one outlet’s account of his statement, and we have not seen the offer documents, the opening date or the share price. Elsewhere on the pump-price question, Channels Television quoted Rabiu Kwankwaso saying his camp would consider a fuel subsidy to bring down the petrol price. The idea runs against the reform line that Tinubu defends and that Oyedele, in Punch, says must still protect the vulnerable.

For context on the cost of fuel, the market feed shows US crude (WTI) at $96.12 a barrel at Thursday’s close, down 0.39%, with gold at $4,192.50 and the S&P 500 at 7,744.06, up 0.33%. Those are single-feed readings and are not a forecast for Nigerian pump prices. In the same news run, Punch reported Washington’s sanctions on Cuba’s Banco Internacional de Comercio, which has no direct Nigerian angle but shows how fast financial rules are shifting abroad.
