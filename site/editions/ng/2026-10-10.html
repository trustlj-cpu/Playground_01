Trump says Putin will release Russian diesel and eases sanctions for six months to cut fuel prices

Treasury issued a waiver for Russian-origin diesel after Trump posted that Moscow agreed to send 300,000 tons at once. President Zelenskyy condemned the move and analysts doubt it will lower US prices much.

President Donald Trump said on Friday, October 9, that Russian President Vladimir Putin had agreed to release 300,000 tons of diesel immediately, 500,000 tons in November and 1 million tons in December, according to CBS News, CNBC, NPR and Fortune. In his Truth Social post he added that another 3 million tons could follow, depending on the state of Russian refineries. The US Treasury's sanctions office issued a general license covering the sale and import of Russian-origin diesel, reports said. Outlets differ slightly on the end date, with some saying April 7, 2027, and others calling it a six-month waiver.

The reports say the waiver cuts against a sanctions law Trump signed in September, though the president can waive many of its provisions. US diesel stood at about $6.23 a gallon, and the move comes before the November 3 midterm elections. NBC News and NPR described it as relaxing pressure on Moscow to ease prices. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy condemned the deal and warned that easing sanctions without a Russian commitment to de-escalate will prolong the war. A Forbes analysis argued that sanctions relief would do little for US fuel prices. DailyDrop has not seen a Kremlin confirmation of the quantities.

For Nigeria, diesel powers generators, trucks and farm machines. A rise or fall in world diesel prices reaches local pump prices with a delay, whether the fuel is refined at home or imported. WTI settled on Friday at $91.85, up 0.39 percent, so oil stayed near the level reached after the week's tanker attacks.

Navi Pillay, South African jurist and former UN human rights chief, wins the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize

The Norwegian Nobel Committee announced on Friday, October 9, in Oslo that the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize goes to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, a South African jurist, according to Euronews, CNN, RTE, Time, CBS News and NPR. The committee cited her efforts to promote peace and international law. Pillay was UN High Commissioner for Human Rights from 2008 to 2014 and served as a judge at the International Criminal Court. She is the fifth South African to win the prize.

Committee chair Jorgen Watne Frydnes said the international legal system is under tremendous pressure and that the rule of law underpins ways of resolving conflict without violence. Pillay dedicated the award to survivors of international crimes and defenders of international justice. Mr Frydnes read the name at the Norwegian Nobel Institute. A Peace Research Institute Oslo (PRIO) director said the choice was not a direct rebuke to President Trump, who had been listed among bookmakers' contenders. The prize will be presented in Oslo on December 10.

INEC says it will meet to decide what a Supreme Court ruling means for party candidates

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) said it received the certified Supreme Court judgment only recently and will hold an internal meeting to decide what action to take, according to Legit, the Guardian Nigeria and ThisDay. The Supreme Court ruled on September 24 that sections 77(5), 77(6), 77(7) and 84(2) of the Electoral Act 2026, which the Court of Appeal had struck down, are valid. They concern party membership registers.

National commissioner Mohammed Haruna warned that some political parties may be barred from fielding candidates, including governorship candidates, if they violated membership register rules, the reports said. Accord Party candidate Gbenga Olawepo-Hashim called for INEC to be disbanded and reconstituted. The Nation's Saturday front page focused on enforcing the ruling. Reports differ on which sections are involved, and on the date of INEC chairman Joash Amupitan's remarks.

APC says Tinubu will flag off his re-election campaign in Kaduna on October 24

Abdulaziz Yari, Director-General of the APC Presidential Campaign Council, said Tinubu will launch his 2027 re-election campaign in Kaduna on October 24 after briefing the president at the Villa, according to Naija News's Saturday roundup and TVC's review. DailyDrop did not find a second independent report of the date.

The council was named in August with Tinubu as chairman, Vice President Kashim Shettima as vice chairman and Governor Hope Uzodimma as secretary, outlets reported then. Yari said earlier the campaign would begin with town halls and consultations rather than big rallies, so a Kaduna flag-off would be a change of approach. The election timetable cited by one report puts the presidential vote on January 16, 2027.
