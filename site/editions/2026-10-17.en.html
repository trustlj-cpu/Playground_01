Firefighter in cardiac arrest while battling Bucheon paint plant fire dies in the line of duty; 8 injured including 6 firefighters

Fire Lieutenant Kim Jin-seok (44), the first to go in to fight a fire at a factory in Ojeong District, Bucheon, on the morning of the 16th, died around 12:55 a.m. on the 17th. The plant held about 100 20-liter cans of Class 2 petroleum.

A fire broke out around 8:38 a.m. on the 16th at a metal-parts paint plant in Naedong, Ojeong District, Bucheon, Gyeonggi (Gyeongin Ilbo, Insight, JoongAng Economy News). Fire authorities issued a Level 1 response after 19 minutes and a Level 2 response around 9:09 a.m., committing 80 personnel and 38 pieces of equipment (Gyeonggi Shinmun). Two crew members near the entrance got out, but two further inside were trapped; one regained consciousness during treatment, while the other was taken away in cardiac arrest (Gyeongin Ilbo, Kyunghyang Shinmun). Korea Economic Daily reported that 6 firefighters collapsed, and Gyeonggi Shinmun counted 8 injured, 6 firefighters and 2 plant officials. Fire Lieutenant Kim Jin-seok (44) of the Ojeong Center, Bucheon Fire Station, who was taken away in cardiac arrest, died during treatment around 12:55 a.m. on the 17th, Financial News, MBC and Cheonji Ilbo reported, citing the National Fire Agency and Gyeonggi Province. SBS and MBC reported that he was called out about 20 minutes before the end of his shift and arrived 5 minutes after the report, that he went in first in a two-person team with a colleague and fought the fire for more than 20 minutes before being trapped by flames and smoke, and that he was in cardiac arrest when rescued 30 minutes later. He was a firefighter in his 13th year (SBS).

The plant stored about 100 20-liter cans of Class 2 petroleum, a Class 4 hazardous material, and fire authorities determined that the plant had no record of a hazardous-materials handling permit, Gyeonggi Shinmun reported. MBC reported that police believe metal-washing work was under way at the time and held a joint inspection, giving weight to the possibility that static electricity ignited oil vapor. Fire authorities decided to promote him one rank posthumously and to support his burial at the National Cemetery, and President Lee Jae-myung, who visited the memorial altar, said, “The people will remember the sacrifice forever,” and conferred the Green Stripes Order of Service Merit, MBC reported. At the family’s wish the funeral will be a Gyeonggi provincial funeral, with the memorial service on the 19th (Cheonji Ilbo, Financial News). Financial News reported that ruling and opposition parties spoke with one voice in mourning and said they would look after fire-service budgets.

Economy · EmploymentPress ✓ 7 outlets · months since manufacturing returned to growth given as 26 and 27 in different reports

September employment up 113,000, manufacturing rebounds after 27 months; youth employment rate falls for 29th month

In the September employment trends released by the National Data Office on the 16th, the number of employed was 29.266 million, up 113,000 from a year earlier (Asia Economy, E-Today, JoongAng Economy News). It is the third straight month of gains in the 100,000s, but smaller than August’s 184,000. Manufacturing employment rose 16,000, the first gain since June 2024; Financial News and Seoul Economic Daily gave it as 27 months, E-Today as 26 months. Bin Hyeon-jun, head of the Social Statistics Bureau, explained that automobiles and textiles fell but chemicals, metal processing and other transport equipment rose. Accommodation and restaurants fell by 97,000 and construction by 48,000. The employment rate for ages 15–29 fell 1.1 percentage points to 44.0%, down for the 29th straight month, and employment in that age group fell by 147,000, down for 47 straight months (Segye Ilbo, Seoul Shinmun, Newspim). The youth unemployment rate rose 0.6 percentage points to 5.4%. In contrast, the employment rate for ages 15–64 was 70.6%, the highest ever for a September (Kukmin Ilbo, Newspim). In the figures for ages 15–34, added from this release, employment fell by 98,000 and the employment rate fell 0.4 percentage points to 56.1% (Newspim).

Incheon Guwol-dong knife-attack suspect identified: Choi Chung-rak, 59, accused of injuring 8

The Incheon Metropolitan Police Agency held its Identity Disclosure Review Committee on the 16th and decided to release the name, age and face photo of Choi Chung-rak (59), detained on charges of attempted murder and carrying a knife in a public place, Ajunews, YTN, Incheon Ilbo and Edaily reported. The committee, weighing the brutality of the crime, the serious harm, sufficient evidence and the public interest, voted unanimously, and because the suspect raised no objection the information is posted on the Incheon Metropolitan Police Agency website from the 16th to the 16th of next month (Incheon Ilbo, Topstar News). Choi is suspected of wielding a knife at a commercial facility in Guwol-dong, Namdong District, on the 13th, injuring 8 people (Korea Economic Daily, Gukje News). Incheon Ilbo reported that a woman in her 20s who was taken away in cardiac arrest is in intensive care without having regained consciousness. The court issued a detention warrant citing flight risk (Incheon Ilbo). YTN reported that police are considering rewards for 5 people, including a citizen who helped subdue him by throwing a chair.

KOSPI up 2.05% to 5,668.05, first gain in 8 sessions; Kosdaq jumps 4.46%, triggering buy-side sidecar

The KOSPI closed on the 16th up 113.83 points (2.05%) at 5,668.05, ending a streak of 7 straight sessions of losses that began on the 6th (Segye Ilbo, Gyeongbuk Domin Ilbo, Gongam Shinmun). The Kosdaq finished up 33.96 points (4.46%) at 794.78, and reached 807.54 intraday, briefly regaining the 800 level (Segye Ilbo, Financial Post). Around 12:55 p.m. a buy-side sidecar was triggered on the Kosdaq market, suspending the effect of program buy orders for 5 minutes (Special Times, Good Morning Economy). It was the 34th sidecar on the Kosdaq market this year and the 19th buy-side sidecar. On the KOSPI, foreign investors recorded net buying of 341.3 billion won, while individuals and institutions net sold 485.8 billion won and 682.4 billion won respectively (Segye Ilbo). On the Kosdaq, institutions were net buyers of 168.4 billion won and lifted the index. Segye Ilbo wrote that rebound buying came after the sharp fall, while CBC News carried a cautious view that, even with foreigners returning, staying power is lacking.

Politics · LawPress ✓ 7 outlets · poll figures are from a single Gallup Korea survey

Special counsel seeks 30 years again for Yoon Suk-yeol on appeal in ‘Pyongyang drone’ case; verdict December 4

At the final hearing of the appeal on charges of aiding the enemy, held on the 16th before Criminal Division 1 of the Seoul High Court (presiding judge Yoon Seong-sik), the insurrection special counsel team sought 30 years in prison each for former President Yoon Suk-yeol and former Defense Minister Kim Yong-hyun, YTN, Kyunghyang Shinmun, Sisa Journal and Segye Ilbo reported. The sentence request for Yoon is the same as the first-instance request and sentence, and for Kim it is higher than the first-instance request (25 years). Yeo In-hyung, former commander of the Defense Counterintelligence Command, faces a request of 20 years, and Kim Yong-dae, former commander of the Drone Operations Command, 5 years. The special counsel said the defendants had sought to provoke North Korea into an armed attack to create a pretext for martial law and were willing to risk a limited war, calling it “a graver crime than insurrection,” while Yoon’s side rebutted that it was a security measure responding to North Korean provocation (Kyunghyang Shinmun, Digital Daily). The verdict is at 3 p.m. on December 4 (Special Times).

In a Gallup Korea survey released the same day (13th–15th, 1,003 respondents), President Lee Jae-myung’s job approval was 39%, up 2 percentage points from a week earlier, and disapproval was 52% (Segye Ilbo, Munhwa Ilbo). Party support was 36% for the Democratic Party of Korea and 27% for the People Power Party. 47% said many opposition candidates should win the 2028 general election to check the government, and 34% said the ruling party should be supported (Startup Ilbo, Asia News Agency).
