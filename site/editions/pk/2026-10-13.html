Hundreds of PTI members detained as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa stays under emergency; army deployment notified

The Centre said on Monday that PTI politicians were detained after the provincial government was suspended; police put the number in the hundreds. Schools were shut and transport was suspended in Lahore.

Politicians from Imran Khan’s Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) were detained after the suspension of the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) government, the federal government said on Monday 12 October, in a Reuters report carried by the Straits Times. Police said hundreds of party members had been held, according to CNA and the Straits Times, and Le Monde, which described a further step in the crackdown on the movement demanding Mr Khan’s release, also spoke of arrests by the hundreds. The Straits Times added that thousands of his supporters have been marching in the province and airing grievances over harsh economic conditions. The detentions followed the emergency under Article 232 and governor’s rule under Article 234 that President Asif Ali Zardari approved on Sunday. The Centre says the PTI government had disengaged from the fight against terrorism; PTI calls the step a coup, the Guardian reported. Business Recorder reported that PTI and its allies in the TTAP alliance vowed to fight the decision, that an 11-member advisory council had been named and that the deployment of the army in KP had been notified; the Economic Times headlined that the army is eyeing a bigger role in the province. The law minister said on Sunday that the governor would now lead the administration, and ARY News quoted Governor Faisal Karim Kundi saying the province backs the emergency to restore peace.

Daily life was affected beyond Peshawar. ARY News reported late on Sunday that educational institutions would stay closed on Monday over security concerns, and Dawn reported that intercity transport was suspended in Lahore with bus terminals sealed, in what it called a bid to curtail PTI workers’ mobility. In Karachi, the commissioner’s Section 144 order, imposed on Sunday, bans gatherings of more than five people until 20 October, Dawn reported. Dawn also asked where Chief Minister Sohail Afridi was: PTI says he was taken into custody on Sunday, but the official statements DailyDrop found speak of detained politicians without naming him, so his arrest stays unconfirmed. The crisis grew out of PTI’s long march towards Islamabad, which began on 4 October to demand Mr Khan’s release. Information Minister Attaullah Tarar alleged a nexus between the PTI government and terrorists, a claim that has not been tested in court, and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi said PTI had not answered the Centre’s counterterrorism efforts (Dawn, Pakistan Observer). Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif vowed there would be no more silence on terrorism, the Express Tribune reported. Under the Constitution the proclamation must be laid before Parliament within a fixed period and courts can review it, and PTI has said it will challenge it in court; DailyDrop saw no report of a parliamentary vote or a court hearing in the sources it checked.

IMF–World Bank meetings open in Bangkok; IMF chief warns of a “slow-moving tsunami” from AI for jobs and taxes

The IMF–World Bank annual meetings opened in Bangkok on Monday, with the Middle East war, high public debt and artificial intelligence (AI) set to dominate, according to Reuters, Yonhap and Premium Times. IMF managing director Kristalina Georgieva told the meetings that AI is already hollowing out demand for human workers by taking over routine tasks, calling it a “slow-moving tsunami” for jobs and for tax revenue, the Straits Times and the Japan Times reported; City AM’s headline said it could hit 60 per cent of jobs in advanced economies. Separately the Fund said the surge in AI investment is keeping global growth near 3 per cent despite the energy shock, Reuters, Yonhap and ANSA reported. For Pakistan the news is mixed. Bloomberg reported that a newly formed borrowers’ collective of developing countries elected Pakistan to chair it, as UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned of a “perfect storm”; DailyDrop found no second outlet. Bloomberg also carried Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb saying Pakistan targets growth of more than 4 per cent for 2026, a single-source figure. Yonhap also reported Georgieva saying AI productivity could offset the shrinking workforce of ageing economies such as Korea, China and Japan, and NHK reported that Japan’s finance minister, Katayama, will chair the joint Development Committee of the two institutions next. Premium Times also listed the energy supply shock and mounting debts among the headline issues.

Riyadh airport shut until Saturday as Houthis claim a new missile attack on Monday; Pakistan condemns attacks on Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia said on Monday 12 October that it faced a new barrage of missile and drone fire and that Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport would stay shut, days after a Saturday missile strike on a departure terminal claimed by the Iran-allied Houthis, Reuters reported through the Straits Times and CNA. The Houthis said they targeted the airport with ballistic missiles on Monday, GMA News reported, and the Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed a ballistic missile fired towards Riyadh (FinancialJuice). Livemint reported the closure was extended to 17 October. Air-traffic data showed aircraft avoiding the capital, and cancellations across all Saudi airports reached 46.4 per cent, the Straits Times reported. Delta said it would postpone its Atlanta–Riyadh launch until March (CNBC, Bloomberg, The Hill).

Pakistan’s position is shaped by its defence pact with Riyadh and by millions of citizens working in the kingdom. Reuters listed Pakistan among the countries that condemned the attacks, and Business Recorder reported the Foreign Office reaffirming its commitment to Saudi sovereignty and security. Dawn reported that a Pakistani resident was killed and 11 people wounded in Sunday’s strike. Separately, the New York Times and the Straits Times reported that Iran offered to mediate between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia; Riyadh had not accepted by the time of writing. Yemen’s armed forces also warned airlines and passengers that no Saudi airspace or airport was safe except Mecca and Medina, a claim carried by Fars through FinancialJuice that Saudi officials had not endorsed.

Markets · Oil and HormuzPress ✓ 5 outlets · IRGC claims unverified

Oil jumps 2% as tanker strikes near Hormuz revive supply fears; WTI settles at $93.74

Oil first fell in Asian trade on hopes of more Russian supply (CNA, The Hindu) but US crude rose in New York as attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz and the Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia outweighed hopes that Russian diesel would ease supply. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) November futures settled at $93.74 a barrel, up $1.89 or 2.06 per cent, FinancialJuice and ANSA reported. The UK Navy’s maritime trade centre (UKMTO) reported a crude tanker struck by an unknown projectile in Hormuz, FinancialJuice relayed, and Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy (IRGC) said a third supertanker had been hit in two days, Fars reported through FinancialJuice; the IRGC claim is unverified. India’s The Hindu reported that two Indian-crewed ships were attacked on Sunday and two Indians remain missing, and that Indian security adviser Ajit Doval spoke to his Iranian counterpart.

Iran said the US response to its proposal to reopen Hormuz was “insufficient” (Haaretz, Rappler) and that it will reply once its review is over, Reuters reported. Indian shares fell on the oil spike: the Sensex lost 548 points, The Hindu reported. For Pakistan, which buys most of its fuel abroad, higher crude feeds into petrol prices and the import bill; the rupee figure for Monday was not confirmed in two sources.
