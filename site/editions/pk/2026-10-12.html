Politics · Khyber PakhtunkhwaPress ✓ 6 outlets · arrests reported by PTI, not officially confirmed

Centre imposes emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, ending the PTI government’s hold; PTI vows a court challenge

The President approved the cabinet’s recommendation on Sunday under Articles 232 and 234 of the Constitution, citing the provincial government’s “deliberate disengagement” from the fight against terrorism. PTI says its chief minister has been arrested.

President Asif Ali Zardari approved the federal cabinet’s recommendation on Sunday 11 October to impose an emergency under Article 232 and governor’s rule under Article 234 in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), the President House said, according to Dawn, ARY News, Business Recorder and Reuters. The cabinet said the PTI-led provincial government had deliberately disengaged from the fight against terrorism. The law minister said the governor would now lead the administration and the provincial government’s tenure had ended; Dr Muhammad Fakhr Alam was named chief secretary, and Governor Faisal Karim Kundi backed the step. ARY News reported that an 11-member advisory council had been formed. The step follows weeks of tension. PTI’s long march demanding the release of the jailed party founder, Imran Khan, began on 4 October, and ministers had been openly discussing Articles 232 and 234 for days; on Friday the Express Tribune reported, citing sources, that preparations for an emergency in 12 districts were complete.

Security measures followed quickly. Containers were placed around Peshawar, Dawn reported, and it also reported that intercity transport was suspended in Lahore with bus terminals reportedly sealed. FinancialJuice, citing an interior ministry order, reported that military forces will be deployed in the province; DailyDrop did not find a second outlet. Information Minister Attaullah Tarar alleged a “nexus” between the PTI government and terrorists, and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi said PTI had not answered the Centre’s counterterrorism efforts. PTI chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan said the party would challenge the order in court; PTI called it an assault on the province’s mandate and condemned the reported arrests of Chief Minister Sohail Afridi and Junaid Akbar. The JUI-F chief, Fazlur Rehman, summoned his party’s core committee. Dawn carried a timeline of earlier emergency rule in Pakistan and a piece saying the decision evoked grim October memories, with critics calling it an ill-advised step. Under the Constitution a proclamation of emergency must be laid before Parliament for endorsement within a fixed period, so the National Assembly and Senate are the next stage, and the courts can be asked to review it. DailyDrop found no official confirmation of the arrests, and no report on whether the long march gathering planned at Swabi took place.

Security · KurramPress ✓ 3 outlets · ISPR’s account, not independently verified

Soldier killed and 12 militants dead as security forces foil infiltration bid from Afghanistan in Kurram, army says

A Pakistan Army soldier was killed and 12 militants were killed in an exchange of fire in Kurram district, the army’s media wing, ISPR, said on Sunday, according to Dawn, Geo News and Pakistan Observer. ISPR said a group of what it calls khawarij tried to cross the border from Afghanistan and that Afghan Taliban personnel were among those killed; a clearance operation was continuing in the area. Geo named the dead soldier as Naib Subedar Aman.

ISPR also said the Afghan Taliban government facilitates cross-border terrorism, a charge Islamabad has made repeatedly. Pakistan Observer reported that the soldiers and the militants clashed after the infiltrators tried to cross the international border in the Kurram sector; the figures are the army’s alone. Kabul’s side has not been heard in the reports DailyDrop saw, and the figures come only from the army. Kurram, in the north-west of KP and bordering Afghanistan, has long been one of the most exposed districts. The timing matters: the Centre cited deteriorating security when it imposed the emergency in the same province that day.

World · Saudi Arabia and PakistanPress ✓ 5 outlets · Houthi claims unverified

Second Houthi strike on Riyadh airport in two days; Pakistan condemns it and reaffirms commitment under the defence pact

A projectile hit Terminal 4 at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Sunday 11 October, a day after Saturday’s attack that Saudi authorities said killed 12 people and injured more than 300, according to the Saudi-led coalition as reported by FinancialJuice, and to BBC, Bloomberg and the Guardian. Saudi civil aviation reported injuries, and the coalition said air defences also intercepted a missile near the airport. The Houthis claimed responsibility and said they also struck Dammam airport and Aramco facilities in the Eastern Province; Saudi civil aviation said Dammam airport was targeted on Sunday evening. The claims about Aramco are not independently verified.

Pakistan’s Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar condemned the strike, said Pakistan stays committed to Saudi sovereignty and security under the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement, and told the embassy in Riyadh to help injured Pakistanis, Geo News and FinancialJuice reported. The Saudi-led coalition vowed retaliation (Guardian). Saudi stocks fell (Reuters) and oil chiefs withdrew from an energy congress in Riyadh (Financial Times).

World · Russia and UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · Trump’s announcement disputed by Kyiv

Trump announces an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine; Zelenskyy says it was news to him

President Donald Trump said on Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy ceasefire”, effective immediately, Reuters, the BBC and Bloomberg reported. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told Axios the declaration was news to him, then said Ukraine would agree if Russia does, and that it awaited details from Washington; Russia kept up its attacks, Reuters reported, and the Kyiv Independent said Russia struck a power plant in Kharkiv shortly after the announcement. Zelenskyy also said Ukraine would halt strikes on Russian refineries if Moscow stops attacking its energy sites.

The Financial Times reported that Washington threatened to block weapons for Ukraine over attacks on Russian refineries. Separately, a senior Republican senator said Trump’s Russian diesel deal may be illegal, Reuters reported, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said it benefits Americans. For Pakistan, which buys fuel at world prices, the question is whether more Russian diesel and fewer refinery strikes ease oil prices, which stayed near $92 on Friday.

Markets: PSX reopens today after the KP emergency; Wall Street ended Friday higher and oil near $92

Pakistan’s markets were shut on Saturday and Sunday; the next PSX session is today, Monday 12 October. The KSE-100 last closed at 167,089.27 on Friday 9 October (−0.21%) and the rupee at 277.00 in the interbank market, so the first reaction to the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa emergency and the Riyadh strikes will come at the opening bell. Saudi stocks slid after the airport attack (Reuters). Bloomberg noted that US bond traders await inflation data and the Federal Reserve’s next steps, and that the IMF–World Bank meetings in Bangkok will focus on the Middle East war and high debt, Reuters said.
