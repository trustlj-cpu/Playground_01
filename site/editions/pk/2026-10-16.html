School roof collapses in Peshawar, killing at least 33 children and a teacher; more than 50 hurt

Reports began on Thursday morning at 17 dead and rose through the day. Later counts range from 32 to 35 children plus one teacher. DailyDrop has not seen an official statement on the cause.

A school roof collapsed in Peshawar, the capital of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), and the first wire report, from Reuters, put the dead at 17 students and one teacher. Sky News used the same figure of 17 children. As Thursday went on the count climbed in steps. German and Italian outlets gave 21 and 22, the Guardian and the Straits Times 27, AP 29 and ANSA 30. The BBC said at least 33 pupils and a teacher, Sky News, CTV and ANSA said 34 children, and De Telegraaf 35, while ABC News and the Japan Times had 32 students and a teacher. ANSA’s latest headline, in the evening, said 33 children and a teacher. AP, CTV and De Telegraaf reported more than 50 people injured. These counts differ by outlet and by hour, so none of them is final.

The BBC published a video in which a “cracking” sound is heard moments before the roof comes down. No report DailyDrop saw gave a cause. The Dutch daily AD, alone among the outlets we checked, wrote that it was a private school and that its owner had been arrested; we treat that as unconfirmed. The questions that follow a collapse like this are plain: how old the building was, when it was last inspected, whether the roof had been repaired and who certified the school as fit for use. No Pakistani outlet in our feed had carried the story before our cut-off at 23:41 Pakistan time on Thursday, so there is no list of the dead, no hospital figure and no official account from Peshawar to quote. Families looking for a child should go to the hospitals and the district administration, not to forwarded lists and videos on social media. The collapse happened in a province now under governor’s rule (see the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa story).

Politics · Khyber PakhtunkhwaPress ✓ 3 outlets · the vote itself is single-source (Business Recorder)

Parliament endorses the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa emergency and governor’s rule; opposition demands it be withdrawn

Business Recorder’s Thursday edition reported that the National Assembly and the Senate have endorsed the governor’s rule and emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, the step that has suspended the province’s government led by the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI). That is the only report of the vote DailyDrop saw, so the outcome is attributed to it and we have no count. The same paper said the opposition demanded that the emergency be withdrawn and that a minister called it “unfortunate”. The Pakistan Observer reported that the Prime Minister justified the measures, and a commentary in the same paper called the emergency a “national compulsion”. The Straits Times wrote on Wednesday that the move compounds the troubles of the jailed Imran Khan. Business Recorder also reported that Punjab is ready to help any other province that needs support, and that Section 144 was extended in Dera Ismail Khan. Whether the PTI goes to court is not yet reported.

World · Yemen, Saudi Arabia and PakistanPress ✓ 6 outlets · casualty and attack counts attributed

Houthis fire missiles at the presidential palace complex in Aden; EU–Gulf summit due in Riyadh postponed

On Thursday the Houthis fired several missiles at the presidential palace complex in Aden, in government-held southern Yemen. Reuters, citing officials, said the prime minister was unharmed. The BBC quoted a minister saying two people were killed, and FinancialJuice relayed witnesses who heard four booms. FinancialJuice also quoted the Houthis as saying that the strike on a “Saudi military operations HQ” in Aden was part of a policy of hitting Saudi reinforcements. The Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis had tried to attack dozens of airports, oil facilities and ports in the kingdom, and that October has brought 20 Houthi attacks on Riyadh’s airport with 17 deaths (FinancialJuice). Saudi civil defence issued an early warning for Najran and later said the danger had passed. AFP Fact Check found that a video of a Saudi airliner exploding is AI-generated.

Diplomacy slowed. The European Union said the second summit with the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), planned for 24 October in Riyadh, is postponed and a new date will follow in due course (Reuters, FinancialJuice). France said it is starting to put into action parts of an agreement to help protect Saudi oil facilities. Tasnim, citing AFP, said Yemen rejected a Saudi request for talks in Oman; that is second-hand and single-source. Pakistan’s link is its defence pact with Riyadh and its workers in the kingdom. The Saudi Gazette reported that the Saudi ambassador, Al-Baqami, presented his credentials to the President, and Geo that Pakistan and Oman signed six agreements. DailyDrop saw no Pakistani statement of any military step.

Economy · IMF–World Bank meetingsPress ✓ 3 outlets · Pakistan items single-source (Business Recorder)

Aurangzeb asks lenders to match terms to Pakistan’s better ratings; IMF sees the Middle East and Pakistan region shrinking in 2026

Business Recorder reported that Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb urged international financial institutions (IFIs) to align their financing terms with Pakistan’s improved ratings, and that he spoke of fiscal prudence. The same paper said the Prime Minister ordered banks to expand lending to farmers, small businesses and homeowners, and that export industries oppose a proposed rise in electricity tariffs for the distribution companies, known as Discos. It also reported that no fresh approval is needed for the privatisation of the Discos. No other outlet we checked carried these items, so they are attributed, not confirmed.

The wider backdrop came from the IMF’s annual meetings. FinancialJuice relayed the Fund’s forecast that the Middle East, North Africa and Pakistan region will shrink 1.3 per cent in 2026, after expanding 3.3 per cent in 2025, with a recovery by 2027. The Straits Times and CNA reported the Fund saying a prolonged war and renewed disruption are the biggest risks to the Middle East, and Reuters separately reported the Fund warning of slower Asian growth and spillover from rising yields. Business Recorder said the IMF urged low-income and vulnerable states to stock up on funds. CNBC, via FinancialJuice, said the US Treasury dismissed the IMF’s debt warning. DailyDrop saw no Pakistan-only figure in these reports.

Markets: Pakistan’s Thursday close not confirmed; tech rout deepens in Asia and on Wall Street; WTI settles at $97.97

DailyDrop could not confirm Thursday’s KSE-100 close or the interbank dollar rate in two independent sources, since no Pakistani market report reached our feed in time, so both ears show a dash. The regional mood was set by technology. Reuters said Asian markets sold off as AI trades crumbled after the hot US inflation figures, and CNBC’s live blog had South Korea’s Kospi down 7 per cent. Reuters also reported that US chip stocks were set to lose $2 trillion in value, with debt worries at CoreWeave in the middle of it (Reuters, Bloomberg, FinancialJuice). On the last completed US session, Wednesday 14 October, CBS said the Dow closed in the red, the Straits Times said the Nasdaq ended sharply lower, and Bloomberg said the S&P 500 ended its longest spell of calm since 2020. DailyDrop did not see closing levels in two outlets, so it prints none.

Thursday’s US session was still open at our cut-off. MarketWatch said the S&P 500 and Nasdaq opened lower while the Dow rose as Treasury yields fell, and Reuters reported a network disruption at Nasdaq affecting US equities data feeds. The data were hot: the producer price index (PPI) posted its largest rise in four and a half years in September (Reuters), which FinancialJuice put at 1.3 per cent on the month and 6.3 per cent on the year, and retail sales beat forecasts (Reuters). Reuters quoted the Fed’s Daly saying rate hikes are likely needed but not urgently, and said the dollar retreated from an 18-month high. The International Energy Agency (IEA) agreed to speed up its stock release so that the total tops 400 million barrels, including over 20 million of diesel (Reuters, Bloomberg, FinancialJuice). FinancialJuice reported WTI for November settling at $97.97, up $2.88, on Thursday, after $95.09 on Wednesday, when Brent settled at $106.06. For Pakistan the chain runs from oil to petrol to prices; the rupee is the level we could not confirm.
