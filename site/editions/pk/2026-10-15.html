Sindh’s chief minister meets Hubei’s leaders as Pakistan and China mark 75 years of ties; talks cover farming, IT and trade

Syed Murad Ali Shah and the top leadership of China’s Hubei province discussed wider provincial cooperation on Wednesday. The reports DailyDrop reviewed name areas of interest, but no figure, no project and no signed agreement.

Syed Murad Ali Shah, Sindh’s chief minister, met the top leadership of China’s Hubei province on Wednesday 14 October, Business Recorder and the Pakistan Observer reported. Business Recorder said the meeting marked 75 years of diplomatic ties between the two countries and was meant to widen cooperation between the provinces in agriculture, information technology, education and vocational training. The Observer’s headline lists technology, agriculture and trade. The two papers agree, then, on the shape of the talks, with education and training appearing only in the first and trade only in the second. The reports DailyDrop reviewed go no further than that: no investment amount, no named project, no date for a follow-up.

Hubei lies in central China, and its capital, Wuhan, is a large industrial and university city. Sindh has Pakistan’s biggest city, major seaports and a large farm economy, so on paper the agenda fits. It is also a provincial agenda. Since the 18th Amendment of 2010, agriculture and education have been largely provincial subjects, which lets a chief minister negotiate on them directly, although larger foreign-funded projects still tend to need federal approval. On the same day Business Recorder reported, from a single source, that a Pakistani firm had signed an MoU with a Chinese company to set up a joint venture making electric buses in Pakistan, at an event in Islamabad. An MoU states an intention and commits no money. The meeting was one of several foreign-facing events that day: Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar met Russia’s interior minister in Islamabad, and President Asif Ali Zardari urged lifting trade with Türkiye to an agreed $5 billion target (both Business Recorder). Each report offers a target or a warm word and none a delivered result, which is normal at the start of a relationship and the reason this story deserves a follow-up rather than a verdict. The test for the Hubei talks is whether any of the four areas becomes a named programme with a budget, a partner institution and a timetable, and whether the Sindh government publishes it.

Politics · Khyber PakhtunkhwaPress ✓ 2 outlets · vote per Business Recorder alone; the Observer carried the government’s case

Parliament endorses the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa emergency and governor’s rule amid opposition protests; Section 144 extended in Dera Ismail Khan

The National Assembly and the Senate each adopted a resolution on Wednesday approving the proclamation of emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa that the President issued on 11 October, while opposition members protested, Business Recorder reported. DailyDrop found the vote in that paper alone; an earlier Business Recorder item expected the two houses to act separately on Wednesday. The government’s case had been made a day earlier. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif defended the emergency and governor’s rule on Tuesday, accusing the PTI-led provincial government of failing to act decisively against terrorism and warning that political agitation threatened the economy (Business Recorder); the Pakistan Observer said he took the allies of the ruling PML-N into confidence. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi told the Senate that no provision of the Constitution or the law had been violated (Business Recorder). An Observer editorial called the measures a national compulsion, which is opinion rather than reporting. In Dera Ismail Khan, the deputy commissioner extended Section 144 from 13 October to 11 November, banning public gatherings and pillion riding among other curbs (Business Recorder). The reports DailyDrop reviewed do not describe what the emergency changes on the ground, and the opposition’s arguments are not set out in them.

World · Saudi Arabia and the GulfPress ✓ 6 outlets · damage accounts differ; Houthi and Revolutionary Guards claims attributed

Houthi strikes leave Aramco’s headquarters badly damaged, sources say; a container ship sinks near Hormuz as Iran widens attacks on shipping

Saudi Aramco’s headquarters at Dhahran suffered extensive damage in the Houthi attack, sources told Reuters on Wednesday 14 October, and the group vowed more strikes on Saudi airports and oil facilities. Bloomberg said the Houthis hit several Saudi cities with missiles and drones, and AP quoted Riyadh residents who heard new explosions. FinancialJuice relayed the Saudi-led coalition’s statement that it had intercepted a ballistic missile fired towards the capital, and two sources saying Aramco had told staff to work from home while operations went on normally. That is a harsher picture than the first night’s Saudi description of a limited fire. Business Recorder, carrying a wire report, said the coalition had struck oil sites across western Yemen, Sanaa included, on Tuesday in response to what the report called the deadliest attack on Saudi soil in years.

At sea, Reuters reported that a container ship sank near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile, and the Guardian said a second vessel was reportedly sinking amid what it described as a sharp uptick in Iranian attacks on shipping. Bloomberg, citing Singapore’s maritime authority, said a strike set a cargo ship on fire and its crew were evacuated. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said through state media that the ship had hit a naval mine, a claim the other accounts do not support. Reuters quoted officials saying Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks on shipping before the US vote. Saudi Arabia is a major employer of Pakistani workers and a leading source of remittances; the items DailyDrop reviewed carried no new report of Pakistani casualties.

Economy · Fuel pricesPress ✓ 4 outlets · Pakistan price per Business Recorder alone

Petrol rises Rs1.20 to Rs393.86 a litre as freight costs climb; the IEA warns diesel shortages could last into 2027

Petrol went up by Rs1.20 to Rs393.86 a litre, from Rs392.66, in the latest price change, Business Recorder reported, while high-speed diesel fell by 17 paise. The paper said the increase came even though global oil prices had been falling, and traced it to the oil premium and freight charges, which rose from $16.12 to $17.94 a barrel. Freight is a problem beyond Pakistan. Bloomberg wrote that soaring oil freight is becoming a risk to fuel supply, and FinancialJuice relayed an official saying Washington is discussing easing some sanctions on shadow fleet vessels to bring freight rates down.

Crude itself ticked up on Wednesday. Brent settled at $106.06, up 37 cents, and US crude at $95.09, up 95 cents (FinancialJuice), and Reuters and Bloomberg also reported gains as Middle East and Russia risks weighed on supply. On diesel, the IEA said shortages could persist well into 2027 (Reuters) and that Russia’s easier diesel export curbs were unlikely to change the global balance much (FinancialJuice). Italy’s Prime Minister Meloni called the US–Russia diesel deal a “very risky choice”, and Reuters reported more Republicans balking at it. Russia’s deputy prime minister, Novak, said the first export shipments were under way (FinancialJuice, citing TASS).

Markets: Pakistan’s Wednesday close not confirmed; US inflation hits 3.7%, Fed hike bets build and Wall Street slides

DailyDrop could not confirm Wednesday’s KSE-100 close or the dollar–rupee rate in two independent sources, so both ears show a dash. The day’s main market event came from Washington. US consumer prices rose 0.6 per cent in September and 3.7 per cent over the year, against a forecast of 3.6 per cent, with gasoline up 9.1 per cent on the month (Bloomberg, FinancialJuice). The core inflation rate, which leaves out food and energy, was 2.6 per cent on the year (FinancialJuice). Treasuries fell, traders added to bets that the Federal Reserve will raise rates this month and again later, and Bloomberg said the dollar rose to its highest since May 2025. Fed speakers sounded cautious about speed: Daly told Reuters more hikes are likely but not urgent, Bowman said there was time to wait and see, and Goolsbee called the data discouraging (FinancialJuice, quoting Yahoo).

Stocks fell. Bloomberg said the S&P 500’s longest spell of calm since 2020 ended as the AI trade sank, the Financial Times reported chip stocks sliding on inflation and AI-debt fears, and Bank of America dropped after its $1.2 billion fine. The Russell 2000 index of smaller companies fell into a correction, 11 per cent below its peak (Bloomberg). Closing levels were not confirmed in two outlets, so DailyDrop prints none. Gold held a loss as hot inflation lifted rate-hike bets (Bloomberg). A stronger dollar and dearer US borrowing generally press on import-dependent economies such as Pakistan.
