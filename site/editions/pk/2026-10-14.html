Saudi-led coalition strikes Houthi oil depots in Yemen; Aramco headquarters hit in Dhahran, Saudi Arabia says the fire was limited

After a week of deadly attacks on Riyadh’s airport, coalition air strikes hit fuel sites around Sanaa, and a Houthi missile later struck Aramco’s headquarters in the Eastern Province. Brent eased to $105.69; Pakistan, tied to Riyadh by a defence pact, is being asked how far it would go.

The Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen’s Houthi movement struck oil sites across western Yemen on Tuesday 13 October, including in the capital Sanaa, Business Recorder reported, describing it as a response to the deadliest attack on Saudi soil in years. Earlier in the day the coalition had told civilians to leave the Yemen Oil Company’s Sabah central station, a fuel depot on the outskirts of Sanaa, ahead of a planned operation (Straits Times); the BBC and the Guardian then reported strikes on fuel and oil depots near the capital. The Houthis warned that they would hit Riyadh and Dammam (BBC) and told staff to leave all Aramco sites (Mint). Late on Tuesday, six sources told Reuters, through the Straits Times, that loud booms were heard near Aramco’s headquarters in Dhahran, which had been evacuated, and Bloomberg reported that a Houthi missile hit the complex and started a fire. Saudi Arabia’s account was milder. It said the attack on an Aramco building in the east caused a “limited fire”, and that debris from intercepted missiles wounded four people in Riyadh (New Straits Times, FinancialJuice); Saudi state media, as relayed by FinancialJuice, reported no casualties at the Aramco site. The coalition said it destroyed four cruise missiles and three drones over Riyadh and the south. One Malaysian outlet, FMT, wrote that the top three floors of an office tower were damaged; DailyDrop has no second source for that.

Oil barely moved. Brent settled at $105.69 a barrel, down 96 cents, and US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) at $94.14, up 40 cents, according to FinancialJuice; Bloomberg Television described crude as easing from a three-day gain while traders watched the Middle East and Russian diesel. President Donald Trump called the Houthis “behaving very badly” and, in Reuters, Al Jazeera and Financial Times accounts, said they were trying to push up oil prices before the US midterm elections. CBS read his “I will never forget” remark as a sign that he would hold off action until after the vote; DailyDrop saw no White House confirmation of that reading. For Pakistan the question is narrower and harder. Islamabad’s defence pact with Riyadh, and the large Pakistani workforce in the kingdom, turn this war into a home matter, and on Tuesday the Financial Times, CNA and the South China Morning Post (SCMP) each weighed Pakistan’s role. The SCMP added that Chinese-made jets and an air-defence system had reportedly been deployed to Saudi Arabia, which DailyDrop could not confirm. Oman’s foreign minister spent Tuesday and Wednesday in Islamabad (Business Recorder, Geo). Singapore told its citizens to defer all travel to the kingdom (CNA, Straits Times), and India asked its nationals to stay calm and vigilant after an Indian citizen was killed in the airport attack (Mint, The Hindu). Riyadh airport stays shut until Saturday (Business Recorder), and four Indian airlines have cancelled Riyadh flights until 17 October (Hindustan Times).

Politics · Khyber PakhtunkhwaPress ✓ 6 outlets · endorsement vote reported from sources; clash account is the party’s

Parliament expected to endorse the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa emergency today as the Prime Minister defends it; party says police fired on supporters

President Asif Ali Zardari called the National Assembly and Senate into session on Tuesday over the emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), and Business Recorder reported that both houses are likely to adopt separate resolutions endorsing them today, according to unnamed sources. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif defended the steps, accusing the provincial government of the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) of failing to act decisively against terrorism and warning that agitation threatened the economy. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi told the Senate that no provision of the Constitution or the law had been violated. A day earlier the Prime Minister named a committee under Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar to oversee the Centre’s handling of KP affairs (Business Recorder). On the ground, Reuters, carried by the Straits Times, quoted party officials saying police fired tear gas and live rounds at supporters of the jailed Imran Khan in the north-west on Tuesday; DailyDrop found no official account of that and no casualty figure. The BBC reported that the government has locked down Mr Khan’s stronghold, and some fear the standoff could bring more instability, not less. Peshawar’s schools were due to reopen on Tuesday after a one-day closure, the district administration told Geo. DW asked how the move will affect relations with Kabul, where the Taliban authorities separately suspended BBC radio broadcasts nationwide (Reuters, BBC).

At least five killed and 41 hurt as the Rehman Baba Express derails near Multan

The Peshawar-bound Rehman Baba Express, a passenger train from Karachi, derailed near Sher Shah railway station in Multan district, southern Punjab, early on Tuesday. Business Recorder, whose Wednesday report carries the latest figures, put the toll at at least five dead and 41 injured. Earlier reports gave four dead, with AFP (via IOL) saying more than a dozen were hurt, and the Straits Times and Mint agreeing on four, while the Times of India already said five; Brazil’s G1, citing police and a railways spokesman, counted about 30 injured. Railways Minister Hanif Abbasi confirmed four deaths, Business Recorder said, so the fifth death is not yet officially stated in the reports we saw. Mint reported that all 14 coaches left the track.

Rescue work and an inquiry began, and Pakistan Railways arranged alternative transport and refunds for passengers (Mint). The cause was unknown on Tuesday (Straits Times). Business Recorder wrote that the crash raised fresh concerns over railway safety and maintenance. DailyDrop saw no names of the dead, no speed for the train and no word on the condition of the track; those facts will decide whether this is judged a maintenance failure. Anyone travelling on the Karachi–Peshawar line should check with Pakistan Railways before setting out.

Economy · IMF–World Bank meetingsPress ✓ 4 outlets · Pakistan figures single-source (Business Recorder)

IMF projects Pakistan’s growth at 3.5 per cent for 2026-27, below the government’s 4 per cent target

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) projected Pakistan’s growth at 3.5 per cent for fiscal year 2026-27, below the government’s 4 per cent target and just above its global figure of 3.4 per cent, with India at 7 per cent, Business Recorder reported; its headline adds inflation of 9.9 per cent, though the text we saw did not make the period clear. Only Business Recorder carried the Pakistan numbers, so they are attributed, not confirmed. Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb, for his part, told Bloomberg Television and The Nation that Pakistan is aiming for growth of more than 4 per cent this fiscal year, and in Bangkok he asked the Group of Twenty-Four (G-24) for stronger World Bank and IMF support for developing countries as trade barriers and climate change add to their burdens (Business Recorder).

The wider picture came from Reuters and the Fund’s own briefings in Bangkok: the IMF flagged risks from a correction in AI-linked share prices, from war and from debt, and said the AI boom bolstered global trade, to the benefit of China and India. Spiking bond yields are among the market risks it listed. At home the clearest signal is at the pump. The government cut petrol by Rs2.71 to Rs392.66 a litre for 14 October and diesel by 3 paise, Business Recorder said, citing the official notification, after Monday’s cut of Rs2.93. A parliamentary panel asked why cuts were so small when stocks were sufficient (Business Recorder). Textile exporters, meanwhile, want temporary DLTL support of 6 to 8 per cent tied to a 10 per cent rise in exports (Business Recorder).
