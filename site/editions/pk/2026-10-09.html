PTI redraws the march: Peshawar on Thursday night, then Charsadda, Mardan and Swabi, with a “final plan” promised on 11 October

New since Wednesday: the Khairabad gathering is no longer the decision point. Afridi’s revised schedule stretches the march to Swabi on Sunday, and the announcement moves with it.

PTI has announced a revised schedule for its long march, ARY News, Minute Mirror and The Pakistan Connect reported. The convoy is due to reach Peshawar from Khyber on Thursday night, 8 October, with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi expected to address gatherings in the city. It moves to Charsadda on Friday 9 October, where participants will spend the night, then to Mardan on Saturday 10 October and Swabi on Sunday 11 October. Afridi has said the party will unveil its “final plan” after Swabi, and that it will put pressure on its opponents.

The change replaces the earlier plan, under which all caravans were to gather at Khairabad on 8 October and Afridi was to announce his next course of action there. Dawn’s headline on the fifth day said the convoy had entered Peshawar. The Pakistan Connect carried a journalist’s claim that about 3,000 workers had entered the city; DailyDrop could not confirm the figure. The government line is unchanged: no march on Islamabad.

Economy · ReservesPress ✓ 3 outlets · figures from the State Bank of Pakistan weekly release

State Bank reserves edge up $15 million to $21.45 billion; total liquid reserves near $26.8 billion

The State Bank of Pakistan’s own foreign-exchange reserves rose by about $15 million in the week ended 2 October to roughly $21.45 billion, pkrevenue.com and Mettis reported on the basis of the central bank’s weekly release published on Thursday 8 October. Mettis put the gain at $14.5 million, or 0.07%. Total liquid reserves, including commercial banks’ holdings of about $5.34 billion, were around $26.80 billion, according to pkrevenue.com.

The State Bank’s holdings were $21.439 billion a week earlier, ProPakistani reported, and Mettis said they are up about $2.98 billion, or 16%, in the current fiscal year. The cushion matters because oil imports are costing more with the Strait of Hormuz shut and the IMF programme sets reserve targets.

Anne Carson wins the Nobel prize in literature for an “inventive oeuvre” in dialogue with the classics

The Swedish Academy announced on Thursday 8 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature goes to the Canadian poet, essayist and translator Anne Carson, for a “bold and inventive oeuvre” that creates new forms for contemporary literature in dialogue with the classical tradition, according to nobelprize.org, blue News, Il Foglio and charter97. Carson teaches Ancient Greek.

She is best known for the 1998 verse novel Autobiography of Red, which retells a Greek myth in a modern setting. The prize is 12 million Swedish kronor, a million more than in recent years. Last year’s laureate was the Hungarian novelist László Krasznahorkai. The ceremony is on 10 December.

Markets · KarachiSingle outlet for the index: ARY News · rupee per Business Recorder and Daily Times

KSE-100 falls 1,138 points to 167,441 on Thursday, giving back Tuesday’s rebound

The KSE-100 index closed on Thursday 8 October at 167,441.91, down 1,138.50 points or 0.68% from Wednesday’s 168,580.41, ARY News reported. That takes it below Friday 2 October’s 168,155.49 and about 2,500 points under the end-of-September level of 169,969.32. DailyDrop has seen no second outlet’s report on the session or an explanation of the fall.

In the interbank market the rupee closed at 277.01 to the dollar, a gain of one paisa on Wednesday’s 277.02, according to the previous-day reference in Daily Times’ APP report on Friday and Business Recorder’s closing table. Traders are likely to be watching the new march schedule and the oil price.

Markets · Global backdropClosing data · cause of the oil move not confirmed

WTI jumps 3.6% to $91.50 on Thursday as Wall Street slips 0.47%; no confirmed trigger

U.S. crude, WTI, rose 3.60% to $91.50 a barrel on Thursday 8 October, while the S&P 500 fell 0.47% to 7,765.36 and gold rose 0.20% to $4,157.0 an ounce. DailyDrop did not find a report that explains the jump in crude, so the cause is not confirmed.

A rebound of this size would feed into Pakistan’s fuel-price expectations if it lasts, since petrol and diesel prices follow the international market and the rupee, which is steady near 277. The Strait of Hormuz remains closed.
