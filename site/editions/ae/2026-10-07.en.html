Hormuz between two accounts: Iran says it is closed, US Central Command says oil is passing, and seven ships crossed Tuesday

Tehran said on October 4 that the strait is closed until its conditions are met, and Washington replied that 20 million barrels had crossed. Reuters counted only seven commodity ships on October 6, the lowest figure in more than two months.

Iran announced on October 4 that the Strait of Hormuz would stay closed until its conditions are met, according to Al Jazeera and Fox News. In response, US Central Command said the claims of a closure were false and that about 20 million barrels of crude oil had crossed the strait, according to Iran Wire and Open Magazine. Iran International reported on October 5 that a third tanker had been hit and that Tehran sees the talks as meaningless; this is a single-source account.

Gulf News reported that Reuters counted only seven commodity ships crossing the strait on Tuesday, October 6, the lowest figure in more than two months. The G7 had decided on October 2 to release up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel stocks over four months. Brent closed Tuesday at $100.58. According to Gulf News, UAE authorities say life is proceeding normally, with travel and shipping affected. In DailyDrop’s reading, both accounts may be true at once: some oil passing does not mean traffic has returned to normal.

Dubai Financial Market nearly flat at 5,907.2 points, Abu Dhabi market falls 0.17%, and Abu Dhabi Aviation jumps 14.84%

The Dubai Financial Market ended Tuesday’s session down about 0.016% at 5,907.2 points, according to Al Khaleej, and the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange index fell 0.17% to 9,993.24 points. The Dubai index had been up 0.009% at 5,908.67 in mid-session.

Small stocks stood out: Drake & Scull rose 4.9% to 0.298 dirhams on liquidity of about 50 million dirhams, and Abu Dhabi Aviation jumped 14.84% to 6.19 dirhams, close to the daily price limit. World · SciencePress ✓ 5 outlets

Nobel in Physics to Francis Halzen for detecting the universe’s neutrinos in South Pole ice

On Tuesday, October 6, Belgian physicist Francis Halzen was announced as the winner of the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for his study of high-energy neutrinos, according to Rai News, La Repubblica, La Stampa, Il Sole 24 Ore and the Dutch NRC. He is behind the IceCube project, the observatory buried in the South Pole ice, which detected the first neutrino of cosmic origin.

A neutrino is a very light particle that passes through matter almost without a trace, and detecting it makes it possible to study cosmic events that light does not reveal, such as active black holes. Il Sole 24 Ore reported that Halzen was on a call from Bergamo at the moment of the announcement.

Oil · EnergyNow ✓ 5 outlets · was a single-source report

G7 decides to release up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel, with a focus on diesel

The G7 agreed on October 2 to release up to 100 million barrels of strategic oil and diesel stocks over four months, in coordination with the International Energy Agency, according to Al Jazeera, Euronews, Meduza, Nippon.com and Wealth Professional. According to the statement, a large release of diesel is expected in the first 20 days, and members agreed not to restrict energy exports among themselves.

The decision came after Washington threatened to ban diesel exports unless European countries drew on their emergency stocks, and it adds to a coordinated release in March, about two-thirds of which has been used, according to the agency’s director Fatih Birol. A former agency official said it does not address the underlying problem, which is that global supply is below normal. Brent closed on Tuesday, October 6, at $100.58, and TheStreet had broken the news in an earlier report.
