Gulf · ShippingPress ✓ 6 outlets · attacker not identified

Tanker hit by multiple projectiles off Qatar with casualties, and Brent returns above $100

UK Maritime Trade Operations announced the incident on Wednesday evening, the first attack on a tanker deep in the Gulf in about a month. Neither the attacker nor the vessel’s nationality has been identified.

UK Maritime Trade Operations said a tanker was hit by multiple projectiles about 51 nautical miles north of Madinat ash-Shamal in Qatar, at around 19:00 GMT on Wednesday, October 7, with casualties recorded, according to Al Jazeera, the BBC, The Guardian and Infobae. The attacker and the tanker’s flag were not identified. Reports described the incident as the first attack on a tanker deep in the Gulf in about a month.

Spain’s EFE agency reported that at least eight tankers have been attacked in recent days, most of them on the Omani side of the strait, and Bloomberg, via Perfil, relayed that Iran had stepped up its attacks on tankers as crude flows recovered. Brent jumped above $100 to about $101.5 in early trading. In DailyDrop’s reading, the attack’s proximity to Qatari waters near the UAE raises sensitivity for shipping and insurance, but we do not attribute the attack to anyone without confirmation.

UAE · Foreign policyPress ✓ 4 outlets · official statement

UAE strongly condemns the Houthis’ continued targeting of civilian sites and Saudi ships

The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs on Wednesday, October 7, condemned the Houthi group’s continued targeting of civilians and of civilian and economic facilities in Saudi Arabia, and of Saudi ships in the southern Red Sea and Bab al-Mandab, according to Khaleej Times, Gulf News, Al Khaleej and Asharq Al-Awsat. The ministry called it a flagrant violation of the Kingdom’s sovereignty and a threat to its security and stability.

The UAE renewed its full solidarity with Saudi Arabia and its support for any measures it takes to protect its security. The UAE had issued similar statements, including one on September 8 on attacks on Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan and Najran. The statement did not include any practical UAE action.

Dubai Municipality designates more than 44 areas for shared housing and gives owners a year to comply

Dubai Municipality on Wednesday, October 7, issued its planning and building requirements guide for shared housing, implementing Law No. 4 of 2026, and designated more than 44 areas where shared housing is permitted in buildings and villas, according to Gulf News, Khaleej Times, Gulf Today and Al Khaleej. Examples include Al Souq Al Kabeer, Ras, Al Warqa 1, Al Barsha 1, Al Muraqqabat and Al Rigga; it is permitted only in specified parts of Abu Hail, Al Buraha, Al Hamriya, Mankhool, Al Jafiliya, Al Hudaiba and Al Satwa.

Shared housing is classified in two categories, for individuals and for families, and an existing building may not combine the two. A minimum of five square meters per person is required in bedrooms, and a separate bedroom with a bathroom for each family. The law gave owners and management companies one year to bring existing buildings into compliance, starting from September 8, the date it took effect, and this does not exempt them from building violations. The rules exclude collective workers’ housing.

UAE · Digital governmentPress ✓ 5 outlets · survey figures

UAE ranks first globally in customer satisfaction with digital government services, at 89%

The UAE ranked first globally in customer satisfaction with digital government services, at 89%, according to the Boston Consulting Group’s 2026 report, which covered 48,500 participants in 44 countries, per WAM, Gulf News, Emirates 24/7, The National and Zawya. The UAE also came first in use of these services, at 53%.

The report was launched on the first day of the AI Everything exhibition in Abu Dhabi on October 6. The findings said the Gulf is the only region where a majority of citizens, 72%, see digital government services as better than private-sector services. Al Khaleej said Mohammed bin Rashid announced that a major change in how government services are delivered will be announced in November.

Markets · United StatesPress ✓ 5 sources · closing figures

Fed meeting minutes: most officials see another rate hike, and Treasury yield reaches its highest level since 2002

The minutes of the Federal Reserve’s September meeting, published on October 7, showed that most participants see another rate hike as likely this year, and that the decision to raise rates to 3.75 – 4.00% on September 16 was taken unanimously by 12 votes, according to Babypips, Scotsman Guide, Kitco and The Star. Twelve officials projected one additional hike, four two hikes and two none.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,801.77, down 0.22%, West Texas Intermediate at $88.28 (−1.30%) and gold at 4,140.7 (−1.11%). The ten-year Treasury yield reached as high as 5.36% during trading, according to two agencies, the highest since 2002. Because the dirham is pegged to the dollar, US interest rates feed through to loans in the UAE.
