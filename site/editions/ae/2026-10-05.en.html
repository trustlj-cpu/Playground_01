UAE Attorney General: flydubai co-pilot set out to carry out a terrorist operation; investigation continues

Hamad Saif Al Shamsi said the co-pilot attacked the captain with an emergency ax and tried to take control of the flight controls of flight FZ1073 on September 30. The motives have not yet been announced, and no party’s responsibility has been established.

The UAE’s Attorney General, Hamad Saif Al Shamsi, announced, according to the Emirates News Agency (WAM) and as relayed by Khaleej Times, the Asharq channel and Al Jazeera Net, that the investigation into the incident on flydubai flight FZ1073 showed that the co-pilot set out to carry out a terrorist operation. He said the co-pilot attacked the captain inside the cockpit with the emergency ax and tried to take control of the flight controls. The aircraft had taken off from Dubai on Wednesday, September 30, bound for Israel, with 174 passengers and eight crew members on board.

Passengers and crew overpowered the attacker, and a relief pilot who was on board took over flying the aircraft, which landed at Tabuk in Saudi Arabia. The Saudi Interior Ministry said on October 1 that the preliminary investigation showed the co-pilot had attacked the captain. Reuters, citing sources, reported that the co-pilot is an Omani national named Hammam al-Hamami (29), and the UAE authorities have not confirmed the name. The Indian captain, Sumit Machhar, was seriously injured and underwent surgery, according to Indian reports.

The motive has not been announced so far. Media outlets reported, citing sources, that the suspect was influenced by extremist ideas and that the Sultanate of Oman had previously barred him from flying; these are accounts the authorities have not confirmed. Benjamin Netanyahu said the party that sent him would pay a “heavy price,” and President Trump said he believes Iran is involved; neither presented any evidence publicly. Royal Air Maroc denied, according to Euronews, that he had worked for it. In Australia, police said on Sunday that a counterterrorism team is examining his ties to the country, according to Australian reports.

Khaleej Times reported that flydubai declined to comment further until the investigation is complete, and one report said it temporarily suspended its flights to Tel Aviv at the authorities’ request. In DailyDrop’s reading, the case puts cockpit security and pilot screening front and center at a global aviation hub like Dubai, but judging the motives is premature. Today we follow the final results of the investigation and any statement on screening procedures.

Ghalibaf: Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until Iran’s seven conditions are met, and Trump says a decision is close

Iran’s parliament speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said on Sunday that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the seven conditions set out in the Islamabad memorandum are carried out, according to Pajhwok, Kurdistan 24, Al Jazeera and Seoul Economic Daily. Iran’s seven-day plan proposes opening the strait if Washington lifts the naval blockade, releases the frozen funds, exempts Iranian oil from sanctions and halts fighting on all fronts.

Trump had rejected the plan on September 26, and on Saturday said Iran has been “exhausted” and that the matter is “the easy way or the hard way,” according to reports. The UAE has a direct stake in the file: it exports part of its oil through the Habshan-Fujairah pipeline outside the strait, but shipping, insurance and oil prices are all affected by its closure. In DailyDrop’s reading, the public hard line may be a negotiating stance, and we have no evidence of that.

OPEC+ keeps November production targets unchanged, and the UAE has been outside the alliance since May

The seven core OPEC+ countries agreed in an online meeting on Sunday to keep production targets for November unchanged, according to Reuters, CNBC and The National. The next meeting is on November 1. Brent closed Friday near $102.25, according to The National. The UAE was not party to the meeting, as it left OPEC and OPEC+ effective May 1 after announcing the decision on April 28.

The International Energy Agency estimated that UAE output rose from 3.3 million barrels a day in May to 4.1 million in June, according to Hellenic Shipping News, and that exports reached a record near 3.7 million barrels a day in June, according to ship-tracking data from Kpler and Vortexa. Estimates say output could exceed 5 million barrels in 2027. All of these are estimated figures from months ago, and we found no official data for September.

UAE fuel prices for October: Super 98 at 4.40 dirhams a liter, up about 15.8%

The Fuel Price Committee announced October prices on Wednesday, September 30: Super 98 at 4.40 dirhams a liter, against 3.80 in September, Special 95 at 4.28, E-Plus 91 at 4.21 and diesel at 4.80, according to Gulf News, Khaleej Times and Emirates 24/7. Gulf News said the increase in Super 98 is about 15.8%.

Khaleej Times linked the increase to the end of the 60-day truce between the United States and Iran and to a Houthi escalation against oil facilities in the Red Sea. Prices are set monthly in line with global markets. This is the first full working week at October prices.

Yemen · Saudi ArabiaPress ✓ 5 outlets · battlefield claims attributed to those making them

Chairman of Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council announces operations to retake Houthi areas, and the Houthis claim to have targeted Aramco facilities

Rashad al-Alimi, chairman of Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council, announced on Sunday, October 4, the start of military operations to retake the areas under Houthi control, according to Defense News, CNN, NBC and The Jerusalem Post. The Houthis have controlled Sanaa and most of the northwest since 2014, and last month, according to reports, they had seized Bab al-Mandab and about 90 miles of coast. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said coalition aircraft carried out about 50 strikes in 12 hours (Xinhua), a figure for which we found no independent confirmation.

The Houthis said they targeted two Aramco facilities in Riyadh and Khurais with missiles and drones and that fires broke out in them (Reuters via Dawn, Xinhua and Kurdistan 24). Neither Aramco nor the Saudi authorities confirmed any hit or effect on production, and reports said coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki described the claims as misleading. The UAE has a direct interest in the security of the Red Sea and the Gulf, but we have not seen an official UAE statement on Sunday’s announcement.
