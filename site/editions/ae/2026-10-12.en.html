Saudi Arabia · Aviation · SecurityPress ✓ 6 outlets · Toll from Saudi authorities

Second strike on King Khalid airport in two days, Dammam airport attacked on Sunday evening, and the Houthis claim responsibility

The Saudi-led coalition said an unidentified object hit a passenger terminal at Riyadh airport and started a fire, a day after a strike that killed 12 people. Riyadh vows a major response, and flydubai has cancelled its flights to Riyadh until October 14.

King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh was hit by a second strike in two days on Sunday, October 11, after Saturday’s strike that Saudi authorities said killed 12 people and injured more than 300. Houthi attacks on the Riyadh and Abha airports on October 8 killed three and injured dozens, according to Gulf News, which makes this the third wave of attacks on Saudi airports in four days. Saudi Arabia extended the airport’s closure after the first strike, according to the Associated Press. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defenses intercepted a missile near the airport and that an unidentified object hit a passenger terminal and caused a fire, according to the BBC and FinancialJuice. On Sunday evening the Saudi Civil Aviation Authority said Dammam airport in the Eastern Province had been targeted in an attack and that damage assessment was under way, according to Sky News Arabia and FinancialJuice. The coalition had described Saturday’s strike as a terrorist attack amounting to a war crime, UN Secretary-General António Guterres condemned it, and US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Washington stands with Saudi Arabia, according to FinancialJuice and Reuters. The UAE condemned the attacks on King Khalid airport, according to Sky News Arabia.

The Houthis claimed responsibility for the attacks on Riyadh airport and for targeting Dammam airport and two Aramco facilities, according to Sky News Arabia and FinancialJuice, and we found no Saudi confirmation that Aramco facilities were hit. The Houthis renewed their warning to airlines and travelers against using Saudi airports and said Riyadh must bear responsibility, according to Reuters and FinancialJuice. Saudi Arabia said it would respond forcefully, according to the Washington Post, and the Financial Times reported that oil company chiefs pulled out of the World Energy Congress in Riyadh after the first strike; Saudi shares fell, according to Reuters. flydubai cancelled all its flights to Riyadh until October 14, according to Emarat Al Youm, and Emirates, Etihad and Air Arabia announced changes or cancellations, according to Gulf News. Sky News Arabia said Saudi Arabia carried out raids on the Houthis in four Yemeni cities, and a Houthi-affiliated channel reported a strike on Sanaa airport, according to Reuters; we found no independent confirmation of the second strike. Bloomberg said the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia raise the risk of the war in the Middle East widening, which weighs on oil prices and investor confidence in the Gulf. In DailyDrop’s reading, the threat has moved from one airport to several, which matters to travelers between the UAE and Saudi Arabia, but the shape of the Saudi response has not yet been announced. What is known so far is the toll of Saturday’s strike and Sunday’s attack on Dammam; the extent of the damage in Dammam, whether Aramco facilities were actually hit, and when Riyadh airport will resume operations have not been announced.

Gulf · ShippingPress ✓ 4 sources · Accounts differ

Incidents involving three tankers near the UAE and Oman: 22 sailors rescued off Ras Al Khaimah and two missing near Sohar

The Indian consulate in Dubai said 22 Indian sailors were all rescued from the oil tanker Gem No. 2 off the coast of Ras Al Khaimah on Sunday, October 11, according to IANS and Yeni Şafak. The Revolutionary Guard said a fire broke out in the engine room after an explosion and that the tanker violates its rules, a claim from a single party, and we found no UAE statement on the circumstances.

In Sohar, in the Sultanate of Oman, India’s Ports Minister Sonowal said the tanker Petro Dolphin was hit in a drone attack, and nine of its 11 sailors were rescued, with two missing, according to The Tribune and Siasat. In the Strait of Hormuz the tanker Valthera was hit by an unidentified projectile and caught fire, and its 11 sailors are safe, according to the same minister and according to FinancialJuice.

World · Ukraine · EnergyPress ✓ 6 outlets · Moscow has not confirmed

Trump announces an “energy truce” between Russia and Ukraine, Zelensky: the news is new to me, and Russia hits a power plant after the announcement

President Trump announced on Sunday, October 11, that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy truce” effective immediately, according to Reuters, the BBC, Bloomberg and France 24. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told Axios that the announcement surprised him, that Ukraine will comply if Russia agrees, and that it is waiting for details from Washington, according to Reuters. He told ABC that he would hold elections if Putin agrees to a 60-day truce.

Sky News Arabia said Zelensky reported that Russia shelled energy facilities after the announcement, and the Kyiv Independent reported a strike on a power plant in Kharkiv; we found no Russian confirmation of the truce. The Financial Times reported that Washington threatened to halt weapons purchases for Ukraine unless its strikes on Russian refineries stop, and Kyiv said it would stop them if Russia stops hitting energy. The matter concerns the UAE because the flow of Russian diesel and oil affects global prices.

World · PakistanPress ✓ 4 outlets

Pakistan declares emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, ends its government and deploys the army

Pakistan’s president declared a state of emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, which is governed by the party of former prime minister Imran Khan, according to Reuters, Anadolu and Sky News Arabia. The law minister said the province’s government ended with the declaration of emergency, that the governor will run the province, and that the army will be deployed there, according to FinancialJuice.

We found no comment from Imran Khan’s party in the sources we reviewed. Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, speaking on another matter, said Pakistan condemns the attack on King Khalid airport and is committed to Saudi Arabia’s sovereignty. A large number of Pakistanis live in the UAE, which makes developments there close to the community.

Before today’s open: Dubai Financial Market closed Friday at 5,818.47, up 0.33%, after a week in which it lost 1.4%, and Saudi shares fall after the Riyadh attack

UAE markets open today, Monday, October 12, after the weekend. The Dubai Financial Market index closed on Friday, October 9, at 5,818.47 points, up 0.33%, according to Argaam and Al Khaleej, but it lost about 1.4% over the week. In the US, the S&P 500 closed on Friday at 7,811.54, up 0.59%, the Nasdaq at 27,366.17 (+0.64%), and the Dow Jones at 51,654.95 (+0.83%). West Texas Intermediate reached $91.85, and gold $4,216.3, up 1.43%.

Saudi shares fell after the attack on Riyadh airport, according to Reuters.
