International · U.S.–China–Russia summit✓ 4 outlets · China’s official confirmation, venue, time and agenda unconfirmed

U.S., Russia: “Three-way U.S.–China–Russia summit on the 19th on the sidelines of APEC”… China’s announcement not confirmed

The U.S. announced it first and Russia confirmed afterward. The U.S. side said North Korea could be discussed, and said no Trump–Kim Jong Un meeting is scheduled.

The U.S. and Russian governments each said on the 14th (local time) that the leaders of the United States, China and Russia will hold three-way talks on the 19th. News arrived in the early hours of the 15th, Korean time, that the U.S. had announced it first in Washington and Russia had soon officially confirmed the same schedule. The meeting is arranged on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, which is to be held in China from the 18th to the 19th. U.S. President Donald Trump is to attend the ASEAN summit in the Philippines next week and then APEC in China. The U.S. side said North Korea could be discussed at the meeting, while saying that no meeting between President Trump and Kim Jong Un, chairman of North Korea’s State Affairs Commission, is scheduled. The core of the announcement is the three-way format, with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin attending together.

A three-way meeting is one in which the leaders of three countries discuss matters together in one place, as distinct from bilateral talks in which two countries meet separately. However, the specific venue, time and agenda have not yet been made public, and no official announcement from the Chinese government had come by the night of the 14th. Concern has been raised that Korean Peninsula issues could be handled with Korea left out of the discussions, and the phrase ‘a second Yalta conference’ has even appeared, likening it to the 1945 Yalta Conference, where the great powers discussed the fate of other countries without the parties concerned. A U.S.–China bilateral meeting is also mentioned, but no fixed schedule has been confirmed. President Lee Jae-myung’s ASEAN and APEC tour runs over the same period, and on the 16th in Manila the president is to hold bilateral talks with the leaders of the Philippines and Thailand, each separately. Whether the three-way talks will produce a joint document, what it would contain if so, and what explanation the Korean government received in advance have not been confirmed.

Personal data of 3,083 people leaked from Korea Mint Corporation’s mobile Local Love gift certificate platform ‘Chak’

On the 14th, the Korea Mint Corporation said there had been unauthorized access from outside and viewing of personal data on ‘Chak’, the mobile Local Love gift certificate platform it operates. The personal data that leaked covers 3,083 people. The corporation expressed apology to users. A Local Love gift certificate is a voucher issued by a local government and usable only within that locality, and ‘Chak’ is a service for buying and paying with it by app. Unauthorized access means a third party without permission getting into the system and viewing information. It has not been made public what information left by what route, when the access began, whether the information was merely viewed or downloaded, or whether the users concerned have been notified individually. Following the leak of Phase 2 ‘Everyone’s Startup’ participants’ personal data covered in the previous issue, another leak from a public service has come to light.

International · Ukraine✓ 4 outlets · U.S. side’s position unconfirmed; accounts differ on whether it was a ‘query’ or a ‘proposal’

Zelensky: “U.S. asked whether Ukraine would cede Donbas in exchange for NATO membership”… U.S. side’s position not confirmed

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told reporters on the 13th that, in the U.S.-mediated talks to end the war with Russia, the U.S. negotiating team asked whether Ukraine could give up the eastern Donbas region in return for joining the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO). Donbas is an eastern Ukrainian region spanning Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, part of which Russia occupies. The remarks became known on the 14th (local time), and President Zelensky said relations with NATO are back on the negotiating agenda.

The word ‘asked’ follows President Zelensky’s own account. Some reports describe it as a formal proposal, and the U.S. government’s position has not been confirmed to date. The remarks came out of the course of negotiations after the U.S. envoys’ meeting with President Putin, covered in the previous issue, and how the Ukrainian government responded to the idea has not been confirmed.

Technology · Cloud outage✓ 4 outlets · outage length (around 24 hours vs 36 hours) and detailed cause unconfirmed

Microsoft business services’ global outage restored… some public-sector work in Europe also disrupted

The global outage that ran across the 13th and 14th in Microsoft business services such as Outlook and Teams has been restored. Outlook handles email, and Teams handles video meetings and messaging. Microsoft said the services were restored and explained that the outage was a technical problem unrelated to a cyberattack or artificial intelligence agents. Tallies differ on how long the outage lasted, with one side saying close to 24 hours and another 36 hours.

In Europe, government and public-sector work in some countries was disrupted. This prompted talk in parts of Europe that ‘a detox from American technology is needed.’ The risk of a structure that relies on one provider’s cloud services has come up again. The detailed cause of the outage and the number of users affected have not been made public.

Health · Infectious disease✓ 3 outlets · body that made the status ruling, timing and case tally unconfirmed

Report says U.S. has lost measles-free status after 26 years… official announcement from health authorities unconfirmed; thousands of recent cases

It has been reported that the U.S. lost its measles-free status after 26 years. An official announcement from U.S. health authorities has not been confirmed. Measles-free is the state of being recognized as having effectively eliminated measles because it no longer keeps spreading within the country. The U.S. had long succeeded in managing measles through steady vaccination, but with thousands of cases occurring recently it was not able to keep this status.

The U.S. was recognized in 2000 as a country where measles had disappeared. Measles is extremely contagious, and when vaccination rates fall below a certain level, herd immunity collapses and it spreads quickly. Reports also noted that vaccination rates had fallen, but how an official body explains the cause of this rise in cases has not been confirmed. The body that made the status ruling, the exact timing and the final case tally have also not been made public.
