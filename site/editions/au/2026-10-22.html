Helen Haines has both legs amputated after alleged deliberate car attack; man charged

Her husband has confirmed a second amputation as the suspect, named as Brendan Nugent, faces three charges.

Independent MP Helen Haines, who represents the north-east Victorian seat of Indi, has had both legs amputated after an alleged deliberate car attack, according to the BBC, The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today. Her husband confirmed that she has undergone a second amputation, the outlets reported, after earlier live coverage said surgeons were trying to save her remaining leg. Reports described her condition as critical. A vigil was held in Wodonga, the live coverage reported. A man identified as Brendan Nugent has been charged with three offences over the alleged attack and has appeared in court, the outlets said. The BBC reported that police charged the man over what it described as an alleged 'targeted' attack, while live coverage of the case used the word 'intentional' in describing the allegation. Further reporting said more details have emerged about Nugent's past.

The sequence reported by the outlets runs from the first accounts of one leg lost, with surgeons trying to save the other, to the husband's confirmation of the second amputation. The Sydney Morning Herald and The Age also published an analysis headed 'Caution needed on linking Helen Haines attack to PM's grievances', which dealt with Prime Minister Anthony Albanese's remarks on the confusing set of factors that may have led to the incident. The same mastheads carried a column, 'A politician's job is to listen. It's becoming increasingly dangerous to do so', in which Australian politicians said that, despite the obvious challenges, they do not want 24/7 security and do not want to hide away. The reports do not state a motive, and the extent of Haines's recovery has not been confirmed.

France sends troops onto Paris streets as riots aftermath continues

French soldiers will reinforce police on the streets of Paris as the government continues its crackdown after riots, according to The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today, which carried the report under the headline 'France sends in troops as Paris simmers in aftermath of riots'. The reports described the capital as still simmering in the aftermath of the unrest. AP News published a fact check reporting that Donald Trump and Republicans have blamed France's immigrants for the riots without evidence. The reports used here do not give figures for injuries or arrests, and do not say how long the soldiers will stay on the streets.

Melbourne set to drop out of the $1 million club as house prices fall

Melbourne's median house price is expected to be in the high $900,000s by the end of the year as rate hikes take hold, according to The Age, The Sydney Morning Herald, Brisbane Times and WA Today. The reports said that would take the city out of the $1 million club.

The same mastheads reported that the Reserve Bank has lifted interest rates four times this year and that the chances of another move have climbed, leaving it in what one report called a 'nightmare' position ahead of its November meeting. A separate column argued that food inflation is a bigger issue than oil and that the Reserve Bank knows it. The reports do not say what the bank will decide.

NextDC shares plummet as power protests hit $2 billion data centre plan

ASX-listed NextDC has told investors it would slash its expected profits and construction spending as it deals with a $2 billion data centre project that has run into protests over power, according to The Sydney Morning Herald, Brisbane Times, WA Today and The Age. The outlets reported that the company's shares plummeted after the announcement.

The Herald and its sister mastheads described the protests over power use as the trigger for the project's trouble, under the headline 'Power protests light fuse on $2 billion data centre implosion'. The reports used here do not give the exact fall in the share price or the revised profit and spending figures.

US envoy tells Putin 'You are a humanitarian' after deal to secure teacher's release

Stephen Hubbard has been imprisoned in Russia for four years, after the Ukrainian town where he lived was caught up in the war, according to The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today. The outlets reported that a US envoy told Russian President Vladimir Putin 'You are a humanitarian' after a deal to secure the teacher's release.

The reports describe Hubbard as a teacher and carry the envoy's remark to Putin as their headline. The material does not set out the terms of the deal or say when Hubbard will be freed.
