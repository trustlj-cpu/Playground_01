Three men found guilty of murdering Jake and Callum Robinson and Carter Rhoad in Mexico. Sentences due on October 16

A judge in Ensenada convicted the three over the April 2024 killings of the Perth surfing brothers and their American friend. Prosecutors have asked for “exemplary” prison terms, and sentencing is a week away.

A Mexican judge on Friday found three men guilty of murdering Australian brothers Jake and Callum Robinson and their American friend Carter Rhoad, the Guardian, BBC, CNN, ABC and Reuters reported. The brothers, aged 30 and 33, and Rhoad, 30, were killed on a surfing trip in Baja California in April 2024. The BBC said their bodies were found at the bottom of a well. DW said the case shocked Mexico and drew international outrage.

AP reported that the court in Ensenada convicted Jesús Gerardo García, known as “El Kekas”, Irineo Francisco and Ángel Jesús of aggravated homicide. Judge Raúl Hernández Arellano is due to announce their sentences on October 16, according to AP and Newscord. Prosecutors asked for “exemplary” terms for each man. AP put the request at 210 years each, while Newscord reported up to 150 years. We could not reconcile the two figures.

The three victims had been camping in a remote beachside area when they were killed, NBC stations reported. An earlier report said all three defendants chose to go to trial rather than plead. Aggravated homicide generally means a killing with an added element, such as robbery, that carries heavier penalties; the reports we saw did not say which element the court found.

The Robinsons were from Perth, and The West Australian, ABC and the Herald followed the trial closely. On Friday, before the verdict, the Herald and The Age reported that the brothers’ parents had asked the court for the maximum sentence, saying “our world stopped”.

The case has been followed closely in Australia since the three went missing. The Herald’s headline called it the murders of Australian surfers in Mexico, and ABC and Nine ran the verdict as breaking news within minutes of the ruling. The West Australian led with the guilty finding for the Perth brothers and their friend, reflecting how closely their home city followed the trial.

One woman has already been sentenced. Ary Gisell Silva Raya, García’s former partner, received 20 years in prison after admitting at trial that she had instigated and taken part in the robbery of the surfers’ belongings, CBS News and NBC stations reported. Investigators believe the group was killed in an attempt to steal their pickup truck, those reports said.

For the families, the verdict answers the central question of the trial but not yet the question of punishment. The next date is October 16. The ruling is a first-instance verdict by a single judge, so the process is not over until the sentences are set and any appeal is decided. We have not seen a statement from the defendants’ lawyers, so it is not clear whether any will appeal, and we did not find a response from the Australian Government.

All Blacks flatten the Wallabies 50-19 at Eden Park to keep the Bledisloe Cup, ending Les Kiss’s unbeaten run

New Zealand beat Australia 50-19 in Auckland on Saturday and kept the Bledisloe Cup, the BBC, the Guardian, Reuters, the Herald and L’Équipe reported. The Wallabies, chasing a first win at Eden Park since 1986, started well, and Tom Wright scored their first points, but the All Blacks pulled away after half-time with seven tries, according to L’Équipe.

The result ended Kiss’s unbeaten run as Wallabies coach. The Guardian counted the 24th year of New Zealand’s hold on the cup, while the BBC said 23rd; the outlets count differently. The Herald reported that All Blacks winger Leroy Carter was hurt scoring a try after a hip-drop tackle by Rob Valetini. The date of the second Test was not in our sources.

US sanctions the International Criminal Court hours after ex-judge Navi Pillay wins the Nobel Peace Prize

The United States imposed sanctions on the International Criminal Court on Friday, Reuters, AP, the Washington Post, the Guardian and Politico reported, hours after the Nobel Peace Prize went to Navi Pillay, a former judge of the court. The Post said the measures aim to cut the court off from the global financial system and threaten punishment for countries that do business with it. AP called them part of a drive to dismantle the tribunal. The ICC prosecutor condemned the move and said the court’s work would continue, Reuters reported.

Allies pushed back. The foreign ministries of Canada, Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and the UK issued a joint statement of support for the court, GOV.UK published. Australia is not among the listed signatories. The Guardian said the administration accuses the court of unfairly targeting Americans for war-crime charges and says the US “doesn’t need” it. The Herald called the sanctions an escalation.

Trump says Russia will send the US diesel as prices near records. Zelenskiy calls it an “investment in war”

President Donald Trump announced on Friday that Russia would immediately supply 300,000 tons of diesel to the United States after a call with Vladimir Putin, Reuters, AP, the Washington Post, NBC and the Guardian reported. The Japan Times said diesel prices are near a record and put Republicans at risk ahead of the November 3 congressional elections. The New York Times said Trump spoke of potentially millions of tons in the coming months, but noted that Russia has a ban on diesel exports.

Kyiv objected. Volodymyr Zelenskiy called the deal an “investment in war”, Reuters said, and CBS reported that Russian strikes killed 11 people in Ukraine the following day. Al Jazeera said Ukraine hit a Russian fuel site within hours. Germany said it would stick to its Russia sanctions, according to Reuters. The Washington Post noted the amount is small against US demand.

In Canberra, energy minister Chris Bowen said on Saturday, “We don’t need or want Russian fuel in Australia”, and that purchases would stay barred, the Guardian, Bloomberg and the South China Morning Post reported. US crude (WTI) ended Friday at US$91.85 a barrel, up 0.39 per cent.

Matt Renshaw’s unbeaten century rescues Australia on day two in Durban after Maddinson’s 33-ball duck

Matt Renshaw, whom the Herald described as demoted after the top order faltered on day one, scored an unbeaten century at Kingsmead on Saturday and took Australia to 277 for 8 at lunch, Reuters and the Herald reported. CNA called him record-breaking; we did not confirm which record. Hindustan Times’ live score had Australia on 390 for 9 at tea, with Renshaw eyeing a double century.

The recovery softens a wobble on day one, when Nic Maddinson was bowled for a duck after 33 balls on his Test return. ABC analysis said the batting order has issues; Renshaw shrugged them off, according to Nine. The final total and South Africa’s reply were not available when we went to press. This is the first Test of the series, and it runs to Tuesday.
