Oil pushes up the inflation threat as the Reserve Bank, with its cash rate at 4.60 per cent, weighs a fifth rise this year

The cash rate is at its highest since 2011, WTI crude closed Friday at US$99.02, and the major banks are split on whether the board moves on 3 November.

The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today all ran a Monday analysis headlined as the Reserve Bank's worst nightmare, saying the RBA has pushed interest rates to 4.6 per cent and may have to go higher, especially if oil prices keep climbing. The board lifted the cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.60 per cent on 29 September, which SBS and other outlets described as a 15-year high, and the Reserve Bank has said risks to inflation are skewed to the upside. Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter named the Middle East conflict as one of those risks. Coverage of the minutes, including CommBank's newsroom summary, says the board concluded it could not wait on inflation and remains open to further rises if required. Several outlets note it is the fourth increase of 2026.

The oil link is concrete in the market data. West Texas Intermediate crude closed on Friday at US$99.02, up 2.61 per cent, and commentators quoted by finder.com.au and the Adviser tie the rebound in energy prices to the disruption around the Strait of Hormuz, which was reported to have helped push Australian inflation to 4.6 per cent in the year to March. Forecasters are divided on what happens next. ANZ and Westpac are reported to expect a fifth rise at the 3 November meeting, taking the cash rate to 4.85 per cent, while CBA and NAB expect the board to hold, though CBA's economists call a rise very much live. Unemployment was 4.7 per cent in September, according to figures reported by ABC News on 15 October, and one economist quoted argues the steady rise in the jobless rate supports a pause. The September-quarter consumer price index, due on 28 October, is the main data the board will see before it meets. Independent Australia has argued that higher rates cannot fix an inflation problem caused by oil and risk deepening a downturn, while the RBA's own statements stress persistent inflation and energy supply disruption as the reasons for acting.

World · Canada✓ 5 outlets · cause not officially confirmed; the number of wounded is given only as dozens

Four Canadian service members are killed and dozens seriously wounded in a fire at CFB Wainwright in Alberta

CBC News, CNN, CBS News and NPR reported that a fire broke out around 4 pm local time on Saturday 17 October within the training area of Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta. The Canadian Armed Forces said it spread rapidly beyond the area's boundaries, and the municipal district said high winds drove it. Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed on Sunday that four service members died and that dozens more were seriously wounded, and next of kin have been notified, CBC reported. The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today carried the story as one of the worst peacetime tragedies for the Canadian military. CNN reported that the cause remains unclear. The Department of National Defence told CBC News there were no indications of any terrorist activity. ABC News in the United States relayed an unconfirmed Global News account, citing unnamed Armed Forces sources, that a live-fire exercise struck a gas line or gas well and ignited the blaze, and officials have not confirmed it. The RCMP has been involved in the response, and the town of Wainwright provided hospital and firefighting support.

Politics · Greens✓ 4 outlets · no findings or investigator named; demand from two signatories seen in headlines only

Greens senator Jordon Steele-John rejects claims as members demand the findings of the party's inquiry be made public

The Sydney Morning Herald first reported that seven Greens members, five current or former staffers and two party members, wrote to leader David Shoebridge about Western Australian Senator Jordon Steele-John. The letter covers claims from 2017 to 2025 that he made inappropriate comments to women, and News.com.au reported that none of the seven is understood to be a direct complainant. Steele-John has stepped aside from the party room and will not attend parliament while an independent investigation is arranged, according to SBS and the Canberra Times. He rejected the allegations as made without specifics and said one complaint related to a relationship in which he was the victim of abuse, and that he is working with police.

SBS reported that Shoebridge would not say whether the investigation's findings would be released. The SMH, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today reported on Monday that two of the seven signatories have come forward to demand public findings. The same four outlets reported that in a statement of nearly 2400 words posted to social media on Sunday night, Steele-John said rumours had been weaponised in what he called nasty political games.

Cricket · Second Test✓ 4 outlets · play on day two not yet reported

Rain and Scott Boland leave South Africa 20 for 2 on day one of the second Test against Australia in Gqeberha

ESPNcricinfo, Outlook India and the Citizen reported that Australia won the toss and chose to bowl at St George's Park on Sunday 18 October, and that rain disrupted the day. South Africa were 11 without loss after 5.3 overs at lunch, with debutant Marques Ackerman, who opened with Aiden Markram, getting off the mark with a single off Josh Hazlewood. After the break Scott Boland bowled Markram for 11 in the ninth over and next ball had David Bedingham caught behind for a golden duck. Play ended early with South Africa 20 for 2, Ackerman on 5 and captain Temba Bavuma on 4, according to the same reports.

The Times of South Africa and Sporting Life reported before play that Matthew Breetzke was the only new name in the squad, Tristan Stubbs was omitted and Ryan Rickelton would take the gloves from Kyle Verreynne. Australia lead the series after the 214-run win in the first Test in Durban, Reuters-sourced previews said.

Perth · Public safety✓ 3 outlets · no charges laid; number of youths given only as dozens

Fighting between groups of youths forces lockdown and early closure of the Kelmscott Agricultural Show in Perth

ABC News reported that fighting between groups of youths forced the long-running Kelmscott Agricultural Show into lockdown and then an early closure on Saturday 17 October. AAP-distributed copy and the Sydney Sun described the same scene, with ABC's headline putting the number of fighting youths at dozens and saying the show closed on Saturday evening. Police said they received reports of a large group of juveniles causing a disturbance at the show just after 4 pm, and a police spokesperson said no charges had been laid but one juvenile received a caution for disorderly behaviour. The show's president told ABC that people causing disruption were ejected, that the trouble built up outside on River Road, and that the gates were padlocked until police arrived.
