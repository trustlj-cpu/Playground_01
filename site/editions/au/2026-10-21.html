Brendan Nugent charged over Helen Haines attack as reports say the MP has lost a leg

Police have charged a 43-year-old man with three offences, and ABC News and the Sydney Morning Herald report the Indi MP had a leg amputated.

Victoria Police charged 43-year-old Brendan Nugent on Tuesday 20 October over the attack outside Helen Haines's electorate office in Wangaratta, ABC News and Guardian Australia reported. He faces three charges: dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and the Commonwealth offence of causing harm to a Commonwealth public official, according to ABC News and Reuters. Police described the incident as targeted, The Nightly and Reuters reported, and Nugent is due to appear in Wangaratta Magistrates' Court on Wednesday 21 October. ABC News said Haines was hit by a Toyota LandCruiser at about 3:30pm on Monday 19 October. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called the attack a test of the country's character, Reuters reported, and declined to speculate on motive while the investigation continues.

The Sydney Morning Herald and ABC News reported that Haines had a leg amputated after surgery at The Alfred hospital in Melbourne, and the Herald's live coverage said surgeons were fighting to save the other leg. The Alfred said she remained in a critical condition, with no change since the morning, and that its next update would come on Wednesday morning, according to ABC News; the hospital had not confirmed the amputation when the outlets published. The Conversation reported that Nugent's online posts concerned the environment and Gaza, while ABC Verify found that in 2019 he replied to Haines on social media and appeared to compliment her. Police have not publicly stated a motive, and the charges have not yet been tested in court.

NAB to cut up to 2,500 jobs, about 6 per cent of its workforce, in a restructure

National Australia Bank said on Tuesday 20 October that it will cut up to 2,500 roles, about 6 per cent of its workforce, as it overhauls its operating model, ABC News reported. The Sydney Morning Herald said the cuts would come over the next financial year, while ABC News described them as running to mid-2027. Capital Brief reported that up to 1,500 of the roles are expected to go before the end of 2026, and that the bank expects a pre-tax restructuring charge of about $300 million in the 2027 financial year to cover redundancy, redeployment and organisational redesign. Chief executive Andrew Irvine said the bank's ambition and strategy remain unchanged and that the changes focus on corporate functions and senior management, with no change to customer-facing staff, Capital Brief reported. The Finance Sector Union called the timing close to Christmas disgraceful, with national assistant secretary Nicole McPherson saying a wrecking ball was being taken to 6 per cent of one of the country's largest workforces. Guardian Australia carried the announcement under the headline NAB to cut 2,500 jobs in massive restructure, and Capital Brief said NAB's stock exchange statement does not mention artificial intelligence.

Albertans reject a move towards independence in a referendum, a win for Prime Minister Mark Carney

Voters in the Canadian province of Alberta rejected a move towards independence, with about 75 per cent backing the option to remain in Canada in the early count, Al Jazeera and CBC reported on 20 October. The Guardian said CBC projected the result, and the Sydney Morning Herald described it as a victory for Prime Minister Mark Carney. The ballot did not ask directly about leaving: the alternative to remaining was to support holding a binding referendum on independence, possibly as early as 2027, Al Jazeera reported. Carney said Albertans had made a clear choice, and argued that some of the roughly one quarter who backed the proposal were expressing frustration with Ottawa rather than seeking independence, according to Al Jazeera. Premier Danielle Smith said she was very pleased and would address the province, Al Jazeera reported, while Jeffrey Rath of the Alberta Prosperity Project said the push for independence would continue. The Sydney Morning Herald said Donald Trump's trade war and his threats to make Canada the 51st US state helped Carney rally support.

Mother of first Alan Jones complainant tells trial she trusted the broadcaster as her teenage son's mentor

The mother of the first complainant in Alan Jones's trial gave evidence on Tuesday 20 October that she trusted the 85-year-old radio host as a mentor to her son, the Sydney Morning Herald and ABC News reported. She told the court her son's mood was different after a two-night stay at Jones's property at Fitzroy Falls when he was 17, and that about ten days later he wrote her a letter saying something had happened, ABC News reported. The Sydney Morning Herald said she broke down in tears while the letter was discussed, and Guardian Australia reported that she said she had absolutely trusted Jones. Jones has pleaded not guilty to 21 counts of indecent assault and sexual touching involving five male complainants, ABC News reported. The first complainant alleges that Jones kissed and touched him during the visit, according to ABC News. Prosecutor Georgina Wright SC asked the mother why she kept corresponding with Jones after her son told her of the alleged assault, ABC News reported.

World · Africa✓ 5 outlets · sentences still to come

Kenyan cult leader Paul Mackenzie and 28 co-accused convicted of murdering 191 people, 190 of them children

A court in Mombasa convicted Kenyan preacher Paul Mackenzie and 28 co-accused of murder on Monday 19 October over the Shakahola forest deaths, TRT World and Pulse Kenya reported. The Sydney Morning Herald and Brisbane Times said the finding covered 191 victims, 190 of them children who starved, and described Mackenzie as a taxi driver turned apocalyptic preacher. Pulse Kenya reported that Justice Diana Kavedza-Mochache rejected the defence claim that the victims fasted voluntarily.

Police found shallow graves and severely malnourished survivors in the Shakahola forest in Kilifi County, and more than 400 bodies had been recovered by 2025, according to the reports. Autopsies showed most victims died of hunger while others appeared to have been strangled, beaten or suffocated, Pulse Kenya said. Mackenzie denied the murder charges and said he had not forced followers to starve, according to the same reports. Pulse Kenya reported that probation reports are due on Friday 30 October and sentencing is set for Friday 6 November.
