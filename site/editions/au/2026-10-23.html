Helen Haines has lost both legs, her husband says, as vigils are held for the Indi MP

The independent MP remains in a critical condition after surgeons removed her right leg on Wednesday, and the man accused of the attack has been remanded in custody.

Helen Haines, the independent member for Indi, has lost both legs after being struck by a vehicle outside her Wangaratta electorate office on Monday 19 October, her husband Phil Haines said on Wednesday. ABC News and Yahoo News Australia reported that her left leg was amputated on Monday and that surgeons removed her right leg on Wednesday morning, after hopes of saving it ended. "The two most important parts of Helen for us as a family are her heart and her mind," Phil Haines said, adding that her road to recovery would be long and difficult but that she is a fighter. ABC News reported on Thursday 22 October that she remained in a critical condition more than two days after the attack, and the Sydney Morning Herald and The Age reported that a vigil was held in Wodonga. Counter-terrorism authorities are investigating, and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has called the incident a deliberate act, according to the Canberra Times.

The accused, Brendan Nugent, appeared in Wangaratta Magistrates' Court on Wednesday 21 October, ABC News reported. He is charged with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury and causing harm to a Commonwealth public official, along with two cannabis offences. He did not apply for bail or enter a plea and was remanded in custody until a committal mention on 4 February 2027, with police due to complete a brief of evidence by 24 December. Victoria Police Chief Commissioner Mike Bush said the investigation was far from complete and that further serious charges, including attempted murder, were not ruled out. ABC News reported that such a charge would move the case to the Supreme Court of Victoria. The Townsville Bulletin reported that federal MPs are unhappy with security briefings given after the attack, while ABC News reported a minister saying threats against MPs are not new.

Ben Roberts-Smith says he "didn't bloody murder anyone" in a long 2GB interview as he launches a clothing line

Ben Roberts-Smith, the Victoria Cross recipient charged in April with five counts of the war crime of murder over alleged killings in Afghanistan between 2009 and 2012, gave his first long interview since the charges to 2GB host Ben Fordham, The Nightly and The New Daily reported. He denies the charges. In extracts aired on Thursday 22 October he said he had never committed a war crime and "I didn't bloody murder anyone", The New Daily reported. The interview runs about two and a half hours, is being played on 2GB Breakfast across Thursday and Friday from 7am, and was released in full on YouTube, Mediaweek reported. It covers his childhood, service, arrest, bail and the ten days he spent in Silverwater prison, The Nightly reported. The interview coincides with the launch of a clothing line named after his old radio call sign, Epic One Two, with t-shirts, a tank top and caps, The Nightly reported. The Sydney Morning Herald published a live fact-check of his answers, noting that Fordham did not challenge him on court findings, and a column in the same paper described the publicity push as aimed at an audience of 12, a reference to a future jury. The ABC reported that Roberts-Smith said the failed defamation case cost media owner Kerry Stokes upwards of $30 million. Justice Besanko found in the civil case that he murdered an unarmed Afghan civilian, a finding Roberts-Smith disputes, while the criminal charges have not been tested in court.

Business · Courts✓ 4 outlets · Meta says it will appeal

Meta ordered to pay US$25 billion to New Mexico after a jury found it misled users about privacy

A New Mexico state judge on Wednesday 21 October ordered Meta Platforms to pay the state US$25 billion after a jury found the company misled consumers about the privacy of their data, Reuters, CNN and PBS reported. Judge Francis Mathew reduced the penalty from the figure the law would allow: he found a penalty of more than US$219 billion was warranted at up to US$5,000 per violation, then settled on US$25 billion. Jurors in September found that 26 of 29 statements examined in the trial were misleading and that Meta committed more than 43 million violations of New Mexico's consumer protection laws, according to the reports.

The state had asked for US$35 billion to US$40 billion, while Meta asked for no more than US$3.45 billion, the reports said. The money will go to the state's school system, with interest accruing at 15 per cent a year until it is paid. Meta plans to appeal, and its chief legal officer said the company is confident it will win on appeal. The case was not directly about data taken by Cambridge Analytica but about what Facebook said over the years about user data, privacy settings and content moderation, CNN reported. Australian outlets including the Sydney Morning Herald reported the amount as about $36 billion in Australian dollars.

South Africa beat Australia by an innings and 31 runs in the second Test to level the series 1-1

South Africa won the second Test at St George's Park in Gqeberha by an innings and 31 runs on day five, levelling the three-match series at 1-1, ABC News and Outlook India reported. Captain Temba Bavuma made 179 to lift South Africa from 6 for 110 to 9 declared for 425, then Marco Jansen took 5 for 22 as Australia were bowled out for 99 and made to follow on, according to Outlook India. Australia's second innings reached 295, with Travis Head reaching 111 not out when Australia were 4 for 192 and Scott Boland alongside him, Outlook India reported. Jansen dismissed Josh Hazlewood for a duck to finish the match.

News.com.au described the day-four batting collapse as gutless, and the Sydney Morning Herald headlined its report that the Australians crumbled. Rain delays hit the opening stages of the match, Outlook India reported. Australia's batting problems in South Africa have also prompted debate about selection, with the Sydney Morning Herald publishing a column on whether Sam Konstas, who made a century, and Kurtis Patterson should be considered by selectors.

Perth house prices fall 7.4 per cent in the September quarter, the steepest drop on record, Domain reports

Domain's September-quarter report found house prices fell in all eight capital cities, the first time in 15 years and only the second time on record, ABC News and Domain reported. Perth recorded the sharpest fall, with its median house price down 7.4 per cent, or $88,664, to $1,115,227, which leaves values 8 per cent below the March 2026 peak and is the steepest quarterly fall in Perth on record, according to Domain. Across the combined capitals, house prices fell 4.7 per cent, or $59,870, the steepest quarterly decline Domain has recorded, while unit prices fell 3.5 per cent.

Brisbane house prices dropped 5.4 per cent to $1.15 million, ending almost four years of uninterrupted growth, Domain reported. Cotality, which measures differently, reported on 1 October that Perth fell 1.2 per cent in September and 4.7 per cent over the quarter to a median of $975,022, while remaining about 10.1 per cent higher than a year earlier. MacroBusiness reported on 20 October that Perth now leads the national decline, citing a non-seasonally adjusted fall of 1.7 per cent over the past month. Cotality's Gerard Burg pointed to rising rates, lower affordability and cost-of-living pressure reducing demand.
