Bostock’s three tries carry the Kangaroos past New Zealand in a tight World Cup opener

The debutant scored three tries in a match that began with a freak Kiwi try and a 90-metre intercept. Mitchell Moses is now in doubt for the rest of the tournament, Fox Sports reported.

The Kangaroos survived a scare to beat New Zealand in the opening match of the Rugby League World Cup in Sydney on Thursday night. Debutant Jack Bostock scored three tries, the Guardian, News.com.au and Brisbane Times reported, and the Guardian said he also set up another. It gave the Pool A score as 24–22 and called Bostock’s match-winning try the decider. We have not seen the score repeated by a second outlet, so treat it as one newspaper’s figure for now.

The match began at a sprint. WA Today reported that New Zealand’s Casey McLean scored the first try of the tournament “in freakish fashion”, and that Kangaroos winger Tom Trbojevic then raced 90 metres to score after an intercept. News.com.au called it an “insane, extraordinary” start. ABC’s live coverage framed the night around a blockbuster between two powerhouse nations, with several State of Origin players making historic starts for the Kiwis.

The cost for Australia may be the bigger story. Fox Sports reported that Mitchell Moses is in doubt for the remainder of the World Cup, and other mastheads ran the headline “Moses limps off in huge Aussie blow”. We did not see what the injury is, a scan result or a club or team statement, so the length of his absence is not known. Nine’s live blog also carried a headline saying Brad Fittler had admitted a “major” concern before the game; we have not seen what it was.

Before the game Brisbane Times profiled debutants Jacob Preston and Jack Bostock, who both spoke to reporters ahead of their first World Cup appearances. Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today also ran a piece on the Jillaroos, Australia’s women’s team, who say they are pumped to play a home World Cup.

The rest of the draw is already part of the conversation. Brisbane Times ran a piece headlined “The 20 reasons people should be taking Samoa seriously at the World Cup”, a single-outlet view we cannot test, and ABC’s live coverage described the opener as a blockbuster between two powerhouse nations. The Guardian’s headline for the match was that Australia survived a scare, which fits a two-point margin. We did not see the live scoreline at any point, so we do not describe how the lead moved.

A two-point margin for the hosts against their oldest rival is the kind of result that sharpens a tournament rather than settles it. We did not see the full fixture list, the team’s next match or its date in our sources, and we have not guessed at them. What is plain from the reports is that Australia found a match-winner in a newcomer and lost, perhaps, a key playmaker in the same night.

Unemployment hits a five-year high, putting the Reserve Bank on notice before its next rate decision

Australia’s jobless rate rose to a five-year high of 4.7 per cent in September, according to the Australian Financial Review’s headline, which the Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today matched with reports on Thursday that the Reserve Bank faces pressure to hold off on further rate rises. ABC ran video under the headline “Unemployment rate hits five-year high”. The Herald said the figures covered September, before the Reserve Bank’s most recent rate hike.

The Herald separately reported that the New South Wales rate hit 4.7 per cent last month, the highest since November 2021, and listed three factors behind it. We did not see the three factors, and we have not confirmed whether the national and state rates are the same number. AFR’s report said rate expectations cooled after the data.

Market Index’s evening wrap said the ASX 200 shrugged off the weak jobs data as banks surged and BHP and Rio Tinto tumbled. We did not see employment, participation or hours figures. Rising unemployment is the kind of evidence a central bank weighs when deciding whether higher rates have done enough.

Word of the day
Unemployment rateEconomy

The share of people in the labour force who want a job and are actively looking but do not have one. A rate of 4.7 per cent means roughly 5 in every 100 people in the workforce are unemployed.

Senate dumps the cut to private health rebates for older Australians as Labor introduces its online safety duty

The Senate has voted down the Labor government’s proposed cuts to private health insurance rebates for older Australians, the Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today reported on Thursday. ABC said the Coalition and Greens united against the government. The live blogs said Butler vowed the bill will return to the Senate. We did not see the vote count or the amount the cut was meant to save.

ABC’s analysis said the government is working to negotiate its way through numerous roadblocks from all sides of politics with just three sitting weeks of parliament left. The same day, the government introduced its Digital Duty of Care bill. ABC reported it scrapped the controversial ministerial discretion in the proposed laws and beefed up protections for news content. The Herald reported that Australians will be able to opt out of social media algorithms by the end of 2027, and that the laws would penalise platforms promoting gender-based violence.

Anika Wells, who introduced the bill, said in a Guardian video that the laws still allow “manosphere” content and conspiracy theories. That is a statement of how the bill works, not a verdict on whether it will pass. We have not seen the text of the bill.

Australia · ConsumerPress ✓ 5 outlets · penalties sought not in our sources

ACCC takes Kmart to court over nail glue that allegedly burned customers and children’s watches with button batteries

The consumer watchdog has launched legal action against Kmart, alleging it breached Australian consumer law by selling millions of bottles of nail glue that caused burns, ABC reported. The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today said the watchdog alleges customers complained of burns, went to hospital and even needed skin grafts.

Brisbane Times’s summary said the court action also covers untested button batteries in children’s watches. Nine reported that Kmart said it was disappointed by the ACCC’s action. We did not see the penalties sought, the court or the number of customers affected. These are allegations; no court has made findings.

As background, not from the reports: button batteries are a known danger to young children if swallowed. We have not seen the ACCC’s specific claims about the watches.

Markets · WorldPress ✓ 5 outlets · index levels not in our sources

Hot US inflation and an AI debt scare send Wall Street lower and rattle Asia, with the Australian dollar sliding

US consumer prices rose 0.6 per cent in September from August and 3.7 per cent from a year earlier, Bloomberg reported, citing the Bureau of Labor Statistics; it said petrol rose 9.1 per cent. Reuters said prices accelerated and core CPI, which excludes food and energy, was firmer. The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WA Today ran an AP story saying Wall Street slumped as inflation worsened, pulling the US stock market further from its record.

The Financial Times reported that Nvidia, Oracle and CoreWeave fell as inflation and AI debt fears unsettled investors. Reuters said Nasdaq futures dived 1 per cent as an AI rout deepened on debt concerns and that US chip stocks were set to wipe out US$2 trillion in value; both are single-source figures. Asian markets sold off, with Seoul and Tokyo bearing the brunt, Reuters, FMT and CNA reported. The dollar retreated from an 18-month high as Fed officials tempered rate-hike bets, Reuters and FMT said, and Reuters reported Fed official Daly saying hikes are likely needed but not urgently.

At home, the Herald and The Age said the ASX was set to fall at Thursday’s open. Our market data shows the ASX 200 closed down 0.73 per cent on Thursday and the Australian dollar was around 69.15 US cents, down 0.85 per cent, early on Friday; we have not seen a second source for either figure.
