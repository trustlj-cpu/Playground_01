Houthi missiles reach Riyadh's airport and a tanker is hit off Qatar. Oil jumps again before the ASX opens

The Houthis say they fired a ballistic missile at King Khalid airport, and the Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed two over the capital. US share futures fell overnight. The ASX, which lost 0.8% on Thursday, faces another test today.

Yemen's Houthi movement said it fired a ballistic missile at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Thursday, the second strike it has claimed on the Saudi capital in three weeks. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two ballistic missiles launched towards the city. Saudi authorities said shrapnel fell after the interception and that debris from the rocket landed on a medical complex. Reuters reported smoke rising from a stationary aircraft at the airport, which people briefed on the matter said was thought to be empty. We saw no report of casualties by early Friday, Sydney time.

The attack came hours after the BBC reported that a tanker had been hit by multiple projectiles in the Gulf off Qatar, citing a maritime security agency. The Financial Times said oil prices jumped on the tanker attack and on slowing flows through the Strait of Hormuz. The Houthis also warned workers at Saudi oil facilities to stay away from sites they described as targets, and renewed their warning to airlines and airports. AP reported that US share futures headed lower after explosions in the Saudi capital sent crude racing higher, and Bloomberg said futures slipped on a mounting energy shock and fears of an AI bubble. We did not have a confirmed overnight settlement for Brent at the time of writing. On Thursday afternoon, Sydney time, it was trading near US$102 a barrel.

Australia felt the first round on Thursday. The S&P/ASX 200 closed down 66.8 points, or 0.77 per cent, at 8,660.9, which the Herald put down to oil jumping again on renewed Iran tensions. The index is more than 6 per cent below its August peak. The Australian dollar was trading near 69.7 US cents early on Friday. Higher crude helps energy producers, but it reaches petrol bowsers and airfares within weeks, and it keeps pressure on the Reserve Bank, which meets on November 3.

The oil shock is landing on a market already nervous about artificial intelligence. Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey said on Thursday that markets were volatile and that fiscal policy risked becoming less effective, Bloomberg and FinancialJuice reported. SBS asked whether an AI sell-off could hit Australians' super. At home the test case remains Firmus. The AFR described the data-centre company's float as on the precipice as investor scepticism grows, and reported that dilution clauses were driving price talks behind the scenes. The ABC said the nation's second-largest ASX float was in doubt. We saw no ASX notice of a revised price or a withdrawal by early Friday.

For Australian readers the order of events matters more than any single headline. Friday's session will show whether the energy shock or the AI wobble weighs more on local shares. Further attacks on Gulf shipping or on Saudi oil sites would be the clearest sign that the risk premium in oil is still rising. A quiet night in the Gulf would let it ease.

Politics · MigrationPress ✓ 6 outlets · policy figures from the Coalition's announcement

Taylor and Abbott tie the migration cut to Bondi, as universities warn of a 'very significant hole'

The Coalition's migration plan spent its third day under fire from both directions. Released on Tuesday by Opposition Leader Angus Taylor, it would cut net overseas migration to 100,000 a year for two years before letting it rise to 160,000 by the fourth year, according to Reuters, The New Daily and The Nightly. It would also halve humanitarian places to 10,000, cap international student enrolments at 240,000 and abolish the temporary graduate visa. Taylor has called it the biggest cut to immigration in the country's history, and argued it would take pressure off roads, schools, infrastructure and housing. The Nightly put net overseas migration at about 301,000 in 2025.

On Thursday the Herald and The Age reported that Taylor and former prime minister Tony Abbott said the Bondi shooting had driven the politics on migration. Universities warned in the same papers that the student cuts would leave a very significant hole in their finances, and a Herald analysis said the policy risked pleasing no one except perhaps John Howard. The Guardian compared the major parties' targets and asked whether any of them could be achieved. The pressure from the right is real: One Nation, which has outpolled the Coalition in some recent surveys, has promised to cut temporary migration by 750,000 over three years. The government has not matched the Coalition's number, and the Coalition has not released costings.

Business · ResourcesPress ✓ 3 outlets · claim untested; amount sought not reported

Fortescue takes a pipe maker to court over the 'defective' water line at its troubled Iron Bridge mine

Fortescue has taken the manufacturer of a water pipeline at its Iron Bridge magnetite project to court, alleging the pipe it supplied was defective, the Herald, The Age and the AFR reported on Thursday. Iron Bridge, in the Pilbara south of Port Hedland, was meant to move Fortescue into higher-grade ore that earns a premium from steelmakers. Instead it has been beset by cost overruns and has never reached its nameplate capacity of 22 million tonnes a year since it opened in 2022, according to AAP. In its latest annual results Fortescue took a US$525 million impairment on the mine.

The pipeline has been a known problem for almost three years. In January 2024 Fortescue cut its shipment guidance because of the raw water pipeline, and a report at the time said more than 30 per cent of the 220-kilometre line, about 65 kilometres of steel pipe, would need replacing. The amount Fortescue is seeking and the manufacturer's response were not clear from the reports we reviewed, and the claim has not been tested in court. In a separate Pilbara case reported by the same papers, the company Linkforce is suing a former employee over an alleged breach of secrecy. For shareholders the question is whether a court can recover part of the cost of a project that has so far destroyed value rather than created it.

Courts · QueenslandPress ✓ 5 outlets · allegations as put by police in court; untested

Sam Wood refused bail after police allege the former Bachelor star choked his wife

Former Bachelor star and fitness entrepreneur Sam Wood was refused bail on Thursday after a court heard police allege he choked his wife until she thought she would die, the Guardian, the AFR, the ABC, the Herald and The Age reported. He was arrested after police were called to a property in Noosa on the Sunshine Coast on Saturday night, and is charged with strangulation in a domestic setting and assault occasioning bodily harm, according to The New Daily and The Nightly. Reports said the family had been staying on Hastings Street at the time. He had been refused police bail and remanded in custody. His lawyer, Mathew Cuskelly, has said the charges will be defended.

The allegations have not been tested, and Wood is presumed innocent. The couple issued a joint statement after his arrest saying they were working through a difficult time as a family and co-operating with authorities. The fitness business 28, which he founded, has ended his role, and its owner DBG Health has said the business will continue under new leadership. The Guardian traced his path from reality television to the dock. Queensland made non-fatal strangulation a separate offence in 2016 because it is one of the strongest warning signs of later domestic homicide. World · UkrainePress ✓ 6 outlets · toll from Ukrainian officials as reported

A Russian bomb kills 33 civilians in eastern Ukraine, as Kyiv's negotiators head to Washington

A Russian bomb killed 33 civilians in a street in an eastern Ukrainian stronghold on Thursday, Reuters reported, citing officials. The ABC said the strike hit a bus stop, and the BBC said buses were struck as deadly attacks on Ukraine surge. The Guardian, reporting an earlier toll of 30, said two buses were hit near the front line. The Herald and The Age carried the Reuters report overnight.

The diplomatic track moved on the same day. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking in Lisbon, called the war a dangerous stalemate and said there needed to be a negotiated settlement that was permanent, enduring and fair, Reuters and FinancialJuice reported. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Ukrainian negotiators would visit the United States for talks on Friday. SBS reported on the war at sea, where two ships have sunk in the Black Sea. Australia has given Ukraine military aid, including Bushmaster vehicles, and has sanctioned hundreds of Russian people and entities since 2022.
