Ben Roberts-Smith joins 600 veterans in Canberra as Albanese marks 25 years since Australia went to war in Afghanistan

At the Australian War Memorial on Sunday the prime minister honoured the 47 Australian personnel who died as a result of their service. Veterans used the day to press for the release of official war histories.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese on Sunday marked 25 years since Australia joined the war in Afghanistan, at a ceremony at the Australian War Memorial in Canberra, the Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, the Guardian and ABC reported. The Herald said about 600 veterans attended. It reported that Albanese honoured the 47 Australian personnel who died as a result of their service, and the families who continue to live with their loss. The Guardian said he used the anniversary to describe the troops’ heroism.

ABC reported that Albanese quoted former prime minister John Howard on what drove Australia to join the conflict after the September 11 terrorist attacks in 2001. Australian forces went in as part of a US-led coalition within weeks of those attacks and stayed, in changing roles, for about two decades. That timeline is background, not something the day’s reports set out.

The guest who drew most attention was Ben Roberts-Smith, a Victoria Cross recipient, whom the Herald, The Age and the Guardian all named among those attending. In 2023 the Federal Court found, on the civil standard of proof, that he had unlawfully killed unarmed Afghans while serving as a soldier. He has denied wrongdoing and lost an appeal. Those details are background drawn from earlier reporting, not from Sunday’s coverage. The Australian reported that Phil Thompson greeted him despite what it called a Coalition rift; we did not see the rift explained.

Veterans used the occasion to press a demand. ABC reported that former soldiers called on the prime minister to release the official war histories of the conflict, and a second ABC report spoke of “the empty places that remain” since Australia joined the fighting. We did not see a government reply to the request.

The ceremony matters beyond the headline guest. For many who served, the anniversary is a reminder that the costs of a deployment continue long after the troops come home, including injury, trauma and the loss of mates. The Herald’s account put the families of the fallen at the centre of the prime minister’s remarks.

The Age said politicians joined the veterans in Canberra, but we could not confirm which parties were represented or how many. Nor did we find a response from the Opposition Leader, Angus Taylor, or any new policy announcement from the day. The attendance figure of 600 comes from one outlet and has not been checked against a second.

Former and serving personnel who want support can contact Open Arms, the free counselling service for veterans and their families, or the Department of Veterans’ Affairs. The next national commemoration is Remembrance Day on November 11.

Eliud Kipchoge, 41, wins the Melbourne Marathon and breaks the race record in his first marathon victory in three years

Eliud Kipchoge, the Kenyan marathon great, won the Melbourne Marathon on Sunday and broke the race record, ABC, The Age, Al Jazeera, the Guardian and the Japan Times reported. Al Jazeera and the Guardian called it his first marathon win in three years. The Guardian quoted him saying “My body is still peaking”. ABC said he had time to slow down and wave to the crowd at the MCG and still break the record. RUN247 quoted him calling the day “bigger than sport”.

A pre-race preview by Marathon Handbook put the old course record at 2:09:12, set in 2022, and said organisers had cut the climbing by about 20 per cent. We did not see Kipchoge’s finishing time. Melbourne was the third stop on his seven-continent world tour, NOS reported. The Age said New Zealand’s Camille French beat the elite women’s field, and an Irish outlet said Nichola Sheridan took third in a record run; we have only one source for that.

The new route drew praise. The Herald quoted an athletics great calling the Melbourne track the “best ever”, and the Herald Sun headlined the day “Kipchoge-mania”. Thousands of runners took part in the marathon festival, the Herald said, though a car incident near the route on Flinders Street overshadowed the morning; see Inside. For recreational runners the lesson of the day is practical: a flatter course with fewer turns can shave time, but it does not change the training behind a personal best.

Houthi missile attack on Riyadh airport kills 12 and injures 309. Saudi Arabia vows a response and Trump says he will “look at” joining

A missile attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday killed at least 12 people and injured 309, Saudi authorities said, according to AP, the Washington Post and Asharq Al-Awsat. The Saudi-led coalition blamed Yemen’s Houthis and called the strike a war crime, Al Jazeera and the Post reported. Le Monde called it the deadliest Houthi attack on Riyadh in ten years. ABC said it was the third attack on the airport in days. A Frenchwoman and Filipinos were among the injured, BFMTV and the Philippine Star reported.

On Sunday Saudi Arabia extended the airport closure and moved Riyadh’s schools online, Stuff and Global News reported. The Houthis renewed their warning to airlines, travellers and airport workers to stay away from Saudi airports, and ABC quoted them saying “worse is yet to come”. EgyptAir suspended flights to the city, and Reuters said Saudi shares slid. The UN Secretary-General, the EU’s foreign-policy chief Kaja Kallas, Italy’s Meloni and Canada’s prime minister condemned the attack. The Herald said the United States, Britain, Germany, Australia and Canada had warned citizens to avoid the airport.

Donald Trump called it “a terrible thing”, and CNBC reported he said he is “going to look at” joining Saudi Arabia against the Iran-backed Houthis. The Guardian said the attack undercuts his plan to park the conflicts in Iran and Yemen while blaming Ukraine for high diesel prices. WTI crude closed on Friday at US$91.85 a barrel, up 0.39 per cent, before the attack, so Monday is the first test for oil prices.

Kostecki wins the Bathurst 1000 after the leader runs out of fuel on the final lap

Brodie Kostecki won the Bathurst 1000 on Sunday after leader Anton De Pasquale ran out of fuel on the final lap, the Herald, ABC, Nine, CODE Sports and The Courier-Mail reported. The Herald said it was the second Bathurst win for Kostecki and his co-driver Todd Hazelwood. ABC’s headline named Davidson as the co-driver; we could not reconcile the two. CODE called it the cruellest finish in the race’s history and quoted De Pasquale’s team and driver in shock, and Nine called it a fuel bungle.

Earlier, Kostecki had lost the lead after what the Herald called a horror slow stop for his Shell V-Power team, handing it to pole-sitter Zach Bates. It was the 61st running at Mount Panorama. Seton led early and Morris spun at the start. The Courier-Mail said a race record had been looming before the disaster. Speedcafe said the Supercars Finals Series field was set after the race.

Australia · PoliticsPress ✓ 3 outlets · poll figures not in our sources

One Nation and Pauline Hanson soften in the polls, but voters blame Albanese rather than the Reserve Bank for cost of living

Support for One Nation and its leader, Pauline Hanson, is softening, while voters blame the Albanese Government rather than the Reserve Bank of Australia for cost-of-living pain, the Herald reported on Sunday. News.com.au headlined the same finding “Hanson slips as Labor cops blame”, and The Australian’s Newspoll coverage said Labor was warned its “spendathon must come to an end”. We did not see the primary-vote numbers, sample size or dates, so we have not repeated any.

The poll arrives after a conservative conference in Brisbane where Hanson was the favourite. ABC said Angus Taylor, the first Liberal leader to attend CPAC, came second to her, and the Herald said he looked more like her support act. SBS reported that Hanson named a Liberal she would prefer to what she called “smarmy” Taylor, and 7NEWS that she attacked the Coalition’s planned migration cuts. Separately, Taylor said he would welcome Josh Frydenberg’s return to politics, ABC reported, and dismissed his call for reform of the GST, The Australian said.
