Greens senator stays away from parliament as party's handling of complaints comes under scrutiny

Jordon Steele-John will not attend while an investigation runs, and a Sydney Morning Herald report details how staff dealt with member complaints.

Greens senator Jordon Steele-John will stay away from parliament while an investigation into allegations involving him is under way, according to reports published on Friday 16 October. The Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WAtoday, all Nine mastheads, carried the story, and Google News headlines list it across the Australian domestic feed. Their headline quoted the line 'Leave means leave', a reference to the terms on which the senator has stepped back from the chamber.

Greens leader David Shoebridge was asked whether an independent inquiry into the allegations would be held and, according to the same reports, refused to say. That gap matters. An internal investigation and an independent one are very different things for the people who made complaints, and for the public, who have so far been told little about what exactly is alleged. None of the material we reviewed sets out the allegations in detail, and nothing has been established or tested by any finding.

A second piece in the Herald, published the same afternoon, goes further on process. Under the headline 'How to kill a story', it describes what it calls a secret dossier the Greens used to protect Steele-John. The paper reports that Greens staffers took screenshots of member complaints as part of a plan to bury the allegations, and quotes the instruction 'Reach out to them'. These are the newspaper's claims, based on documents it says it has seen. We have not seen a Greens response in the coverage available to us, so treat the account as one outlet's reporting rather than a confirmed fact.

The timing is awkward for the party. The Greens are in the middle of talks with Labor over private health changes in the Senate, where the government needs their votes, and a party that wants to be seen as the moral conscience of the chamber is now being asked to explain its own internal complaints process. What to watch: whether Shoebridge announces an independent inquiry, whether the senator's absence is formalised, and whether the Greens respond to the dossier report. Because every outlet in this cluster belongs to one publisher, readers should look for confirmation from other newsrooms before treating the details as settled.

Five-year-old boy found dead in NSW home; police describe how he was restrained

Police say a five-year-old boy found dead in a home in New South Wales had been duct-taped into a sleeping bag and blindfolded. The detail was reported on Friday 16 October by the Sydney Morning Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WAtoday, with a separate Jev-ranked report describing an investigation after the boy was found. It is among the most widely carried stories of the cycle, which is one reason we have kept the account to what police themselves have said. The reports add that the family had been on the radar of the Department of Communities and Justice for at least a year. That is the state agency responsible for child protection, and it will almost certainly face questions about what it knew and when. We have not seen any statement from the department in the coverage reviewed, and we have not confirmed whether anyone has been charged, so we have deliberately left out names and any speculation about how the boy died. Those facts are for police and, eventually, a coroner or court to establish. If you are affected by this story, Lifeline on 13 11 14 and Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800 offer support.

Labor could lose half its savings under private health compromise with Greens

Labor's plan to scrap private health bonuses for about three million older Australians has hit a wall in the Senate, and a possible compromise with the Greens could cost the government around half of the savings it expected, according to the Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WAtoday on Friday 16 October. The reports say the government does not have the numbers on its own to pass the change.

That puts the Greens in the position of broker. Their support in the Senate would let Labor claim part of the saving, but only by softening the policy for the people affected. The coverage does not give a final dollar figure for the budget impact, and it does not say whether the Greens have agreed to anything, so the 'half' figure should be read as the papers' estimate of where a deal could land, not a settled outcome.

The story lands on the same day the Greens leadership is being questioned over the Steele-John investigation, which could complicate the negotiations. For older Australians holding private health insurance the practical question is simple: will the bonus they receive survive, and for how many of them? Expect that to be answered only when a bill, or an amendment, is put to the Senate.

Rookie hat-trick and Samoan rout set the tone for the Rugby League World Cup

The Rugby League World Cup opened with a flurry of tries. Australia and New Zealand produced what the Herald and its sister mastheads called a classic opener, with debutant Jack Bostock scoring a hat-trick, and Queensland fullback Reece Walsh was described as just as impressive. Walsh also spoke about teammate James Tedesco and the effort to solve a mystery issue, which is a storyline to keep an eye on as the Kangaroos build through the tournament.

Elsewhere, Samoa routed France, with winger Sua Fa'alogo scoring four tries; Stephen Crichton also scored after a heavy tackle by Brian To'o. In the women's competition the Jillaroos opened against Samoa, and hooker Keeley Nizza scored a solo try. Together the results suggest the Pacific sides are arriving with real attacking weight. We have not reproduced scores here because we did not check them against the match centre, and readers wanting results should use the official draw.

Media figure Antony Catalano appears in court over domestic violence charges

Media figure Antony Catalano appeared in court on Friday 16 October for the first time since being charged over an alleged attack on his wife. The Herald, The Age, Brisbane Times and WAtoday report that police are pursuing the domestic violence charges without the cooperation of his wife. One report says he is accused of choking her. The charges are allegations, and he is entitled to the presumption of innocence.

Coverage differs on where he is headed. One headline says he is bound for Bali while his lawyers try to resolve the matter, another says he will be allowed to move his family interstate, and a third says overseas. We have flagged this as unresolved, because the court outcome and the conditions attached to any move matter, and the snippets do not settle them. Beyond Catalano, Friday's news covered a wide spread of other stories, listed below.
