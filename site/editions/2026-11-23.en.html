Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigns, takes responsibility for confusion over CSAT AI cheating countermeasures… President Lee accepts at once

Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin said on the 22nd that Choi takes responsibility for the confusion over measures to prevent cheating on the CSAT and offered to resign, and President Lee Jae-myung accepted at once. A successor has not yet been made public.

Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin offered to resign on the 22nd, and President Lee Jae-myung accepted it at once. Minister Choi takes responsibility for the cheating using artificial intelligence (AI) that came to light in the 2027 College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) held on the 19th and for the confusion over countermeasures that followed. Kang Yu-jung, the Blue House chief spokesperson, said the selection of a successor would proceed by the proper procedure. On the 16th, three days before the exam, Minister Choi drew a line against calls to postpone the CSAT, saying that postponing would bring greater confusion, and on the 17th was also named in a criminal complaint on a charge of dereliction of duty. The head of the Korea Institute for Curriculum and Evaluation was also reported to have stepped down earlier, but the exact timing has not been confirmed. Before the exam, the government mentioned plans to discuss blocking communications at exam sites with the Ministry of Science and ICT and to declare immunity for proctors, but a vice minister of education explained that blocking communications 100% is technically difficult. Even after that, cheating using high-tech devices was caught, and distrust of exam management as a whole grew.

Reactions in politics were split. The Democratic Party of Korea said it hopes someone with the expertise to respond proactively to a fast-changing education environment is appointed, and the People Power Party said it has become a country where even the CSAT cannot be trusted and that this is not something the minister’s resignation can end. The Korean Teachers and Education Workers Union assessed that the minister’s leadership had shown its limits in the CSAT response. President Lee had earlier ordered reporting rewards of at least 100 million won per case and called for an overhaul of national exam systems, and proposals to increase descriptive and essay-type questions instead of multiple choice are also emerging. The government is reported to be considering further investigation into whether AI cheating also occurred in past exams, but the scope and timing have not been set, and the timing of naming a successor minister has not been made public.

Missing fishing boat Haegwang-ho found sunk on seabed off Seogyeokryeolbi Islands on fifth day of search… fate of 7 crew not confirmed

The Haegwang-ho, a 7.93-ton coastal trap fishing boat that lost contact at sea near the Seogyeokryeolbi Islands off Taean, South Chungcheong, was found sunk on the seabed on the 22nd, the fifth day of the search. The Taean Coast Guard Station finally confirmed that an object found at a point about 22 nautical miles (about 40.7 km) northwest of the Seogyeokryeolbi Islands, 52 m deep, around 3:26 p.m. that day was the hull of the Haegwang-ho. The Coast Guard had detected an object presumed to be a vessel with a Navy ship’s sonar in the same waters around 7:55 p.m. the previous day, and has continued checking since. Seven people are known to have been aboard, three Koreans including the captain and four Indonesian crew. The Coast Guard says it will continue searching for the seven missing inside and outside the hull. Whether the crew survived has not yet been determined, and why the boat sank has not been disclosed. The Coast Guard also found a fishing-net buoy in the same waters on the 21st. Whether and when the hull will be raised, and the plan for investigating the cause, are not yet known.

Politics · Judiciary✓ 4 outlets · dates for filing and voting on the impeachment motion not set

Democrats pursue impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae as party line… People Power Party counters with street rally

The Democratic Party of Korea adopted an impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae as its official party line and moved to pursue it in earnest. If the impeachment motion passes the National Assembly, the chief justice is placed under suspension from duty until the Constitutional Court’s ruling ends. Democratic Party spokesperson Cho Kye-won reportedly said there is a consensus to file it starting from the day the Dec. 3 emergency martial law was declared, but dates for filing and the vote have not been set. At a press briefing on the 22nd, the Democratic Party’s impeachment task force argued that, in ruling on former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo’s case on charges of participation in key insurrection duties, the Supreme Court held that there is no room for doubt that the Dec. 3 emergency martial law was itself unconstitutional and illegal, but quashed and remanded in part on the ground of acquittal, and that no part was found to constitute the crime of insurrection.

The People Power Party held a street rally in Daegu and countered that if Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae is impeached, the Lee Jae-myung administration will collapse. Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk argued that the one who should be impeached is President Lee, that this impeachment is a protective shield meant to block President Lee’s trials, and that it will ultimately be a self-destructive impeachment. Because the People Power Party has issued no official position on the Supreme Court’s judgment that the Dec. 3 emergency martial law was insurrection, voices inside the party are also saying the leadership needs to settle its position and apologize. Whether the impeachment motion will actually be filed, and what plan there is to secure the seats needed for a vote, are not known.

Industry · Semiconductors✓ 3 outlets · scale and routes of smuggling and investigation conclusions not confirmed

Waves from suspicion of Nvidia chips diverted to China… report says key operator is Chinese national in 50s, fears of reignited US-China trade war

Suspicion that Nvidia’s advanced AI chips leaked to China through third countries resurfaced on the 21st (local time) with a new report in the United States. According to the report, the key operator of a corporate network suspected of diverting AI chips subject to export controls to China is a Chinese national man in his 50s, and Alice Huang, who worked as managing director at the Singapore firm Megaspeed, served as the public face. However, this is only what was reported, not fact confirmed by investigators or a court, and the scale and routes of the smuggling have not been officially confirmed.

As the suspicion spread, reports said Nvidia’s share price fell for seven straight trading days and the Nasdaq dropped in the 5% range in a day. In Korea, the KOSPI gave up the 5,000 mark amid foreign selling, and a tally put the paper losses of seohak ants who bought Nvidia at low prices in the 6 trillion won range, but the figures differ by institution. In the United States, analysis emerged that the affair could reheat the US-China trade war, with two months left in the truce, and Chinese reactions and any official US government measures have not been confirmed.

Economy · Labor✓ 3 outlets · age of extension and timing in the government plan not confirmed

Employment and Labor Minister Kim Young-hoon: “On retirement age extension, if no agreement at ruling party special committee on the 27th, government will step in”

Employment and Labor Minister Kim Young-hoon said on a broadcast on the 22nd that if no agreement comes out of the Democratic Party of Korea’s special committee on retirement age extension, meeting on the 27th, the government will step in. Kim said the time to announce the government’s position around the committee meeting on the 27th is approaching, and answered yes when asked whether this means the government will act responsibly if no agreement is reached. This was taken to mean considering putting forward a government plan first. It is pointed out that the National Assembly’s discussion of extending the retirement age has gone on for nearly 10 months without progress.

The current statutory retirement age is 60. Behind the discussion is the fact that, as the age at which the National Pension starts rises in stages to 65, a gap opens after retirement in which income stops before the pension begins. Issues that have been raised include how to change the wage system and the effect on youth jobs. The age of extension and the timing the government plan would contain, and the chance of labor-management agreement, are not yet known.
