Jensen Huang return announcement sends Nvidia up about 31%, Nasdaq up 6.51%… KOSPI gains 6.32% to close at 5,006.03

Eleven days after the resignation, Nvidia announced CEO Huang’s return; New York stocks soared and the KOSPI won back the 5,000 level

On the 23rd (local time) Nvidia announced that it was immediately reinstating Jensen Huang, who resigned on Nov. 12, as president, chief executive officer (CEO) and director, and New York stocks soared. Nvidia shares rose about 31% (some tallies give 31.5% or 32%), the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite jumped 1,471.82 points (6.51%) to close at 24,089.56, and the S&P 500 rose 4.73% to close at 7,237.17. The Nasdaq’s gain is its largest in 1 year and 7 months. The board decided unanimously, said it had heard from senior U.S. government officials and said it is convinced the return poses no national security risk. The allegations about private life and about China that had been cited as the background to the resignation have not been officially confirmed.

The board was reported to have declined requests to return until a few days earlier. President Trump said on the 22nd that the president had personally persuaded the board. A report by one foreign outlet that the vice president had made contact has not been confirmed elsewhere. Beyond its statement, the board has not officially disclosed the specific course by which it changed its position. Korean stocks followed the trend. The KOSPI, which had plunged 3.31% the previous day to close at 4,708.54, rose 6.32% on the 24th to end the session at 5,006.03, regaining the 5,000 level. A broadcast report said foreign investors and institutions bought together during the session, lifting everything from semiconductors to growth stocks. Some in U.S. politics criticized the return on security grounds, and it was also pointed out that export controls on chips to China are a new variable.

Confirmation hearing for Justice Minister nominee Lee Yong-cheol… on directing an indictment withdrawal: “I will not exercise directive authority unreasonably”

The confirmation hearing for Justice Minister nominee Lee Yong-cheol was held on the 24th at the National Assembly Legislation and Judiciary Committee. The biggest question was whether the nominee would direct an indictment withdrawal in President Lee Jae-myung’s case. An indictment withdrawal is when prosecutors themselves pull back a charge they have already filed with the court. The nominee answered, “I will not exercise directive authority in an unreasonable way that the public would find hard to accept.” However, when People Power Party lawmaker Joo Jin-woo asked whether an indictment withdrawal could be directed if the special counsel investigating the suspected ‘fabricated prosecution’ indicts some of those involved, the nominee called it a hypothetical question and avoided a definite answer. In written answers submitted up to the day before the hearing, the nominee repeatedly said that the truth needs to be established and that the law and principles would be followed. Asked about having left out part of the blind trust of the spouse’s shares, the nominee answered, “It was my fault, and I am sorry.” A blind trust is a system in which a public official disposes of shares that could relate to official duties, or entrusts them to a trustee, so as to have no involvement in managing them. The hiring of staff and an allegation of ‘forcing people out’ from the time as chief executive of the Seongnam Industry Promotion Foundation were also raised as issues, and the details of the nominee’s answers on them are unconfirmed. The nominee is known to have been designated on the 6th, and whether the hearing report will be adopted and when an appointment will come have not been set. The president can appoint a minister even if the National Assembly does not adopt the report.

Financial Services Commission unveils Digital Asset Basic Act government bill… exchange major shareholders capped at 20%, won stablecoins to be bank-led

The Financial Services Commission unveiled the government bill for the Digital Asset Basic Act on the 24th. It is reported to limit major shareholders of crypto exchanges to holding up to 20% in principle, with exceptions subject to the commission’s approval. Won stablecoins are to be issued under bank leadership, and domestic initial coin offerings (ICO) of crypto assets are being pushed to be allowed again after 9 years. A stablecoin is a crypto asset whose value is pegged to a fiat currency such as the won so that its price does not swing widely. An ICO is a method in which a company issues a new coin to raise investment money.

User protection is also strengthened. Digital asset businesses take on internal control duties comparable to those of financial firms, and in the event of a hack or system failure they must compensate for damage even if the business was not at fault. In the industry, there is talk of expectation that the way is opening for stablecoins and ICOs, together with an expectation of pushback against the cap on exchange major shareholders’ stakes and against bank-centered issuance. When the government bill will be submitted to the National Assembly and what deliberation schedule it will follow have not been confirmed.

Politics · Judiciary✓ 2 outlets · motion introduction and vote schedule unconfirmed

Democratic Party speeds up push to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-de… Cheong Wa Dae keeps distance: “a matter for the National Assembly to discuss”

On the 23rd Cheong Wa Dae drew a line on the Democratic Party’s push to impeach Chief Justice Cho Hee-de, saying it is “a matter for the National Assembly to discuss.” Spokesperson Kim Sung-wan answered a question about its position at a briefing: “It is somewhat difficult for Cheong Wa Dae to comment beyond that.” The impeachment motion is the procedure by which the National Assembly asks the Constitutional Court to remove a senior official; when the motion is passed in the Assembly, the official’s duties are suspended. Meanwhile, the Democratic Party is reported to be speeding up, aiming to handle the motion in early next month despite concerns within the party and pushback from the judiciary.

The issue is whether the grounds for impeachment meet the requirements for removal. Some point to doubt over whether the allegations the Democratic Party raised amount to grounds for removal, and concern was also raised that if the motion passes and the chief justice’s duties stop, major insurrection trials, including that of former President Yoon Suk Yeol, could be halted one after another. The motion has not yet passed the National Assembly, and no vote date has been fixed. Actual removal is decided only after a Constitutional Court trial.

Goldman Sachs raises Korea’s growth forecast for next year to 3.2%… sees 1280 won per dollar, base rate peaking at 3.5%

Goldman Sachs raised its forecast for Korea’s economic growth next year from 2.8% to 3.2%. Kwon Goo-hoon, senior economist for Asia, said so on the 24th at a briefing titled ‘2027 Korea Macroeconomic Outlook’ held in Seoul. The judgment is that semiconductor exports will stay strong on expanded artificial intelligence (AI) investment. The current account surplus is expected to grow sharply on strong exports, and the won-dollar exchange rate was seen falling to 1,280 won by the end of next year, with a level below 1,250 won also possible if a weaker global dollar and rising demand for the won coincide.

The Bank of Korea base rate was projected to have an upper bound of 3.5% next year. Also offered was the diagnosis that even with growth above 3%, domestic demand will not be smiling. This means the semiconductor export boom does not spread to household consumption and investment as a whole. These figures are one investment bank’s forecast and are separate from the forecasts of the Bank of Korea and the government.
