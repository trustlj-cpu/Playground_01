After Cabinet meeting, Erdoğan promises fund victims: principal payments of up to 1 million lira to begin in October; investigation opened into those who shared confidential documents

Payments depend on Parliament passing the draft law; it will be discussed on Wednesday in the Plan and Budget Committee.

The Cabinet met on Monday; on the agenda was the fund crisis that caused sharp falls on Borsa Istanbul in September (BirGün, Cumhuriyet, Sözcü, BBC Türkçe). Speaking after the meeting, President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan announced that principal payments of up to 1 million lira to fund investors would begin in October, once the draft law passes Parliament (Bloomberg HT, Sözcü, Halk TV). Erdoğan also said that those who made unjust gains through speculation would pay the price (Milliyet, Rudaw); he described criticism aimed at the ruling ranks as “character assassination” (BirGün). A fund is a structure that pools the money of many investors in a common pool and puts it into shares and bonds; what is being discussed in this crisis is how and when the principal of the investors in those pools will be returned. “Principal” means the original amount deposited; we saw no statement on whether returns will be paid.

The number of investors affected by the crisis is 455,758; Cumhuriyet wrote on Monday morning that 43,000 of them had been paid their claims and more than 400,000 were waiting; we have not seen this breakdown separately confirmed by the government. The 9-article draft law concerning investors will be taken up on Wednesday, Oct. 14, in the Plan and Budget Committee of the Grand National Assembly of Türkiye (TBMM) (Cumhuriyet). So the “October” date Erdoğan gave rests on a text that must first pass the committee and the General Assembly; we have not yet seen the details of the text.

On the investigation front, the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office opened an ex officio investigation into those sharing documents marked “confidential” on social media and digital platforms; according to Cumhuriyet, the basis is Article 285 of the Turkish Penal Code, which regulates breach of the confidentiality of an investigation (Sözcü, Cumhuriyet, DW Türkçe). Cumhuriyet also wrote that high-value outflows from the Tera and Pusula funds in the first half of September are being examined and that these, at “401 billion lira in 16 days,” raised suspicion of a leak; we saw this figure only in that newspaper. The Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF) announced it would recruit assistant fund auditors, assistant experts and lawyers (Sözcü). Cevdet Yılmaz, for his part, spoke of decisions on measures and liquidation covering 7 companies and 131 funds (Sözcü).

Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz, who chairs the Fund Coordination Board, said public resources would not be used to redress the victims’ grievances and said “Let no one cast a shadow over these markets” (BirGün); he also noted that the State Supervisory Council was examining the institutions (BBC Türkçe). What is certain now: the Cabinet’s taking up the matter, Erdoğan’s October pledge and the confidentiality investigation. What is not certain: the exact day of the first payment, who will be paid first, the fate of claims above 1 million lira and the final text of the law. What has been announced does not yet fully answer the question of how much of their money the victims will get back, and when. The parliamentary calendar will be decisive this week. Erdoğan’s word is not a payment schedule with a fixed date but a conditional sentence: if the law passes, it will begin in October. A text that passes the committee must also be voted on in the General Assembly; we have not yet seen the dates of these stages announced.

On the opposition front, fund victims visited the Yeni Parti group; Murat Emir called on investors to organise (BirGün, Cumhuriyet). So the matter is not only a payment timetable; who will be held responsible, why names have not been disclosed and how the investigation will be conducted are also on politics’ agenda. The figures and dates in these columns are given to the extent that government sources and two independent publications agree; single-source claims are in the unverified section.

World · GulfPress ✓ 9 outlets · Houthi claim and Saudi statement differ

New missile attack on Riyadh on Monday evening: Saudi coalition says the missile was destroyed, Houthis say they targeted the airport; Erdoğan says “We stand with Saudi Arabia”

In Riyadh on Monday evening the early warning system sounded a danger alarm and three separate explosions were heard (Cumhuriyet, BirGün, Sözcü). The Saudi-led Arab Coalition said a ballistic missile fired from Yemen at Riyadh had been destroyed by air defence; the Houthis announced that they had targeted Riyadh airport with ballistic missiles (BirGün, FinancialJuice, GMA). We saw no casualty information from this attack. A Houthi official said that if the US enters the Yemen war they would respond with a “swift and painful” response (Straits Times, Le Figaro, Vanguard). Figures for the dead and injured vary by source; we could not confirm a single total. According to DW Türkçe, the attack on Sunday had killed 1 person and injured 11; in the first attack on Saturday 12 people had died.

BirGün wrote that 10 attacks had been carried out on five airports in a week and that 19 people had died. Livemint reported that Saudi Arabia had closed Riyadh airport until Oct. 17, and NOS and Interia reported that the closure had been extended; Saudi Arabia announced that it would keep global energy supply flowing under all conditions (Dünya, Bloomberg HT); Delta Air Lines postponed Atlanta–Riyadh flights to March 2027 (Sözcü, Bloomberg HT, The Hill). Erdoğan said that Türkiye stood with Saudi Arabia “against unlawful attacks” (DW Türkçe). Iran was reported to have offered mediation between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia (Straits Times, NYT); a Saudi prince’s pledge of a response “of equal seriousness” is single-source. AP and PBS wrote that Saudi officials were examining why defence systems had failed to stop the deadly attack, while the Financial Times wrote that the attacks targeted Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman’s economic vision.

In trial over the Saraçhane protests, 44 of 45 young people acquitted; one defendant sentenced for “incitement to commit a crime”

In the case with 45 defendants brought against young people who were at Saraçhane during the March 19 protests, the court ruled on Monday: acquittal for 44 people (Sözcü, Cumhuriyet). Cumhuriyet wrote that the young people were tried on the grounds that they took part in the protests held at Saraçhane after the detention of IBB Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, and that one defendant was sentenced for “incitement to commit a crime”; we could not verify the length of the sentence or the reasoning.

This is not the first acquittal among the Saraçhane cases: according to Turkish Minute, 87 people were acquitted in similar cases last November, and according to MLSA, 35 people in March this year. We could not find the court’s name, the prosecutor’s opinion or the reasoning for the decision in two sources. Acquittals can, as a rule, be taken to an istinaf appeal (the regional court of appeal); we did not see whether the prosecutor’s office will take such a step.

World · SecurityPress ✓ 8 outlets · Russia link is Poland’s claim

Two Turkish citizens detained in Germany over fire at Starlink station in Poland; Warsaw links the incident to Russia

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk announced on Monday that two Turkish citizens had been detained in Germany in connection with a fire last month at a Starlink ground station in Poland (Reuters, DW, Tagesschau, Spiegel, Zeit, FAZ, Straits Times). Polish officials linked the incident to Russia; official Tomasz Siemoniak said prosecutors had no doubt about cooperation with foreign intelligence services (TVN24).

The identity of the suspects, the grounds on which and the place where they are held in Germany, and whether they will be extradited to Poland are not clear in the first reports; we saw no indictment information. We saw neither Moscow’s response nor a statement from Türkiye. The “Russia link” claim for now belongs to the Polish authorities.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 8 outlets · no official Kremlin approval seen

Trump’s “energy ceasefire” faces first test: Zelensky says Russia keeps hitting energy facilities, EU says “we have yet to see whether Russia will comply”; Trump defends Russian diesel deal

Zelensky said that despite the energy ceasefire Trump announced, Russia continues to hit Ukraine’s energy facilities (PBS, LBC). According to the Moscow Times, the Kremlin said talks were continuing. EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs Kaja Kallas welcomed the ceasefire but added that it has yet to be seen whether Russia will abide by it (FinancialJuice, Ukrainska Pravda). NBC News wrote that energy prices rose after the ceasefire failed to move either Russia or Ukraine.

Trump defended the diesel deal made with Russia, saying he made it “for the American people”; lawmakers from both parties criticised the deal (Reuters, Ukrainska Pravda, Mint). US envoy Kushner said the deal was an effort to soften Russia (FinancialJuice). Zelensky said that if Putin rejects the ceasefire Ukraine will continue its attacks (Bloomberg HT); in the evening he announced on his own Telegram account that a Shahed-type UAV had struck a multi-storey building in Kyiv, killing two people and injuring nine.
