Tour bus and TIR collide at Manisa motorway toll booths: 11 dead, 27 injured; according to first findings, the bus missed the booth and was struck while reversing

A detention order was issued for two drivers and the judicial investigation continues. The exact cause of the crash is not yet known.

On Sunday morning at the toll booths of the Gebze–Orhangazi–İzmir Motorway in Akhisar district, Manisa, a tour bus and a TIR collided; 11 people died and 27 were injured (Hürriyet, Milliyet, Habertürk, DHA, Sol, Cumhuriyet). Health Minister Kemal Memişoğlu said the injured were being treated in hospitals. The bus, travelling from Bursa to the hot springs in Denizli’s Pamukkale district, was, according to first findings, struck by the vehicle behind it while reversing after missing the Akhisar toll booths. Sources vary on the time of the crash between 11.00 and 11.11; the governor’s office said “around 11.00.”

The prosecutor’s office issued a detention order for the drivers of the two vehicles involved; most reports say two drivers are in custody, while one site reported three detentions, and the source of this difference is unclear. The type of the colliding vehicle also varies by source: Hürriyet, Milliyet and Habertürk wrote TIR, while Anadolu Agency, TRT Haber and Sol used the word tractor unit (çekici). One report says the vehicle was loaded with timber; we saw this in only one source. The judicial investigation will determine the exact cause of the crash. “Reversing” is relayed as an initial finding, not a settled conclusion.

Under the Highway Traffic Law, reversing on motorways is prohibited; DailyDrop adds this as context, and it does not mean the drivers are guilty. Sözcü, Cumhuriyet and Ekonomim wrote that footage of the moment of the crash and of the bus during its rest stop had been published; because we have not independently verified the content of this footage, we give no detail. Yeni Parti chair Özgür Özel issued a message of condolence for those who lost their lives (Cumhuriyet). We saw no official collective statement on the identities of the dead or on aid to their families.

What is certain now: that the incident took place at the Akhisar toll booths, the figures of 11 dead and 27 injured, and that the prosecutor’s office issued a detention order for two drivers. What is not certain: the type and cargo of the colliding vehicle, the number of detentions, the exact time of the crash and the reason for the reversing. A driver who misses a toll booth must continue to the next exit and turn around there; reversing creates a deadly risk both for the driver and for those behind. In the investigation, the expert report, camera footage and the drivers’ statements will be decisive; any arrest request depends on the decision of the prosecutor’s office and the judge.

World · GulfPress ✓ 8 outlets · Aramco claim not confirmed

Second attack on Riyadh airport, Dammam airport also targeted; Houthis claim responsibility, four countries including Türkiye condemn

The Saudi Civil Aviation Authority said a new attack was carried out on Sunday on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and that there were injuries; the BBC wrote of a second strike in two days. In the first attack on Saturday, 12 people died and 309 were injured (Sözcü, Cumhuriyet, AP). The Houthis claimed responsibility; they also said they had hit the airport in Dammam and Aramco facilities. Saudi civil aviation officials confirmed on Sunday evening that King Fahd Airport in Dammam had been targeted; the Aramco claim was not confirmed (Ekonomim, Dünya, FinancialJuice).

Türkiye, Egypt, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia condemned the attacks in a joint statement, called for them to stop immediately and backed Riyadh (Halk TV, Haber7, Habertürk, Dünya). US Secretary of State Rubio condemned the attack (Reuters). According to the Financial Times, oil executives pulled out of the energy congress in Riyadh; Reuters wrote that the Saudi stock exchange fell. Saudi Arabia extended the airport’s closure after the first attack (AP); the Houthis again warned airlines and passengers not to use Saudi airports (Reuters). The report that the death toll rose to 13 is single-source.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 8 outlets · no Kremlin approval seen

Trump declares “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine; Zelensky says “I know nothing about it, but we support it” and that Russia hit a power plant afterwards

On Sunday, US President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that an energy ceasefire “taking effect immediately” had been reached between Russia and Ukraine and urged the parties not to break it (Reuters, USA Today, CNBC, The Guardian). Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky was reported to have greeted it to NBC as “news to me”; on Telegram he wrote that what matters is for the ceasefire to become reality, and said Ukraine was awaiting details from the US. We saw no statement that the Kremlin had approved the ceasefire.

Zelensky said Russia hit an energy plant after the announcement (ABC News, ANSA, Le Monde, Reuters); independent confirmation is limited. The Financial Times wrote that the US had warned it could block arms shipments to Ukraine because of attacks on Russian refineries; Zelensky said that if Russia stopped energy attacks, Ukraine would stop its refinery attacks (Reuters). Republican Senator Tillis noted that Trump’s Russian diesel deal could be illegal (Reuters, Politico). Macron said “We stand behind a genuine energy ceasefire backed by Ukraine” (Ekonomim).

Agenda · MaritimePress ✓ 7 outlets · cargo details differ by source

Tanker breakdown off Kanlıca: ship narrowly avoids running aground, Bosphorus ship traffic suspended in both directions

The Malta-flagged tanker IVYAN suffered an engine failure off Kanlıca in the Istanbul Bosphorus at about 19.10 on Sunday evening, drifted and dropped anchor; the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure temporarily suspended ship traffic in the Bosphorus in both directions (T24, BirGün, Anadolu Agency, Cumhuriyet, Habertürk, Dünya). The ship, bound from Bulgaria to Italy, was reported to have narrowly avoided running aground; Coastal Safety boats and the Kurtarma-7 and Kurtarma-11 tugboats were sent to the area.

The ship is written to be 107 or 110 metres long; sources diverge on the cargo: BirGün and Habertürk say “chemical tanker” while Anadolu Agency writes that it carried 6,000 tonnes of vegetable oil. The reports gave no information on a leak. The ship was said to be going to be secured at the Küçükçekmece anchorage; we could not verify when traffic reopened.

Agenda · Public healthPress ✓ 6 outlets · the cause will be settled by laboratory results

Mains water alarm in Tokat: 2,914 people went to health facilities, governor’s office asks that tap water not be drunk until sample results arrive

According to the Tokat Governor’s Office, as of 08.00 on Sunday morning 2,914 people had gone to health facilities with complaints of nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhoea since Friday evening; some headlines write that the number “approached 3,000,” while the official figure is 2,914 (Cumhuriyet, Yeni Şafak, Aydınlık, BirGün, Ekonomim, Diken). The governor’s office said 18 children were being monitored in hospitals and their general condition was good.

Water samples were sent to the Public Health Reference Laboratory in Ankara; results were expected within three days, and one report said new sample results could be announced on Monday. The governor’s office said mains water should not be drunk or used in cooking, and could be used for non-drinking purposes such as cleaning. The municipality said there was no finding that sewage had mixed into the network. That the complaints come from the water is not settled until laboratory results arrive.
