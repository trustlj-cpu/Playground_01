In the fund crisis SPK presses on with liquidations, BIST 100 ends the week down 4.88 percent; bankers say “no risk”

About 130 funds of seven portfolio management companies are being liquidated. The Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office is running the investigation; no interim payment schedule for investors has been announced yet.

The Capital Markets Board (SPK) has decided to liquidate the funds of seven portfolio management companies. Bloomberg HT gives the number as 130 and other reports say 131; because of that gap DailyDrop writes “about 130.” Seventeen funds of A1 Capital, Bulls and Pardus are on the list. According to SPK’s October 1 announcement, liquidation starts with funds that have “money market” in their name and moves from those with the most investors to those with the fewest.

Under SPK’s September 30 decision, investors in the Tera, Pusula, Atlas and Hedef funds whose reconciliation is complete will receive an interim payment of up to 1 million lira; the payment date has not been announced (Hürriyet). On Sunday Vakıfbank General Manager Osman Arslan said the situation was temporary and posed no risk to the banking sector, and that no customer had asked for liquidity because of the funds (Bloomberg HT, Foreks). The chair of the Banks Association of Türkiye, Alpaslan Çakar, gave a similar assessment. According to agenda summaries, SPK also extended the equity ratio requirement for margin trading.

On Borsa Istanbul, the BIST 100 closed on Friday at 12,270.18, up 0.17 percent; trading volume was 124.8 billion lira. The index finished the week down 629.17 points, or 4.88 percent, from 12,899.35. Its lowest level of the week was 11,926.04 (Halk TV, CNN Türk, Referans). The bankers’ “no risk” remark is their own assessment; DailyDrop cannot verify it independently.

Back-to-back quakes of 4.8 and 4.9 in Adana and 5.2 in Osmaniye; no serious damage reported

According to AFAD data, two earthquakes of magnitude 4.8 at 14:19 and 4.9 at 14:36 struck the Saimbeyli district of Adana on Sunday, October 4. Seven minutes later, at 14:43, another quake of magnitude 5.2 was recorded in the Sumbas district of Osmaniye at a depth of about 7 kilometers (Hürriyet, Habertürk, Haberler.com, 24 Saat).

Interior Minister Mustafa Çiftçi said there had been no adverse developments up to that point. The shaking was also felt in Mersin, Gaziantep and Kilis, and people went out into the streets. We could not verify the value Kandilli Observatory gave for the Sumbas quake.

Detained Central Referee Committee chair Ferhat Gündoğdu resigns

The Turkish Football Federation (TFF) announced that the detained chair of the Central Referee Committee (MHK), Ferhat Gündoğdu, has submitted his resignation in writing. The petition arrived through his lawyers; the reasons given were the proper course of the judicial process and keeping the MHK running without disruption (Milliyet, Euronews Türkçe, ensonhaber, Net Haber). Sources give the date of the resignation as October 3 or 4.

Gündoğdu is in custody as part of the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office’s illegal betting and sports crimes investigation, known as the “referees file.” According to the reports, seven TFF officials, including him, were detained on September 29. TFF said the new MHK chair and members would be determined in the coming days; there is no report of an appointment yet. His lawyers said they believe in his innocence.

Politics · ProcessPress ✓ 5 outlets · remarks are DEM Party’s Buldan’s own

Pervin Buldan: Öcalan’s meetings with journalists and NGOs could begin in October

Pervin Buldan, a member of the DEM Party’s İmralı delegation, said Abdullah Öcalan could meet journalists, writers and representatives of civil society organizations in October. Buldan said “we may see a concrete development in the coming days” (T24, BirGün, Artı Gerçek, Bold Medya). Some headlines say the date has been “set,” but the remarks themselves point only to the month of October and “the coming days”; there is no report that a meeting has taken place.

Buldan said Öcalan would take on a coordinating role in both the disarmament stage and the political and democratic restructuring stage, and that a “council” should be formed by common reason. She asked for the meetings to be placed under legal guarantee and expects the National Security Council (MGK) decision on implementing the law to be taken without delay so that the process can move forward. These demands are the expectations of a political party; there is no commitment to this effect from the government.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 5 outlets · prices attributed to market data

Tanker traffic in Hormuz at a two-month low, oil stays above $100; WTI fell 1.90% on Friday

US President Donald Trump had rejected Iran’s plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz on September 28; Iran’s offer made the reopening conditional on the release of frozen assets and an end to the US naval blockade (Al Jazeera). Before the war, about one fifth of the world’s oil passed through the strait. On the last day of the week, tanker traffic in Hormuz was reported to have fallen to its lowest level in two months, and a dozen attacks were counted between September 28 and October 2 (IndexBox, OilPrice).

On Friday, US crude WTI closed at $91.11, down 1.90 percent; Brent was reported to be trading in the $102-103 range. For Türkiye, which depends heavily on imported energy, high oil means pressure on fuel prices and the current account deficit. There is no official statement or report of a new agreement; there is no verified information that Hormuz has been reopened.
