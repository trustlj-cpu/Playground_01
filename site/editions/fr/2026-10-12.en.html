World · UkrainePress ✓ 8 outlets · Moscow has not confirmed that the truce is being applied

“Energy ceasefire”: Trump announces it, Zelensky says he knew nothing of it, and Russia hits a power plant. Senators call the diesel deal possibly illegal

On Sunday, Donald Trump claimed that Moscow and Kyiv had agreed to stop targeting each other’s energy infrastructure. Volodymyr Zelensky is waiting for the details, sets his conditions and offers sixty days of full truce in return for elections, while Russian strikes continue.

On Sunday, Oct. 11, at around 4:30 p.m. in Paris, Donald Trump announced that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy ceasefire,” “effective immediately”: each side would stop targeting the other’s power plants, electricity grids and refineries (Reuters, PBS, NBC News, CNA). Volodymyr Zelensky said he was learning of the announcement: “it’s news to me” (NBC News). Kyiv then said it was waiting for the details of the American proposals, while noting that Russia was continuing its attacks (Reuters). Indeed, a power plant was hit after the announcement (ABC News, Inquirer), in Kharkiv according to Mr. Zelensky (Yahoo), and Russian strikes killed six people in two Ukrainian cities (Reuters). The sources consulted cite no official confirmation from Moscow. The Ukrainian president sets his conditions: Kyiv will stop targeting Russian refineries if Russia halts its attacks on energy (Reuters), and he will hold elections if Moscow accepts sixty days of full ceasefire (Reuters, CNA, Straits Times, Le Figaro). This is his answer to Donald Trump, who had demanded “a new president” for Ukraine on Saturday, the day after the Russian diesel deal. In Kyiv, residents refuse to let a foreign president dictate whom they should elect (Le Figaro).

In the United States, the diesel deal is contested. Republican senator Thom Tillis considers it possibly illegal (Reuters, Politico, USA Today), and a bipartisan group of senators is asking Mr. Trump to “back down” (PBS). The Washington Post notes criticism both in the United States and abroad. Germany backs Mr. Zelensky (Reuters), and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney says he supports an energy truce (FinancialJuice, Inquirer). British Prime Minister Andy Burnham discussed the idea of a lasting energy truce with Mr. Trump (FinancialJuice, single source). For the European Union, lifting sanctions on Russian diesel gives Moscow “the means to wage war” (Le Monde), and American, Ukrainian and European negotiators had held talks in Miami (Le Monde, Reuters). According to the Financial Times, cited by Reuters, Washington is said to be threatening to block arms deliveries because of the strikes on refineries: single source, unconfirmed. On the markets, Wall Street ended Friday higher: the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83% to 51,654.95. A barrel of WTI rose 0.39% to $91.85, and gold 1.43% to $4,216.3. On Sunday evening, oil was still climbing with the attacks in the Middle East (Bloomberg). The euro was worth $1.1206 according to Friday’s ECB reference rate. Friday’s CAC 40 close could not be confirmed with two sources; the Paris Bourse reopens this morning.

Politics · 2027 presidential electionPress ✓ 9 outlets · score given by France 24

Raphaël Glucksmann wins the social-democratic left’s primary outright in the first round, with 66% of the vote

On Sunday evening, Oct. 11, Member of the European Parliament Raphaël Glucksmann, founder of Place publique, was chosen as the social-democratic left’s candidate for the 2027 presidential election, in the first round, with 66% of the vote according to France 24 (franceinfo, Le Monde, Le Figaro, BFMTV). Olivier Faure, first secretary of the Socialist Party, Ségolène Royal, Jérôme Guedj and Emmanuel Maurel are beaten (Le Figaro, BFMTV). The second round, scheduled for Oct. 16 and 17 if needed according to LCP, will therefore not take place. Former president François Hollande congratulated the winner by message, and Mr. Glucksmann held out his hand to him (Le Figaro, BFMTV, Le Monde). He says he embodies those who believe the left must break with La France insoumise (Le Monde) and promises a “punk campaign” (Libération). Olivier Faure accepts defeat “with wisdom” (BFMTV); Ségolène Royal quipped: “The intern won the primary, unfortunately” (BFMTV); Green candidate Marine Tondelier regrets “the choice of retreat” (BFMTV). As he left his election-night party, the winner was hit with a throw of flour (BFMTV). Final turnout is not given in the sources consulted: on Saturday at 8 p.m., more than 105,000 of the 140,000 registered voters had voted. The same day, in Clermont-Ferrand, Jean-Luc Mélenchon promised to scrap Parcoursup (Le Monde, Libération, Le Figaro). Yannick Jadot, a supporter of the winner, says he is “very happy” (BFMTV).

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 7 outlets · tolls given by the Saudi civil aviation authority

Riyadh airport: new attack on Sunday, one dead and eleven wounded, a day after a toll of 12 dead

On Sunday, Oct. 11, the passenger terminal of King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh was hit by a projectile and a fire broke out (PBS, GMA, Inquirer, 1News). The Saudi civil aviation authority reports one dead, an Indian national according to Hindustan Times and The Hindu, and eleven wounded; the airport in Dammam, in the east of the country, was also targeted on Sunday evening (Asharq Al-Awsat, FinancialJuice). On Saturday, the first attack had left 12 dead and more than 300 wounded; one Frenchwoman is among the wounded (Le Monde).

The Houthis, Iran-backed rebels from Yemen, claimed several strikes on Saudi airports and infrastructure (France 24, RTBF, Le Monde) and renewed their warning to airlines (Reuters, CBS News). Canada and the United Kingdom advise against traveling through Saudi airports (Global News, FinancialJuice). The foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia and Iran spoke with each other (FinancialJuice, single source).

World · MigrationPress ✓ 7 outlets · UN toll, revised upward

Shipwreck off Djibouti: at least 139 migrants dead and 84 missing

The sinking of a boat that left Djibouti for Yemen was announced on Sunday, Oct. 11. The toll, released by a UN agency, is 139 dead and 84 missing (Le Monde, franceinfo, Le Temps, PBS, CNA). The first tolls spoke of 109 dead and 116 missing (RFI, Le Figaro, Reuters). Children are among the victims: 15 according to Reuters.

This crossing between the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula is known for its shipwrecks. The figures may still change: not all of the missing have been found.

Society · EducationPress ✓ 5 outlets · figure from the Defender of Rights, cited by BFMTV

High-school anger: the Defender of Rights has received 28 referrals, Mélenchon vows to abolish Parcoursup

The Defender of Rights, the independent authority that watches over citizens’ rights, says it has received 28 referrals since the start of the high-school movement (BFMTV). On the government’s consultation platform, class schedules and the deterioration of school buildings top the list of concerns (BFMTV, 20 Minutes).

On Sunday in Clermont-Ferrand, before a packed hall, Jean-Luc Mélenchon, the La France insoumise candidate, promised to scrap Parcoursup, which he calls “the ignominy” (Le Monde, Libération, Le Figaro, 20 Minutes, franceinfo). A woman protester filed a complaint with the IGPN, the police oversight body, against a police officer for sexual assault during a march in Paris (single source, unverified).
