Strikes hit all three Kyiv power plants without warning: Zelensky accuses Washington and says he feels “betrayed”

Russia targeted Ukrainian energy despite Donald Trump’s call for a truce, and Kyiv accuses the United States of no longer warning it of launches or letting it use Starlink as before. The reasons for these restrictions are not established.

On the night from Tuesday, October 13 into Wednesday, October 14, Russia again struck Ukraine, first its energy grid. A few days after Donald Trump’s call for an energy truce, all three of Kyiv’s thermal power plants were targeted, according to Le Monde; the Kyiv Post speaks of ballistic missiles and other sites hit in neighboring regions. At least six people died (France 24, CBS News; franceinfo counts seven), and Volodymyr Zelensky says 600,000 people were left without power. He denounced “a show of contempt by Russia toward America” (Le Monde). Only the Kyiv Post reports the Kremlin’s response: that the truce was never precisely defined. On Wednesday, a drone strike killed at least six people and wounded twelve in Lutsk, in the west of the country, a region rarely targeted (Le Figaro, BFMTV). In the night from Wednesday to Thursday, three people died in the Kirovohrad oblast, according to the local military administration cited by Le Monde.

For the second night in a row, the population was not warned in advance (RFI, Reuters). Kyiv blames its American allies, who reportedly did not pass on their intelligence about the launches (RFI, franceinfo); CBS News writes that the Trump administration “may have” withheld these warnings. Our sources report no American reaction. At the same time, Ukraine accuses SpaceX of throttling the military use of Starlink and Starshield, its military version, which disrupts its drones (Reuters, DW, PBS). Ukrainian sources cited by BFMTV say the connection cuts out above 160 km/h, which penalizes fast drones. Elon Musk passes the decision on to the American government (20 Minutes, Ouest-France, BBC, The Hill); Kyiv says it suffered these limits without notice. The Japan Times, the only one to report it, cites a senior Ukrainian official saying Washington had threatened to stop this intelligence.

On Wednesday, Volodymyr Zelensky was in London, received at Downing Street by Prime Minister Andy Burnham (RFI, BFMTV). In an interview with Piers Morgan, excerpts of which circulated that same evening, he says he feels “betrayed” by Donald Trump’s recent announcements, notably the agreement on Russian diesel, and finds it “painful” to see the American president “choose a side, not ours” (BFMTV, franceinfo, Reuters). He nonetheless says he is “counting on the partnership” with the United States (BFMTV, Le Figaro). The Kremlin claims the delivery contracts will be signed “in the very next few days” (Le Figaro, BFMTV); the head of the Italian government, Giorgia Meloni, sees in it a “very risky choice” (Reuters). The Financial Times, picked up by Reuters, adds that American negotiators raised in Miami the unfreezing of some of the Russian assets to restart talks, which European officials refused.

Economy · United StatesPress ✓ 11 outlets · no index level confirmed

United States: inflation hits 3.7% in September, AI stocks slide from Wall Street to Tokyo

U.S. inflation reached 3.7% in September, driven by the surge in oil prices (Le Monde); CBS News adds that gasoline prices jumped 9% over the month and that Donald Trump is looking for “bright spots” in these figures. Reuters notes that core inflation, excluding energy and food, is also firmer. The release revived bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike. On Wednesday, Wall Street fell, dragged down by artificial intelligence stocks (VnEconomy, MarketWatch); Le Figaro cites the plunge of CoreWeave, amid worries over the debt that finances AI. On Thursday morning, Asia followed: the Nikkei plunged at the open (New Straits Times), SK Hynix lost about 8% (CNBC) and gold gave up ground (Bloomberg, Straits Times). The dollar has nonetheless retreated from its eighteen-month high, as Fed officials tempered expectations (Reuters, FMT): Mary Daly considers further hikes likely but not urgent, and not necessarily at the October 27-28 meeting. In Tokyo, Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama renewed her warnings on the yen, which fell to its lowest of the month against the dollar (Bloomberg, single source). Le Figaro notes that the interest rate on U.S. debt is soaring in the face of this acceleration, and American retirees will see their pensions raised by 3.6% in 2027 (Reuters, AP).

Sport · Champions LeaguePress ✓ 11 outlets · scores confirmed by several sources

Champions League: PSG beat Manchester City 4-2, Barcola scores a hat trick, Real Madrid come from behind against Roma

On Wednesday evening at the Etihad, Paris Saint-Germain beat Manchester City 4-2 on matchday two of the Champions League (Le Monde, Le Figaro, RFI, France 24). Désiré Doué and Ousmane Dembélé scored within 1 minute 39 seconds (L’Équipe) and the Parisians led 3-0 at the break (Ouest-France) before being shaken up: Erling Haaland scored twice in the second half (Punch, Dân trí). Le Monde speaks of a “two-faced Paris”; Luis Enrique admits his team was lucky, City having wasted many chances (New Straits Times, FMT). According to the Japan Times, it was the Citizens’ first home match since their conviction for serious breaches of the Premier League’s financial rules; The Conversation calls it a scandal that has become political.

Elsewhere, Bradley Barcola scored the first hat trick of his career with Liverpool, winners 6-3 over LASK in Linz (Le Figaro, L’Équipe, Punch). In Rome, Real Madrid won 4-3 against Roma, scoring three times late in the match; Kylian Mbappé, who scored on his hundredth appearance in the competition, became the youngest player to reach that mark, ahead of Lionel Messi (Le Figaro, Ouest-France, Punch). The evening produced 78 goals in 18 matches, a record (L’Équipe, TNT Sports), and all four Italian clubs lost on the same night, a first (FMT, Straits Times).

France · JusticePress ✓ 9 outlets · motive not established

Aubagne: 14-year-old middle schooler placed under formal investigation for attempted murders “in connection with a terrorist enterprise”

The 14-year-old who fired on Monday, October 12 at the principal and a teacher at the Lakanal middle school in Aubagne was placed under formal investigation on Wednesday for attempted murders in connection with a terrorist enterprise, then remanded in pretrial detention (Le Monde, Le Figaro, franceinfo). Before the national anti-terrorism prosecutor’s office, he admitted the facts and said he had been “influenced” by a movement fascinated by mass killings, the True Crime Community, which he “probably joined” (franceinfo, RFI). He disputes, however, having wanted to kill the people targeted (Le Figaro, BFMTV), and Le Figaro writes that his act had reportedly been planned for several months.

This is the first time French anti-terrorism justice has directly linked an act of violence to this online community, which glorifies the perpetrators of killings (Le Monde); according to a researcher cited by that paper, nine attacks carried out in French schools since 2008 could be linked to it, an estimate that is his alone. Ouest-France reports that the teenager said in police custody that he wanted to take “revenge” for harassment he attributes to his teachers and classmates; this motive, given by a single outlet, is not established. At the school, the senior education counselor who disarmed him is hailed as a quiet hero (Libération, 20 Minutes).

Society · EducationPress ✓ 9 outlets · injury tolls disputed

High-school mobilization: 7,950 people in police custody, 937 police officers and gendarmes injured according to Matignon, and October 17 looming

The Justice Ministry reported 7,950 people in police custody since the start of the movement, including 6,773 minors and 1,177 adults, which led to 2,735 prosecutions, 2,225 alternatives to prosecution and 1,263 cases dropped (Le Figaro, Libération, Le Monde). Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu counted 937 police officers and gendarmes injured, 248 students and nearly 150 education staff (franceinfo, BFMTV). Libération points out that the law-enforcement figure rests solely on the officers’ own statements and says nothing about the severity of the injuries. The Alliance police union has filed a complaint against the State for “endangering the lives of others” (franceinfo, 20 Minutes), the FCPE, a federation of parents’ associations, denounces disproportionate interventions against parents (franceinfo), and the actor Omar Sy calls the authorities’ response “lamentable” (Le Figaro, Le Monde).

Police violence is fueling the anger. In Lens, the family of Evan, 15, whose hand was torn off by a grenade, has joined the case as a civil party (Le Monde, Libération). In Tours, a police officer has been placed under formal investigation for “aggravated violence” after a 16-year-old high-school student lost the use of an eye on September 30; the officer’s lawyer says it is “not established” that the injury came from an LBD shot (franceinfo), and the IGPN has opened 36 investigations (Libération). On Wednesday, National Education Minister Édouard Geffray told the Senate that two-thirds of the 3,000 promised substitute-teacher positions had been assigned; Le Monde notes they will not cover all absences.

On Thursday at 5:30 p.m., the youth organizations are due to meet the ministers of National Education and of Higher Education (Le Monde). One of them, with a few student unions, is calling on people to join the Yellow Vests on Saturday, October 17, a day of calls to protest against the high cost of living that the CGT is also relaying; the scale of the day remains hard to predict (Le Monde, franceinfo, Ouest-France).
