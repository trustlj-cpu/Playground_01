Society · EducationPress ✓ 8 outlets · turnout disputed: 266,000 per the Interior Ministry, 450,000 per the organizers

Between 266,000 and 450,000 march for high-school students. Lecornu defends law and order and targets La France insoumise

“Act III” of the movement brought marches to cities across France, with union backing and clashes in Paris. The government promises a gradual return to classes and a “corrected” Parcoursup.

Marches formed from late morning in the big cities. The Interior Ministry counted 266,000 demonstrators nationwide, the organizers 450,000, a range that Le Monde cites in its count in pictures. Clashes broke out in the Paris march early in the afternoon: burning trash cans, tear gas, thrown stones, according to Le Figaro. In Marseille, a “tense” march chanted “down with the police state.” In Lyon, a collective of parents rallied behind the young people. Education unions, the CGT and the Confédération paysanne are backing the movement, and Le Monde notes that the mobilization is “growing and drawing in many supporters.”

On the government side, Sébastien Lecornu defended public order and again targeted La France insoumise, accused of fanning the flames. Minister Édouard Geffray promises an answer “within eight days” for high schools without replacement teachers and a “progressive and gradual” return to classes from Wednesday, with a half-day set aside as “high-school time.” France 24 mentions a “corrected” Parcoursup. Students and high-schoolers presented “ten emergency measures.” At the Palais-Bourbon, lawmakers pressed the executive over what they call “the shame” of the blockades. The movement is spilling across the border: in Belgium, high-school protests turned violent in several cities, including Liège. The first defendants are being tried in Paris and Bobigny, on charges ranging from vandalism to violence against police officers.

World · GermanyPress ✓ 7 outlets · vote count per the German press

In Germany, the far right presides over a regional parliament for the first time

At the Landtag of Saxony-Anhalt, Tobias Rausch, 35, a real estate agent and the AfD candidate, was elected speaker of the assembly with 48 votes out of 82. The AfD has 39 members, so the extra votes came from other benches. The ballot was secret, so which ones is unknown, but the firewall built against the AfD has been breached. The five BSW members had declared for him, and the CDU group had advised abstaining. It is a first for the far right since 1945, stress franceinfo, Le Monde, RFI and France 24. The AfD wants to go further: its local leader, Ulrich Siegmund, will stand in December for election as minister-president. German authorities fear a far-right government in the state.

Politics · 2027 presidential electionPress ✓ 6 outlets · RN costing, not validated by third parties

The RN promises “140 billion euros in net savings” over five years. The government calls it a “papier-mâché” budget

Marine Le Pen presented her party's counter-budget on Tuesday. It promises 140 billion euros in net savings over five years, built around an overhaul of the state and local authorities and the abolition of many agencies, according to Acteurs publics. The government replied that the budget “would shave for free” and is “papier-mâché.” Les Échos calls the promised recovery “unrealistic.” Le Figaro points to “overestimated savings, underestimated spending.”

The week is going badly for the party on another front. According to Le Monde, the affair of antisemitic writings attributed to Jordan Bardella, revealed by Mediapart, is “spoiling” the budget moment. The RN president once again “formally” disputes the authentication material published by the site.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · Houthi claims not independently verified

Houthis strike Riyadh's airport and a refinery. Turkey and Pakistan give Saudi Arabia military backing

Houthi rebels in Yemen say they targeted Riyadh's airport and a refinery, report France 24 and Les Échos. Riyadh retaliated by sending, according to Les Échos, about a hundred combat aircraft against the rebels after an attack on an oil pipeline. On Monday, at an emergency meeting in the Saudi capital, the committee of the Mecca defense pact, which binds Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey, agreed to deploy Pakistani and Turkish forces. The size and role of these troops are not specified. The conflict is part of the wider war pitting the United States, Israel and Iran against each other.

Oil, with Brent crude near $100, fell on Tuesday: that easing helped the Paris Bourse end up 0.40%, at 7,865.07 points, after Monday's slump.

Technology · Artificial intelligencePress ✓ 3 outlets · performance claims made by the company

Mistral AI unveils a new model meant to “close the gap” with the best Chinese rivals

The French start-up launched a new large language model on Tuesday, presented by Telquel as Mistral Large 4. It succeeds the previous flagship model, from December 2025. Mistral says it ranks among the best open models in the world. Its cofounder Guillaume Lample says it is on a par with the best Chinese models of a month or two ago. These comparisons are the company's own, not independently verified. According to Telquel, Mistral's models had slipped to 24th place in the Artificial Analysis ranking.

Le Monde and Les Échos present the launch as a way of keeping Europe in the race after months of waiting. Mistral is also investing in its own data centers in Europe.
