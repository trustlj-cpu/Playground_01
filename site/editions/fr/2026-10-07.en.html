After a hand was torn off in Lens, Nuñez suspends stun grenades for the high school protest movement. Eighteen IGPN investigations are open

The interior minister is acting “until light has been shed.” Student anger persists, and their organizations are asking to be received by Lecornu.

Laurent Nuñez announced on RTL on Wednesday that he was suspending the use of stun grenades “for the high school students’ movement until light has been shed” on the Lens case, where a 15-year-old student lost a hand on Monday. The director general of the national police had already suspended, as a precaution, the grenade model potentially involved. The mayor of Wingles, the young man’s home town, says he had his left hand amputated and received a graft. The IGPN has been handed eighteen investigations into injuries during the movement, the minister said. He cites a toll of 6,547 arrests, including 488 on Tuesday alone, and more than 700 police officers and gendarmes injured.

The suspension does not calm the street. Franceinfo notes that student anger persists despite the dialogue offered by Sébastien Lecornu, whose first decisions will be presented “at the end of the month.” Student and university organizations are asking to be received by the prime minister, as the ministers have announced no concrete measure. The main union of school heads rejects the ministry’s proposed “time for democracy.” The government wants to regain the initiative but remains on the defensive over policing, Le Monde notes. In Liège, Belgium, student gatherings are now banned.

Nobel Prize in Chemistry goes to France’s Henri Kagan and Japan’s Kenso Soai

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences on Wednesday honored a French-Japanese pair for a key discovery in drug manufacturing, franceinfo headlines. Henri Kagan is rewarded for his work on asymmetric synthesis, that is, on how to make selectively just one of the two mirror-image forms of a molecule, known as chirality. Kenso Soai showed, with the reaction that bears his name, that a tiny excess of one form can amplify itself by autocatalysis, a clue to the origin of life’s single “handedness.” Le Monde called it a “much anticipated” Nobel.

ChiralityScience

The property of a molecule that exists in two mirror-image forms, like a right hand and a left hand, that cannot be superimposed. Drugs are often active in only one form, hence the value of being able to make one without the other.

Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 6 outlets · close confirmed by two sources

Paris stocks lose 1.22%. French yields surge again and banks slump

Tuesday’s rebound proved to be only a parenthesis. The CAC 40 ended Wednesday at 7,769.21 points, down 1.22%, according to BFM Bourse, Boursorama and Zonebourse. Banks led the decline: Société Générale dropped 5% and BNP Paribas 3.9%. BFM Bourse attributes the slide to tension in the bond market and to oil moving higher, with Brent trading around $102 in the morning. Eurozone yields rose again. As a reminder, the 10-year OAT yield hit 4.96% intraday on Oct. 1, a high since 2002. The euro finished around $1.118, down about 0.7%.

Institutions add to the worry. The IMF laments the lack of “decisive action” on public debt, according to Le Figaro. Pimco’s chief, Emmanuel Roman, says in Le Monde that “markets are sending a serious signal to France.” Le Figaro notes that the foreign press is lashing the country’s financial situation: “the sick man of Europe,” a “Greek moment.” Les Échos ask about the risks the surge in yields poses for banks.

Politics · BudgetPress ✓ 6 outlets · committee vote, not final

2027 budget: in committee, lawmakers reject the trim to retirees’ tax allowance and unpick other savings

The National Assembly’s finance committee on Wednesday deleted Article 3 of the finance bill, which would have lowered from €4,439 to €3,000 per household the cap on the 10% tax allowance that retirees enjoy on their pensions. The government expected €1.4 billion from it. The amendments to delete, from LR, Horizons, the RN, the UDR, LFI and the Communists, were adopted, against a few votes from the government camp that defended “tax solidarity between generations.” Le Figaro adds that lawmakers are deleting other planned savings, on severance pay and on illnesses. Les Échos quote one member: “this is not sustainable.”

This is only a first round: the original text will return to lawmakers in the floor debate from Oct. 13. In all, the government wants €5.5 billion in effort from retirees, combining the allowance with a partial indexation of pensions above €1,260. Franceinfo notes that tension over pensions rose from the very first debates.

Economy · EnergyPress ✓ 4 outlets · cut targeted, not guaranteed

Fuel: Lecornu releases 10 million barrels of diesel from strategic reserves and aims for a 12 to 18 cent cut from Nov. 1

In a televised address on Wednesday evening, the prime minister announced that France will draw 10 million barrels of diesel from its reserves over three months. The diesel was bought before the surge in prices and will be sold at cost price, which should mechanically bring pump prices down by “an order of magnitude” of 12 to 18 cents per liter, he said. The government aims to apply it from Nov. 1. Les Échos headlined earlier that he “pulls out a shock measure on diesel.” The measure responds to the oil surge tied to the war in the Middle East: Brent is flirting with $100.

The measure rests on finite reserves and settles neither supply nor demand: its reach will also depend on the attitude of fuel distributors.
