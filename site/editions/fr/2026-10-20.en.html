Sébastien Lecornu asks prefects for partial, localized curfews from Monday evening and announces short-time work and a support fund for shops

The Prime Minister does not rule out a state of emergency but considers territorially targeted curfews better suited; a yellow vests coordination announces an “Act II” for Saturday, October 24.

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu announced on Monday, October 19 that he had asked prefects to “trigger a certain number of partial and localized curfews” that same evening, report BFMTV, franceinfo and Le Monde, after a weekend of violence and looting in several cities. According to franceinfo, the map of the areas concerned was due to be released in the afternoon after talks with mayors; the measure is aimed above all at protecting shops, and a curfew across all of Paris has been set aside in favor of targeted zones such as Les Halles. A state of emergency remains possible: the head of government called it “something heavy” that he could use “if it is necessary,” according to franceinfo. Le Figaro, Libération and Ouest-France detail the rest of the announcements: short-time work, a support fund for shopkeepers and curfews. Franceinfo adds talks with insurers and short-time work activated at 100% from Tuesday.

On the ground, local measures are multiplying. The prefect of Guadeloupe imposed a curfew for minors from 8 p.m. on Sunday, October 18 to 6 a.m. on Monday the 19th, according to BFMTV and Ouest-France. The town hall of Denain, in the Nord, decreed a curfew until Sunday, reports Le Figaro, with BFMTV specifying that the town hall took the decision after the damage and violence of October 17, and Ouest-France reports measures for minors in Coulaines, near Le Mans. The Minister for the City, Vincent Jeanbrun, argued for allowing a curfew “when it is necessary,” according to BFMTV, which also gathered the “dismay” of Alliance Police secretary general Stanislas Gaudon at “the absence of a response.” The mayor of Paris Centre, Ariel Weil, believes that “if we are caught off guard, a curfew or the army is better” in certain neighborhoods, according to BFMTV. For its part, a coordination of yellow vests announced an “Act II” of the demonstrations for Saturday, October 24, report franceinfo and ICI.

Justice · Tally of police custody✓ 5 outlets · final number of convictions not published

Gérald Darmanin announces 2,513 people in police custody and 67 jailed, and wants to extend custody from 48 to 72 hours for “organized crime”

Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin announced on Monday evening 2,513 people in police custody and 67 jailed “at this stage,” according to BFMTV and 20 Minutes; BFMTV specifies that more than a quarter of those in custody are awaiting a sentence. Franceinfo reports that custody linked to the violence could be extended from 48 to 72 hours for “organized crime.” Earlier, the minister had admitted “We undeniably failed this weekend” (franceinfo) and announced dozens of placements in pretrial detention (BFMTV). In Paris, Libération describes the first fast-track trials with harsh sentences, most defendants being sent to prison, while BFMTV also mentions acquittals and community service. Le Figaro notes that 64% of custody periods in the capital were extended. In Rouen, BFMTV counts twenty-nine people in custody and five fast-track trials. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez acknowledged on Monday “a loss of control” in certain large cities and offered his resignation to Emmanuel Macron, who refused it, according to BFMTV.

Politics · 2027 presidential election✓ 2 outlets · government response not reported

Bruno Retailleau asks to change the date of the presidential runoff, set for May 2, 2027, the day after May Day

Bruno Retailleau, president of the Les Républicains party and a presidential candidate, asked the government on Monday, October 19 to change the date of the 2027 election runoff, set for May 2, according to BFMTV and Le Figaro. “It is a bad date,” he said on BFMTV. Le Figaro specifies that he was a guest on the channel after the rampages of October 17 and 18 and that the runoff falls the day after May Day. The day before, on Sunday, October 18, BFMTV reported that he was already asking Emmanuel Macron to declare a state of emergency. Le Figaro headlines a request addressed to “the government” and BFMTV the Républicains presidential candidate calling for the voting day to be changed. Neither outlet reports, at this stage, any response from the government to the request on the calendar.

Emmanuel Macron begins a Gulf tour in Kuwait to secure fuel supplies, in the middle of a domestic crisis

Emmanuel Macron began a tour of the Gulf on Monday, October 19, first in Kuwait and then in the United Arab Emirates, according to BFMTV and France 24. He had announced it the day before: “To guarantee our fuel, our gas, I will continue these actions starting tomorrow morning by going to the Gulf countries,” he said, according to BFMTV. Le Monde writes that he is seeking a way out of the surge in fuel prices, and RFI describes a trip “with oil in its sights.” Le Figaro speaks of a forty-eight-hour tour to “secure supplies,” where Le Monde describes a three-day trip. Ouest-France cites energy, defense and artificial intelligence among the themes of the program. France 24 stresses that the president is leaving “far from the criticism” weighing on him in France after the weekend of violence, and Le Figaro headlines “Emmanuel Macron sets sail in the Persian Gulf.”

World · Israel✓ 7 outlets · effect of the endorsement on the vote not established

One week before Israel’s parliamentary elections, Donald Trump gives his “full and complete support” to Benjamin Netanyahu

Donald Trump gave his “full and complete support” to Benjamin Netanyahu on Sunday, October 18, one week before the parliamentary elections in Israel, report France 24 and franceinfo, which place the message on Truth Social. BFMTV headlines a “total and unreserved support for Bibi,” and RFI speaks of a late endorsement. Le Monde writes that the Israeli prime minister stages his closeness to the American president, and France 24 notes that he highlights their alliance. Gadi Eisenkot, Benjamin Netanyahu’s main opponent, replied that “only Israelis can choose their leaders,” according to Libération and franceinfo. According to i24NEWS, an average of ten polls puts the Likud and Eisenkot’s Yashar party level, with 22 seats each, with no bloc reaching on its own the 61 seats needed. The vote is set for October 27.
