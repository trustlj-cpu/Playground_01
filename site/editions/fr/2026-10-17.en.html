Europe · Ukraine✓ 7 outlets on the requested figure · EU decision not known

In Brussels, Zelensky asks for 70 billion and Macron promises not to let go, without putting a figure on it

At the European Council, the request for extra Ukrainian funding for 2027 runs up against Paris’s caution, while Starlink and the strikes on Russian refineries muddy the picture with Washington.

The European Council meeting in Brussels this Friday, October 16, was dominated by the war in Ukraine. Volodymyr Zelensky asked the European Union for an additional €70 billion in 2027, a request picked up by seven of the outlets we follow. The president of the European Commission, Ursula von der Leyen, also spoke on the subject, according to Le Monde. None of the articles consulted reports a decision: at this stage, it is a request, not a commitment.

Emmanuel Macron, who held a press conference early in the evening after a meeting with the Ukrainian president, declared: “We will not let Ukraine down” (franceinfo, BFMTV, Le Monde). He called it “vital” to give Kyiv the means to defend itself and insisted on the need to “hold the line” in the face of “ambiguities” (Le Monde). But Le Monde also observes that the head of state is stalling on the 70 billion extra funding: the political promise is clear, the budgetary answer is not.

Second file, more technical: Starlink. Two days after Kyiv’s complaint about a limit on this satellite service, indispensable to the Ukrainian army, Donald Trump says he did not give the order to restrict it (Le Figaro, Google News). Le Monde reports, in its live coverage, that the EU acknowledges it cannot replace this capability in the short term. Who limited what, and why, remains unestablished: we have only Trump’s version and the Ukrainian complaint.

Third source of friction: Ukraine has confirmed the resumption of its strikes on Russian refineries, which Trump called a “disgrace” (four outlets). Asked about the idea that it would be “harder” to talk to Zelensky than to Putin, as Trump had judged on Thursday, the Ukrainian president answered that it was on the contrary “easier” to communicate with Trump (Le Monde). The Europeans, for their part, are closing ranks behind Kyiv, while Moscow wants to appear conquering, sums up 20 Minutes. What to watch for: a European figure for 2027 aid.

France · Society✓ 3 outlets on the order · tally of figures not consolidated

October 17: Paris bans marches near the Élysée, after a month of the high-school movement

The Paris police prefect issued an order on Friday banning demonstrations on Saturday in several areas of the capital, around the Élysée and Matignon (Libération, 20 Minutes, Élysée relays). That is the best-established point of the day. According to nine outlets, Minister Laurent Nuñez expects 250 gatherings in France and authorizes the use of stun grenades, which feeds the tension. On the sidelines of the European Council, Emmanuel Macron appealed for calm, asking demonstrators “not to insult and not to break things” (BFMTV, Google News). The high-school movement is entering its second month. Le Figaro draws up a tally it judges damning: arrests, property damage, missed classes, with figures for injuries, police custody placements and the cost of the damage that, it writes, keep climbing. These figures are given only by that paper and have not been cross-checked. In Saint-Nazaire, a high school was evacuated on Friday after a deliberate fire in which young people filmed themselves (Le Figaro). Le Monde notes for its part a growing distrust of law enforcement among young people, the denunciation of police violence having become omnipresent in the marches. Ouest-France details the disruption to expect on Saturday on the roads and in transport. To watch: compliance with the ban around the Élysée, and any arrests during the day.

France · Accident✓ 3 outlets · cause not specified

Channel Tunnel: a bus and a train collide, at least 19 injured

A serious accident occurred on Thursday, October 15, at the entrance to the Channel Tunnel, in the Pas-de-Calais, near Calais: a bus and a train collided (BFMTV, Le Monde, Google News). The reported toll speaks of at least 19 injured; several outlets say instead “about twenty,” which does not contradict the first figure but shows it may still change.

Nine outlets picked up the story in our monitoring, a sign of the scale of the event. The articles consulted give neither the precise circumstances, nor the severity of each injury, nor the state of rail traffic after the accident; we do not invent them. We must therefore distinguish what is established (the collision, the place, the approximate number of injured) from what is not (the cause, any responsibilities).

The first bulletin dates from Thursday evening; the aftermath, notably the investigation and the restoration of traffic, belongs to the coming days. Travelers planning to use the cross-Channel link this weekend would do well to check their carrier’s information before leaving.

Economy · Industry✓ 2 outlets · details of the deal not public here

Automobile: the Franco-German deal goes easy on Stellantis but does not spare Renault

According to Le Monde, the Franco-German agreement on the European car industry makes subsidies conditional on production in EU countries, with at least 70% of components or value of European origin. The paper judges that Stellantis comes out rather well while Renault is not spared, though we cannot, with the excerpts available, detail why.

Le Figaro picks out another aspect: carmakers are, in its view, the big winners of the deal, because the easing of the CO2 emissions-reduction trajectory delights them, even if it comes with a quid pro quo whose detail is not in the excerpt we read. The two readings do not conflict: a more flexible climate timetable on one side, a European industrial preference on the other.

For the reader, the stakes are twofold: the price and choice of electric cars offered with public aid, and employment in European factories. This text is not yet law: its translation in Brussels and the reaction of the other capitals will need to be followed.

Politics · Budget✓ 2 outlets on the vote · limited scope

Budget: after the State’s, lawmakers reject the Social Security budget in committee

On Friday, lawmakers rejected the Social Security budget in committee, after doing the same for the State’s (Le Monde, Libération). This vote has no direct consequence for the debate in the chamber of the National Assembly, but it sends a signal about the balance of forces: no clear majority is emerging to back the government’s texts as they stand.

Meanwhile, on the 2027 budget, the départements denounce a “sleight of hand” by the government over aid to seniors, according to an article in our monitoring: they consider that the transfer of costs does not amount to genuine compensation. We do not have the exact figures put forward by the elected officials; they should be checked in the official documents.

Another decision with concrete scope for individuals: the Court of Justice of the European Union restates that in the event of an unauthorized transaction on an account, the bank must first refund its customer (Le Monde, Google News). The principle is not new, the paper stresses, but it is poorly applied.
