High school blockade enters its fourth week. Lecornu sets five workstreams, a teenager loses a hand in Lens

Between 400 and 500 high schools suspended classes on Friday, according to the education minister. The prime minister wants decisions before the end of the month. A “third act” is announced for Tuesday.

Launched on September 17 in the Île-de-France region, the student movement has spread across the country. On Friday, Education Minister Édouard Geffray counted between 400 and 500 of 3,700 high schools with classes suspended, and expected as many on Monday. In Île-de-France, regional president Valérie Pécresse announced that more than 160 of the 468 public high schools would stay closed on October 5. Le Monde sees a “singular and already historic” movement, rooted in a lack of resources and the “failures of mass schooling.” The first tallies of the crackdown are heavy: more than 5,000 people placed in police custody since the movement began, according to franceinfo.

On Sunday evening, Sébastien Lecornu sent his ministers a roadmap of “five workstreams”: replacing absent teachers, teaching conditions and school buildings, organization of the school calendar, Parcoursup and student democracy. He demands first decisions, with cost, financing and timetable, before the end of October, a first review of replacements on October 16 and a faster track for the “casseur-payeur” bill. On Monday morning in Lens (Pas-de-Calais), a 15-year-old student had his hand torn off by a grenade explosion near the Auguste-Béhal high school. He is not in danger, according to the prefect. The IGPN is investigating, and it is not yet known whether the teenager picked up the device or whether it was thrown at him. The CGT wants to “protect high schoolers’ right to demonstrate,” and the Confédération paysanne, a farmers’ union, is calling on farmers to join them on Tuesday.

In Brazil, Flávio Bolsonaro edges Lula in the first round. Runoff on Oct. 25

The 45-year-old candidate, son of the former president, won 47.03% of the vote against 45.16% for Lula, according to Superior Electoral Court figures relayed by AFP and Euronews. The gap, under two points, is the narrowest in a first round since the return to democracy. Polls had given the sitting president a slight lead up to the day before. Donald Trump hailed “a great victory.” The winner of the runoff will take office on January 5. Les Échos speaks of an “immense Bolsonarist wave,” Le Monde of a Lula who “paid the price of discontent.”

Schneider Electric buys US group PTC for more than €20 billion. Its shares fall 10% and drag the CAC 40 down

The French electrical equipment group announced on Monday the purchase of PTC, a US industrial software publisher, at $205 a share in cash, a 42.3% premium to the last price. Media reports put the equity value at about €20.1 billion, and the enterprise value at $23.7 billion including debt. It is the biggest acquisition in its history, far ahead of the 2023 purchase of Britain's Aveva, for nearly €11 billion. Regulatory approval is expected in the third quarter of 2027.

The stock market did not applaud. The share lost nearly 10% by the morning, to around €273, the group's worst session in at least a year according to Culture Financière. Analysts find the industrial logic acceptable but worry about the bill and the debt, in a software sector that artificial intelligence is weakening. With Schneider weighing heavily in the index, the CAC 40 closed down 0.80% at 7,834.10 points, the weakest market in Europe: the DAX was stable and the FTSE MIB gained 0.7%.

Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 6 outlets · intraday levels vary by source

Euro falls to its lowest level in 17 months. French debt and Spain worry markets

On Monday, the single currency fell as low as $1.116, its lowest level since May 2025, before recovering to around 1.12: it is on a fifth straight weekly decline. Currency traders point to two European trouble spots. In France, the 2027 draft budget presented on October 1, a €43 billion effort, revives doubts about the debt path, at 119% of GDP at the end of the second quarter, according to Reuters. The ten-year yield gap between the OAT and the Bund exceeded 150 basis points on Friday, a first since late 2011, before easing back to 140 on Monday. Moody's judges a parliamentary compromise on the budget “far from certain.”

In Spain, Pedro Sánchez's call for early elections adds a layer of uncertainty. The dollar also benefits from rising US rates: its index hit an eighteen-month high. In Le Monde, Banque de France governor Emmanuel Moulin warns that France risks being “strangled by interest rates.” Les Échos headlines the euro “caught up by the French debt crisis.”

Politics · BudgetPress ✓ 5 outlets · measure written into the finance bill, not yet voted

2027 budget: the state wants to take €2.1 billion from Unédic. Joint union front calls a strike for November 5

Labor Minister Jean-Pierre Farandou confirmed that the government will “levy” in 2027 the €2.1 billion surplus expected from the unemployment insurance scheme, report Le Figaro and Le Monde. Les Échos announces “a showdown” ahead. According to Unédic projections, the state will have taken more than €14 billion over five years, €4.1 billion of it in 2026. The finance bill, which aims for a deficit of 5% of GDP, provides for a €43 billion effort. It has not yet been voted: the Assembly has no absolute majority.

The same day, the joint union front called a day of strikes and demonstrations on November 5 over wages and public services. In the Senate, LR senator Christine Lavarde was named general rapporteur of the budget. Le Figaro also quotes Maxime Saada, who calls the planned VAT increase on Canal+ “absurd.”
