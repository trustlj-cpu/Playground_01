The European Union adopts nearly 1,600 new sanctions against Russia, a record, as Kyiv accuses Moscow of violating Trump’s “energy truce”

On Monday, Brussels widened its blacklist targeting Russia’s war. The Kremlin dismissed Ukraine’s idea of a sixty-day ceasefire, and the American deal on Russian diesel divides even Donald Trump’s own camp.

On Monday, Oct. 12, the European Union adopted nearly 1,600 new listings on its sanctions lists against Russia (Reuters). Le Temps counts nearly 1,650 names, “a record since the start of the war,” and the Kyiv Post 1,646. According to the latter, the package targets missile supply chains, drone makers and submarine shipyards, as well as 77 people involved in the elections organized in the occupied Ukrainian territories and seven North Korean entities or individuals. “Now is not the time to ease the pressure on Moscow,” said the EU’s foreign policy chief, Kaja Kallas (BFMTV). Europeans are also discussing restricting visas for Russian nationals (Le Monde). Le Temps notes that hybrid warfare, made up of sabotage and attacks with no signature, complicates Brussels’ task: how to sanction those who hide behind it. For its part, Moscow claims to have sold $320 billion of hydrocarbons to Europe since 2022 (Le Figaro, Ouest-France), a Russian figure that these outlets do not verify.

The truce announced on Sunday by Donald Trump remains uncertain. On Monday, Volodymyr Zelensky accused Russia of violating it, after a thermal power plant in Kharkiv was hit (Reuters, Straits Times), and said Ukraine “will respond” if Moscow does not respect it this winter (RTS, 20 Minutes). Moscow refuses to confirm that it has taken effect (RTS). The Kremlin also rejected Kyiv’s idea of a sixty-day ceasefire to hold elections, demanding a comprehensive peace agreement (Reuters, Straits Times); according to the Kyiv Post, it offers only a one-day truce, on election day. In Kyiv, a drone attack on a 25-story building left one dead and eleven wounded, according to the mayor, Vitali Klitschko (Le Monde). Donald Trump said he thinks “Putin wants to make a deal more than Zelensky” (BFMTV).

The diesel deal, which authorizes Russian fuel exports, is contested on both sides of the Atlantic: Donald Trump defends it while lawmakers from both parties complain about it (Reuters, USA Today). Three sources told Reuters that Washington sees it as a way to bring Moscow back to the negotiating table; Russia could export up to 3.5 million tonnes of diesel a month by the end of the year (Reuters). These volumes are modest and should not bring prices down, but the revenue will help Moscow refill its war chest (PBS).

Unprecedented cyberattack: refrigeration at hundreds of supermarkets paralyzed, tonnes of products thrown away

In the night from Sunday to Monday, the cold rooms and freezers of several hundred stores overheated, causing tonnes of losses (Le Monde). The chains cited by Le Figaro are Coopérative U, Intermarché, Monoprix, Carrefour and E.Leclerc. Reuters speaks of 150 Intermarché stores targeted, BFMTV of at least 300 supermarkets, CNA of more than 400 points of sale, Ouest-France of “thousands” of French and European stores: these figures diverge and none is officially confirmed. The flaw would come from software made by the Danish group Danfoss, identified as such according to Le Monde; Ouest-France mentions a common Danish supplier. An investigation has been opened, and the Paris prosecutor’s office confirmed the attack (Straits Times). No data was stolen, but fresh products had to be destroyed (Le Temps). “We threw everything away,” a store manager in Étréchy, in the Essonne, told franceinfo. The perpetrators remain unknown (Ouest-France).

The Enfants-Rouges covered market in Paris “ravaged” by fire

A fire destroyed the Enfants-Rouges covered market, in Paris’s 3rd arrondissement; City Hall announced it early on Monday, Oct. 12, saying it was “ravaged by flames” (franceinfo, Le Monde, Reuters). The mayor of Paris, Emmanuel Grégoire, lamented that the entire market had been destroyed (BFMTV). The Telegraaf mentions about a thousand square meters of stalls and restaurants destroyed. The cause was not known in the sources consulted, and only one outlet, Il Fatto Quotidiano, says there are no serious injuries.

Presented as the oldest covered food market in the capital (RTS, NYT), it has four centuries of history (Le Figaro). The market’s flagship restaurant, Les Enfants du Marché, says its story does not end here and is looking to bounce back (Le Figaro). Residents and tourists express their dismay.

France · SocietyPress ✓ 8 outlets · the motive is not established

Aubagne: a 14-year-old middle schooler fires a hunting rifle at his principal and a teacher

On Monday morning, at the Joseph-Lakanal middle school in Aubagne (Bouches-du-Rhône), a 14-year-old student came in with a hunting rifle and fired. The principal and a teacher, hit in the lower limbs, were hospitalized (Le Monde, Reuters, AP). BFMTV speaks of three wounded; most outlets count two. The teenager was arrested, his parents were taken into police custody and the home was searched; weapons were found there (BFMTV). Ministers Laurent Nuñez and Édouard Geffray went to the scene; the latter denounced “a criminal act” (Le Monde).

The motive remains to be established (Le Monde). Reactions are pouring in: the Snalc union voices its “anger” after years of warnings (franceinfo), Minister Philippe Baptiste demands that school remain “a sanctuary,” and Éric Zemmour says these young people must be treated “as adults” (BFMTV).

After his primary victory, Raphaël Glucksmann must bring people together: Ségolène Royal sets her conditions

Chosen on Sunday evening as the social-democratic left’s candidate for the 2027 presidential election, Raphaël Glucksmann pledged on Monday to “bring people together” (franceinfo, Le Figaro, Libération). The Socialist Party’s first secretary, Pierre Jouvet, acknowledged “a clear and unappealable victory” (franceinfo, BFMTV). Defeated, Ségolène Royal wrote on X that she was making her support conditional (“if it drifts toward Macronism, count me out”) and wondered whether the 80,000 new registered voters were on the left (Le Figaro, Libération).

The challenges are piling up: uniting the Socialist family and beyond, making room for his former rivals (Libération) and appealing both to the left and to former voters of Emmanuel Macron, facing Jean-Luc Mélenchon and Édouard Philippe, according to an analysis in Le Figaro.
