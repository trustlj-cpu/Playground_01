October 17 protests: 550,000 people according to the government, 800,000 according to organizers, and urban violence in several cities

The march against the “high cost of living” drew crowds across the country; by evening, the interior minister announces about 400 arrests and the head of state denounces “sowers of chaos.”

Saturday’s day of mobilization, October 17, ended on two images that do not overlap. On one side, large marches, described by Le Monde as calm in places, which brought together, according to BFMTV, different “angers”: former yellow vests, high-school students, university students, activists. On the other, scenes of clashes with law enforcement, looting and fires in several cities, which Le Figaro illustrates with images from Paris, Lyon, Marseille and Limoges.

On the scale, the figures diverge, as often. In its live coverage, Le Monde reports that the government announces 550,000 demonstrators while the organizers claim 800,000. Le Figaro also cites “some 800,000 people” for actions and marches across the country against the “high cost of living.” The interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, for his part said on Saturday evening that “roughly 400 arrests” had taken place, a number Le Monde repeats while reporting “urban violence of unprecedented gravity.”

The clearest damage is reported in Lyon, where more than 20,000 people marched according to Le Monde. The tourist office on Place Bellecour was looted and then set on fire, which BFMTV and Le Figaro also report. In Marseille, Le Figaro writes that the central police station on the Canebière was targeted by rioters and that the city hall was singled out; the mayor, Benoît Payan, “demands accountability.” In Caen, Ouest-France describes looting, clashes and burning barricades, while BFMTV reports in Saint-Étienne a demonstrator in his twenties seriously injured, his right hand in shreds, taken to the university hospital.

In Paris, the prefecture had issued the day before an order banning demonstrations in several areas around the Élysée and Matignon, according to Libération and 20 Minutes. President Emmanuel Macron responded in the evening by denouncing, according to Le Monde, “sowers of chaos who only seek to bring down our institutions,” after scenes of ransacking; BFMTV also quotes his phrase “we will close ranks.” What remains to be established: a consolidated toll of the injured, the cost of the destruction and the exact share of those arrested who will be prosecuted. These elements were not published in the articles consulted at the time this page was closed.

Politics · Reactions✓ 2 outlets · causes of the disorder still debated

After the October 17 violence, Macron promises to “close ranks” while Marine Le Pen and Jean-Luc Mélenchon clash over public-order policing

From Saturday evening, the political class reacted to the day of mobilization. Emmanuel Macron denounced, according to Le Monde, the “sowers of chaos who only seek to bring down our institutions,” after scenes of ransacking in several cities. BFMTV for its part reports his promise that the country will “close ranks” against them. The interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, gave the figure of “roughly 400 arrests,” which Le Monde cites in its live coverage of the day. The debate quickly shifted to responsibilities. Again according to BFMTV, Marine Le Pen, the National Rally candidate, attacked La France insoumise, lambasting the speeches of its deputies. Jean-Luc Mélenchon replied by calling the public-order policing strategy “deplorable.” The two camps are thus not talking about the same thing: one blames political words, the other the government’s operational choices. Another message circulated, that of a Parisian high-school student, Henry Blunden, cited by BFMTV: he does not want the violence to “camouflage” the demands of the high-school movement. In the articles consulted, no new government measure on the substance of the “high cost of living” is announced at this stage.

World · Ukraine and Russia✓ 3 outlets · legal scope of the decree to be clarified

Donald Trump exempts Russia from new sanctions by decree, as Romania says it shot down two drones over the Black Sea

In a decree published Friday evening by the White House, US President Donald Trump “exempts the application” of new sanctions targeting Russia, Le Monde reports in its live coverage of the war in Ukraine. The paper specifies that Kyiv sees it as an encouragement to continue the attacks. International reactions are tallied through Saturday, and the text of the decree is not reproduced in the excerpts consulted.

It is necessary to distinguish what the decree says from what can be inferred from it. The sources speak of an exemption from applying new sanctions, not of a lifting of existing measures; none of the articles consulted mentions an easing of those already in force. For his part, Emmanuel Macron assured that France “will not let go of Ukraine,” according to a headline published Saturday.

On the ground, Le Figaro and franceinfo report that Romania shot down two drones in its airspace over the Black Sea. Both outlets note that since the start of October, incidents attributed to Russian drones have been multiplying off Romania and the neighboring area. The attribution to Russian forces is presented in the text as an allegation, not as a fact established by an independent investigation.

World · Middle East✓ 2 outlets · mayor’s condition not detailed

In the West Bank, a European diplomatic convoy is attacked by settlers and a French mayor, Bernard Baudoux, is injured

On Saturday, October 17, Israeli settlers attacked a convoy of European diplomats on a visit to the occupied West Bank, RFI reports. Le Figaro specifies that Bernard Baudoux, mayor of Aulnoye-Aymeries, was taking part that day in an olive harvest in Taybeh, alongside diplomatic representatives, when the attack took place. The two outlets agree that the French elected official was injured; RFI writes that the settlers hit him.

The excerpts consulted detail neither the severity of the injuries, nor the number of vehicles hit, nor the nationality of all the diplomats present. Nor do they mention any official reaction from the French government or the European Union at the time of publication. These are the points to follow in the coming hours, in particular a possible summoning of an ambassador or a statement from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

The olive harvest is a seasonal farming activity in the region, often accompanied by foreign visitors who come to support Palestinian farmers. It was in that context that the French mayor was in Taybeh, according to Le Figaro. Both outlets published the story on Saturday late in the afternoon.

Justice · Cyberharassment✓ 3 outlets · content of the messages not detailed

Attacks targeting judge Youssef Badr: the Paris prosecutor’s office opens a cyberharassment investigation

A cyberharassment investigation has been opened in Paris after attacks targeting judge Youssef Badr, report Libération, Le Monde and Ouest-France. According to the excerpt published by the outlets, the magistrate had already come under attack in June and received, notably, the support of Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin. The information was published on Friday, October 16, in the evening.

The excerpts consulted specify neither the content of the targeted messages, nor the identity of the authors, nor the platforms concerned. They also do not say which police or gendarmerie unit is in charge of the investigation, nor whether a complaint was filed by the magistrate himself. Cyberharassment is an offense: it consists of repeating malicious remarks or behavior online,.

A minister’s support for a judge targeted by online attacks is a strong signal but one that is anything but automatic. It shows that the Justice Ministry chose to react publicly. The timeline of the investigation is not known.
