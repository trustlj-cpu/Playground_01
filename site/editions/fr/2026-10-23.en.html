Animal virus Shamonda detected in a human for the first time: an immunocompromised patient has died in Lyon

Authorities announce the death, in early October, of an immunocompromised man infected with this virus of ruminants, until now known only in animals.

An immunocompromised patient died in early October in Lyon after being infected with the Shamonda virus, a virus of ruminants never before identified in a human being, authorities announced on Thursday, October 22, according to Ouest-France, Le Figaro and franceinfo. Le Figaro reports that the man died of cerebral complications “very probably linked” to the virus. Reuters, which mentions a man in his sixties, says the authorities believe he was bitten by an infected midge in eastern France, where the virus was circulating in animals. According to franceinfo, the authorities see no health risk for the general population, and the outlet presents the case as a world first. Ouest-France specifies that the virus has been identified in livestock farms in 54 departments. Al Jazeera adds that clinical, biological and epidemiological investigations are continuing with the World Health Organization and the ECDC, to determine how the infection was contracted.

Reuters places the virus’s appearance in Europe in late July and says more than a dozen European countries had reported cases in animals by October 20. The World Health Organization told Reuters that, based on what is known about the transmission of similar viruses, “this is an isolated human case” and that it is unlikely to lead to wider spread for now. Reuters notes that no transmission from cattle to humans, or from human to human, has been reported. Al Jazeera relays the position of the French authorities, who say that no health risk to the general population has been identified at this stage. Ouest-France dates the death to early October, in Lyon. The outlets cited do not say whether the virus caused or only contributed to the patient’s death, or where the man was infected.

Pierre Moscovici resigns from the European Court of Auditors, less than ten months after arriving

Pierre Moscovici has resigned from the European Court of Auditors, less than ten months after arriving, the institution announced on Thursday, October 22, according to Le Monde, franceinfo and Le Figaro. Le Monde reports that he is acting notably to preserve his freedom to speak out. According to the outlets cited, he says: “I think that with the experience I have, my place is also to contribute to the public debate here in France.” Eunews reports that the Court cites “personal reasons” without further detail. Pierre Moscovici took up his duties as a member of the Court on January 1, 2026, for a term running until December 31, 2031, Eunews specifies. Eunews adds that he remains in post until his successor is appointed. The seat goes to a French national, and it is for the Council of the European Union, after consulting the European Parliament, to appoint the new member. franceinfo headlines a resignation coming less than a year after his appointment. The outlets cited do not say at this stage who might succeed him or what role he will take on in France.

Claude Monet’s “Iris” sells for 50 million euros in Paris, a record for an auction in France

Claude Monet’s painting “Iris” was sold on Thursday, October 22 for 50 million euros at Sotheby’s in Paris, a record for an art auction in France, BFMTV and franceinfo report. According to franceinfo, this monumental canvas, painted in the final years of the artist’s life, carried an estimate of between 22 and 30 million euros, one that the outlet had described before the sale as a record for France. Ouest-France, for its part, uses the figure of a 43 million euro hammer price, and makes it the most expensive painting ever sold at auction in the country.

Bloomberg reports that the never-exhibited canvas sold for $56 million at Sotheby’s Paris. The price announced by BFMTV and franceinfo is 20 million euros above the top end of the estimate put forward by franceinfo. The articles consulted do not all detail the difference between the 43 million euros cited by Ouest-France and the 50 million euros cited by BFMTV and franceinfo, nor the identity of the buyer.

Mirage 2000D crash near Nancy: Metz prosecutor’s office confirms the death of both service members

The Metz prosecutor’s office confirmed on Thursday, October 22 the deaths of the two military personnel aboard the Mirage 2000D that crashed near Nancy, Le Monde, Ouest-France and BFMTV report. The aircraft crashed on the evening of Tuesday, October 20 in Meurthe-et-Moselle, during a night training flight. Ouest-France had initially reported one serviceman killed and another still missing. L’Essentiel identifies the pilot as Lieutenant Quentin Klysz, 27, and the navigator and weapons systems officer as Lieutenant Colonel Antranig Sarkissian, 44, who died “in commanded air service.”

Speaking to AFP, as quoted by L’Essentiel, prosecutor David Touvet said that neither the pilot nor the navigator had time to eject before hitting the ground, the motors of both ejection seats having been found on the ground. The aircraft was an unarmed two-seat Mirage 2000D, according to the same source. L’Essentiel reports that the causes of the accident remain to be established by the judicial and safety investigations. The crash took place in the commune of Rogéville, L’Essentiel specifies.

Finland grants the world’s first operating license for a final nuclear waste repository

The Finnish government on Thursday, October 22 granted the company Posiva an operating license for its encapsulation and final disposal facility for spent nuclear fuel at Eurajoki, in the southwest of the country, the Finnish government announced. Le Figaro and BFMTV present the site as the world’s first for the final disposal of nuclear waste. Le Figaro specifies that it is dug into stable bedrock 1.9 billion years old. BFMTV cites a “great historic day,” 22 years after construction began.

The license runs until the end of 2070 and covers spent fuel from the Olkiluoto and Loviisa plants, the Finnish government says, which relied on the favorable opinion of the safety authority STUK of August 4, 2026. France 24 reports the same decision. The Finnish government specifies, however, that this license does not authorize an immediate start: Posiva cannot operate the site before an inspection by STUK, and no detailed commissioning timetable is given. Posiva’s owners, TVO and Fortum, remain responsible for financing the operations.
