Trump calls for “a new president” for Ukraine a day after the Russian diesel deal. Strikes on Zaporizhzhia kill more than 15

After the Russian diesel deal, Donald Trump says it is time for Ukraine to have another president. The Europeans are closing ranks behind Volodymyr Zelensky as Russian strikes continue.

On Saturday, Oct. 10, Donald Trump said that “it is time for Ukraine to have a new president” (BFM, franceinfo, Le Figaro, RFI, BBC). He blames Volodymyr Zelensky for hitting Russian refineries, which, he says, worsens the global diesel shortage, and he wants a leader “who can reach a deal” with Moscow (Ouest-France). The remarks come a day after the announcement of a deal with Vladimir Putin: Russia would supply 300,000 tonnes of diesel “immediately” (Reuters, AP). Zelensky sees an “investment in the war” (Reuters); Le Monde reports that he calls the decision “unworthy” and “weak.” The same day, Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia, in southeastern Ukraine, killed at least 15 people, including three children. The toll rose to 20 or 21 dead according to France 24 and the Straits Times. Kyiv also suffered massive power cuts (Le Monde). A Kremlin adviser described the phone call between Trump and Putin as “very friendly,” with neither wanting to hang up first (Reuters). According to FinancialJuice, a single outlet, Moscow partly lifted its ban on diesel exports after the deal.

The Europeans have closed ranks. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham assured Zelensky of his “total solidarity” and is due to meet him in the coming days (Le Monde). Emmanuel Macron expressed his “solidarity” (Le Monde). Germany is keeping its sanctions on Russia (Reuters), and the European Union considers that lifting them on diesel gives Moscow “the means to wage war” (Le Monde). Ukraine’s foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, points out that the choice of leader belongs to Ukrainians. At dawn on Sunday, Trump said that Putin “would like to see a settlement” (Le Figaro) and that Ukraine must hold an election (FinancialJuice), while American, Ukrainian and European negotiators held talks in Miami, exchanges that envoy Steve Witkoff calls “constructive” (Le Monde, Reuters). Envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner could travel to Moscow within two weeks, according to the Russian agency TASS as cited by FinancialJuice: a single outlet. The effect on prices remains uncertain: Bloomberg Economics estimates that the November cargo equals about one day of U.S. consumption. On Wall Street on Friday, the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83% to 51,654.95. The ten-year yield stood at 5.244%. WTI gained 0.39%, to $91.85, and gold 1.43%, to $4,216.3. The CAC 40 had closed at 7,803.33 points (+0.95%).

Riyadh airport: attack blamed on the Houthis kills 12 and wounds more than 300

On Saturday, Oct. 10, at around 3 p.m., a missile struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the Saudi capital (RFI, Le Figaro, France 24). The Saudi civil aviation authority reported 12 dead and 309 wounded (Le Figaro, Ouest-France): four Saudis and eight foreigners, including one American. The Saudi-led coalition accuses the Houthis, Yemeni rebels backed by Iran, and calls it a “war crime” (20 Minutes, Le Figaro). The embassies of Canada, Spain and Germany advised their citizens against traveling through Riyadh (franceinfo), but organizers of major Saudi conferences say they will go ahead (Reuters). According to FinancialJuice, a single outlet, the UN secretary-general “firmly” condemned the assault and Donald Trump called it “terrible.” The context is one of escalation: Riyadh and the Yemeni government launched an offensive against the Houthis in early October (Libération), who had seized the Red Sea coastline in September (Le Monde). In the Strait of Hormuz, Centcom says it immobilized a cargo ship that was trying to break the blockade of Iran (FinancialJuice).

Cycling: Paul Seixas, 20, wins the Tour of Lombardy, his first Monument

On Saturday, Oct. 10, in Como (Italy), Frenchman Paul Seixas (Decathlon-CMA CGM), 20, won the Tour of Lombardy, one of the five biggest classics on the calendar, known as the Monuments (Le Monde, Le Figaro, L’Équipe). He becomes the youngest winner of the race and, according to franceinfo, the youngest winner of a Monument since 1944.

With 19 kilometers to go, he attacked on the climb of the Civiglio, where Belgium’s Remco Evenepoel and Mexico’s Isaac Del Toro could not follow (Le Figaro). He won solo ahead of Spain’s Enric Mas and Italy’s Giulio Ciccone (L’Équipe). He succeeds Thibaut Pinot, the last Frenchman to win the classic (Le Figaro). “My legs were making me dream,” he said. He is due to ride on Sunday at the Roc d’Azur, in Nice (L’Équipe).

High-school movement: 54% of the French approve, Emmanuel Macron falls to 15% favorable opinions

According to a poll cited by Le Figaro, 54% of the French approve of the blockade of high schools. Opinion supports limiting the number of pupils per class but rejects the abolition of Parcoursup. The same paper reports, citing an Ipsos BVA-Cesi barometer, that 15% of the French have a favorable opinion of Emmanuel Macron, his lowest since 2017.

In Lens, the lawyer for a 15-year-old high schooler whose hand was amputated by a police grenade denies that he picked it up (Le Monde, BFM); the Béthune prosecutor ruled the shot compliant. On Saturday evening, PSG ultras left the stadium after a banner supporting the high schoolers was removed (L’Équipe). Gabriel Attal presented a “youth pact” in Lyon (Le Monde, franceinfo).

Left primary: more than 105,000 voters out of 140,000 registered, results expected this Sunday

On Saturday at 8 p.m., more than 105,000 of the 140,000 registered voters had cast a ballot to choose among the five candidates of the social-democratic left’s primary (Le Monde). On Friday at 7 p.m., only 20,000 voters had been able to do so, because of the cyberattack on the platform. Voting is extended until Sunday at 6 p.m.

The Paris prosecutor’s office has opened an investigation, entrusted to the Anti-Cybercrime Office (BFM). Jean-Luc Mélenchon mocked the primary (Le Monde).
