A suspect on the S file arrested after the Limoges town hall fire, Nuñez promises a stronger police presence on Saturday, October 24

The RAID arrested a 40-year-old man on Tuesday; the government announces new curfews, a larger police presence for the next demonstration and a cash-flow loan for the businesses hit.

A 40-year-old man from the Creuse was arrested on Tuesday, October 20, at 6:15 a.m. in Val-d’Issoire, in the Haute-Vienne, by the RAID unit from Bordeaux, in the investigation into the fire at Limoges town hall on Saturday, October 17, on the sidelines of the demonstrations against the “high cost of living.” Le Figaro and BFMTV report that he is on the S file because of his ties to the ultra-left movement; Ouest-France specifies that the Sub-directorate for Counterterrorism (SDAT) has been co-assigned to the investigation and that the old part of the building, inaugurated in 1883, is “almost 100% destroyed” inside. The belfry collapsed on Saturday, according to Le Figaro, which quotes mayor Guillaume Guérin (LR): the police “lacked resources” and “fell back” in the face of the rioters. Presidential candidate David Lisnard denounced in the same daily “terrorist training camps on the Millevaches plateau,” and Matignon announced that Sébastien Lecornu would travel to Limoges on Friday.

On public order, Laurent Nuñez promised Marseille mayor Benoît Payan on Monday evening in Marseille “a very substantial police presence” on Saturday, October 24, “larger than last Saturday,” for the next demonstration, report BFMTV and Le Monde. Sébastien Lecornu judged on Tuesday that the partial curfews decided the day before in several cities had “contributed to the calm of last night” and that new ones would be decided on Tuesday evening “where the situation requires it”; he invited the political parties to Matignon on Thursday, according to Le Figaro. Medef president Patrick Martin said that the State “has failed” and that “at least” 3,000 businesses were affected according to first government estimates (Ouest-France). Economy Minister Roland Lescure announced an interest-free loan capped at 50,000 euros, operated by Bpifrance, available from Friday to businesses that suffered damage (BFMTV, franceinfo), and the French Banking Federation (FBF) reported more than 200 branches affected (Le Figaro). Gérald Darmanin said there is “no need for a state of emergency” while not ruling it out “as a matter of principle,” according to Le Monde.

World · War in Ukraine✓ 4 outlets · timetable for missile deliveries not confirmed

Zelensky accuses the Pentagon of delaying missiles “already paid for,” Rubio denies it and Witkoff receives him in Oslo

Speaking on Monday evening to US senators, Volodymyr Zelensky asserted that “the Pentagon is delaying the delivery of missiles already paid for” by the Europeans under the PURL program, reports Le Figaro. According to Le Monde, he added that European countries were ready to deliver Patriot interceptors but were waiting for “American authorization”; BFMTV quotes his remark that these missiles “could already be in Ukraine,” and 20 Minutes notes that he believes a “political decision was made to slow support” for Kyiv. On Tuesday, from India, Marco Rubio replied that “nothing is deliberately being refused” to Ukraine (Le Figaro, BFMTV). Bloomberg reports that he added that the United States must weigh Kyiv’s requests against those of other allies and the needs of its own military. Volodymyr Zelensky arrived in Oslo on Tuesday, where he is to meet US envoy Steve Witkoff as well as the Norwegian and Finnish leaders, while the other envoy, Jared Kushner, takes part remotely, the Norwegian government announced, according to Le Monde. Le Figaro, citing Axios, says that Steve Witkoff plans to meet Vladimir Putin afterwards this week, and that a peace proposal is being prepared with the Europeans. The Ukrainian president said he had a “substantive” exchange with Marco Rubio on restoring Starlink and on interceptors (Le Monde). The Kremlin hopes Steve Witkoff will soon be able to brief Vladimir Putin on the state of Ukrainian and European thinking, according to Reuters. L’Indépendant also reports that Donald Trump reportedly said it might “perhaps be time” to turn to someone other than Volodymyr Zelensky to negotiate.

Macron secures 200,000 tonnes of diesel a month from the Emirates and Bahrain, on top of 95,000 Saudi tonnes

Emmanuel Macron “has secured” additional deliveries of 200,000 tonnes of diesel a month, the Élysée announced on Tuesday, October 20, according to BFMTV, Libération and RFI. Le Figaro details that the head of state obtained commitments for 100,000 tonnes a month with the United Arab Emirates and another 100,000 with Bahrain, on top of 95,000 tonnes already released by Saudi Arabia for October and November. The French president, on a visit to the region from Monday through Wednesday, is stopping in Kuwait, the Emirates, Bahrain and Qatar, according to BFMTV and Libération. He said he wants “the French to lack nothing,” reports BFMTV.

Ouest-France specifies that the effect of these volumes on pump prices cannot be quantified “at this stage,” according to the Élysée. 20 Minutes says the fuel comes half from the Emirates and half from Bahrain. Bloomberg Television also relayed on Monday remarks by US Energy Secretary Wright, who said Europe should release more diesel from its stocks.

Alberta rejects holding an independence referendum with about 76.5%, a win for Mark Carney

Alberta residents voted overwhelmingly on Monday, October 19 against holding a referendum on the province’s independence, report franceinfo, RFI and Le Monde, which speak of more than 75% “no.” Le Figaro, relying on a projection, puts it at about 76.5% for staying in Canada against 23.5% for supporters of a referendum promoted by the separatist movement. It was the first consultative vote of its kind in this oil-rich province, according to RFI and franceinfo.

The result is presented as a win for Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, who hailed a “clear choice” for “unity” in the midst of a trade war with the United States, according to France 24. Le Monde stresses that the Albertan separatist movement, fueled by resentment toward Ottawa, differs from Quebec’s. The Guardian reports that broadcaster CBC announced the projection of the result.

World · Justice✓ 2 outlets · sentences not yet handed down

Kenya: pastor Paul Mackenzie and 29 co-defendants found guilty of 191 murders, including 190 children

In Kenya, Paul Mackenzie and his 29 co-defendants were found guilty of the murder of 191 people, including 190 children, found in the Shakahola forest, reports France 24. According to the channel, the self-proclaimed pastor preached extreme fasting in order to “meet Jesus.” Judge Diana Kavedza Mochache delivered the verdict on Monday, October 19, after thirteen months of hearings and testimony from more than 120 witnesses, Le Monde specifies.

France 24 says the ruling on the sentences is expected on November 6; Le Monde writes that the sentence will be handed down later. Le Monde describes this first trial of the pastor as the epilogue of one of the worst tragedies in the country’s history.
