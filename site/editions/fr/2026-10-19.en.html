Society · October 17 mobilization✓ 10 outlets · consolidated toll of damage and injured demonstrators not published

October 17 violence: at least 1,800 arrests according to the AP and about 2,000 according to the interior ministry cited by France 24, after 1,200 announced Saturday evening by Sébastien Lecornu

The government counted 550,000 demonstrators on Saturday, the organizers 800,000; tallies of arrests and injuries followed one another from Saturday evening through Sunday.

The day of mobilization against the “high cost of living” on Saturday, October 17 brought together 550,000 demonstrators according to the government and 800,000 according to the organizers, Le Monde and ICI report. Around 8 p.m., the interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, reported about 400 arrests, according to Le Monde, which also mentions “urban violence of unprecedented gravity.” Later in the evening, at a press briefing in Paris alongside law enforcement, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu announced 1,200 arrests, a toll that Yahoo Actualités presents as provisional and that franceinfo accompanies with his phrase about “unacceptable degrees of violence.” Cedarnews also attributes to him the figure of 150 police officers and gendarmes injured and the remark that it is “a miracle” the toll is not heavier. On Sunday, France 24 cited the interior ministry for some 2,000 arrests and 600 injured among police officers, gendarmes and firefighters, while the AP reports at least 1,800 arrests nationwide and more than 500 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters injured according to Laurent Nuñez. CNews alone puts forward 2,397 arrests and 997 police officers and gendarmes injured, figures the other outlets cited did not pick up.

According to RTS, the violence targeted Paris, Lyon and Limoges. CNews relays the Paris police prefecture, which reports looting in the Châtelet–Les Halles area. According to ICI and the AP, the capital recorded 855 arrests on Saturday; on Sunday, the police prefecture told the AFP that an undeclared call to gather at Place de la République at 2 p.m. was circulating on social media, and ICI reports clashes, tensions and looting on the spot, with a fresh deployment of CRS 8. France 24 and FashionNetwork, which draw on the AFP, cite the estimate of the retail federation that 1,500 shops were directly hit, for damage of “several hundred million” euros. The interior minister is the target of calls to resign, notably from the Green leader Marine Tondelier and the LFI deputy Éric Coquerel, according to France 24 and franceinfo.

Politics · Presidential address✓ 6 outlets · dates of the meetings announced by Matignon not specified

Emmanuel Macron’s address: “anger” and “shame,” an “uncompromising” response to rioters and “transparency” on public-order policing

Emmanuel Macron spoke on television on Sunday, October 18 at 8 p.m., the day after the mobilization. “Tonight we all feel anger, shame,” he said, according to La DH and France 24. According to RTS and Le Singulier, he promised an “uncompromising” response to rioters, whom he described as “seditionists” from far-left splinter groups joined by many delinquents, while speaking of legitimate demands on the part of demonstrators. He also promised “transparency” on the public-order policing operation and asked Laurent Nuñez to draw every lesson from it, France 24 and FashionNetwork report. According to franceinfo and Le Singulier, Sébastien Lecornu will bring together parties, unions and representatives of high-school and university students in the coming days, and will chair a crisis unit starting Monday. Bruno Retailleau, president of LR, judged the president “totally overwhelmed” and asked him to declare a state of emergency, according to La DH and France 24; franceinfo also quotes him accusing LFI elected officials of having “armed the violence with their slogans.” Jordan Bardella called it a “lamentable address” and the LFI coordinator, Manuel Bompard, judged that Emmanuel Macron had “spoken to say nothing,” according to La DH. According to Le Singulier, Édouard Philippe is awaiting answers on intelligence and the police response, and the mayor of Marseille, Benoît Payan, asked the head of state to listen to what is happening in the country.

Society · Public-order policing✓ 5 outlets · exact circumstances of the injury not established

In Mulhouse, a 17-year-old demonstrator loses the use of an eye during the October 17 mobilization and the prosecutor’s office refers the matter to the IGPN

In Mulhouse, a young woman of 17, born in the city, lost the use of an eye during the mobilization of Saturday, October 17, report franceinfo, ICI, France 3 and 24 heures. Franceinfo speaks of a minor demonstrator, and Le Monde cites the prosecutor’s office, according to which the first investigations establish that the victim is “a young woman aged 17, born in Mulhouse.” The Mulhouse prosecutor, Nicolas Heitz, referred the matter to the General Inspectorate of the National Police (IGPN); according to ICI, the Metz delegation is conducting a flagrance investigation for aggravated violence, and the prosecutor warned that the investigations would be complex.

On the circumstances, accounts differ: France 3 writes that the teenager was wounded by a tear-gas grenade, while the prosecutor’s office mentions a blow to the eye without specifying the conditions. According to the prosecutor’s office, about 1,000 people took part in the gathering near the SNCF station in Mulhouse.

Politics · Local officials✓ 4 outlets · content of Monday’s talks still to come

After October 17, the mayor of Marseille demands explanations from the interior minister, expected Monday, while the mayor of Paris denounces “security failures”

Benoît Payan, mayor of Marseille, said “Tomorrow, I want explanations” and announced the interior minister’s visit on Monday, franceinfo reports, specifying that the ministry confirmed the trip. The mayor says he had warned in advance of a risk of things getting out of hand and had called the president, the prime minister and the minister to ask for reinforcements, according to franceinfo. Le Singulier reports that Benoît Payan also asked Emmanuel Macron to listen to what is happening in the country.

In Paris, the Socialist mayor, Emmanuel Grégoire, went on Sunday to meet shopkeepers who described systematic looting, and denounced “security failures,” Le Monde reports. Le Figaro adds that he issued an appeal to residents on X, “I invite everyone to come and help us,” praising the surge of solidarity from Parisians who came to clean the streets.

World · Ukraine and the United States✓ 4 outlets · content of the talks with US envoys not detailed

Donald Trump accuses Volodymyr Zelensky of “not wanting to end” the war, after the Ukrainian president’s interview on NBC

Donald Trump accused Volodymyr Zelensky on Sunday, October 18 of “not wanting to end this war” with Russia and of “probably never having wanted to,” Le Monde and NBC News report. The Ukrainian president had told NBC’s “Meet the Press” that it would be a “historic mistake” if the United States sided with Russia, and that Ukraine would not prevail in that case, according to NBC News. He added that US intelligence sharing, briefly interrupted, had resumed, according to the Jerusalem Post.

CNN also reports that Ukraine carried out one of its largest drone attacks of the year against Russia, targeting an oil terminal, despite Donald Trump’s request to stop striking Russian energy infrastructure.
