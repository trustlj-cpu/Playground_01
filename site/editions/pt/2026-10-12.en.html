World · VaticanPress ✓ 5 outlets · announcement by the Pope himself

Pope Leo XIV to undergo surgery in the coming days for a lung nodule detected “at a very early stage”

The Pontiff, 71, broke the news on Sunday, during the Angelus. As this edition went to press, neither the exact date of the operation nor other clinical details had been released.

Pope Leo XIV announced on Sunday, October 11, during the Angelus prayer, that he will undergo surgery “in the coming days” to remove a nodule “confined to the lung”. He said the lesion was detected “at a very early stage” in recent follow-up tests and that doctors advised the operation (RTP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian, Observador). The Pontiff is 71, is the first pope born in the United States and was elected in May 2025.

A lung nodule is a small rounded spot in the lung, under three centimeters, usually discovered on a CT scan or an X-ray. Most are benign, but only further tests or analysis of the removed tissue can say what it is. As this edition went to press, the Vatican had not said whether serious illness is suspected. Doctors tell these nodules apart by size, shape and how they change over time, and surgery makes it possible to remove the lesion and analyze it. Some outlets mention a hospital stay of about a week, which is not confirmed by a second source.

In Portugal, RTP recorded a wave of reactions: Cardinal Américo Aguiar praised the simplicity with which the Pope shared the news and dozens of worshippers wished him a speedy recovery. On CNN Portugal, a commentator said that by speaking about his health “in this way”, Leo XIV comes closer to people. The Vatican did not say whether the agenda for the coming days will change.

Brazilian outlets mention a trip to South America scheduled for November 6 to 17; the Vatican did not say whether it will go ahead, and this has not been confirmed by a second source. The Angelus is the prayer the Pope says with the faithful every Sunday at noon in St. Peter’s Square, and it is often the moment when the Pope gives personal news or makes public appeals. In the coming days, what matters is the Vatican statement on the date of the operation, the result of the analysis of the nodule and the recovery time, as well as what happens to the audiences and ceremonies on the papal agenda.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 6 outlets · ceasefire not yet confirmed by Russia

Trump announces “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine; Zelensky says he was caught off guard and Russia hits a power plant in Kharkiv

The US president wrote on Sunday, on the Truth Social network, that “with immediate effect” there is an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine and that “both sides” have accepted (Reuters, BBC, Axios, Bloomberg). Zelensky told Axios the news was “new” to him, that Kyiv accepts if Moscow accepts and that he is waiting for details from Washington; according to Reuters, Ukraine will stop attacking refineries if Russia stops its attacks on energy. Hours later, Russia hit a thermal power plant in Kharkiv, Zelensky said; he counted more than 200 attacks on the power grid in a week (Kyiv Independent, The Hill). The Kremlin had not commented by press time. Zelensky told ABC he will hold elections if Russia accepts a 60-day truce (Reuters).

The announcement follows Friday’s agreement between Trump and Putin to supply 300,000 metric tons of Russian diesel to the US and world market (Observador, Jornal de Negócios). Six US senators, four Republicans and two Democrats, say the deal violates a law of Congress (Observador), and Reuters quotes a prominent Republican senator saying it may be illegal. Canada criticized the deal, Macron supports a “genuine energy truce” and Ursula von der Leyen defended Zelensky after Trump said on Saturday that Ukraine needs a new president. British Defense Minister Wes Streeting told the BBC that the United Kingdom does not support the diesel deal.

Portugal · EconomyPress ✓ 5 outlets · statements by the prime minister and the finance minister

Montenegro calls for an end to the “folly” of oil; Finance Ministry rules out ISP cuts and Zero VAT, even as inflation accelerates

Prime Minister Luís Montenegro said on Sunday, at the centenary celebrations of São João da Madeira, that the “difficult” international situation is driving the “folly” of rising oil prices, which has “penalized economies and people”, and asked for the issue to be resolved “in the coming weeks” (RTP, ECO, Observador, Público). According to Público, he admitted that solving the fuel problem is not enough, because there are other factors of instability: political, geopolitical and trade wars. He named no one. WTI oil closed on Friday at $91.85, up 0.39%.

Finance Minister Joaquim Miranda Sarmento told Jornal de Negócios that the Government “will not fail to adjust” its action to inflation, which is showing “renewed signs of acceleration”, but that a cut in the petroleum products tax (ISP) and Zero VAT are “definitively ruled out”. He described the 2027 State Budget as “pragmatic”. The PS will propose abstaining, but called for urgent hearings of 16 entities from sectors affected by the cost of energy (Público), and the DN/Aximage barometer shows the Portuguese divided over that abstention.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · Sunday toll from a single source

New attack on Riyadh airport a day after 12 deaths; Houthis claim responsibility and say they also targeted Dammam

An unidentified projectile hit Terminal 4 of King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Sunday, according to the Saudi-led coalition, in the second attack in two days (BBC, Bloomberg, Observador). Saudi civil aviation said Dammam airport, in the east, was targeted in the late afternoon and, according to FinancialJuice, spoke of one dead and 11 injured, without it being clear which attack these casualties refer to. The Houthis, an armed movement in Yemen, claimed the attacks, say they hit facilities of the oil company Aramco, which is not confirmed, and again warned travelers and airlines to avoid Saudi airports (Reuters, RTP, Jornal de Negócios).

On Saturday, the first attack killed 12 people and injured more than 300 (AP, Bloomberg, Jornal de Negócios). The coalition promised to “respond firmly” (The Guardian). Ursula von der Leyen spoke of a “dangerous escalation” and EU foreign ministers discuss the region today (RTP, Observador). The Saudi stock exchange fell (Reuters) and, according to the Financial Times, oil executives pulled out of an energy conference in Riyadh. Iran told its Saudi counterpart that it can help with dialogue with Yemen, and the secretary of the Iranian security council described the US response to the latest proposal as “insufficient” (FinancialJuice, citing Fars).

Europe · EU budgetPress ✓ 3 outlets · negotiation under way

António Costa to be the “main negotiator” of the EU budget after criticism of the Irish presidency’s proposal

European Council president António Costa becomes the “main negotiator” of the European Union’s multiannual budget, after the proposal from the Council’s Irish presidency was criticized “from north to south of Europe” (RTP, Observador, Jornal de Negócios). The proposal provided for cuts of about 8%, according to Observador. It is now up to Costa to bring the “frugal” countries and the “cohesion” countries closer together to close a deal by the end of 2026.

The calendar is the main obstacle: the goal is a deal this year, so that the next budget takes effect on January 1, 2028 (Jornal de Negócios). For Portugal, which receives cohesion funds and money from the common agricultural policy, every cut weighs: the debate decides how much money reaches roads, trains, schools and farmers between 2028 and 2034. The “frugals” (countries such as the Netherlands, Sweden or Austria) prefer to spend less; the cohesion countries want to keep the funds.
