Airlines cancel flights to Saudi Arabia and Riyadh is left with almost empty skies after Houthi attacks on the airport

Saudi Arabia is investigating how its air defenses failed, oil companies pull executives from an event in the capital and injured foreigners are in intensive care.

On Monday, Riyadh airport and the airspace around the Saudi capital continued to feel the effects of the attacks claimed by the Houthis, the armed movement in Yemen backed by Iran. Reuters wrote that several airlines cancelled flights to Saudi Arabia and that the skies over Riyadh were “largely empty”. The Associated Press (AP) reported that Saudi authorities are investigating how the defense systems failed to stop the attack, which caused deaths. RTP, in the early hours of Monday, recorded at least one death in Sunday’s attack.

The impact extends to the economy and to sport. Reuters reported that top executives of oil companies were removed from the program of an event in Riyadh, and The Economic Times wrote that BP pulled out of an energy conference in the capital. In sport, CNA wrote that Alalshikh, a Saudi sports figure, says Álvarez’s title fight in Riyadh is still on, despite the attacks. Among the injured are foreigners: the Philippine Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) said four Filipinos were injured, three of them in intensive care (Inquirer, Philstar, Manila Times), and the Indian embassy said three Indians are in intensive care but stable (The Economic Times). The Manila Times spoke of hundreds injured in total; the figure was not confirmed by a second source.

Reuters also reported that Turkey, Pakistan and Egypt condemned the attacks, and yesterday’s edition reported that the Houthis claimed them. In this window we did not find two independent sources for the total death toll: yesterday’s edition recorded 12 dead in a first attack, and RTP at least one in the following attack, but adding the two numbers would not be rigorous.

What to follow in Portugal and Europe is the indirect effect. Saudi Arabia is one of the world’s largest oil exporters and a hub of air connections to Asia; anyone with booked flights should talk to the airline, and the oil price continues to react to any news from the region. Reuters also reported that Iran will respond to the United States’ response to its proposal on the Strait of Hormuz, the sea passage through which a large share of the world’s oil travels, once the review it says it is carrying out is finished.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · terms of the deal unconfirmed

Trump defends diesel deal with Putin; lawmakers from both parties complain and the Kremlin rejects a 60-day truce

On Monday, President Donald Trump defended the deal with Russia on diesel, while lawmakers, Republicans and Democrats alike, complained, according to Reuters. The same agency wrote, citing sources, that Washington hopes the deal could “soften” Moscow in the peace negotiations, and that Russia says it can export up to 3.5 million tons of diesel a month by the end of the year. The New York Times wrote that, after the deal, Trump faces a new crisis over Ukraine, and Libération spoke of an “important diplomatic gain for Russia”.

On the ground, the so-called energy ceasefire remains in dispute. AP reported that Kyiv says Russian attacks on energy facilities continue, and Reuters that Ukraine accuses Russia of violating the truce while the European Union adopted nearly 1,600 new sanctions against Moscow. The Kremlin rejected the Ukrainian idea of a 60-day ceasefire and says it wants a comprehensive peace deal (Reuters). The exact terms of the diesel deal do not appear in our sources.

World · United StatesPress ✓ 5 outlets · no charges reported

Federal agents search home of congresswoman Ilhan Omar in a criminal investigation, weeks before the midterms

Federal agents searched the home of Democratic congresswoman Ilhan Omar on Monday as part of a criminal investigation, according to sources cited by the Associated Press. Reuters wrote, based on a Fox News report, that it was an immigration operation; the two descriptions do not match and we could not confirm which is correct. The Straits Times, also citing Fox News, speaks of an immigration fraud investigation. Democrats criticized the operation, according to The Hill. So far, none of the sources we consulted has reported charges.

Omar arrived in the United States as a refugee from Somalia and is one of Trump’s best-known critics in Congress. Clarín quotes her: “They will not silence me”. The operation comes a few weeks before the November midterm elections, in which the House of Representatives is renewed. Readers looking for news should wait for the court documents and the Department of Justice statement.

World · VaticanPress ✓ 2 outlets · discharge date not yet announced

Pope Leo XIV is admitted to hospital on Tuesday, ahead of lung surgery, says the Vatican

The Vatican said Pope Leo XIV will be admitted on Tuesday, October 13, ahead of the operation on the lung nodule he announced on Sunday, according to Reuters and the Italian agency ANSA. ANSA wrote that admission will be in “tarda mattinata”, that is, late morning, and that the tenth floor of the Gemelli hospital in Rome is ready to receive him. ANSA added that the nodule was discovered in routine tests.

Neither of the two sources gave the date of the operation or the length of the stay. ANSA added that all of the Pope’s audiences are canceled until October 20.

Economy · Nobel PrizePress ✓ 5 outlets

Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford win the Economics Nobel for work on stabilizing economies

The 2026 Economics Nobel was awarded to the Americans Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford, according to the Associated Press, Reuters, CNN, NOS and ANSA, and Reuters sums up their work as the study of stabilization, that is, of how to keep an economy in balance when shocks hit. The Economist headlined its article “A Nobel for knowing how to keep economies in balance”.

The prize is presented in Stockholm and has to be read with care: its official name is the Prize in Economic Sciences of the Bank of Sweden in Memory of Alfred Nobel, created in 1968, and it is not one of the original prizes in Nobel’s will. For the reader, the subject is concrete: research on how central banks react to inflation and unemployment lies behind the decisions on interest rates that affect mortgages.
