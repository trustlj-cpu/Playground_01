FPF opens disciplinary proceedings against Ronaldo and provisionally suspends him from the national team

The Disciplinary Council announced on Saturday the captain’s provisional suspension until a final decision, after he left the training camp and criticized the national team coach. The case was treated as urgent.

The Disciplinary Council of the Portuguese Football Federation announced on Saturday, October 10, that it had opened disciplinary proceedings against Cristiano Ronaldo, 41, and provisionally suspended him until a final decision is made, after he left the national team’s training camp and criticized national team coach Jorge Jesus (BBC Sport, The Guardian, Le Monde, L’Équipe, Straits Times). El Mundo writes that the case was treated as urgent. No hearing date has been set yet.

Le Monde and France 24 date his departure to September 30, L’Équipe to October 1. FPF president Pedro Proença again said he wants Ronaldo back, according to L’Équipe. On Tuesday the player released a statement in which he accepted responsibility and said he would be available after serving the sanction; meanwhile the national team beat Denmark 4-2 and Norway 2-1 without him, and fans of Al Nassr, the Saudi club he plays for, backed him, according to ESPN. Former player Jamie Carragher and journalist Piers Morgan traded accusations on X over the case.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · toll according to Saudi authorities

Houthi missile hits Riyadh airport, killing 12 and injuring more than 300; Trump weighs joining the offensive

Saudi authorities said Saturday’s attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 12 people and injured more than 300 (309, according to the civil aviation authority), according to NPR, CBS, AP, Reuters and the Guardian. The dead include four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, a Palestinian, an Egyptian, a Jordanian and a US citizen. The Japan Times describes the attack as the deadliest in the kingdom in more than a decade and says no alert sounded. The Saudi-led coalition blamed the Houthis, called it a war crime and promised a firm response.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said an American was among the dead, UN Secretary-General António Guterres condemned the attack and Trump said he will take action; NPR writes that he is weighing joining the offensive. The US, the UK, Germany, Australia and Canada advised citizens to avoid the airport. Separately, US Central Command said it disabled a cargo ship that had left an Iranian port and ignored warnings of the naval blockade, in the Gulf of Oman.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · death toll unconfirmed

Trump says “it’s time for Ukraine to have a new president” after Zelensky criticizes the Russian diesel deal

The US president said on Saturday that it is time for Ukraine to have a new president, after Zelensky said the decision to accept Russian diesel was weak and a gift to Putin (BBC, Washington Post, Politico). Trump also warned that Zelensky must stop attacks on Russian refineries and that Ukraine should hold elections; CBS reports that Kyiv says it will not stop attacking Russian oil facilities.

Zelensky wrote on Telegram that Russia “thanked” Washington for the easing of diesel sanctions by dropping five guided bombs on homes in Zaporizhzhia, and reported 12 dead, three of them children; Reuters had reported seven dead. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham spoke with Zelensky and promised to step up sanctions; Germany is keeping its own. Republicans criticized the deal, according to The Hill.

Environment · USPress ✓ 4 outlets · provisional toll

Isaias weakens to a post-tropical storm, leaving at least four dead and 670,000 customers without power

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida, on Friday night as a Category 2 hurricane and weakened within hours to a post-tropical cyclone. CBS and NBC count at least four dead, and one news agency reported about 670,000 customers without power in Florida, Alabama, Georgia and Mississippi (GMA News). A tree that fell on a house in Birmingham, Alabama, reportedly killed one person.

The New York Times writes that coastal towns were relieved the damage was not worse, with downed trees and power cuts, but that there is a risk of flash floods in the Southeast. A Bloomberg Intelligence analyst allowed that insured losses could run to several billion dollars. Bloomberg had reported on Friday that almost three quarters of Gulf of Mexico oil production was shut down.

World · SpainPress ✓ 4 outlets · votes according to EFE

Sánchez opens pre-campaign on housing and accuses PP and Vox of spreading rumors about postal voting

Pedro Sánchez held on Saturday, in Dos Hermanas, near Seville, the first rally of the pre-campaign for the November 29 general election, called after the Cortes were dissolved, and promised to “strengthen and maintain” the housing decrees and that this policy should “have no expiry date” (ABC, Expansión, El País, El Mundo). Eight days ago Congress rejected two decrees with the votes of the PP, Vox and Junts, by 172 to 178 and by 166 to 184, according to EFE.

Sánchez accused the PP and Vox of spreading rumors about postal voting: “They are afraid of losing.” The president of the Community of Madrid, Isabel Díaz Ayuso, said she fears “fraud” on November 29, and ABC reports that Feijóo is preparing a plan against manipulation. The reports offer no evidence of fraud. For Portugal, Spain is the main trading partner.
