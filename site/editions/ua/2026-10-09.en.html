Aerial bomb hits two scheduled buses in Kramatorsk: 33 dead, 18 wounded

The strike on Thursday morning fell on a stop almost in the center of the city. Donetsk region authorities halted public transport in Kramatorsk and Sloviansk; some of the dead have still not been identified.

On Thursday, October 8, at around ten in the morning, Russian aircraft dropped a guided aerial bomb on two scheduled buses at a stop almost in the center of Kramatorsk (Meduza, Gulf News, Slovo i Dilo). The city council first reported 12 dead, then 30, and by evening 33 dead and 18 wounded; this was reported by the State Emergency Service and Vadym Filashkin, head of the Donetsk regional military administration. Zelensky said that some of the bodies could not be identified because of the severity of the injuries, and declared that the strike “will not go unanswered.”

Filashkin ordered public transport stopped in Kramatorsk and in Sloviansk; under the procedural supervision of the regional prosecutor’s office, a pre-trial investigation into a war crime has been opened. In the evening a Russian drone attacked a civilian car in the city, two people were killed, and the total number of dead over the day, according to the city council, reached 32 (kurs.com.ua) — a different counting method from the 33 from the strike on the buses. Russia denies that it strikes civilians.

Zelensky: Ukraine is preparing a response to a new wave of Russian escalation, seeking air defense and ammunition for F-16s

On Thursday, October 8, Zelensky said on Telegram that Ukraine is preparing responses to a new wave of Russian escalation, following reports from SBU head Oleksandr Poklyad and Deputy Head of the Office of the President Pavlo Palisa (Censor.NET). According to him, Kyiv is working with all partners who can provide additional packages of air defense and ammunition for the F-16. The president gave no details.

The statement came after a week of deadly strikes: Pryluky on October 7, Kramatorsk on October 8. Zelensky also said that Ukraine is fulfilling the requests of corps and brigade commanders.

Ukrainian drones apparently hit the Salavat refinery in Bashkortostan: a fire, but no official data

Overnight into October 8, Ukrainian drones, according to the monitoring channels Exilenova+ and Supernova+, hit the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat complex in Bashkortostan; the independent channel Astra concluded that the fire really did break out on the plant’s grounds (Meduza, Kyiv Independent). The authorities of Bashkortostan declared an alert but did not report any consequences. Kyiv Independent could not verify the information on its own.

The plant has been hit before: in July its primary units were damaged, and in August the General Staff stated that facilities had been hit. This is part of the campaign of strikes on Russian refineries that Kyiv has been waging in the summer and autumn of 2026.

Dollar rate barely changed: NBU sets 44.8526 hryvnias, euro 50.1385

The National Bank set the official rate for Friday, October 9: the dollar at 44.8526 hryvnias, 1.1 kopecks less than on Thursday (44.8639); the euro at 50.1385 hryvnias, 1.8 kopecks less (kurs.com.ua, Minfin). For comparison, on Wednesday the dollar cost 44.9453, and on Tuesday 45.0564. This is the third decline in a row after the peak of October 6.

We could not confirm the PFTS index for October 8. Interbank quotes at noon on Friday were around 44.9 hryvnias, but they are not the official rate.

WTI crude rises 3.6% to $91.50; S&P 500 −0.47%, gold +0.20%

On Thursday, October 8, WTI closed at $91.50 a barrel, +3.60% on the day. The S&P 500 fell 0.47% to 7,765.36 points, and gold added 0.20% to trade at $4,157.0 an ounce. We did not separately establish the reasons for the oil jump. For comparison, a day earlier WTI cost 88.28.

Oil remains sensitive to news about the Strait of Hormuz and to statements from Iran. The risk is growing that the rise in price will reach Ukrainian filling stations too.
