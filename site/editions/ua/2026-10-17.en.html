Trump on Zelensky: “not the man worth dealing with”

On Friday the US president spoke sharply about the Ukrainian leader and criticized strikes on Russian refineries — and Putin promised a response.

On Friday, Oct. 16, US President Donald Trump said that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky is “not the man” Washington should deal with, and that in his view it is easier to reach agreements with Putin. This was reported by Ukrainska Pravda, NV, RBC-Ukraine and TSN, and the substance of the quotes is the same in all four. Later NV and one more outlet carried another of his statements: Trump does not care who started the war, he only wants it to end. We note separately that these are words, not decisions taken: in the publications we gathered there is no new document or order behind these statements.

The second layer concerns strikes on oil refineries. According to NV, Trump called the renewed Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries a “disgrace.” Ukrainska Pravda and NV also write about something else: in his view Russia has “plenty of targets,” so Ukraine does not have to hit refineries in particular. For Kyiv this is not abstract: strikes on fuel infrastructure remain one of the few levers of pressure on the Russian economy that Ukraine applies with its own forces.

Moscow answered with rhetoric of its own. On the evening of Oct. 15 Putin said that Russia would respond to Ukrainian strikes on refineries and energy facilities, and added that “they started it”; Ukrainska Pravda, NV and other outlets note that he disregarded the so-called energy truce that Trump had announced earlier. Note the chronology: Putin’s words are dated Oct. 15, while Trump’s criticism appeared on the 16th, so these are two separate events that the media present side by side.

There is also a third topic that we can present from only one source. NV relays Zelensky’s words that Ukraine asked the US for anti-ballistic missiles to protect its energy sector during the so-called diesel arrangement with Russia, but was refused. There is no independent confirmation from the American side in our materials, so this is the Ukrainian side’s version. What remains unknown: whether this rhetoric will change actual aid, and whether Kyiv will respond publicly. We will watch whether actions follow the words.

It is important for the reader to distinguish three levels of information. The first is Trump’s direct quotes, which several Ukrainian outlets conveyed independently; they can be considered reliably reproduced, although the publications give the context of the conversation in which they were said only briefly. The second is Putin’s statement, dated the previous evening, about a response to strikes on the energy sector and oil refining. The third is the Ukrainian president’s claim about the US refusal, which for now remains a one-sided version. That is why in this issue we do not claim that Washington’s policy has already changed: we record the words, the sources and the dates, and mark separately what has been verified and what has not.

After the EU summit: Costa calls Ukraine a priority

European Council President António Costa, after the two-day summit, assured that support for Ukraine remains an unchanged EU priority; this was reported by Ukrainska Pravda and Yevropeiska Pravda. The latter separately analyzes what the summit changed for Kyiv: its headline mentions Poland, the Hungarian leader Magyar and Trump, as well as the question of who backed Zelensky. This is the outlet’s assessment, not an official conclusion of the summit. On the Hungarian track, two outlets report an invitation for Zelensky to Budapest next week against the backdrop of restored relations. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said that there are currently no political obstacles to opening the remaining clusters in the EU accession talks, because relations with Hungary are improving. In addition, Poland, according to Zelensky, provided “non-public aid” for the defense of Kyiv, and Nawrocki said he opposes easing sanctions on Russia. The trip to Budapest is so far only an invitation, and the date is not confirmed. Separately, it is worth noting that opening the remaining clusters depends on the unanimity of the member states, so any warming with Hungary has practical significance for the pace of the talks. At the same time, neither of the two outlets names specific dates for decisions, so we do not promise readers quick results: this is only a political signal that should be checked against the next official statements from Brussels and Kyiv over the coming weeks.

Gripen, F-16 and French packages: Kyiv counts on aircraft

According to Zelensky, Ukraine is to receive a batch of Swedish Gripen fighters “very quickly”; this was reported by RBC-Ukraine and Ukrainska Pravda after his conversation with Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson. The quotes give no exact dates or numbers. He also said that Ukraine is counting on F-16s from Belgium to counter drones (RBC-Ukraine).

After meeting Macron, Zelensky announced that in October Ukraine would receive new packages from France to strengthen its aviation; NV and Ukrainska Pravda confirm this. Separately, NV writes that British Defense Secretary Wes Streeting went to a meeting with Hegseth, seeking to become a “bridge” between the US and Ukraine. All of this is so far statements; actual transfers should be awaited from the official agencies.

Why it matters: every night Ukraine fends off attacks by drones and missiles, and fighter jets, especially Western ones, help shoot down part of the air targets and strengthen mobile air defense. However, none of the outlets reports contracts already signed or dates when the aircraft will arrive, so expectations are best kept within the bounds of politicians’ promises. For those following the topic, it is useful to compare new statements with earlier ones and to watch for responses from the defense ministries of partner countries.

Starshield: the order to restrict it came from the White House

NV, citing Reuters, reports that the order to restrict the operation of the Starshield satellite communications for Ukrainian forces reached the US Defense Department from the White House. We have only this one secondhand source, and the materials we gathered contain no details on the scale of the restrictions, so the confidence level is medium. Separately, we see no official comment from the Pentagon, from the operating company, or from the Ukrainian Defense Ministry, and therefore cannot assess whether the new restrictions are already in effect in reality.

Another item: according to Ukrainska Pravda and Google News, the U.S. Treasury Department extended until April 24, 2027 the general licenses that permit operations with the foreign assets of Russia’s Lukoil. That is, sanctions pressure in this area has effectively been eased in time. How this relates to Trump’s statements above, the documents do not explain, so we draw no conclusions about a causal link.

For the reader this means the following: the two stories concern different instruments of pressure — technological and financial — and each has its own mechanism for decision-making. We advise waiting for official documents from the US Defense Department, Reuters and the Treasury before drawing conclusions about a change of course, because first reports are often refined over the following days.
