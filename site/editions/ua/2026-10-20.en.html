Zelensky: Washington’s steps look like an attempt to force Ukraine to capitulate

In an interview with Bloomberg the president spoke of US pressure, a delay in missiles for Patriot and restrictions on Starlink.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said in an interview with Bloomberg, published on Oct. 18, that Washington’s latest decisions look like pressure aimed at forcing Ukraine to surrender to Russia. As UNIAN, Ukrainska Pravda and Censor.net relayed, he clarified that he is not accusing the partners directly, but believes steps were taken to persuade Kyiv to capitulate. NV and RBC-Ukraine reported that Ukraine has paid for missiles for Patriot through the PURL mechanism, yet the US is supplying only 12 such missiles a month, and some of the deliveries have still not been approved; American media compare this with volumes four times larger under the administration of Joe Biden. According to the same accounts, Kyiv is ready to stop strikes on Russian oil refineries if it receives more missiles for air defense, but in response hears only a demand to halt attacks on diesel infrastructure. Ukrainska Pravda quoted his words: if the US officially sides with Russia, Ukraine will not be able to hold out.

On the same day Zelensky said that the message about restricting Starlink for Ukraine came from the US government and that the restrictions are still in force, Ukrainska Pravda and NV reported. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged him to be “very careful” with statements about Starlink; Censor.net relayed his assessment of these statements as “very irresponsible.” US President Donald Trump, according to Ukrainska Pravda and Yevropeiska Pravda, said that Zelensky never wanted to end the war and that Ukraine lies in ruins. In the night into Oct. 19 Russia struck Kyiv with ballistic weapons; NV, citing Radio Liberty, reported that the US did not warn Ukraine about these strikes. RBC-Ukraine also relayed a statement from Trump’s circle expressing indignation at the words about Starlink. No official response from the White House to the accusations of pressure to capitulate has been published.

Russian ballistic missiles hit Kyiv: one woman killed, two metro stations closed

In the night into Oct. 19 Russia attacked Kyiv and the surrounding region twice in one night with ballistic weapons, NV and Ukrainska Pravda reported. According to Zelensky, 16 ballistic missiles were launched, and again no warning came from the partners. Rescuers reported one woman killed — Censor.net named her as a 21-year-old girl — and eight injured in several districts of the capital, the AP agency relayed. A metro station on the left bank and the tracks beside it, a medical facility, a multistory residential building and an educational institution were damaged. The Chernihivska and Lisova stations of the red line were closed, extra bus runs were added in the city, NV, RBC-Ukraine and Censor.net reported. RBC-Ukraine also wrote that on Oct. 19 outage schedules were in effect in the country because of the consequences of the shelling; Ukrenergo had reported the hours starting from the evening of Oct. 18.

World · Middle East✓ 3 outlets · US conditions and exact opening date not confirmed

Iran says it is ready to open the Strait of Hormuz in six days if the US meets its demands

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said that Tehran is ready to open the Strait of Hormuz within six days provided the country’s demands are met, and the next day to resume talks with the US, Censor.net and TSN reported. According to TSN, Tehran proposed to Washington a plan for restoring shipping and returning to talks, but for this the US must meet a number of Iranian demands. Separately, Araghchi said on Oct. 19 that responsibility for the danger and tension in the strait lies with the US, the FinancialJuice agency relayed.

The Guardian wrote on Oct. 19 that at least 15 vessels had been hit in the strait over the past ten days, and local residents are collecting cargo that fell from container ships. An IRGC spokesman, according to Tehran Times, said that Tehran has not yet closed the strait completely and could extend the blockade to the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait and the Suez Canal. An adviser to Iran’s supreme leader, according to Tasnim, called claims that the strait is open groundless. Neither the start date of the six-day countdown nor the list of demands is specified in these reports.

World · France✓ 7 outlets · final damage from the riots still being counted

France introduces partial curfews after riots

Protests over the cost of living and education funding in France turned into riots with arson attacks on public buildings and mass looting, PBS, CBS News and NBC News reported. According to the Interior Ministry, more than half a million people took part in the protests, almost 2400 were detained, almost a thousand police officers and firefighters were injured; the authorities estimate that about 3000 businesses were looted or damaged. President Emmanuel Macron promised in a televised address on Oct. 18 to punish the culprits and blamed the ultra-left for the violence, Ukrainska Pravda and Yevropeiska Pravda reported; ABC News added that he did not accept the resignation of Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu urged local authorities to introduce partial curfews and said he was ready for a state of emergency “if necessary”: in Paris they were announced near the Place de la République and the Les Halles district, in Lille and three other northern cities — for two days, PBS and Al Jazeera reported. Ukrainska Pravda added that listings had appeared on the Vinted platform offering allegedly looted goods for sale.

Economy · Sanctions✓ 5 outlets · deliveries to Turkey not yet confirmed

Trump’s diesel deal with Putin: Turkey will buy part of the Russian fuel

Turkey’s Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar said that the country will buy part of the diesel fuel that Russia will supply to world markets under an agreement with Donald Trump, RBC-Ukraine, Censor.net and UNIAN reported. Trump announced the deal on Oct. 9 on Truth Social: according to him, Russia will immediately supply more than 300 thousand tonnes of diesel, another 500 thousand in November, then a million and another 3 million tonnes, American TV stations, including WAFB, reported. The US Treasury Department issued a temporary license for operations with Russian diesel until April 7 the same day. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright, according to Censor.net and UNIAN, called the permission to export “pressure on Russia” and a “carrot.” Bloomberg TV reported dissatisfaction among some Republicans with such a concession on sanctions.
