Ballistic strike on Kyiv: missiles hit CHP-4 and a nine-story apartment block, at least one dead and 21 injured

Zelensky says that for the second evening in a row Ukraine received no warning of a launch from its partners; the Air Force confirms this. On the night before Wednesday Kyiv again came under threat from ballistic missiles.

On the evening of Tuesday, Oct. 13, Russia struck Kyiv with ballistic missiles. The main target, according to Zelensky on Telegram, was the energy sector — CHP-4 — and the missiles hit it; a residential building was also damaged, where rescuers put out a fire (the president’s Telegram, Ukrainska Pravda, NV, RBC-Ukraine, Censor.NET). Kyiv Post and RBC-Ukraine describe the building as a nine-story block, while the State Emergency Service, cited by NV, reported a fire in a high-rise in the Desnianskyi district and the evacuation of residents. The figures rose through the evening: first one dead and 13 injured (NV, Kyiv Post), then 17, and later 21 injured (TSN, Kyiv Independent). Reuters writes of two dead, Ukrainian outlets of one person; the final number is to be clarified by rescuers.

For the second evening in a row Kyiv received no warning from its partners. The Air Force said that neither that day nor the day before had it received any information about launches from partners (Ukrainska Pravda, Kyiv Independent, Censor.NET); the presidential adviser Litvin confirmed this (NV, UNIAN). According to Zelensky, the system detected the missiles when they were almost at the city. The siren, per Censor.NET, sounded only after loud explosions, and per TSN just before the explosions. Whether this is pressure from Washington, nobody in Kyiv knows: a source in the government told RBC-Ukraine, “we do not yet know whether this is pressure.” Trump claimed the same day that the US is still sharing intelligence (Reuters, Bloomberg, Kyiv Post) — more alongside.

The strike was not the only one. As Reuters wrote, during Tuesday, until the evening, Russia, for the first time since the “energy truce” announced by Trump, did not touch energy targets, and at night it hit urban transport and logistics. On the night of Oct. 13 Russia launched five Iskanders, nine Banderol missiles and 108 drones, with hits recorded at 17 locations (NV, UNIAN). In the morning a drone hit a university building in central Rivne: Kyiv Post writes of 30 injured and students moving to remote learning, while RBC-Ukraine, NV and Ukrainska Pravda reported wounded. During Oct. 13, per NV, RBC-Ukraine and UNIAN, Epicentr’s logistics center in Khmelnytskyi was attacked ten times: about 85% of the goods burned, and employees were not hurt. In Lutsk, fragments of a jet-powered Shahed set fire to the Adrenalin City entertainment center (Ukrainska Pravda, NV, RBC-Ukraine). According to an assessment by “Flash,” the jet-powered drones that Russia is testing against the western regions travel almost 900 kilometers over Ukrainian territory (NV, UNIAN).

On the night before Wednesday the alert was repeated. The Air Force warned of ballistic missiles toward Kyiv and Zhytomyr, and in the capital explosions sounded again; RBC-Ukraine calls this already the second shelling of the night (RBC-Ukraine, TSN, Ukrainska Pravda). We saw no official data on the consequences at the time this issue was prepared. Zelensky urged people to take care during alerts that night and the next day because of the threat of new attacks (TSN), and a jet-powered drone, per Ukrainska Pravda, damaged an administrative building in Ternopil.

What this means. A CHP plant provides both heat and electricity, so a strike on it affects both resources, and winter is already approaching. How much capacity the station lost, the sources do not say, so the scale of the damage is not yet known. Separately, the silence of the partners hurts: with no warning, both people and air defense have less time. The practical advice is unchanged: at the alert signal go to shelter at once, without waiting for explosions, and charge power banks in advance.

No warnings for two evenings in a row: Kyiv says it received no data on launches, Trump says the US provides it

Zelensky said on Telegram and in a conversation with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney that for the second time in a row Ukraine had not received a warning of a ballistic launch from its partners (Ukrainska Pravda, Censor.NET, NV, BBC). The Air Force confirmed this for both days. After the strike on Oct. 12 it had already explained why the alert signal came late (Ukrainska Pravda, RBC-Ukraine, TSN); this time it stated directly that nothing had come in from partners. NV also examined how far Ukrainian air defense depends on American intelligence and gave no clear answer.

On Tuesday Trump said that the US is still sharing intelligence with Ukraine and is working with both Kyiv and Moscow to end the war; he defended the diesel deal with Russia (Reuters, Bloomberg, Kyiv Post). In our sources there is no response from the White House specifically to Kyiv’s words about “two evenings without a warning.” In the government, per RBC-Ukraine, they do not know whether the absence of data is pressure. In his conversation with Carney, Zelensky also touched on future contacts with the US; earlier, per Ukrainska Pravda, Kyiv passed proposals for the next meetings to Washington. So there are claims from both sides and no independent confirmation: whether the transfer of data has stopped, we cannot say.

EU formally opens clusters 2 and 3 for Ukraine after Hungary drops its objection

On Tuesday, Oct. 13, the EU Council opened negotiations with Ukraine on two clusters — “internal market and competitiveness” and “inclusive growth” (Yevropeiska Pravda, Ukrainska Pravda, RBC-Ukraine, Reuters, Kyiv Post). A day earlier all 27 EU states had agreed to this unanimously, after Ukraine passed a law on education for national minorities, which Hungary had demanded (Kyiv Post). According to Reuters, Ireland’s Minister for European Affairs Thomas Byrne said the step became possible when Budapest dropped its objection. Zelensky on Telegram called it another important step on the road to EU membership and thanked Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin, European Council President António Costa, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and every EU country.

The road ahead is less smooth. European Commissioner Kos said that member states will decide special terms for Ukraine on the agricultural sector (Yevropeiska Pravda). Ireland, per Kyiv Post, wants to open all the remaining clusters by the end of the year; Yevropeiska Pravda writes more cautiously: Kyiv expects to open two more. A poll reported by Yevropeiska Pravda and Ukrainska Pravda showed that only 47% of Europeans support quick accession for Ukraine. Lithuania, per Kyiv Post, urged against “artificial delays.”

World · DiplomacyPress ✓ 8 outlets · statements by politicians; details of the deals not made public

Trump’s diesel deal: 12 senators from both parties ask to cancel it, Witkoff and Kushner call it leverage for talks

Twelve senators — six Republicans and six Democrats — demanded that Trump cancel the Russian diesel arrangement concluded on Oct. 9; the letter calls it a “gift to our adversary” and a betrayal of allies (Kyiv Post, Censor.NET, NV). Reuters writes that more and more Republicans are distancing themselves from the deal. Trump defended it (Reuters, Bloomberg). He also said that Putin wants a deal more than Zelensky does (RBC-Ukraine, NV, Ukrainska Pravda) and insisted that Ukraine and Russia had agreed to an “energy truce” because he said so; at the same time he acknowledged violations on both sides (Ukrainska Pravda, Kyiv Independent, NV).

Trump’s envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, per Reuters, explained to the Ukrainians that the deal is an attempt to bring Russia to the negotiating table, and asked Kyiv to stop strikes on refineries (Ukrainska Pravda, NV). According to the NYT, cited by NV, Witkoff joined Trump’s conversation with Putin, and US contacts with Russia may be broader than what is public. The Washington Post, per RBC-Ukraine and NV, writes that the US and Europe are preparing a deal with security guarantees for Ukraine, a ceasefire along the front line and energy arrangements; there are no details or timelines. In the Kremlin, per RBC-Ukraine and NV, they stated that Russia can export more diesel than Trump promised. German Chancellor Merz urged Moscow to accept an energy ceasefire (Kyiv Post), and Zelensky said that Germany is organizing a meeting of the “coalition of the willing” (Ukrainska Pravda, Yevropeiska Pravda).

Economy · EnergyPress ✓ 8 outlets · US closing figures for Tuesday not confirmed by two sources

Winter and money: rolling outages from 06:00 to 23:59 on Wednesday, the government allocates another UAH 60 billion for defense, and world exchanges are shaken by a chip stock sell-off

Ukrenergo reported that on Oct. 14 in some regions rolling outages for all consumers, from 0.5 to 2 queues, and power limitation schedules for business and industry will be in force from 06:00 to 23:59 (NV, RBC-Ukraine). The Cabinet of Ministers allocated an additional UAH 60 billion for the needs of the Defense Forces, most of it for payments to service members and their families, Prime Minister Koretskyi said (NV, RBC-Ukraine). According to him, Ukraine is changing the protection of energy facilities for Russia’s new attack tactics (Censor.NET). Budanov reported that the authorities are preparing crisis scenarios for the period from November 2026 to March 2027 (NV, RBC-Ukraine).

The US exchanges closed on Tuesday, Oct. 13, at 20:00 Greenwich time, but there is no numerical data in our pool that two outlets would confirm, so we give no figures. Something else is known: South Korea’s Kospi index fell by more than 5% on Oct. 13 in a sell-off of semiconductor makers’ shares over fears about the artificial intelligence boom (AP, Reuters), American chip stocks also continued to fall (Reuters), and on the morning of Oct. 14 Asian stocks recovered somewhat, with the market awaiting US inflation, the CPI (Reuters). The IMF warned of correction risks in the AI market, war and debt (Reuters). Oil, per Reuters, rose on supply risks from Saudi Arabia and the flow of Russian diesel. Goldman Sachs, Citi and Wells Fargo beat profit forecasts, JPMorgan did not (Reuters); Bank of America was fined more than $1.2 billion (Reuters, Bloomberg).
