Politics · US and Starlink✓ 6 outlets · who exactly ordered the speed restriction not confirmed

Zelensky: Starlink restrictions on the military are still in place, Bessent calls such statements irresponsible

In an interview with NBC News the president also said that the US had resumed intelligence sharing, and called Washington’s official support for Russia a historic mistake.

In an interview with NBC News on “Meet the Press,” published on Sunday, Oct. 18, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said that restrictions on Starlink for Ukrainian troops are still in force. In his words, it was a “message from the American government” meant to limit the flight speed of Ukrainian drones, as NPR and Axios report. Zelensky added that connection speed has not been fully restored. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, in response, warned Zelensky to be “very careful” with statements that, in his words, do not correspond to reality (CNBC). As Censor.net and UNN relayed, Bessent called such words irresponsible and said that neither President Donald Trump nor Secretary of State Marco Rubio had given instructions to slow down satellite communications; about the causes of the network failures, he said, he knows nothing. Earlier, on Oct. 16, Reuters reported, citing sources, that the order to restrict Starshield for Ukraine came from inside the White House; CNBC and Forbes also wrote about this. Elon Musk, according to CNN, said that these systems are controlled by the US government.

During the same conversation Zelensky said that the US had resumed intelligence sharing with Ukraine, including on Russian ballistic missiles, which had been suspended for several days (LB.ua, Yevropeiska Pravda). According to Yahoo News, he thanked Washington for unblocking the exchange. Zelensky also told NBC News that it would be a historic mistake if the United States officially sided with Russia, and that he still wants to trust Trump and hopes his decisions will change (Inquirer, The Hill). On Saturday Rubio described the sanctions exemption for Russia as a 60-day pause to analyze the impact on the global economy, while Zelensky called this exemption yet another form of support for Russian aggression (Inquirer, tag24). Forbes notes that the day before, Trump had sharply criticized the Ukrainian president.

War · Overnight attack✓ 4 outlets · final number of casualties being clarified

Russia hits Ukraine with an Iskander-M and 180 drones, hits recorded at 20 locations

In the night into Oct. 18 Russia attacked Ukraine with an Iskander-M ballistic missile launched from the Voronezh region and 180 strike drones, the Air Force reported (LB.ua, Censor.net, Ukrainska Pravda). As of 08:00, 152 drones had been shot down or suppressed, hits were recorded at 20 locations, and debris fell in four more places. The drones included 26 jet-powered Shaheds, as well as S-8000s, Gerberas and decoys; launches came, among others, from the directions of Oryol, Bryansk, Kursk and Primorsko-Akhtarsk (LB.ua, Censor.net). In the morning the Russians struck the Osnovianskyi district of Kharkiv twice: local authorities reported damage to two apartment buildings, power lines and two filling stations, and police reported damage to railway infrastructure (Suspilne Kharkiv). Rivne and its surroundings came under a massive strike, the State Emergency Service put out the fires, and there are no casualties (NV, Suspilne Rivne); in the evening a new attack hit a production enterprise, which stopped work (24 Channel). In Odesa a drone hit the roof of a high-rise residential building, two people were injured, including a 13-year-old girl, Ukrainian outlets reported.

Security · Baltics✓ 4 outlets · origin of the drone not confirmed

Lithuania and Latvia declared an alert over an unidentified drone, debris that damaged a car found in Lithuania

On the morning of Oct. 18 an air raid alert sounded in Lithuania that lasted about an hour, while in Latvia an alert was declared in the municipalities of Bauska, Aizkraukle and Ogre, toward which, according to reports, an unidentified object was moving (Ukrinform, h.ua). According to Reuters, two Italian Eurofighter jets were scrambled to patrol. In the village of Juodžionys in the Pasvalys district, debris was found that, according to LRT, damaged a car door; the driver was not hurt (Ukrinform, Ukrainska Pravda). Residents sent about 50 reports of an object falling, and the area where the debris lay was cordoned off (Ukrinform).

Ukraine’s Foreign Ministry spokesman Heorhii Tykhyi said the incident was probably caused by Russian electronic warfare (Ukrainska Pravda). ZN.ua reported that the debris probably belongs to a Ukrainian drone, and life.kyiv.ua and RBC-Ukraine that the aircraft could have flown in from Poland. Kurs.com.ua writes that Ukraine apologized after NATO fighter jets were scrambled. Romanian F-16 fighters on the day before, Oct. 17, shot down two jet-powered drones over the Black Sea, outlets reported, citing Romania’s Defense Ministry.

World · France✓ 4 outlets · final number of detainees differs between sources

In France more than a thousand people detained after nationwide protests, authorities speak of arson and looting

On Saturday, Oct. 17, large-scale protests swept across France, which, according to CNN, were accompanied by clashes with police in Paris. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu, as CBC reported, cited about 1200 detainees and more than 150 injured police officers. France 24 on Sunday reported 1800 detainees, according to Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez, and damage to about 1500 businesses with losses of hundreds of millions of euros. Some participants set fire to a tourist office and looted shops, and a historic town hall was burned (France 24). In Saint-Étienne a young protester lost a hand, and Nuñez said an investigation had been opened.

President Emmanuel Macron promised to bring those responsible for the violence to justice, and publications name the cost of living and conditions in schools as the causes of the protests (France 24, CNN). Donald Trump linked the protests to migration and urged Paris to close its borders, and Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar, against the backdrop of events, spoke in favor of a tough migration policy (Yevropeiska Pravda, Ukrainska Pravda).

Politics · Elections and diplomacy✓ 5 outlets · timing of possible elections not named

After talks with Witkoff and Kushner, Zelensky says he will run if elections are held during the war

On Oct. 18 Zelensky said he had held a conversation with Donald Trump’s special representatives Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. The topics, according to LB.ua, UNIAN and Ukrainska Pravda, were the results of a meeting between Ukrainian and American teams in Florida, air defense and the next diplomatic steps. The president added that the Ukrainian and American teams are in almost constant contact (LB.ua, Espreso).

In the same NBC News interview Zelensky said he considers it his duty to run for a second term if elections are held during the war (LB.ua, Ukrainska Pravda, RBC-Ukraine, Espreso). In his words, preparation requires at least 60 days of ceasefire without additional negotiations in order to change legislation and create the necessary infrastructure; after the war ends, the country would need about six months (Obozrevatel, NVIP). NVIP notes that Moscow has previously rejected the condition of a ceasefire. Separately, in his evening address Zelensky said that since the start of autumn the Defense Forces have liberated more territory than Russia has managed to seize, and denied Putin’s claim of taking Dobropillia (Ukrainska Pravda, UNIAN).
