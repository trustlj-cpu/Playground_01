KOSPI falls for fourth straight session to below the 5,000 mark… won-dollar exchange rate in the high 1,330-won range

On the 17th, ahead of Nvidia’s earnings report, the KOSPI gave up the 5,000 level and the dollar set a fresh 19-month high.

On the 17th, the KOSPI opened at 5,008.09, down 92.41 points (1.81%) from the previous session, and at one point during trading the index dropped below the 5,000 mark. The KOSPI fell for a fourth straight session and, having given up the 5,000 mark, was reported to have slid to its lowest level in more than nine months. The “5,000 mark” is the line by which the market’s mood has been gauged: whether the index sits above or below the number 5,000. The day came just ahead of Nvidia’s earnings report, and investors, including those in the semiconductor sector, had their eyes on the United States.

On the same day the won-dollar exchange rate also rose to the high 1,330-won range amid a strong dollar. The reference rate at 3:30 p.m., when daytime trading in the Seoul foreign exchange market ends, was reported at 1,338.2 won, up 2.5 won from the previous session. A rising rate means it takes more won to buy one dollar. The dollar was reported to have renewed its 19-month high once again. Weak stocks and a weak won coincided on the same day, but there has been no official announcement tying the two moves to a single cause. The market’s reaction after Nvidia’s earnings report is the immediate thing to watch.

Jensen Huang’s first statement since resigning: “I will return to Nvidia if the board asks”

Jensen Huang, who stepped down as Nvidia’s chief executive officer (CEO), issued his first statement since resigning on the 17th (U.S. time). It said Huang is willing to return to the CEO post if the board asks. Reports also followed that the statement contained wording acknowledging mistakes in personal matters. Earlier, on the 16th, U.S. President Donald Trump said former CEO Huang had not put national security at risk, and also remarked in effect that a return was not objectionable. Reports said Nvidia’s board has never discussed a return and is focused on the inquiry into the security allegations, but no official statement from the board has been confirmed. One report also said the U.S. House will call former CEO Huang to a hearing on the 1st of next month, but the date and whether the appearance will happen need further confirmation. A hearing is a procedure in which a legislature summons those involved and questions them in public. With Nvidia’s first earnings report since the resignation approaching, what position the board will take is still undecided.

Labor, business and government begin a real tug-of-war over a ‘52-hour workweek exception’ in mega-regional special zones

Labor, business and the government sat down face to face on the 17th in social dialogue over the special provisions to be written into the ‘Mega-Regional Special Zone Act.’ The core point of contention was reported to be whether to apply a 52-hour workweek exception to high-income research and development jobs. The 52-hour workweek is a working-hours cap that allows only up to 12 hours of overtime on top of the 40 hours a week set by law. Opinions are divided over whether this cap can be applied differently in a special zone or for particular job groups.

Whether the 17th’s talks produced an agreement has not been confirmed. Special provisions are a system that makes exceptions to the application of general law in particular regions or fields, and how the outcome of the talks will be reflected in the bill is not yet known.

Trump: “Could meet Kim Jong-un in China”… “A China trip is up to Xi Jinping”

U.S. President Donald Trump said on the 16th that a meeting with Kim Jong-un in China was possible. Asked where a meeting could take place, Trump named China, and added that whether Kim Jong-un goes to China is up to Chinese President Xi Jinping. President Trump is about to visit China. President Lee Jae-myung was reported to have arrived on the 17th in Shenzhen, China, where the APEC summit is being held.

On the 17th the Kremlin said that if Kim Jong-un attends the APEC summit, a meeting with Russian President Putin is possible. One report said the Chinese side’s position is that it knows nothing about whether Kim Jong-un has been invited to APEC. Whether the U.S. and North Korean leaders will actually meet, and whether Kim Jong-un will visit China, has not been confirmed. President Trump’s remark raised a possibility; no schedule or agreement has been announced.

Cabinet meeting approves ‘fabricated prosecution’ special counsel act… People Power Party objects: “Lee Jae-myung prison-break act”

On the 17th, at a Cabinet meeting chaired by Prime Minister Han Seong-sook, the government deliberated and approved the promulgation proposal for the so-called ‘fabricated prosecution’ special counsel act. With President Lee Jae-myung on an overseas tour to attend the ASEAN and APEC summits, the prime minister led the meeting. The act provides for a special counsel investigation to establish the truth of allegations of fabricated investigations and fabricated indictments by state agencies during the Yoon Suk-yeol government. According to one report, 12 items are subject to investigation, including 6 cases related to President Lee Jae-myung, with up to 309 investigators and a period of 200 days. Because these details come from a single report, they need further confirmation.

The People Power Party, which had pressed for use of the right to request reconsideration (veto) on the grounds that the president has a possible conflict of interest, objected after the approval, calling it the ‘Lee Jae-myung prison-break act.’ Oh Se-hoon criticized it, saying “an extraterritorial president has been born.” The purpose of the act is described as uncovering the truth behind the allegations of fabricated investigations and indictments. When the act will actually be promulgated and when the investigation will begin have not been confirmed.
