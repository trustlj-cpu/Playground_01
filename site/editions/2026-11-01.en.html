Korea, US, Japan foreign ministers reaffirm ‘complete denuclearization’ of North Korea… only the US raises ‘Chinese coercion in the Yellow Sea’

Meeting again after about a month, the three ministers in Atlanta on the 30th urged North Korea to engage in dialogue and signed an Arctic cooperation memorandum. Wording aimed at China went only into the US document.

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Japanese Foreign Minister Motegi Toshimitsu held a Korea-US-Japan foreign ministers’ meeting in Atlanta, in the United States, on the 30th (local time) and reaffirmed their firm commitment to the complete denuclearization of North Korea and the full implementation of UN Security Council resolutions. The meeting was held on the sidelines of the Group of 20 (G20) foreign ministers’ meeting, about a month after the September meeting in New York. The three ministers urged North Korea to join efforts to ease tensions and settle peace, and agreed to maintain a deterrence posture while pursuing denuclearization efforts through dialogue and diplomacy in parallel. They also highly praised the work of the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team (MSMT), which has been uncovering North Korea’s sanctions violations and evasion. Minister Cho said Korea would play a “pacemaker” role in resuming North Korea-US dialogue. The three countries also signed a memorandum of cooperation (MOC) to expand maritime safety and economic cooperation in the Pacific Arctic waters, and confirmed coordination on economic security areas such as supply chains, small modular reactors (SMR) and artificial intelligence (AI).

The documents the two countries issued on the same meeting carry different weight. The US State Department said Secretary Rubio raised China’s attempts to secure control of the South China Sea, its coercive behavior in the East China Sea and the Yellow Sea, and the problem of cooperation between China and Russia that undermines peace and stability in the Indo-Pacific. The Foreign Ministry’s release, by contrast, has no passage on the Yellow Sea, and puts dialogue and diplomacy first. The Korean and Japanese accounts do not mention this passage either. Minister Cho also discussed bilateral relations, high-level exchanges and visa issues separately with Secretary Rubio. Whether wording aimed at China will go into a joint three-way statement, and whether North Korea-US dialogue will actually resume, has not yet been decided. The date of the next meeting of the three ministers has not been made public.

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman visits Korea for two days… meets some 50 Samsung presidents and lunches with SK Chairman Chey Tae-won

OpenAI chief executive officer (CEO) Sam Altman visited Korea on a two-day, one-night schedule and met Samsung and SK executives in succession on the 31st. That day Samsung held a meeting of its presidents, to which Altman was invited, at the Samsung Human Resources Development Institute in Yongin, Gyeonggi. Some 50 presidents of affiliates in electronics, finance, bio and other fields attended and exchanged views under the theme ‘The Next Decade AI Will Change: Choices for Business.’ Altman said, in essence, that merely using AI is hard to turn into a lasting competitive advantage, and that how fast a company moves with AI and how much better the products it makes will decide the contest. Before the meeting the presidents received hands-on training in generative AI and learned how to organize scattered management materials with AI to build a personal ‘knowledge repository.’ Samsung described this as part of its company-wide AI transformation (AX) drive. The same day SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won had lunch with Altman in Seoul, with SK Hynix and SK Telecom executives present. Altman gave Samsung executives ‘Codex Micro,’ a small input device that OpenAI designed with keyboard maker Work Louder. Built to make the coding agent Codex easier to use, the device is OpenAI’s first hardware product. Last October Samsung and SK signed documents on memory chip supply and data center cooperation tied to OpenAI’s Stargate project, and Altman also sought a visit to Korea this June, but the schedule was cancelled. Whether any specific contract or investment was announced at this meeting has not been confirmed.

Politics · Real estate✓ 3 outlets · whether a downward trend exists unconfirmed

President Lee shares post predicting Seoul home price fall… “On the road to normalizing the asset market, we are enduring for now”

President Lee Jae-myung shared a post predicting a fall in Seoul home prices on X on the 31st and wrote, “On the hard road toward normalizing the asset market, we are enduring for now.” The shared post reflects the view of real estate agencies in Gangnam and Seocho, and says that if current policies continue, Seoul home prices will start falling from the three Gangnam districts and spread to Mapo, Yongsan and Seongdong and the outskirts of Seoul. The president also added, in essence, that once the time of patience passes, people will eventually know. Similar forecast posts have been shared earlier as well.

The statistics still point in different directions. In the Korea Real Estate Board’s weekly trends as of October 26, apartment prices fell 0.38% in Gangnam District, 0.35% in Seocho District and 0.22% in Songpa District. Seoul as a whole, however, rose 0.09%, extending its streak to 90 straight weeks of gains, though the rise was the smallest in two weeks. Whether the fall in the Gangnam area spreads across Seoul will be settled in the next weekly figures. What follow-up measures the government will present is not known.

Ruling and opposition clash over election commission special counsel’s ‘Olgong’ ballot check… People Power Party: “Preserve all evidence,” Democratic Party: “To extend political life”

The National Election Commission (NEC) special counsel team publicly examined about 2.47 million Songpa District ballots from the June 3 local elections, along with on-site CCTV and other material, for 12 hours at the Handball Stadium in Seoul’s Olympic Park on the 28th. Of 433 ballot boxes, the 420 confirmed to have no problems were taken by the NEC, and the 13 to which objections were raised were removed by the special counsel team. People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyeok claimed that storage boxes with damaged or re-processed tamper seals had turned up, and took issue with the special counsel handing over to the NEC the ballots it had dropped from further examination. Jang said, “The NEC is a suspect in this case,” and demanded evidence preservation of all the removed ballots, and the People Power Party kept the same position on the 31st.

The special counsel team said it had asked the NEC to preserve, in their current state, the ballots to which no objection had been raised, in preparation for further examination. According to the special counsel team, these ballots were sorted and repackaged at a third location rather than at the NEC, and the storage place is a security warehouse designated by the NEC, equipped with access control, CCTV and 24-hour security guards. Democratic Party floor spokesperson Lee Ju-hee rebutted that people’s right to vote should not be used as capital for “extending political life.” The claim that the tamper seals were damaged has not yet been confirmed by either side’s account, and no schedule has emerged for further examination of the 13 boxes to which objections were raised.

US tech stocks dumped… Nasdaq down 2.86% on Intel plunge, KOSPI and KOSDAQ also close sharply lower

Tech stocks were swept up in a sell-off on US markets on the 30th. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% to 25,437.00 and the S&P 500 dropped 1.82% to close at 7,580.07, while the Nasdaq 100 declined 3.46%. The trigger was Intel. Intel plunged 28.58% in a single day to close at $65.76 after its fourth-quarter earnings outlook fell far short of market expectations. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.2%, AMD 10.88% and Nvidia 4.22%. Doubts about when Big Tech’s large-scale AI investment will turn into profit added to the selling.

The Seoul market headed into the weekend without yet reflecting this plunge. The KOSPI closed the same day down 206.92 points (3.48%) at 5,730.74 and the KOSDAQ down 28.85 points (3.83%) at 724.38. The KOSPI slid to 5,599.42 intraday, and at around 10:05 a.m. a temporary suspension of program sell orders (a sell-side sidecar) was triggered. When the market opens on Monday the 2nd, ahead of the US midterm elections next Tuesday, Korean investors will face these US figures for the first time.
