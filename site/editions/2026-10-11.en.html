Ceiling collapses at Daegu commercial building, injuring 38. It happened during a sales event for seniors

No deaths have been reported. The 32-year-old building was not subject to mandatory periodic safety inspections, and a joint forensic inspection is scheduled for today

At about 10:16 a.m. on the 10th, ceiling finishing materials collapsed all at once on the second floor of a commercial building in Gwaneum-dong, Buk District, Daegu (Dong-A Ilbo). It was first reported that the ceiling of a first-floor supermarket had fallen, but the actual site was the second floor of the same building. The supermarket had closed at the end of last month, and on the second floor a distributor was holding a promotional event selling kitchenware and other household goods to seniors (Dong-A Ilbo, SBS). The materials kept falling for about 10 seconds (Yonhap), and the 66 people at the venue evacuated outside. The injured described the moment: “There was a rumbling sound, then the ceiling came crashing down” (Yonhap, SBS). The number of injured was first counted at 36, then rose to 38, and 9 were taken to hospital (Yonhap, SBS). Most were reported to have minor injuries, an elderly person in their 70s was also hurt, and no deaths were reported (Dong-A Ilbo, Gangwon Domin Ilbo). Fire authorities began rescue work at the scene right after the report (Yonhap), and SBS reported that an ‘empty space’ above the ceiling spared the building a bigger tragedy. It happened on the morning of the second day of the Hangeul Day holiday.

President Lee Jae-myung ordered “all-out efforts to rescue lives” (Korea Economic Daily, Kyunghyang Shinmun), and the prime minister promised swift transport of the injured and measures to prevent a recurrence (Yonhap). The Daegu Metropolitan Police Agency formed a 33-member dedicated investigation team headed by the Regional Investigation Unit chief, and will hold a joint forensic inspection today with the National Forensic Service (NFS) (Kyunghyang Shinmun, Yonhap). The cause has not yet emerged. Some of the injured said that before the accident they heard a sound as if rats were running above the ceiling (Dong-A Ilbo, Korea Economic Daily), but there is no evidence this was the cause. The issue is building management. The 32-year-old building was small and so was not subject to the periodic safety inspection requirement (Yonhap). How an event gathering elderly people came to be held on the floor above a closed supermarket, and who last inspected the facility, are also subjects of the investigation. Korea Economic Daily described it as a ‘bolt from the blue’ striking elderly people gathered in an old building. Calls may arise to close the blind spot in inspections of small, aging buildings. How the building owner and the event company divided their safety duties also has to be sorted out. Today, the first findings of the joint inspection and the safety measures taken by the event organizer are what to watch.

Trump–Putin diesel deal: 300,000 tonnes of Russian diesel released, sanctions eased for now

U.S. President Trump said on the 9th (local time), after a call with Russian President Putin, that 300,000 tonnes of Russian diesel would be released immediately to the U.S. and world markets (Yonhap, Reuters). Russia also confirmed the lifting of its export restrictions, saying it could supply more diesel to the U.S. and others starting this month (Yonhap). The U.S. has temporarily eased its sanctions on Russian diesel. U.S. media read it as a change of course that relaxed pressure on Russia (The New York Times, PBS). A Kremlin aide said the call was very friendly but ruled out an immediate resumption of Ukraine talks (Reuters). Ukrainian President Zelensky condemned it as a ‘war investment’ and wrote on Telegram that Russia shelled residential areas of Zaporizhzhia, killing 12. The EU said halting sanctions amounts to financing the war, and Germany said it would keep its sanctions (Yonhap, Reuters). Experts see the volume as small and the effect on oil prices as limited (Yonhap). WTI rose 0.39% on Friday to $91.85. New York stocks rose on a tech rebound and on expectations of this deal (Yonhap). Many read it as a price measure ahead of the November midterms (AP, NBC).

Politics · JusticePress ✓ 7 outlets · the allegation is the opposition's claim; Rep. Han denies it

Democratic Party: “Did Han Dong-hoon bury his daughter's case?” Han: “A delusion and a mob smear campaign”

The Democratic Party of Korea demanded on the 10th that independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon say whether, when he was justice minister, he intervened in or covered up the investigation into allegations that his daughter had ‘fake credentials’ (Yonhap). It began with a claim raised on the 8th by Democratic Party lawmaker Park Gyun-taek. Police, while investigating the allegations, requested international criminal judicial cooperation, but prosecutors did not pass it on to the Justice Ministry, the claim goes (Weekly Kyunghyang). The Democratic Party asked, “Did prosecutors bend over backward on their own, or did the minister apply pressure?” (Kyunghyang Shinmun).

Rep. Han fought back: “There is nothing to cover up. It is the Democratic Party's delusion” (JTBC, Korea Economic Daily). Yonhap reported that he called it a “mob smear campaign,” and JoongAng Ilbo and Busan Ilbo reported that he said, “The result of pouncing like a pack of dogs is shabby and pathetic.” How the cooperation request failed to reach the Justice Ministry, and whether the minister was involved, has not yet been confirmed. So far there is only one side's allegation and the other's blanket denial. With the parliamentary audit period under way, questions to the Justice Ministry and prosecutors may come.

ARTEX, the Chinese AI intrusion tool used in financial-sector hacks, halts public release

Yonhap and Chosun Ilbo reported that ARTEX, a Chinese AI penetration-test tool reportedly used in the recent string of hacks on South Korea's financial sector, has stopped its public release (also reported by Kyunghyang Shinmun and Dong-A Ilbo). Penetration-test tools were originally built as programs for checking security weaknesses in advance. Security firm CrowdStrike found the tool's configuration file on an attacker's server and raised the possibility that the attacker is a person in their 20s living in China's Guangdong Province (Money Today Broadcasting, Weekly Kyunghyang). Yonhap also reported circumstances suggesting the suspect was active in a Chinese DDoS-for-hire group. DDoS is an attack that floods a server with connections to paralyze it.

Halting development does not make code that has already spread disappear. There was also a report that a Korean-language version is circulating and raising concerns about abuse (SBS Biz). The parliamentary audit exposed weaknesses on the defense side. Twenty financial firms with frequent IT outages had an information-security budget execution rate below 70% last year (Yonhap), and Woori Bank cut its security budget by 21.7% (Daily NTN). Whether the AI actually carried out the leak of personal data is still up to the authorities' investigation.

Hanwha's manager Kim Kyung-moon and general manager Son Hyuk step down together. Five runner-up finishes, but the longed-for title never came

The Hanwha Eagles officially announced on the 10th that manager Kim Kyung-moon and general manager Son Hyuk are leaving together as their contracts expire (Chosun Ilbo, Yonhap). Kim ended his three years with the club with that day's regular-season final against NC. Hanwha lost 8-9 after a rally in the bottom of the ninth. Hanwha, which fell to ninth this year, missed the postseason (Dong-A Ilbo). Kim said goodbye: “I'm sorry to the fans that I leave without making the team solid” (Chosun Ilbo, KBS).

The club said it will move quickly to pick successors (MBC, Kukje Shinmun). Some outlets named a particular person as the top choice, but the club has not confirmed it. Kim is a manager who finished Korean Series runner-up five times and never won the title (Hankyoreh, Yonhap). In pro baseball, only the race for third place remains. KIA beat SSG 8-5, and Kim Do-yeong hit his 44th home run of the season. KIA's magic number for a direct berth to the Semi-Playoff is 1. LG beat Lotte 4-3 in extra innings but will find it hard to clinch on its own (Yonhap).
