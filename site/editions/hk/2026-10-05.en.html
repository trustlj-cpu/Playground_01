Hong Kong raises foreign domestic helpers’ minimum allowable wage 2.35% to HK$5,220; unions and employer groups both express disappointment

The new wage applies to contracts signed on or after Oct. 3, and the food allowance stays at HK$1,236. The Secretary for Labour and Welfare called the annual adjustment mechanism “balanced and acceptable.”

The Hong Kong government announced on Oct. 2 (Friday) that it is raising the minimum allowable wage for foreign domestic helpers by 2.35%, from HK$5,100 to HK$5,220 a month; where an employer does not provide food and pays a food allowance instead, the minimum stays at HK$1,236 a month (South China Morning Post, The Standard, law firm Lewis Silkin). The new wage applies to contracts signed on or after Oct. 3; contracts signed at the old wage on or before Oct. 2 can still be processed at HK$5,100 as long as the Immigration Department receives them by Oct. 30 (Lewis Silkin). Officials gave the economy’s better performance over the past year as the reason (South China Morning Post).

Chris Sun, Secretary for Labour and Welfare, defended the adjustment, calling the annual adjustment mechanism “balanced and acceptable,” and said that over the years the average annual increase has been about 2% to 2.5% (South China Morning Post). The South China Morning Post also reported that unions and employer groups were both disappointed by the decision. The reports we have seen give no figures for what either side wanted, so we do not speculate. The adjustment concerns foreign domestic helpers only and does not affect the statutory minimum wage for local employees.

Hang Seng Index plunges 640 points on Friday, falls below 24,000, led down by financial stocks

The Hang Seng Index closed at 23,972 on Oct. 2 (Friday), down 640 points, or 2.6%, after touching an intraday low of 23,872. The Standard said rising US Treasury yields and oil prices led investors to cut risk assets. Financial stocks fell hardest: AIA Group fell about 6%, Standard Chartered nearly 6%, HSBC Holdings 5.4% and HKEX about 3%; the Hang Seng TECH Index fell 2.26% to 4,157, and Macau gaming stocks were also weighed down by continuing declines in gaming revenue (The Standard). Trading Economics’ market summary likewise recorded a 2.6% fall on the previous trading day but gave no point figure, so the points figure follows The Standard. Mainland markets are closed for the National Day Golden Week holiday, so turnover in Hong Kong stocks today may be thin.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 6 outlets · amounts as stated by the German and Ukrainian sides

Merz makes surprise Kyiv visit, announces about €1 billion in military aid; Russia attacks the city with drones during the visit

German Chancellor Merz made a surprise visit to Kyiv on Oct. 4 (Sunday) and announced about €1 billion in new military aid, reportedly for long-range strike weapons, combat drones and satellite technology, including joint production of interceptor drones to counter jet-powered drones; a further roughly €350 million is for generators, substations and repairs before winter, bringing the package to about €1.3 billion in total (AP, ITV, Euronews, Euromaidan Press). Air-raid sirens sounded in Kyiv as Merz arrived, and Russia attacked the city with drones at the same time (Business Day, Kuwait Times).

Zelensky said the two countries signed 15 agreements worth more than €8.5 billion, mainly covering security cooperation and arms production (AP). Merz urged Putin to “end this senseless war” and said Germany is “ready to negotiate,” but on condition that attacks on civilians stop (ITV, Euronews). For Hong Kong readers, the impact would come indirectly through European energy prices, defense spending and the euro; no direct impact is visible at this stage.

flydubai jet diverts to Saudi Arabia after cockpit attack: Israeli officials suspect attempted hijacking

flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv diverted to Tabuk in Saudi Arabia on Sept. 30 after an attack in the cockpit. Israeli officials suspect a pilot tried to seize control of the aircraft rather than simply fighting; investigators have not publicly confirmed whether the motive was taking hostages or crashing the plane (Newsweek, The Week, LBC, Airline Geeks). The pilot involved is reportedly an Omani national and the other pilot an Indian national; after passengers and crew subdued the attacker, the other pilots took over and the Boeing 737 MAX landed safely, with everyone aboard surviving. Netanyahu praised the passengers and crew who intervened as “heroes.”

The Standard’s Oct. 2 briefing reported that US President Trump said Iran would be hit hard if it were involved in the incident; the attacked Indian pilot also described what happened to Modi by video call. The Oct. 5 briefing added that investigators are checking the involved pilot’s ties to Australia. Both items appear only in The Standard, and Trump’s remark was hypothetical; no report shows evidence linking the incident to Iran, and we make no judgment. Israel, Saudi Arabia and the UAE are conducting a joint investigation.

Society · CulturePress ✓ 4 outlets · Gazette notice; the proposal process was reported in June

Government gazettes Hung Shing Temple in Wan Chai and Stanley Mosque as declared monuments

The Hong Kong government gazetted on Oct. 2 the declaration of Hung Shing Temple in Wan Chai and Stanley Mosque as declared monuments under the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance (The Standard, Time Out). Both were previously Grade 1 historic buildings; the Antiquities and Monuments Office proposed the upgrade in June this year, the Antiquities Advisory Board accepted it, and the consent of the owners and management bodies has been obtained (The Standard; the South China Morning Post and TVB reported the proposal in June). Hung Shing Temple, on Queen’s Road East, is believed to have been built between 1847 and 1852. It venerates Hung Shing, the “God of the South Sea,” is an example of Lingnan vernacular architecture, and preserves stone carvings, plaster moldings and a Shiwan ceramic roof ridge made in 1909 (The Standard).

Stanley Mosque, built in 1936–37 within the grounds of Stanley Prison, is the second-oldest mosque in Hong Kong and the only one inside a prison; its design is modeled on the Badshahi Mosque in Lahore, Pakistan. It is currently closed to the public, and the authorities are studying opening it to visitors by appointment (The Standard). As declared monuments, neither may be demolished or altered without approval in future.
