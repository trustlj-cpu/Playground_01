SFC, AFRC and HKEX jointly warn: for companies whose auditors issue a “disclaimer of opinion,” rules may change to require trading suspension

Such cases rose from 12 in 2017 to 95 in 2025; 65 of the companies have not shaken off the status in more than a year, the longest for 14 years in a row.

The SFC, the AFRC and HKEX issued a joint statement on Oct. 8 on listed companies whose accounts draw a going concern “disclaimer of opinion” from their auditors. They warned that, unless there is clear improvement, the exchange will consider amending the Listing Rules where necessary, including requiring the issuers concerned to suspend trading (China Daily Hong Kong edition, HKEX announcement, The Standard). On the numbers, such cases rose from 12 in 2017 to 95 in 2025; 65 companies have stayed in this state for more than a year, and the most extreme case has lasted 14 consecutive years (China Daily Hong Kong edition, citing Bloomberg).

The background is that in 2019 HKEX rules introduced a requirement for trading suspension when an auditor issues a “disclaimer of opinion” or an “adverse opinion,” but exempted a disclaimer arising purely from going concern problems, to avoid companies in difficulty being suspended for long periods. Regulators now take the view that an auditor who disclaims an opinion even only on going concern is in effect unable to give an opinion on the accounts as a whole, which weakens their reliability (China Daily Hong Kong edition). The statement sets out expectations for each party: management should assess its ability to continue as a going concern using reasonable assumptions and a feasible action plan, audit committees must scrutinize the assessment closely, and auditors must challenge it and explain why they disclaim an opinion (HKEX announcement). The rules have not been amended so far; the statement is a warning.

Hang Seng Index falls 344 points on Thursday to a three-month low, led down by banks and tech stocks; mainland shares drop after holiday

The Hang Seng Index closed down 344 points, or 1.4%, at 23,785 on Oct. 8 (Thursday), its lowest in three months; banks led the fall, with HSBC Holdings and BOC Hong Kong each down nearly 5%, and the Hang Seng TECH Index fell 2.9% to 4,073. The Standard attributed the sell-off to rising bond yields; Tencent fell about 2% after a report that it is considering issuing bonds to fund AI investment. Mainland investors returned after Golden Week, and both the Shanghai Composite and the Shenzhen Component fell, the Shenzhen Component by about 2.1%; turnover in Hong Kong stocks more than doubled from the previous day (The Standard). All of the above comes from a single outlet; we could not find a second source to check the figures.

Finance · United StatesPress ✓ 2 outlets · rate range and probabilities from a single report

Federal Reserve’s Waller: more rate hikes still needed, but pace “flexible,” October meeting could pause

Federal Reserve Governor Waller said on Oct. 8 (Thursday) that further rate hikes are likely still needed to bring inflation back to the 2% target, but the timing is flexible: “rate hikes need not come at several consecutive meetings,” leaving room for a pause at the October meeting; he did not say how much higher rates still need to go (Kitco, Trading Economics). According to one report, the federal funds target range is currently 3.75% to 4%, and the market puts the probability of no change at the Oct. 27–28 meeting at about 82% and of a hike in December at about 81% (single report, not checked by us).

On Wall Street, The Standard said US stocks opened lower on inflation worries from higher oil prices and high yields, and the S&P 500 closed down 0.47% at 7,765.36 (DailyDrop market data). This does not match the headline circulating online, “Dow up 580 points, Fed hints at no more hikes” (see the “Unverified” column).

World · EnergyPress ✓ 3 outlets · attack is a Houthi claim, damage and casualties await official confirmation; oil price figures differ slightly between reports

Houthis say they struck Riyadh and other Saudi airports again; oil jumps 3.6% in a day to US$91.50

Yemen’s Houthis claimed on Oct. 8 to have attacked King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and Abha airport; Saudi Arabia responded militarily, and witnesses said airport passengers were evacuated; damage and casualties await official Saudi confirmation (Voz, IBTimes, Castanet). Oil prices rose in response: West Texas Intermediate crude futures rose 3.60% to close at US$91.50 (DailyDrop market data); several reports put Brent crude up about 3.8% at around US$104, with figures differing slightly between reports.

Voz’s headline set the rise against the backdrop of renewed mutual threats between the US and Iran. We do not judge the scale of the attack the Houthis claim: until Saudi officials report, losses, casualties and the state of airport operations remain to be confirmed. For Hong Kong, sustained high oil prices would affect electricity bills, fuel surcharges and air fares.

World · LiteraturePress ✓ 4 outlets · reasons for the award as stated by the Swedish Academy

Canadian poet Anne Carson wins Nobel Prize in Literature, the second Canadian writer to receive the award

The Swedish Academy announced on Oct. 8 that this year’s Nobel Prize in Literature goes to Canadian poet and writer Anne Carson, honoring her “audacious and inventive work that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, opens new forms for contemporary literature” (Nobel Prize website, Khaleej Times, Bluewin, The Star/Xinhua). The prize money is 12 million Swedish kronor; the Swedish Academy said she is the second Canadian writer to receive the prize.

Carson was born in Toronto and has also held Icelandic citizenship since 2022; she is known for translating ancient Greek literature, and her 1998 verse novel Autobiography of Red is one of her representative works (Khaleej Times, Wikipedia). Much of this comes from reports and reference sites; we have not checked the Swedish Academy’s full announcement directly.
