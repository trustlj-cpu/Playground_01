Trump says Russia and Ukraine have agreed an “energy ceasefire” effective immediately; Zelensky says he was not told in advance, Moscow has no public response yet

The scope, duration and verification arrangements have not been published; Ukraine says it backs the ceasefire, on condition that Russia observes it too.

US President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social on Sunday, October 11 (around 10 p.m. Hong Kong time), announcing that an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine was in effect immediately, saying both sides had agreed and asking that it “not be broken” (BBC, Bloomberg, Reuters). An energy ceasefire generally means both sides stop attacking each other’s refineries, power plants and oil pipelines, but the actual scope awaits an official text. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told Axios the announcement was “news” to him; he later said on Telegram that Ukraine supports a ceasefire but that Russia must support it too, and that Kyiv is waiting for details from the US; he said Ukraine supports every real and reliable form of ceasefire (France 24, Reuters). In the reports this paper has seen, Moscow has not publicly confirmed it (PM News Nigeria, single source).

No official text has yet appeared setting out the scope, duration or verification arrangements. Zelensky had earlier offered that if Russia stops attacking Ukraine’s energy facilities, Ukraine would stop striking Russian refineries (Reuters); he also told the American Broadcasting Company (ABC) that Ukraine would hold elections if Putin agreed to a 60-day ceasefire (Reuters). The background is two days of wrangling: Trump recently announced a diesel supply arrangement with Russia, Zelensky criticised it, and Trump retorted that it was “time for Ukraine to have a new president” (BBC, South China Morning Post); Germany said it backs Zelensky (Reuters), and Ukrainian officials rejected the talk of changing leaders (New York Times). Treasury Secretary Bessent said the diesel deal is good for Americans (Fox News), while the South China Morning Post reported that a senior Republican senator said the deal may be illegal. The Financial Times reported that the US had lobbied European countries and Kyiv to stop attacking Russian refineries, and threatened to block weapons to Ukraine; meanwhile Ukrainian drones struck a data centre of the Russian technology company Yandex again (Reuters, BBC). For Hong Kong readers this is about energy prices: if the ceasefire holds it could lower supply risk, but until Russia responds and the details are confirmed, one should not assume oil prices will fall as a result. European positions are not uniform: the European Commission said it will increase funding for Ukrainian drones and missiles (FinancialJuice, citing the European Commission), while Estonia’s prime minister urged the EU to help “destroy” Russian refineries (Reddit r/worldnews, single source). On the battlefield, Zelensky said Ukraine continued on Sunday to discuss air-defence assistance with European partners; Russia said it has taken four settlements in Ukraine (Interfax, single source).

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · casualties from the second attack unconfirmed

Houthis attack Riyadh airport on consecutive days: Terminal 4 hit again on Sunday; Saudi Arabia says Saturday’s attack killed 12 and injured more than 300

Saudi authorities said the attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on October 10 (Saturday) killed 12 people and injured more than 300 (Associated Press, Reuters). The airport was hit again on October 11 (Sunday): the Saudi-led coalition said an unidentified projectile struck Terminal 4 and that it intercepted one missile (BBC, Bloomberg); this paper has seen no confirmation of casualties from the second attack. The Houthis admitted responsibility for the first attack and claimed to have hit Dammam airport and Aramco oil facilities, which Saudi Arabia has not confirmed (FinancialJuice, single source). The coalition vowed retaliation (The Guardian); the Financial Times reported that several oil-industry executives pulled out of an energy conference in Riyadh, and Reuters reported that Saudi stocks fell.

US forces disable a cargo ship suspected of running the blockade in the Gulf of Oman; tanker in the Strait of Hormuz hit by unidentified projectile, crew safe

US Central Command said a large cargo ship leaving an Iranian port ignored warnings in the Gulf of Oman and tried to run the US blockade; US aircraft struck the stern with precision munitions, leaving the vessel unable to navigate (Reuters, The Hill, Financial Times). The same day, the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) received a report that an outbound tanker in the Strait of Hormuz was hit on its port side by an unidentified projectile and caught fire, with all crew safe; India’s shipping minister said the ship was the Marshall Islands-flagged M/T Valthera, and that the 11 Indian crew members aboard were unharmed (FinancialJuice, citing UKMTO and Indian authorities).

The secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council said the US response to its proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz was “not sufficient” (The Straits Times, GMA). Al Jazeera described attacks in the Strait of Hormuz as intensifying, with efforts to end the war against Iran at a standstill. An online claim that US forces destroyed 10 Iran-linked tankers worth billions of dollars appears only in a Reddit post title, and this paper cannot verify it. For Hong Kong, the Strait of Hormuz is a key global oil route, and rising shipping risk pushes up freight and insurance costs, but responsibility for each incident has still not been independently confirmed.

New Huanggang Port opens at 6:30 a.m. Monday: Secretary for Security says he is confident of smooth clearance, Guangdong governor says the province will work with the SAR government

RTHK reported that Secretary for Security Chris Tang said on October 11 that he is confident of providing a safe and smooth clearance experience; Guangdong governor Huang Chuping said Guangdong will work closely with the SAR government to manage and make good use of the Huanggang port. The South China Morning Post reported that Chief Executive John Lee called the port a “milestone” in the integration of Hong Kong with the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area; the paper also carried a guide to getting through the new port quickly, and reported that the Secretary for Security had earlier urged travel agencies to postpone arranging visits (South China Morning Post). The port opens at 6:30 a.m. on October 12 (Monday), and the opening ceremony was held on the Shenzhen side on Sunday (The Standard, see this paper’s report of the previous day).

This paper has seen no official figures yet for first-day crowds or actual clearance times; these must wait for Monday. Other local news: RTHK reported that more than 6,000 people took part in events at the Hong Kong Cycling Festival on Sunday; a man who collapsed while hiking at Wu Kau Tang in Tai Po was confirmed dead after being taken to hospital (RTHK, South China Morning Post). The South China Morning Post also reported that Hong Kong has recorded 21 mpox cases since August, and the authorities urged high-risk people to get vaccinated.
