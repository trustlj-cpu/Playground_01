New Huanggang Port opens Monday at 6:30 a.m.; ceremony on Sunday; over 160,000 Hong Kong residents register for “Joint Clearance”

The Lok Ma Chau Control Point and the old Huanggang Port will stop operating the moment the new port opens; the Security Bureau is activating an emergency monitoring and support center.

Hong Kong and Shenzhen have confirmed that the rebuilt New Huanggang Port will formally open at 6:30 a.m. on Monday, October 12; the opening ceremony will be held on the Shenzhen side on Sunday (11th) morning (The Standard, South China Morning Post, China Daily Hong Kong edition). Secretary for Security Chris Tang said on Friday that the Security Bureau’s emergency monitoring and support center and its frontline command center will be activated on Monday to monitor vehicles, pedestrians and surrounding traffic, and that a joint command center has been set up with Shenzhen’s port authorities (China Daily Hong Kong edition). The Immigration Department said that, as of Friday noon, more than 160,000 residents had registered for the e-Channel “Joint Clearance” service (South China Morning Post, China Daily Hong Kong edition).

On the changeover, the existing Lok Ma Chau Control Point and the old Huanggang Port will stop operating the moment the new port opens; the authorities will use road closures, signs and dedicated shuttle buses to guide travelers from the decommissioned facilities to the new clearance building; at first, vehicles entering the port early will be restricted to those already registered, to ensure a smooth launch (China Daily Hong Kong edition). On the old port’s daily passenger flow, two reports in The Standard give 40,000 and 50,000 respectively, and we do not use a single figure. For people who travel to Shenzhen often, it is advisable to allow extra time on the first two days and to watch for traffic notices from the Transport Department and the police.

Trump says he reached a Russian diesel supply deal after a call with Putin, and the US Treasury issues a license; Zelensky calls it a “gift to Putin”

Trump announced on Truth Social on October 9 that, after a call with Putin, the two sides had reached an agreement under which Russia will supply diesel to the US and global markets; his post said a first batch of more than 300,000 tonnes would be supplied immediately, with larger volumes to follow (ITV, The National, ynet, AP via WPXI, Liga.net, AJC). Reports differ on the total: ynet says more than 4.8 million tonnes, while other reports say only “millions of tonnes,” and the timetable for the batches also varies, so we do not use a single figure. The US Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control issued a license covering the sale, delivery, unloading and import of Russian diesel, valid until April 7, 2027, and temporarily exempting some sanctions on Russia’s energy sector so that diesel can supply the global market (multiple reports).

Reports say the White House aims to ease fuel prices before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, but it did not immediately say who would pay, nor whether the November batches could arrive before the election. Zelensky called it an “investment” in a war that must be ended, not prolonged, and said that easing sanctions without a clear and lasting de-escalation agreement is “obvious weakness” (ITV, Liga.net). US Senator Blumenthal criticized the move as making the US “complicit” in Putin’s war, and said it casts doubt on whether the White House will sanction Russia’s major oil buyers this month. The Kremlin said the two sides spent “considerable time” on the prospects for resolving the Ukraine war. Trump also said he had expected the call to be about a suspected plague case in Russia; that claim has not been independently confirmed.

Hang Seng rebounds 425 points to 24,211 on Friday, tech stocks up 3%; turnover HK$209 billion

The Hang Seng Index closed up 425 points, or 1.8%, at 24,211 on October 9 (Friday); the Hang Seng Tech Index rose 3% to 4,197; turnover was HK$209 billion. The Shanghai Composite Index closed flat at 3,813 after falling as much as 1.2% earlier in the session, and the Shenzhen Component rose slightly (The Standard). The Standard said US stocks opened higher, with telecom stocks dragged down by the SpaceX spectrum deal. Hong Kong stocks are closed on Saturday, and the next trading day is Monday (October 12); whether the rebound continues depends on the Middle East situation around the weekend and on US Treasury yields.

South African jurist Navi Pillay wins Nobel Peace Prize; Israel rejects the genocide finding of the commission she led

The Norwegian Nobel Committee announced on October 9 (Friday) that Navanethem “Navi” Pillay of South Africa has been awarded this year’s Nobel Peace Prize; the committee said she “played a key role” in ensuring that war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide are prosecuted (Al Jazeera, South China Morning Post, EWN, The Grio, AP via ABC, The Standard). She was a defense lawyer for Mandela and other anti-apartheid activists, the first woman of color on South Africa’s High Court, and UN High Commissioner for Human Rights from 2008 to 2014; the committee specifically cited her work at the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda, which established that rape and sexual violence can constitute crimes against humanity and genocide.

This year marks the 125th anniversary of the Peace Prize. The prize money is 12 million Swedish kronor, and it will be presented in Oslo on December 10. Pillay, now 85, said she will dedicate the prize to all survivors of international crimes; when the news came she was at the historic courtroom in Nuremberg, Germany, where Nazi leaders were tried. On the dispute: in September 2025, the report of the commission of inquiry she chaired found that Israel had committed genocide in Gaza, and Israel denies the accusation; we do not rule on this dispute and only set out both sides’ accounts.

Tuen Mun child neglect case: Chris Sun promises strict scrutiny of family welfare payments; three young children to go to a care home after leaving hospital

A Tuen Mun family is suspected of child neglect. The case stems from a video of a ten-year-old girl carrying beer in Fu Tai Estate that sparked public anger, and from the girl’s mother claiming that the family received Comprehensive Social Security Assistance (CSSA) while also traveling abroad (The Standard). Labour and Welfare Secretary Chris Sun said on Friday that the Social Welfare Department is examining the family’s assistance records and, if fraudulent claims are involved, will refer the matter to law enforcement; the three young children will be placed in a care home after leaving hospital (The Standard). Police arrested the parents, aged 38 and 32, on suspicion of child abuse or neglect, and both were later released on bail (The Standard).

The case is still under investigation, and no result of the welfare review has been reported yet. Note that the claim that the mother “received CSSA yet traveled abroad” comes from the mother herself and has not been officially confirmed; whether any rule was broken awaits the department’s review. In cases involving children, we do not report further personal details.
