IMF chief warns: energy shock, record public debt and AI risks all threaten global growth at once

Before the annual meetings in Bangkok, Georgieva said high energy prices will persist for some time even if the Gulf war ends soon; public debt is the highest since World War II.

International Monetary Fund (IMF) Managing Director Georgieva spoke on Wednesday, October 7, on the eve of the IMF and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok. She described the Middle East conflict as causing a “negative energy supply shock.” Even if the Gulf war ends soon, she said, the problem of high energy prices is likely to persist for some time. She called the shock “significant but still manageable,” yet said prices could climb again as winter demand rises in the northern hemisphere and countries replenish their reserves (Mining Weekly, Engineering News, Kuwait Times, Kitco, Bernama, TimesLive, Asian News Network).

On debt, the IMF said public debt is at its highest level since World War II and forecast that it will rise above 100% of gross domestic product by 2030; Georgieva named advanced economies, led by the United States, as the “worst” examples. On artificial intelligence, she called AI a “positive demand shock” that also pushes up inflation, with benefits that are very unevenly spread: many countries are being bypassed by the AI boom. She did not reveal whether the IMF’s new World Economic Outlook will change July’s forecast of 3.0% global growth this year; reports say the new forecasts will be released in Bangkok, with the biggest downgrades for the hardest-hit war zones such as Ukraine and the Gulf states, partly because July’s forecast assumed oil prices far below current levels.

Nobel Prize in Chemistry goes to Kagan and Soai for discovering non-linear effects and autocatalysis in “mirror-image molecules”

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced on October 7 that this year’s prize is shared equally between French-born Henri B. Kagan and Japan-born Kenso Soai, for their discovery of the “non-linear effect” and of autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis (Nobel Prize website, Al Jazeera, Khaleej Times, NPR radio, Times of Israel, The Standard). Heiner Linke, chair of the prize committee, said the two had answered a chemical riddle more than a century old: how homochirality arises spontaneously; he said their discoveries are decisive for chemists designing reactions to manufacture drugs. The prize money is SEK 12 million, shared equally by the two.

Kagan is at Paris-Sud University in France and Soai at the Tokyo University of Science. Accounts of the ages differ: Al Jazeera gives Kagan as 95 and Soai as 76, while some outlets wrote Soai as 95, which appears to be an error, so we are not using ages. This is a basic chemistry prize and is related to drug manufacturing, but the reports mention no specific drugs or commercial impact, and we do not extend beyond that.

Thailand flood deaths rise to 60 as Bangkok cleans up ahead of IMF meetings

AFP reports that, by the Thai government’s count, flood deaths since mid-September have risen to 60, and 27 of the 77 provinces are still flooded (Dhaka Tribune, Malay Mail, Cambodianess). Reuters, citing the Interior Ministry on October 5, put the figure at 31, and the number has risen since (Internazionale, reprinting Reuters). Bangkok and Nakhon Nayok each recorded 4 deaths; most of the floodwater in Bangkok has receded, and city authorities are clearing the streets ahead of the IMF and World Bank annual meetings. The highest death toll is in Prachin Buri province (19), followed by Samut Prakan province (9).

The Thai Meteorological Department forecasts heavy rain, strong winds and hail from October 9 to 13. We note that the cumulative dates and counting bases used by different agencies may differ; the death toll is still changing, and the latest official Thai announcement should be taken as the reference. With the IMF and World Bank meetings imminent, flood recovery in Bangkok is also a challenge for the organizers.

Wall Street slips on Wednesday: S&P 500 down 0.22% to 7,801.77, while oil and gold also fall

US stocks closed lower on Wednesday, October 7: the S&P 500 index fell 0.22% to 7,801.77. The Standard’s morning briefing said US Treasury yields rose (single source). West Texas Intermediate crude futures fell 1.30% to $88.28, and gold fell 1.11% to $4,140.7 (DailyDrop market data). The reports we saw do not explain why oil and gold fell on the same day.

For Hong Kong stocks on Thursday, rising US Treasury yields are usually unfavorable to rate-sensitive property and financial shares; with mainland markets also reopening, the market will have to digest both factors at once. We do not predict the index’s direction, only note that fund flows after the open deserve attention.
