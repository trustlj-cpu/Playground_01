Kai Tak Hospital opens first phase: about 350 patients on day one, lawmakers urge better transport links

The first phase is the specialist outpatient building and the oncology building; the full hospital will have 2,400 beds, with emergency and inpatient services expected from 2028.

Kai Tak Hospital opened in phases on October 5 (Monday). The first phase consists of the specialist outpatient building and the oncology building, which offer services in family medicine, specialist outpatient care, allied health, blood tests and radiotherapy (The Standard, China Daily Hong Kong edition). Officials described it as the largest public hospital in Hong Kong, with a floor area of about 500,000 square meters and 2,400 beds, taking over from Queen Elizabeth Hospital as the flagship of the Kowloon Central Cluster (China Daily Hong Kong edition, The Standard). The South China Morning Post reported that the family medicine center received about 350 patients on the first day and is expected to rise to 1,800 a day by the end of the year.

After the opening, lawmakers raised concerns about transport: patients welcomed the new service, but lawmakers urged more feeder services and better publicity of transport options; the Hospital Authority responded that 10 bus routes and 6 minibus routes already link the Kai Tak Development Area with major districts in Kowloon (The Standard, South China Morning Post). Emergency and inpatient services are expected only from 2028, which means that for now the public can use only outpatient and oncology-related services, and emergency patients must still go to existing emergency rooms. We could not check the details of the hospital’s phased timetable from the reports; the Hospital Authority’s announcements prevail.

Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces say they have advanced to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait; Houthis dismiss claim as baseless

Reuters, Al-Monitor, Algemeiner, ARY News and Kuwait Times reported on October 5 that Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces launched a rapid offensive on Monday, advancing along the Bab el-Mandeb coast to the port city of Mocha; officials said they had taken positions including the airstrip in the Dhubab district at the strait’s entrance, and a Defense Ministry statement went further, saying Mocha had fallen to government forces, but Reuters could not independently verify the scale of the gains. The Houthis said the government forces’ claim was baseless and claimed attacks on several Saudi facilities, including King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the Aramco refinery at Rabigh, Abha International Airport and the Khamis Mushait military base; these claims were likewise unconfirmed, and Saudi Arabia and Saudi Aramco did not immediately respond. The reports set the fighting against the backdrop of the US-Iran conflict, saying that if government forces retake the strait’s coast it would weaken the Houthis’ control over the Red Sea, an important oil shipping lane, while Iran’s blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has already unsettled energy markets; Ahmed Nagi of the International Crisis Group cautioned that it is still too early to call this a decisive turning point.

Hang Seng rebounds 68 points Monday to close at 24,040, turnover shrinks to HK$98.1 billion; Hang Seng Tech Index up 0.6%

The Hang Seng Index closed at 24,040 on October 5 (Monday), up 68 points, or 0.3%, back above 24,000 after reversing an early decline; the Hang Seng Tech Index rose 0.6% to 4,183. Market turnover shrank to HK$98.1 billion, which The Standard attributed to limited inflows of mainland money; mainland stock markets are closed for the National Day Golden Week holiday until October 7. Trading Economics recorded that day’s close as 24,020 and said semiconductor and tech stocks led the gains, while high US Treasury yields and oil price worries weighed on rate-sensitive property and financial stocks. The two sources differ by 20 points; this paper uses The Standard’s figure and notes the discrepancy.

The same day, Standard Chartered forecast that the Hang Seng could reach 26,600 in the near term, citing artificial intelligence and oil prices as the main drivers (The Standard, single source). Against Monday’s close, that implies a gain of about 10%, but it is a bank forecast, not a guarantee. The Hang Seng had plunged 2.6% on Friday and Monday only recovered part of the loss; whether that counts as the end of the fall depends on whether turnover can expand over the next few days.

Hong Kong team wins record 54 medals at Nagoya Asian Games; Culture, Sports and Tourism Secretary welcomes returning athletes

The 20th Asian Games were held in Nagoya, Japan, from September 19 to October 4. The Standard reported that the Hong Kong delegation won 54 medals in total, including 11 gold, 19 silver and 24 bronze, ranking ninth on the medal table, the best Asian Games result in Hong Kong’s history, with a record number of golds as well; the previous Games in Hangzhou brought 53 medals (8 gold, 16 silver, 29 bronze). The paper also reported that Rosanna Law made a three-day, two-night visit to Japan, watching fencing, football, handball and wushu sanda competitions and meeting the esports and skateboarding teams, and praised many athletes for setting personal bests, breaking local records or winning their first medals.

The Standard’s Oct. 6 briefing mentioned that Rosanna Law greeted returning Asian Games athletes at the airport. The Hong Kong dinghy sailing team earlier won two gold medals in the youth-class dinghy events (The Standard, Oct. 2). The medal total and ranking appear in only one outlet and we could not check official data, so they are listed as unverified for now; the report also gives no details beyond the size of the delegation.

US stocks close higher Monday, oil falls nearly 2%: S&P 500 up 0.66% to 7,773.95, West Texas crude at $89.43

US stocks closed higher on October 5 (Monday): the S&P 500 rose 0.66% to 7,773.95; The Standard’s briefing said the Nasdaq Composite closed at a record high, led by Nvidia and Microsoft (single source). West Texas crude futures fell 1.84% to $89.43; gold fell 0.13% to $4,156.8 (DailyDrop market data). The reports we saw do not explain the oil price decline, and we cannot confirm that it is linked to the fighting in Yemen.

Saudi Aramco’s chief executive reportedly said it may take up to two years to replenish global crude and fuel inventories (The Standard briefing, single source; we could not check his exact words). For Hong Kong stocks, record highs for US tech stocks may lift tech sentiment today, but the actual move depends on money flows after the open.
