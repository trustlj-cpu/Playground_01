Hong Kong · Border crossingPress ✓ 7 outlets · no official full-day crowd figure yet

New Huanggang Port, day one: more than 5,000 trips in and out in the first two hours, Chris Tang says 60% more than the old port; taxi queue tickets and tour-coach transfers draw trade comment

Officials say operations were smooth and tests showed faster clearance than at the old port; no full-day crowd figure has been published, and the weekend and the Chung Yeung holiday will be the real test.

The rebuilt New Huanggang Port (the new joint inspection building) opened at 6:30 a.m. on October 12 (Monday) (RTHK), with 24-hour clearance (TVB, The Standard); a joint announcement by the two governments said the old passenger clearance facilities were suspended at the same time, and the date was chosen after the National Day long holiday so that staff could adjust on a quieter day (The Standard). Chief Secretary for Administration Chan Kwok-ki and Secretary for Security Chris Tang visited and said operations were smooth; HK01 and Jianliao reported that the Hong Kong side opened all customs and immigration counters on the first day. Tang said crowds in the two hours after opening were 60% higher than at the old port over the same period; as of 8:30 a.m., about 5,170 trips in and out had been recorded (Sing Tao Headline, HKEJ, Yahoo News). Bernadette Linn, Secretary for Transport and Logistics, said that as of 8 a.m. about 1,200 people had gone north and nearly 2,000 south (RTHK). As for the full-day total, the Shenzhen Port Office expects about 40,000 to 50,000 trips on the first day (RTHK); this is only a forecast, and this paper has seen no actual full-day figure. Officials from both sides had earlier said daily clearance capacity rises from about 40,000 trips at the old port to 200,000, but another report gave 50,000 for the old port and 100,000 for initial capacity, so accounts differ (The Standard).

On clearance speed, an RTHK reporter using the self-service channel for the first time took about 5 minutes from Hong Kong to arriving in Shenzhen; another TV station test, relayed by Yahoo News, said clearing the new port was 14 minutes faster than the old one. Cross-boundary drivers said vehicle inspection took about two minutes; the cross-boundary coach trade said traffic from the urban area to the port was smooth that day, and people arriving from Shenzhen also said clearance was convenient and comfortable; a cross-boundary tour-coach company will launch port package tickets to attract passengers (RTHK). Shenzhen tour groups rushed in for one-day trips to Hong Kong, and Hong Kong people praised the convenient MTR link (Yahoo News). The South China Morning Post reported that some travellers praised clearance as “lightning fast”, while others found the building too large, and queues and confusion were also seen. Reactions in the trade were mixed: the taxi trade said that picking up passengers at the new port requires taking a queue ticket first, which may affect drivers’ willingness, and hoped for a review; tour coaches must transfer to other vehicles to reach the port, and Tsui Ting-bong said the trade wants people and vehicles to go direct (RTHK). Linn said she has asked for buses with luggage racks to be deployed (Sing Tao Headline, i-Cable). Chan Kwok-ki said he is confident of coping with the Chung Yeung Festival holiday peak, and described the weekend as the real challenge (Wen Wei Po, i-Cable); he added that the government is studying extending the “one location, two checks” arrangement to other crossings (Wen Wei Po, Ta Kung Wen Wei, same group).

World · UkrainePress ✓ 6 outlets · ceasefire not confirmed by Russia

Ukraine says Russian forces still hit six energy facilities after Trump announced “energy ceasefire”; Kremlin rejects 60-day ceasefire

Ukrainian President Zelensky said on Monday that Russian forces have attacked six Ukrainian energy facilities since Trump announced the “energy ceasefire”; he warned that if Russia violates or rejects the ceasefire, Ukraine will strike back (BBC, Ukrinform, Bloomberg Television). The Kremlin rejected Zelensky’s proposal for a 60-day ceasefire, saying it wants a comprehensive peace agreement (Reuters); the EU on the same day adopted a sanctions list against Russia with nearly 1,600 entries (Reuters). Headlines from Reuters and the AP show that Russian forces attacked a power plant in Kharkiv shortly after the announcement. Canadian Prime Minister Carney said the US–Russia diesel deal is bad for peace in Ukraine (Reuters); Republican Senator Tillis said the deal may be illegal, and senators from both parties asked Trump to cancel it (USA Today, Politico). Trump defended the diesel supply deal with Russia, saying “I made the right choice”, and both Republican and Democratic lawmakers have criticised it (CNBC, Reuters); German Chancellor Merz said the deal is “not under consideration” for Europe (Deutsche Welle).

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · accounts of the death toll differ

Riyadh airport attacked four times in one week, airlines cancel flights; Iran says it will reply to the US response to its Strait of Hormuz proposal

King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the Saudi capital, has been attacked four times by the Houthis over the past week (CBS). CBS said the weekend attack killed 13 people and injured hundreds; an earlier report put the toll at 12, and this paper counts it as “12 to 13”. Reuters reported that several airlines have cancelled flights to Saudi Arabia and there are almost no passenger jets over Riyadh; Singapore’s CNA reported that the airport’s suspension has been extended. The AP reported that Saudi Arabia is investigating why its air defences failed to intercept the deadly attack. Turkey, Pakistan, Egypt and Saudi Arabia condemned the attack (Reuters), and China also condemned it and warned of escalation (CNA); Iran said it is willing to mediate between its ally the Houthis and Saudi Arabia (The Straits Times, single source).

On the Strait of Hormuz, Iran said it would reply once it had reviewed the US response to its proposal on the strait (Reuters, The Straits Times); an Iranian military source said the US response was “not enough” (FinancialJuice, single source). Bloomberg reported that supertankers are avoiding the Gulf’s transshipment hubs. Ministers of OPEC and its allies (OPEC+) issued a statement outside the Riyadh meeting saying that disrupted energy flows have pushed shipping and insurance costs to unprecedented levels (FinancialJuice, single source); Reuters reported that several oil-industry executives have been removed from the meeting’s agenda. For Hong Kong, the most direct effect is on oil prices and freight; this paper does not use Monday’s intraday US oil prices, which are still moving.

World · Chinese diplomacyPress ✓ 6 outlets · details of the measures not fully confirmed

China announces countermeasures against ministerial-level officials of Paraguay and Belize, citing the two countries’ official relations with Taiwan

China’s Foreign Ministry announced on Monday countermeasures against ministerial-level officials of Paraguay and Belize, citing the two countries’ official relations with Taiwan (Reuters, CNA, The Straits Times, South China Morning Post). FinancialJuice, citing the ministry, said the measure against Belize’s foreign minister took effect on October 12 (single source); this paper has seen no full confirmation of which Paraguayan official is concerned or of the specific content of the measures. Paraguay and Belize are both countries that have diplomatic relations with Taiwan. The Chinese government has consistently opposed any country developing official relations with Taiwan. For Hong Kong readers this is a matter of Chinese diplomacy and cross-strait relations, with limited effect on daily life, but it affects how investors view geopolitical risk in the region. The Japan Times and Al Jazeera also reported it; for the latest responses from each side, official statements prevail.

Hong Kong stocks rise 0.25% on Monday to 24,271, banks lead and Xiaomi falls nearly 5%; latest completed US trading day was Friday, all three indices up

The Hang Seng Index closed up about 60 points (0.25%) on Monday at 24,271, with turnover of about HK$162.6 billion; the Hang Seng Tech Index fell 0.67% to 4,169 (The Standard, ad-hoc-news). Banks led the gain, with China Merchants Bank, Bank of China and HSBC Holdings up about 3%, 2.04% and 2% respectively; Xiaomi fell nearly 5%, and the two reports give slightly different figures for the fall. US stocks were still trading on Monday and no closing figures were available when this paper went to press; the latest completed trading day is Friday (October 9): the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to close at 7,811.54; the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.64% to close at 27,366.17; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.83% to close at 51,654.95. WTI crude futures rose 0.39% on Friday to close at $91.85; gold rose 1.43% to close at $4,216.3 (DailyDrop market data).

Monday’s US close will come only after this paper is published, and this paper makes no estimate. Oil changed hands several times on Monday: Reuters reported oil retreating in early Asian trading, while Bloomberg said it later rose as supplies from the Middle East and Russia were threatened. The chief executive of Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing said Hong Kong will expand index, derivatives and renminbi-denominated products (South China Morning Post, single source).
