Diplomacy · Korea–US✓ 4 outlets · specific reason for the visa refusal unconfirmed

US Secretary of State Rubio: “Korean lawmaker’s visa issue is tied to actions decades ago”… Jin Sung-joon gets visa, leaves for Hawaii

The US said it would resolve the matter through consultations between the two countries, and Chair Jin, who received the visa three days after news of the refusal, set off on the trip to the US.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on the 31st (local time), at a press conference after the Group of 20 (G20) foreign ministers’ meeting in Atlanta, Georgia, that the snag over visa issuance for Korean National Assembly members was “an issue related to actions decades ago” and that it would be resolved through consultations between the two countries. At the same event, Rubio said President Trump is open to talks with State Affairs Commission Chair Kim Jong Un if North Korea is ready to discuss the issues that allies such as the US and South Korea are concerned about, but that no schedule is currently set. It began on the 28th. Jin Sung-joon, chair of the National Assembly National Defense Committee, disclosed that issuance of an official visa, used for government business trips, had been refused, and Democratic Party lawmaker Jung Chung-rae had also run into a visa problem earlier. Chair Jin explained that a guilty verdict for violating the National Security Act was handed down in 1991, but that it was connected to the pro-democracy movement and was recognized in 2003 as a pro-democracy movement activity.

The visa came three days after the refusal became known, one day before departure. On the 1st, Chair Jin led the Defense Committee delegation to the US and left for Hawaii, and the delegation is to visit the US Pacific Command and other sites until the 5th. Chair Jin said it was deeply regrettable that the visa remained uncertain until just before departure, asked whether this episode accords with the principles of the alliance, and said the negotiations between the two countries would be closely watched. The ruling party has said the US must set it right, while the opposition has countered that it is a sign that a problem has arisen in Korea-US relations. However, it has not been officially disclosed what exactly the “actions decades ago” that Secretary Rubio mentioned are, or whether the same reason applies to other lawmakers.

Chief Justice Cho Hee-de parliamentary investigation on the verge… Democratic Party takes on ‘hard tasks’ after parliamentary audit, along with special counsel law and Shincheonji case

With the parliamentary audit wrapping up, the Democratic Party faces three tasks at once: a parliamentary investigation of the Chief Justice, a special counsel law on fabricated prosecution, and the Shincheonji case. The Democratic Party is poised for a parliamentary investigation aimed at Chief Justice Cho Hee-de’s so-called ‘allegations of complicity in martial law,’ and says the investigation is a power the Constitution gave the National Assembly and does not infringe judicial independence. The People Power Party calls the investigation ‘testing the waters’ with impeachment in mind, says it is unconstitutional and illegal, and demands that it stop immediately. Voices within the party also question the investigation’s effectiveness. The special counsel law on fabricated prosecution is reported to be pushed right after the audit, but no date has been set for plenary handling. The Shincheonji case remains a source of friction within the party. How to handle these three matters is seen as the first test of the Kim Min-seok leadership.

President Lee answers criticism of multicultural remarks with “double standard”… “To a pig’s eyes, only pigs are visible”: harsh social media posts raise concern even in ruling party

President Lee Jae-myung hit back on social media at progressive-camp criticism of the President’s own remarks on multicultural families, calling it a “double standard” and “partisan logic.” When the opposition criticized frequent overseas trips, the President retorted, “To a pig’s eyes, only pigs are visible.” People Power Party lawmaker Jeong Jeom-sik called this “beyond an outburst of emotion, a warped perception of state affairs,” and said of the President’s ‘IV drip and nosebleed’ remarks, “Isn’t that because of not sleeping and being on social media?” Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon also criticized the President for clinging to social media when things do not go as intended.

The President’s social media posts have increased greatly since September. Cheong Wa Dae had earlier explained that, with approval ratings down, it intended to communicate actively. But worries that the President’s posts could fuel controversy or lead to mistakes are also coming from inside the ruling party.

Society · Culture✓ 4 outlets · burial site after the funeral Mass unconfirmed

‘The Nation’s Father’ Choi Bul-am’s private funeral procession on the 2nd… funeral Mass at Yeouido Catholic Church, farewell stop at Green Umbrella, a 45-year tie

The funeral procession for the actor Choi Bul-am (real name Choi Young-han), who died on the 29th at age 87, will be held at 7:40 a.m. on the 2nd at the funeral hall of Asan Medical Center in Songpa District, Seoul. At the wishes of the bereaved family, the procession will be held entirely in private. In keeping with the deceased’s own wishes, the late actor will stop at the headquarters of the Children’s Foundation Green Umbrella, with which the deceased had a 45-year tie, to say farewell. At 9 a.m., a funeral Mass celebrated by Cardinal Yeom Soo-jung will be held at Yeouido Catholic Church in Seoul, and pop-opera tenor Lim Hyung-joo will sing ‘Ave Maria.’

Throughout the weekend, mourners from all walks of life and fellow actors paid their respects at the funeral altar. Kim Hye-soo kept vigil at the altar for a third day, receiving mourners, and Oh Ji-myung, Baek Il-seop, Lee Je-hoon and Chae Si-ra paid tribute. Lee Je-hoon said that acting together was the glory of a lifetime. Over a 67-year acting life, the late actor won the nation’s love with ‘Village Diary’ and ‘Crime Squad,’ and also served as a member of the National Assembly.

Finance · Security✓ 3 outlets · deadline for submitting improvement plans unconfirmed

Financial authorities urge financial sector to draw up security improvement plans after serial hacks… push for AI-based joint security monitoring by industry segment, review meeting next week

Financial authorities have urged the financial sector to draw up security improvement plans in response to the sweeping wave of hacking that hit it throughout October. The Financial Supervisory Service convened working-level meetings at the association of each industry segment on the 30th of last month, and authorities are discussing measures such as building an artificial intelligence (AI)-based joint security monitoring system centered on industry associations and central federations. A review meeting will be held next week. An industry segment is a group of financial firms doing similar work, such as banks, insurers and securities firms.

In October, hacks followed one after another at financial firms, companies and public institutions. The reservation management system for apartment community facilities at the construction company GS E&C was hacked, raising the problem of residents’ information security. Whether the North Korea-linked hacking group Lazarus abused legitimate websites, such as those of domestic academic societies and construction companies, as communication bases is also under investigation. In which industry segment joint monitoring will start first, and when the deadline for submitting improvement plans is, has not been decided.
