President Lee’s policy address: “Not ‘growth for the few’ but ‘growth for all’”… explains 820.9 trillion won budget bill; People Power Party protests with placards

In his fourth policy address since taking office, President Lee explained the direction of the largest budget bill ever. The Democratic Party responded with 30 rounds of applause, while the People Power Party sat silent with protest placards attached.

President Lee Jae-myung delivered a policy address on the 2027 budget bill in the National Assembly chamber on the 2nd. The government’s bill has total spending of 820.9 trillion won, up 12.8% from this year’s main budget of 727.9 trillion won. Both the size of the increase and the rate of increase are the largest ever. President Lee said, “We must make a great transition from ‘growth for the few,’ in which the opportunities and fruits of growth are concentrated in particular regions, classes and a few companies, to ‘growth for all,’ which every region and every citizen enjoys evenly.” He pointed out that the Korean economy shows a clear V-shaped recovery but the shadow of inequality and polarization remains, and named three budget principles: bold investment, priority for fiscal spending, and preference for the regions. He also said the government would invest boldly in three mega projects, including semiconductors, artificial intelligence (AI) data centers and physical AI, and would raise the defense budget by 8.2% to secure the forces needed for the transfer of wartime operational control, along with nuclear-powered submarines.

Reactions in the chamber were split. Democratic Party of Korea lawmakers applauded 30 times during the address, while People Power Party lawmakers sat silent with protest placards attached. People Power Party chief spokesperson Park Seong-hoon criticized it in a statement as “a ‘declaration of war on the people’ that would pass an astronomical debt bomb on to the next generation.” The Democratic Party called it “a speech that shared a grand dream” and said it would keep the deadline for passing the budget. The point of contention is the Future Response Fund, a new 162 trillion won fund. The government plans to use extra tax revenue to respond to economic and tax-revenue swings. The bill now goes to review by the National Assembly’s Special Committee on Budget and Accounts, and the constitutional deadline for passing it is December 2. How much the Assembly will cut or add, and whether the Future Response Fund will be created as the government proposed, have not been decided.

Election commission special counsel searches 18 departments including National Election Commission chair’s office… suspicion of improper payment of ‘fair-election promotion allowance’

The special counsel team led by Lee Tae-han on the election commission case began at 9 a.m. on the 2nd a search and seizure of 18 departments of the National Election Commission in Gwacheon, Gyeonggi Province, including the chair’s office and the secretary-general’s office, and of the home of a former secretary-general. A total of 65 people were deployed, including 4 seconded prosecutors and 25 digital forensics investigators. The warrants list charges of loss of public funds under the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes, obstruction of rights through abuse of authority, and drawing up false official documents. The target is the fair-election promotion allowance. The suspicion is that the National Election Commission improperly paid this allowance to non-standing commissioners and others, several million won every month. The special counsel moved to compulsory investigation to get to the bottom of this suspicion. Whether the charges have been proven, how much was improperly paid in total, and who is responsible have not been confirmed.

Industry · Nuclear power✓ 4 outlets · the two sides differ on how the talks ended

Daewoo E&C asks for “mediation in Czech nuclear renegotiation”; Industry Ministry says “no authority to mediate”… KHNP moves to pick new builder

Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power (KHNP) said on the 29th of last month that it had ended talks on the construction contract for the new Dukovany nuclear plant in the Czech Republic and would pick a new builder. It had negotiated for 15 months with the Daewoo E&C–Doosan Enerbility consortium over construction costs and contract terms but could not reach agreement. In response, Daewoo E&C on the 2nd asked the Ministry of Industry and Trade to mediate a renegotiation. In its statement it said, “We ask the ministry to directly review the past negotiation process between KHNP, the main contractor, and Daewoo E&C, examine the construction consortium’s final offer, and step in to mediate the renegotiation.” Daewoo E&C’s position is that ending the talks was unilateral.

The ministry’s position is that it has no legal authority to mediate negotiations between companies. Instead, it has activated an emergency management system led by the minister and vice minister in person and is reviewing the project as a whole. KHNP’s position is that it will select a new builder by July next year and keep to the existing construction schedule. Because the project is worth 27 trillion won, the key question is how the schedule and cost would change if the builder changes. How KHNP will pick the new builder has not yet been decided.

KOSPI shakes off weak open to close up 2.24%… 5,859.08, regains 5,800 line on institutional buying

The KOSPI shook off a lower open on the 2nd to close up 2.24% at 5,859.08. Just after the open, it was pushed down to 5,637.77, off 1.62%, in the wake of last Friday’s sharp fall in US stocks, raising fears of a ‘Black Monday’. But steady net buying by institutions turned it higher during the session, and it regained the 5,800 line. The KOSDAQ also rose 1.38% to finish at 734.36.

On the previous trading day, Oct. 30, the KOSPI had plunged 3.48% to close at 5,730.74. That same day in the US, the Nasdaq fell 2.86%, the S&P 500 fell 1.82% and Intel’s shares plunged 29%. Today’s rebound is about two-thirds of Friday’s drop. This week brings the US midterm elections (on the 3rd, local time), jobs data and corporate earnings releases.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard: “2 tankers exploded on naval mines in Strait of Hormuz”… claims a supertanker caught fire; no independent confirmation

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed on the 1st (local time) that two tankers, including one supertanker, struck naval mines and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz, and that one caught fire. The ships’ names and nationalities, crew casualties and the cause of the explosions were not disclosed, and no independent confirmation has come from outside Iran. All that has been confirmed so far is that the Revolutionary Guard announced this.

The Strait of Hormuz is a main route for crude oil that Korea imports. Since the strait was blocked, Korean tankers carrying crude via the Red Sea have reached their 20th voyage. If confirmed, it could feed through to insurance premiums, freight rates and oil prices, but no price change has been confirmed yet. The key question is whether an actual explosion and the scale of damage can be independently confirmed.
