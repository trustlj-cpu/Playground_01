Ukraine war · PoliticsPress ✓ 9 outlets · implementation open, Moscow silent

Trump announces an “energy ceasefire”, Kyiv waits for details. Shortly afterwards Russia hits a power plant in Kharkiv

The US president writes on Truth Social that both sides have agreed. Zelensky says it is news to him; there is no confirmation from Moscow as of Sunday evening. At the same time, according to the Financial Times, Washington is threatening to halt arms deliveries.

US President Donald Trump declared on Sunday afternoon on his platform Truth Social that an energy ceasefire was in force “effective immediately” in the war between Russia and Ukraine. Both sides had agreed, he said, and it should not be broken. Reuters, BBC, Bloomberg, 24 heures and RTS report this in agreement. What is meant is a mutual renunciation of attacks on power plants, refineries and other energy facilities. Trump said nothing about duration or monitoring. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said, according to Axios, that the announcement was news to him. Later he wrote on Telegram that it was good news for everyone, that what mattered was that it become reality, and that Kyiv was waiting for details from Washington (Reuters). There is no confirmation from Moscow as of Sunday evening; according to Watson, even Russians knew nothing about it. Already in the morning Zelensky had offered to stop Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries if Russia ends its attacks on Ukrainian energy supply (Reuters, 24 heures, RSI). He told the US broadcaster ABC that he would organize elections if Russia agreed to a ceasefire of 60 days. On Saturday Trump had demanded that Ukraine needs a new president. Germany then stood behind Zelensky, according to Reuters, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen supported his call for a ceasefire, according to FinancialJuice.

A little later Zelensky reported that Russia had hit a power plant in Kharkiv and was continuing its attacks (Reuters, The Hill, Blick). The extent of the damage is not confirmed. According to Reuters, six people died in Russian attacks on two Ukrainian cities. At the same time pressure on Kyiv is growing: the Financial Times reports that Washington is threatening to block arms deliveries if Ukraine keeps attacking Russian refineries. In the US Congress there is resistance to the diesel agreement with Moscow that Trump announced on Friday and that initially provides for 300,000 tonnes of Russian diesel. Lawmakers of both parties say, according to the Financial Times, that it finances Putin’s war machine and violates US law; an influential Republican senator considers it possibly unlawful (Reuters). Russia, too, reports attacks: the internet group Yandex shut down a data center in the Vladimir region after a drone attack (Reuters), and at Moscow airports operations were suspended because of drone alerts, according to Watson. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney support an energy ceasefire, according to FinancialJuice, and the EU Commission wants to provide more money for Ukraine’s drones and missiles. For Switzerland the energy price matters most: the oil grade WTI closed on Friday at $91.85. Switzerland generally adopts sanctions from the EU; whether anything changes there is open. The NATO commander in Europe spoke, according to Bloomberg, of “windows of opportunity” to end the war. Today we are watching whether the Kremlin confirms the ceasefire, who monitors it and whether the oil price reacts on Monday. Without confirmation from Moscow it remains, for now, a claim by Trump.

Middle East · SecurityPress ✓ 10 outlets · damage at Aramco unconfirmed

Houthis hit Riyadh airport a second time. Oil companies cancel a congress, Washington considers intervening

King Khalid airport in Riyadh has been hit for the second time within two days. In the missile attack on Saturday, 12 people died and over 300 were injured, according to the Saudi authorities (AP, NPR, CBS). On Sunday, according to the Saudi-led military coalition, an unknown projectile struck again; according to Bloomberg and RTS a fire broke out, and the civil aviation authority reports one dead and eleven injured for Sunday (FinancialJuice). The Houthis, a militia in Yemen backed by Iran, claim the attacks (SRF, RSI, Watson). They also say they hit the airport in Dammam and facilities of the oil company Aramco; Saudi Arabia did not confirm this, and the coalition reports one intercepted drone in the Eastern Province. SRF puts it in context: the Houthis are aiming at the kingdom’s business model as a hub for travelers and conferences. According to the Financial Times, BP and other oil companies canceled their participation in an energy congress in Riyadh. According to the Guardian, the coalition announces a “decisive” response, and the US is considering intervening. The British embassy advises against non-essential travel to all Saudi airports except Medina. Russia condemned the attack, according to Interfax, and Pakistan is looking after injured compatriots (FinancialJuice). The foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia and Iran spoke about the situation, according to Saudi state media. On Monday what counts is whether the airport reopens, how the oil price reacts (according to Bloomberg it rose in Sunday evening trading) and whether Washington announces strikes.

Wall Street ends the week higher. At the start of the week the oil price rises after the attacks on Riyadh

US stock markets ended the week on Friday in positive territory. The S&P 500 gained 0.59 percent to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64 percent to 27,366.17 points and the Dow Jones 0.83 percent to 51,654.95 points. Gold gained 1.43 percent to $4,216.3 an ounce, the oil grade WTI 0.39 percent to $91.85 a barrel. In Sunday evening trading the oil price rose after the attacks on Saudi Arabia, and the dollar edged up (Bloomberg). The Saudi stock exchange fell after the attack on Riyadh airport (Reuters).

We could not confirm the closing level of the Swiss benchmark index SMI and the dollar-franc rate from Friday with a second outlet; the boxes above therefore show a dash. SIX Swiss Exchange reopens today. The focus is on the oil price and the question whether the energy ceasefire holds. Bloomberg writes that bond traders were waiting for the US inflation data. The annual meeting of the IMF and World Bank in Bangkok will be shaped by the war in the Middle East and by high debt, according to Reuters. For Swiss savers, price gains feed through with a delay via the pension funds. How the oil price will develop cannot be said in advance; that is not a forecast.

Switzerland · SecurityPress ✓ 7 outlets · participant numbers differ

Bern: unauthorized demo against imperialism and capitalism passes without major incidents

Exactly one year after the Gaza rally that escalated in Bern, another unauthorized demonstration moved through the city center on Sunday afternoon. According to Blick and 24 heures, anonymous far-left circles had called for it under the motto “Smash Imperialism”. The police were on site in large numbers and closed off access to the center (RSI); the Berner Zeitung speaks of a cat-and-mouse game with the police. Accounts of the number of participants differ: SRF and RSI give around a thousand, RTS and swissinfo several hundred. According to Blick and swissinfo, the rally ended peacefully. RSI reports isolated damage to property; one person with several knives was stopped.

The NZZ describes the messages as radical, for example the cry “Tout le monde déteste la police”. Blick had written beforehand of bad memories among police and businesses. This time it stayed calm. For people in Bern this means: the city center is accessible as normal again today. It remains open whether complaints will be filed and how the city and police draw up their balance. We are watching whether the authorities will handle unauthorized rallies differently in future.

Sport · Football and Formula 1Press ✓ 6 outlets

Basel lose 0:3 in Vaduz on coach Jokanovic’s debut. Verstappen wins in Singapore, Derron finishes third in Hawaii

FC Basel lost the debut of its new coach Slavisa Jokanovic in round ten of the Super League 0:3 at FC Vaduz (Blick, SRF, Watson). St. Gallen beat bottom-placed Lausanne-Sport 1:0 after a penalty by Görtler in the 29th minute (RTS, SRF, Blick), and the Young Boys won 2:0 against the Grasshoppers (Blick). In Hawaii, Zurich’s Julie Derron finished third on her debut at the Ironman World Championship (NZZ, Le Temps).

In Formula 1, Max Verstappen won the Singapore Grand Prix, his second win in a row, ahead of Charles Leclerc and Kimi Antonelli (24 heures, SRF, Blick). Antonelli remains the championship leader and, according to Blick and SRF, can become world champion in Austin as early as in two weeks. In the Premier League, Manchester City won 3:0 at Liverpool; Dan Ndoye, from canton Vaud, scored for Nottingham Forest, but the match was lost (SRF, RTS). We are not writing this as a forecast: one debut says little about a season.
