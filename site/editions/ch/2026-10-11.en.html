Parmelin succession: SVP wants at least two candidates. Friedli stays in the Council of States

Group leader Thomas Aeschi announces a two-person ticket, with a decision only on November 20. Esther Friedli, the most frequently named favorite, is out; Christoph Blocher calls Pascal Schmid the strongest man.

The SVP wants to contest the succession of Federal Councillor Guy Parmelin with at least two people. Group leader Thomas Aeschi said on Saturday on Radio SRF’s “Samstagsrundschau” that the party is going for a two-person ticket but does not rule out more than two names. SRF, bluewin, Watson, RSI and Le Temps report this in agreement. According to SRF, the size and composition will be decided by the SVP parliamentary group at its meeting on November 20; the election takes place on December 9 by the United Federal Assembly, that is, by the National Council and the Council of States together. According to SRF, Aeschi named as criteria appropriately represented language regions, command of the issues, leadership qualities and assertiveness. Whether a woman should be on the ticket, he left open, according to bluewin. Aeschi belongs to the party’s search committee and would have to recuse himself if he ran himself (20 Minuten); whether he will run, he wants to say “in due course”. On Friday evening the St. Gallen Councillor of States Esther Friedli declared that she is not available. According to the reports, she does not want to give up her seat in the Council of States after three and a half years. SRF, Tages-Anzeiger, NZZ, Blick, 20 Minuten and Le Temps report this in agreement; the NZZ writes that she would probably have won the election. The Tages-Anzeiger sees in the refusal an opening for the men in the party.

So the names remain open. Parmelin had announced his resignation for the end of 2026 and had himself named Friedli, the Geneva National Councillor Céline Amaudruz and the Zurich cantonal minister Natalie Rickli as possible successors. According to 24 heures, the trio of women has become a duo; Blick writes that Rickli is lying low for now. Former SVP leader Christoph Blocher, according to 20 Minuten, puts the party’s asylum chief, Pascal Schmid, forward as the strongest candidate. That is one person’s opinion, not a decision. According to the reports there are no official candidacies yet; we did not find the deadline of October 23 mentioned in earlier reports confirmed this time. Under the magic formula the seat belongs to the SVP, but the other parties do not have to follow its proposal. Parmelin heads the Economic Affairs Department; when a seat falls vacant, the seven-member national government reassigns the departments, which is why that question matters too. The NZZ reports that even people on the left doubt whether an SP economics minister would only benefit the trade unions; this refers to Federal Councillor Beat Jans. 20 Minuten reports that Defense Minister Martin Pfister apparently is thinking of switching departments. Both are single reports. In the coming weeks we are watching whether anyone officially declares and whether the party proposes a name from French-speaking Switzerland.

Ukraine war · PoliticsPress ✓ 8 outlets · death toll rising

Trump demands a “new president” for Ukraine. Russian attacks on Zaporizhzhia kill at least 20

A day after the diesel agreement with Vladimir Putin, US President Donald Trump sharply attacked Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. It was “time for Ukraine to get a new president,” he said, according to the Washington Post, Japan Times and Straits Times; RTS and 24 heures report the same. Zelensky had earlier criticized the agreement as “unworthy” and “weak”, according to 20 Minutes. The Ukrainian government stood behind him, according to Zeit and Handelsblatt; British Prime Minister Burnham reaffirmed his “full solidarity”, according to the Straits Times. Kyiv will not stop its attacks on Russian oil facilities, reports CBS. In the US, according to the New York Times, some Republicans reject the import of Russian diesel. Germany is sticking to the Russia sanctions, according to Reuters. The US government allows the sale of Russian diesel under a temporary license (Kyiv Independent, The Hindu, yesterday); whether it will adjust it is open. On the ground the attacks continued: a Russian strike on Zaporizhzhia killed at least 20 people according to CNA, the Straits Times gives 21. Earlier reports spoke of 7 to 12 dead, including three children; the number has changed several times and is not final. For Switzerland it is open whether it will react to the easing in Washington: it generally adopts sanctions from the EU. Today we are watching how Brussels and Berlin respond to Trump’s demand and whether the Federal Council comments.

Wall Street ends the week higher, SMI gains 1.1 percent. Markets are closed today

US stock markets ended the week on Friday in positive territory. The S&P 500 gained 0.59 percent to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64 percent to 27,366.17 points and the Dow Jones 0.83 percent to 51,654.95 points. The ten-year yield, that is, the yield on ten-year US government bonds, rose by 1.3 basis points to 5.244 percent. Gold gained 1.43 percent to $4,216.3 an ounce, the oil grade WTI 0.39 percent to $91.85 a barrel. Bloomberg writes that strong corporate earnings supported the rally on Wall Street; CNBC describes the week as a series of ups and downs with new highs and sharp setbacks in the volatile AI trade.

The Swiss benchmark index SMI closed 1.10 percent higher on Friday at 13,787.4 points. In the evening the dollar cost 0.8300 francs, 0.11 percent less than the day before. The SIX Swiss Exchange and the US exchanges are closed at the weekend; the next trading day is Monday, October 12. What happened over the weekend will then occupy the markets: the attack on Riyadh airport, the diesel agreement between Washington and Moscow and the dispute between Trump and Zelensky. For Swiss savers this matters through the pension funds: price gains feed through to retirement savings with a delay, and mortgage rates follow US yields only to a limited extent. How the oil price will move cannot be said in advance; that is not a forecast.

Switzerland · TrafficPress ✓ 3 outlets · No decision, only review

Astra reviews over 120 traffic lights at motorway on-ramps and accepts tailbacks

The Federal Roads Office (Astra) is examining installing traffic lights at more than 120 motorway on-ramps that let entering cars onto the main lane one at a time. The Tages-Anzeiger, 24 heures and the Tribune de Genève report this. The idea is called ramp metering: if too many vehicles push onto the motorway at once, traffic there collapses; whoever slows the on-ramp keeps the main lane flowing longer. The price is that tailbacks form on the municipal streets before the on-ramp. According to the reports, the federal government deliberately accepts them.

Affected cantons such as Basel-Stadt view the plans critically, according to the Tages-Anzeiger, and have so far not been included in the planning. 24 heures asks whether the solution to traffic jams ends up making everything worse. We found no binding timetable; it is a review, not a decision. For commuters nothing changes today. Those who join the motorway daily should follow the debate in their own canton, because cantons and municipalities bear the consequences on their streets. We are watching whether cantons raise objections and whether Astra publishes the locations.

Sport · Ice hockeyPress ✓ 4 outlets · record according to SRF and Blick

ZSC Lions win their tenth game too and set a National League start record

The ZSC Lions beat Fribourg-Gottéron 5:4 after overtime on Saturday and thus won the tenth game of the season. SRF, Blick, Tages-Anzeiger and Watson report that this breaks the National League’s start record; Blick calls the Zurich side the sole record holder. According to Blick, the winning goal came through an own goal. HC Davos lost again to Genève-Servette, according to SRF (2:3, RTS), and Ajoie turned a 1:4 deficit against Langnau into a 5:4 win after overtime (RTS, Le Matin).

It was also a weekend of surprises in football. In round ten of the Super League, Lugano won 4:0 against Lucerne, its ninth win in ten games (SRF, Blick, 20 Minuten). Servette beat Sion 3:0 in the Rhone derby, and FC Zurich turned a match against Thun around in pouring rain. Today the Formula 1 race continues in Singapore, where Max Verstappen starts from pole position. We are not writing this as a forecast: records are snapshots and usually end with the first defeat.
