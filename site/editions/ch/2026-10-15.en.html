World economy · ChinaPress ✓ 10 outlets · figures per customs authority

China’s exports grow 36 percent in September, trade surplus heads toward one trillion dollars

The customs authority reports the biggest jump since the pandemic, driven by the boom in artificial intelligence. Handelsblatt expects more pressure on EU negotiators ahead of the summit in Brussels.

China’s exports rose 36.3 percent in September from a year earlier, measured in dollars (AP, Al Jazeera, Clarín, FinancialJuice). Analysts had expected 25.5 percent on average (FinancialJuice). According to Al Jazeera it is the biggest jump since the Covid pandemic, and the New York Times speaks of the fastest growth in almost any month since the end of the financial crisis. In yuan terms exports gained 29.6 percent (FinancialJuice, Infobae, La Vanguardia); the difference between the two figures is explained by the yuan-dollar exchange rate. The customs authority published the data on Wednesday morning local time. According to Clarín imports grew 31 percent; Reuters writes that they accelerated, and the Financial Times attributes this partly to high oil prices. The Financial Times, Zeit Online and La Vanguardia name the AI boom as the engine, above all chips and electric cars. The South China Morning Post puts chip exports at 45 billion dollars, a rise of 136 percent; no second outlet supports this figure. For the first nine months the customs authority reports an export gain of 16.4 percent in yuan (FinancialJuice). According to FinancialJuice its deputy minister said the outlook for the fourth quarter remains unchanged, but uncertainties remain for the full year, including from geopolitical conflicts.

The trade surplus is thus heading toward a record (New York Times). Bloomberg writes that it is approaching 940 billion dollars; Handelsblatt and Boursorama see the one-trillion mark within reach. One reason is weak domestic demand, which makes China bet on exports (Clarín, Handelsblatt). In line with this, China’s inflation in September was 0.9 percent, against an expected 1.0 percent (n-tv, only this outlet). In a commentary Handelsblatt notes that Beijing is thereby making itself dependent on demand that will not last forever. AP points out that pressure is growing to balance trade again. Europe feels it especially: exports to Germany and the EU kept rising (Handelsblatt), and BFMTV cites a deficit of EU countries with China of 247 billion euros for the first nine months, up 26 percent. At its annual meeting in Bangkok the International Monetary Fund called on China to depend less on exports (El Economista). The South China Morning Post writes that Chinese electric-car makers are at the same time hit by measures from Brussels and Beijing, namely an EU quota and the cut of a value-added tax rebate; analysts therefore lowered their profit forecasts. ASML finance chief Roger Dassen warned, according to De Telegraaf, that tighter US export restrictions would not stop China’s technological rise. Whether and how Switzerland is affected does not emerge from the reports. Today we are watching reactions from the EU and whether the AI boom will carry exports through the autumn as well.

Switzerland · Digital policyPress ✓ 7 outlets · law not before 2027

Federal Council tightens youth protection online, but for now forgoes a social media ban for children

The Federal Council wants to add youth protection rules to the draft platform law. Media Minister Albert Rösti presented the results of the consultation procedure on Wednesday (20 Minuten, SRF, RTS); the Federal Council is thereby responding to criticism of the preliminary draft (SRF, Tages-Anzeiger). The preliminary draft initially covered only Google and social networks; now large AI providers are also to be held responsible, and they may not lure minors with personalized advertising (Tages-Anzeiger). Platforms must assess the risks for minors and then reduce them (24 heures, Tribune de Genève); added to this are new transparency obligations for platforms and search engines, which would also affect Google, Meta, TikTok and YouTube (RTS). There is no access ban for children for now, although several countries are considering restrictions: Le Temps writes that SVP Federal Councillor Rösti is relying “for the moment” on a pragmatic approach, and that the bill divides even the government. Blick speaks of soft regulation of the tech giants. According to SRF, parties, cantons and associations welcomed the preliminary draft. The Federal Council wants to present the law on communication platforms and search engines in June 2027 (Watson). The German government decided on the same day on key points for restrictions: under-13s are to be barred from social media, with transitional rules (Zeit Online, tagesschau). How the obligations will look in detail and be enforced remains open. We are watching whether motions calling for a ban follow in Parliament.

Ukraine war · PoliticsPress ✓ 12 outlets · Starlink claims not independently confirmed

Second night without warning: missiles hit Kyiv, and Ukraine complains of Starlink restrictions

Russia attacked Kyiv again overnight into Wednesday, the second night in a row without a warning from allies (Al Jazeera, Kyiv Post, FAZ, Handelsblatt). A missile hit a combined heat and power plant and a residential building in the Desnianskyi district; Mayor Vitali Klitschko spoke of around twenty injured (El Economista, Infobae). At least six people died nationwide (France 24, SCMP, RFI), and according to Zelensky 600,000 people were without power (ABC News, France 24). The Tages-Anzeiger and AD count 39 ballistic missiles and over 120 drones. Zelensky said Moscow was thereby showing Washington “disrespect”, because Donald Trump had announced an energy ceasefire (El Economista). Reports differ on the number of dead in Lutsk: ANSA gives three dead and ten injured, SRF six dead.

Kyiv accuses Washington of sharing less information. Axios had reported that US officials threatened to suspend the exchange of intelligence over Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries (Kyiv Post, The Hill); Trump denies a halt (Kyiv Post) but criticizes the attacks on refineries (El Universal). In addition, according to Ukrainian accounts, there were sudden restrictions on Starlink, SpaceX’s satellite network; they hampered drone operations and air defense (The Verge, PBS, NBC News, The Hill). Poland’s foreign minister Radosław Sikorski asked Elon Musk whether he was playing politics with his products again (Handelsblatt, The Standard). Musk blames every slowdown on the US government (Le Temps, Watson); our sources do not cite a statement from SpaceX. SRF quotes Zelensky as saying he feels “betrayed” by Trump. The president traveled to London, where Prime Minister Andy Burnham and King Charles III were due to receive him (Sky News, ANSA). Today we are watching for a response from Washington and from SpaceX.

Vatican · HealthPress ✓ 12 outlets · further test results pending

Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer: surgery is considered successful, whether the tumor is fully removed remains open

The Vatican announced on Tuesday evening that Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer. The 71-year-old was operated on at the Gemelli hospital in Rome; doctors removed the lower lobe of the right lung (CBS News, PBS, The Hill, RFI). According to the Vatican the procedure lasted about 90 minutes and went without complications (PBS, RFI). It is an adenocarcinoma, a common form of lung cancer (Evening Standard, 24 heures, New York Times). Only on Sunday had Leo made public a nodule in the lung, which had been discovered at a very early stage (Handelsblatt, National Post).

The pope spent a quiet night after the operation (Tages-Anzeiger). Whether the procedure removed the cancer completely is open (Tages-Anzeiger); further examinations are to clarify whether it has spread (Buenos Aires Times, citing a source in the Vatican). Media highlight the Vatican’s unusual openness: illnesses of popes used to be discussed late, vaguely or not at all (Watson, NZZ, Le Temps). According to CBS News and Watson his older brother Louis Prevost has multiple myeloma. On Wednesday heads of state around the world expressed their sympathy (SRF). Watson reported overnight that Leo had spoken out for the first time since the operation and that the planned trip to South America remains on the calendar; only Watson writes this. Today we are watching for statements from the clinic and for possible cancellations of appointments.

Markets · EconomyPress ✓ 6 outlets · closing prices not confirmed

High US inflation weighs on Wall Street, in Zurich defensive stocks support the SMI

US consumer prices in September were 3.7 percent above the previous year (Handelsblatt, Zonebourse). From August to September they rose 0.6 percent (Handelsblatt). The figure was higher than expected and strengthened bets on interest-rate hikes by the US central bank (Bloomberg, Zonebourse). US market expert Markus Koch considers a hike at the end of October more likely in Handelsblatt. Wall Street slipped, with technology stocks leading the losses (El Economista, Zonebourse); strong quarterly figures from the big banks hardly convinced investors (Handelsblatt). The six biggest US banks earned around 22 billion dollars in trading in the third quarter (City AM). We could not confirm closing prices of the US indices.

In Zurich the SMI rose clearly according to Watson, carried by defensive stocks; Cash writes that it gave back part of the gains at the close. We could not confirm the closing price with any second outlet, which is why the boxes at the top show a dash. During the day, according to Finanz und Wirtschaft, Lonza, Alcon and Sika were in demand, Swiss Re lost ground after a downgrade, and Nestlé recovered from an eight-month low. Chip equipment maker ASML reported a record quarter thanks to AI demand and raised its forecast slightly; it warns that it may not be able to fully meet demand in 2027 and 2028 (Bloomberg, Zonebourse). The oil price rose again in midweek (Watson, Handelsblatt); Folha attributes this to new Houthi attacks on Aramco in Dhahran. For pension funds and savers, such swings take effect with a delay.
