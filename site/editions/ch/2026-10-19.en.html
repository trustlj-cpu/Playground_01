France: After Saturday’s riots the government cites at least 1,800 arrests (AP), Macron addresses the nation on Sunday evening

The number of arrests rose over the course of the day from around 1,200 to at least 1,800 and then to almost 2,400; turnout remains disputed: 550,000 according to the government, 800,000 according to the organizers.

Serious riots broke out in France on Saturday, October 17, during the nationwide mobilization against the high cost of living. The arrest figures rose over the course of the day and come from the prime minister, the Paris police prefecture and the interior ministry, each at different times. On Saturday evening Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu cited around 1,200 arrests and 150 injured police officers, according to CBC and franceinfo. The news agency AP reports at least 1,800 arrests, 855 of them in Paris according to the police prefecture, as relayed by NPR. On Sunday Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez spoke of more than 2,000 arrests and more than 600 injured police officers, according to CNN and The National. The interior ministry’s latest tally puts arrests at 2,397 and injured police officers and gendarmes at 997, seven of them seriously, according to France 24, Al Jazeera and ICI. Le Temps also cites 2,397 arrests. Accounts of turnout diverge: Nuñez estimates it at around 550,000 people, according to RTE and CNN, while the CGT union and the organizers cite 800,000, according to RTE and ICI. According to Euronews, the movement began on social networks as a protest against rising fuel prices, then widened to the cost of living and joined up with protests by schoolchildren.

President Emmanuel Macron addressed the population in a televised speech on Sunday evening, as franceinfo, RTE, Al Jazeera and France 24 report. Le Temps writes on Sunday evening that France is speechless after the chaos on the fringes of the rallies and is looking for explanations. The consequences reach as far as the Swiss border: in Annemasse near Geneva, eleven shops were looted and trashed on Saturday evening, as the Tribune de Genève and ODS Radio report. Four people were arrested, according to these reports, and the riots there followed a demonstration the same day. Mayor Gabriel Doublet condemned the looting and urged parents to be vigilant. The reports thus capture two different levels, the national tally of the interior ministry and the local situation in the border region. Whether the rallies will continue in the coming days and whether the government will publish further figures is not stated in the reports at hand.

Domestic · Federal Council✓ 8 outlets · further candidacies open, parliamentary group decides on November 20

Céline Amaudruz wants to become the first SVP woman on the Federal Council; the party shows a divide between French-speaking Swiss and Zurichers

Céline Amaudruz, a Geneva SVP National Councillor and vice president of the party, announced in Geneva on October 15 her candidacy to succeed Federal Councillor Guy Parmelin. The Tages-Anzeiger, cash, nau.ch, bluewin and 20 Minuten, among others, report this. According to these reports she is the first confirmed candidate and, if elected, would be the first SVP woman on the Federal Council. The SVP parliamentary group decides on November 20 whom it puts on its official ticket; the United Federal Assembly votes on December 9. Within the party the candidacy is not uncontested. The reports note that Amaudruz has repeatedly voted against the party line on social issues. According to 24 heures and the Tribune de Genève, the French-speaking sections stand behind her, while the Zurich wing voices reservations. According to the summaries of 24 heures and the Tribune de Genève, the Sunday press describes a Röstigraben within the SVP, and the NZZ headlines its piece with the observation that Amaudruz must first overcome the Röstigraben. Who else will run is open in the reports.

Cyberattack on software supplier PK Softech: data of the federal pension fund Publica and other funds may have leaked

A cyberattack on the software supplier PK Softech AG may affect data of Publica, the federal pension fund. Publica confirmed the company’s name to the AWP news agency, as cash reports. According to SRF and cash, PK Softech detected the attack at the end of September and filed a criminal complaint; the Office of the Attorney General is investigating. According to netzwoche and SRF, Publica names as possibly leaked address and contact data, AHV numbers, details on salary, pension assets and pension, as well as on spouses and account numbers. The pension funds themselves are not affected and ongoing operations are not impaired, Publica states. Which data actually leaked is open according to the reports.

The circle of those affected is growing: according to netzwoche and bluewin, the Migros pension fund and the City of Zurich pension fund have also reported possible data leaks, and in the canton of Bern the Bernese Pension Fund and the Bernese Teachers’ Insurance Fund may be affected, according to netzwoche. finews and the NZZ report that the attack is spreading further; the NZZ writes that the data of well over 100,000 insured people could be in the hands of criminals, but stresses that this is still unclear. None of the funds gives a number of insured people affected. Publica declined to say whether a ransom was demanded, citing the ongoing investigation.

Politics · Vote on November 29✓ 7 outlets · poll figures come from the first wave

SVP delegates set their recommendations in Bulle for November 29: No to VAT for the AHV, Yes to the War Material Act

At its delegates’ assembly on Saturday, October 17, in Bulle FR, the SVP Switzerland set its voting recommendations for the November 29 vote. According to SRF, cash and nau.ch, the delegates reject the increase in value-added tax to finance the AHV. On the War Material Act they say Yes, and they also support the popular initiative “Yes to fair federal taxes for married couples too”. The NZZ headlines its report with the line that the SVP is celebrating itself; in its statement the party speaks of the “fight against the left and the homeland-weary”.

The first voting poll by Leewas for 20 Minuten and Tamedia dates from October 7 and 8 and is based on 11,239 respondents. For the War Material Act, nau.ch and bluewin report 47 percent No, 44 percent Yes and 9 percent undecided. According to nau.ch, 60 percent reject the higher VAT for the AHV. On the fireworks initiative, 20 Minuten and the Aargauer Zeitung give 68 percent Yes and 30 percent No; the Federal Council recommends a No, the National Council rejected it by 131 to 50 votes, the Council of States by 40 to 3.

Transport · Rail✓ 5 outlets · comparison with the previous year unclear

The SBB counts 97 heat-related track deformations this summer; an ETH Zurich expert warns of derailments

The SBB recorded 97 deformations of tracks due to heat in summer 2026, as 24 heures, Blick, Le Matin, 20 minutes and RTS report. According to 24 heures, the SBB confirmed the figure to the SonntagsZeitung. An ETH Zurich expert warns of the risk of derailments, according to 24 heures and Le Matin. According to 24 heures, the SBB sees no immediate danger of derailment and points to its monitoring network of measuring vehicles, sensors and inspectors. It has set up a working group to determine additional measures if needed. On the comparison with the previous year, the media give contradictory accounts.
