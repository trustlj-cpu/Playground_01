Ukraine war · PoliticsPress ✓ 9 outlets · Moscow does not confirm a ceasefire

Despite an “energy ceasefire”, Russia hits six energy facilities, says Zelensky. EU foreign ministers adopt record sanctions

As of Monday evening Moscow has confirmed no ceasefire, and the Kremlin rejects Zelensky’s idea of a 60-day pause for elections. The EU is imposing new punitive measures on almost 1,600 targets; an SRF correspondent speaks of a “strong signal”.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Monday that Russia had hit six energy facilities in his country, even though US President Donald Trump had announced an “energy ceasefire” on Truth Social on Sunday (Ukrinform, PBS, The Hill). Putin does not read Truth Social, Zelensky said, according to Fortune. At the same time he declared that Ukraine was ready for a ceasefire on energy facilities but was not sure about Putin; Kyiv would respond if Russia failed to keep to the pause in winter (FinancialJuice, Bloomberg, RTS). As of Monday evening Moscow had not confirmed that such a ceasefire was in force (RTS, Watson, BBC). The Kremlin also rejected Zelensky’s idea of a 60-day ceasefire for elections and demands a comprehensive peace agreement (Reuters, UNITED24 Media). Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said, according to 20 Minuten, that Zelensky had not earned a pause on the battlefield. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said, according to FinancialJuice, that a ceasefire does not work if only one side keeps to it. The NATO commander in Europe, US General Alexus Grynkewich, said at a defense forum in Warsaw on Monday that an attack on NATO states would be “completely futile”; a conflict was not imminent, and Moscow should not cross the alliance’s red lines (RTS, Reuters, NZZ). The NZZ writes that his new “theory of victory” envisages a three-stage defense that also includes strikes on the adversary’s own soil. Accounts of the injured and dead differ: Watson reports one dead in a drone attack on Kyiv, TVC News cites 21 dead in Russian attacks; there is no independent confirmation of either.

The EU foreign ministers adopted new sanctions on Monday against almost 1,600 individuals, companies and organizations (SRF). Le Temps counts almost 1,650 names and speaks of the largest package since the start of the war; Handelsblatt, Süddeutsche Zeitung and Watson headline it “Record sanctions against Russian war helpers”. Le Temps points out that hybrid attacks, such as sabotage, are hard to sanction. An EU correspondent for SRF calls the sanctions “little more than a strong signal”. In addition, Trump’s diesel agreement with Moscow, which he announced on Friday, is straining relations with Kyiv: according to SRF, the dispute between Trump and Zelensky mainly benefits Putin. Le Temps sees the agreement as an attempt to lower the gasoline price before the elections. Republicans express unease, according to Bloomberg; Representative Mike Lawler said the US should not do any diesel business with Russia. Seven US senators from both parties urged Trump to drop the agreement, according to Novaya Gazeta Europe. Poland’s Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said, according to Bloomberg, that Trump must put pressure on Russia, not on Ukraine. The oil price reacted: Brent traded at just under $106 on Monday afternoon, according to The Hill. Switzerland generally adopts EU sanctions; whether it will also adopt this package has not been reported. Today we are watching whether Moscow confirms the ceasefire and whether further energy facilities are hit.

Cybersecurity · FrancePress ✓ 8 outlets · perpetrator unknown

Cyberattack defrosts refrigerated shelves in hundreds of French supermarkets, food has to be thrown out

In the night to Monday a cyberattack knocked out the refrigeration systems of supermarkets in France. According to the retailers, 150 to 200 stores of the Intermarché chain and almost a hundred branches of Coopérative U are affected; BFMTV reported first (RTS, Guardian). The hackers switched refrigerators and freezers to defrost mode (SRF); according to 20 Minuten, the units heated up to as much as 50 degrees. Fresh and frozen goods had to be thrown away (Zeit, RTÉ). The retailers call it an “unprecedented” attack (Guardian, 24 heures). Reuters reports around 150 Intermarché stores hit. According to Le Temps, no data was stolen; the consequence is spoiled fresh produce, not a data leak. The Paris public prosecutor’s office confirmed the attack, according to Straits Times; it also says that other large chains were apparently affected. Who is behind it is unclear (SRF). CNA reports more than 400 affected stores and outdated control systems; no second outlet supports this. Blick also names Belgium and the Netherlands, which the retailers have not confirmed. We have no reports from Swiss stores. We are watching whether anyone claims responsibility for the attack and when the stores will be fully cooling again.

Wall Street closes mixed at the start of the week: S&P 500 marginally lower, Nasdaq loses 0.4 percent

US exchanges ended Monday mixed. The S&P 500 slipped 0.06 percent to 7,807.24 points, the Nasdaq 0.44 percent to 27,245.90 points (Yahoo Finance, Reuters). On the Dow Jones, Clarín reports a gain; we could not confirm the closing price. Chip stocks fell (Bloomberg, Jornal de Negócios). Investors held back ahead of US inflation data and the quarterly results of the big banks, such as JPMorgan (Clarín, RTP). Brent cost just under $106 on Monday afternoon, around $1.20 more than on Friday (The Hill); ABC puts it at almost $107 overnight.

We could not confirm the closing level of the Swiss benchmark index SMI or Monday’s dollar-franc rate with a second outlet; the boxes above therefore show a dash. Switzerland · PoliticsPress ✓ 5 outlets · vote on November 29

War Materiel Act: opponents call the easing of arms exports “immoral” and “against Ukraine”

On Monday the referendum committee presented its arguments in Bern against the easing of the War Materiel Act (SRF, Watson). Switzerland votes on the bill on November 29 (RTS, 24 heures). According to RTS and 24 heures, the committee calls it “immoral” and “against Ukraine”. The Federal Council wants to ease the rules for arms exports in order to safeguard the country’s defense capability and preserve jobs (24 heures). The left warns of risks (20 Minuten).

The War Materiel Act governs to which countries Swiss companies may deliver armaments. How Parliament voted on the amendment and which parties support the bill is not clear from our sources from Monday. For eligible voters it means: the campaign is starting, and the vote is in just under seven weeks. We are watching how the supporters speak out and whether defense companies present figures on jobs.

Economy · Labor marketPress ✓ 4 outlets · figures according to media reports

Zimmer Biomet cuts up to 490 jobs in Winterthur instead of 580

The US medical technology group Zimmer Biomet is cutting fewer jobs in Winterthur, canton of Zurich, than announced: up to 490 jobs are now affected instead of the 580 originally cited (SRF, Tages-Anzeiger, Watson). “Now it is definitive,” writes SRF. According to the Tages-Anzeiger, no job is to go before 2028. According to the NZZ, part of the production stays in Winterthur, and the city government says it is relieved.

The number is still large: it is a cut “on a large scale”, as SRF writes. For those affected, the deadline of 2028 means time for retraining or a job search. According to the reports, it is open how many jobs exactly will remain in Winterthur and which parts of production will move where. We are watching whether unions and the city call for a social plan.
