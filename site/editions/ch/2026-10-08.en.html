Hackers steal data from the federal pension fund. The attack came through a software supplier

Salaries, AHV numbers and pension amounts of federal employees may be affected. How many insured members are hit, Publica does not yet know. The Office of the Attorney General is investigating.

Publica, the federal pension fund, confirmed a data leak on Thursday. The target was not the fund itself but its software supplier, PK Softech AG, which develops applications for pension funds; Publica confirmed the name to the news agency AWP. According to Publica, the attack took place at the end of September. Through the supplier, the attackers apparently gained access to data held by the fund. Details on salaries, pension assets and pension amounts, as well as contact details, may be affected. SRF also reports names, AHV numbers and details on life partners; RSI reports similarly. The Tages-Anzeiger writes that the salaries of thousands of federal employees could become public. The software company says it detected the attack itself and filed a criminal complaint. The Office of the Attorney General has opened proceedings. According to Publica, it is unclear how many insured members and pensioners are affected. At the end of 2025 the fund had about 70,000 active insured members and 41,600 pensioners; that is the total membership, not the number of people affected. Publica is one of the largest pension funds in Switzerland. It insures not only employees of the federal administration but also staff of organizations close to the federal government. The circle of people who wondered on Thursday whether their data is among those affected is correspondingly wide. The fund could not yet give them an answer. It announced that it will inform those affected once the analysis is complete.

Publica has informed its insured members. Pension assets are safe, it said, and pensions will be paid out as usual. The fund advises caution with unusual e-mails, calls and messages, because leaked personal data could be misused for fraud. It is open whether data from other pension funds that work with the same supplier is also affected. Several outlets recall the Xplain case: in 2023, hackers stole data from the Bern software firm that also concerned federal offices, and later published it on the dark web. At the time it became clear how vulnerable the federal government is through its suppliers. The Publica case raises the same question: how closely does an authority check what its suppliers do with its data, and how much real personal data sits with firms that develop and test software? For those affected, the danger is concrete. Anyone who knows a person’s name, AHV number and salary can launch convincing fraud attempts, such as fake calls in the name of the fund or the bank. The NZZ describes a cyberattack on the software supplier, not on Publica itself. What exactly was leaked is for the investigation to show; until then the details remain provisional. Politically, the case is likely to raise questions, for instance whether the lessons of Xplain were also applied at an independent federal institution such as Publica. There were no answers to that on Thursday.

Sport · JusticeSource ✓ Lucerne Public Prosecutor’s Office (penalty order, cited) · Press ✓ 6 outlets

Granit Xhaka gets a suspended fine for a forged Covid certificate. He accepts it

The captain of the national football team has been convicted by a penalty order from the Lucerne Public Prosecutor’s Office, as NZZ, SRF, Tages-Anzeiger and Le Temps report. The sentence is 50 day-fines of CHF 3,000, suspended with a probation period of two years. The charge is obtaining a false official record by deception. At the end of September, Xhaka had admitted buying a forged Covid certificate in 2022. On Instagram he wrote that he accepts the decision and takes responsibility; he has already paid the costs of the proceedings. The Swiss Football Association said it was aware of the decision and that it was good for the matter to be legally closed. What the verdict means for his football career is open. Xhaka has to pay the fine only if he reoffends within the next two years.

SMI falls 1.24 percent to 13,637 points. Oil jumps, Iran worries return

The Swiss Market Index ended Thursday down 1.24 percent at 13,637.00 points, after 13,808.42 the day before. It was the sharpest daily loss of the week. The German Dax also closed below 25,000 points. In the oil market, the North Sea grade Brent rose at times above $102 a barrel; the American grade WTI traded in the evening at around $92.80, a good 5 percent higher than the day before. Over the course of the week the benchmark index had still gained; Thursday’s setback more than wiped out those gains.

The agency dpa-AFX names as the trigger a report by the US magazine “The Atlantic,” according to which the White House asked the Pentagon for options for new strikes on Iran. The report is not independently confirmed. In the currency market the franc stayed strong: the dollar cost around CHF 0.8335 in the evening, while finanzen.ch gave 0.8340 in the afternoon. An oil shock hits Switzerland through heating oil, fuel and transport costs; the strong franc cushions part of it, because oil is traded in dollars. In the US, the S&P 500 had closed 0.22 percent lower on Wednesday, and gold closed at $4,140.70 an ounce.

War in UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · casualty figure preliminary

Russian attack on buses in Kramatorsk: at least 30 dead

In a Russian attack on scheduled buses in Kramatorsk in the Donetsk region, at least 30 people were killed and 18 injured, according to the Ukrainian military governor. The NZZ live ticker reports at least 33 dead. According to Ukrainian accounts, a glide bomb hit a street and set the buses on fire. Public transport in Kramatorsk and Sloviansk was halted. The agency dpa had initially reported twelve dead; the number rose in the course of the day. Kramatorsk lies close to the front in the east and is an important hub for the Ukrainian army, but also a city where tens of thousands of civilians still live.

The figures cannot be independently verified. Ukraine also reported drone attacks on the Russian city of Omsk. Off Romania and Bulgaria, cargo ships were hit by drones again; the NZZ writes that Moscow is under suspicion. For Switzerland, the war remains a matter of energy prices, sanctions and humanitarian aid. The attacks on merchant ships in the Black Sea also affect the trade in grain and commodities, a good part of which is handled through Geneva and Zug.

Italy · EuropePress ✓ 5 outlets · content of the reform not checked in detail

Meloni pushes through her electoral law. Italy’s parliament approves despite dissenters and constitutional doubts

The Italian parliament has approved the controversial electoral reform of Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni. SRF, Tages-Anzeiger, NZZ and RSI report in agreement. According to the Tages-Anzeiger, Meloni prevailed despite dissenters in her own majority and despite doubts about its constitutionality. She had previously won a confidence vote; according to the Handelsblatt, one last hurdle remains.

We have not checked in the original wording how the reform changes the distribution of seats in detail. SRF calls it a test of the mood and writes that Meloni is playing for high stakes with the new rules. The NZZ asks whether it is a clever move and describes a thriller over the new electoral law. RSI writes that the reform received the necessary votes. Critics fear the rules are tailored to the needs of the governing parties. For Switzerland, Italy is a neighbor and one of its most important trading partners. A stable government in Rome matters for cross-border traffic in Ticino and for negotiations, for example on cross-border commuters.

Unverified — what we could not confirm

“Xhaka has to pay CHF 150,000.” Misleading. 50 day-fines of CHF 3,000 do add up to CHF 150,000, but the sentence is suspended. It falls due only in case of a relapse within two years.
