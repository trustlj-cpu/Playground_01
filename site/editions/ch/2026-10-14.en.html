Economy · Real estatePress ✓ 7 outlets · figures according to 24 heures and 20 Minuten

Fixed-rate mortgages costlier than at any time since March 2025, even though the Swiss National Bank keeps its key interest rate at zero

Benchmark rates for ten-year fixed-rate mortgages are at their highest since March 2025, according to 20 Minuten; 24 heures cites over 2 percent. Buyers are barely bothered: 43 percent of the contracts signed run ten years or longer.

Benchmark rates for ten-year fixed-rate mortgages are higher than at any time since March 2025 (20 Minuten). This is the case even though the Swiss National Bank is keeping its key interest rate at 0 percent (20 Minuten). Blick writes that the popular fixed-rate mortgages have not been this expensive at any point this year; Watson, Rhône FM, blue News and La Liberté report a rise in mortgage rates in the third quarter. According to 24 heures, the rate for ten years is above 2 percent. There is no single figure, because banks and comparison platforms publish their own benchmark rates; the reports therefore give orders of magnitude, not a binding price.

According to 24 heures, buyers are hardly deterred: 43 percent of the contracts signed have a term of ten years or more. The paper also calculates that more than 35,000 francs can be saved by negotiating; which loan amount and term this refers to is not clear from our sources. Long-term mortgage rates usually follow yields on the capital market rather than the key interest rate; what triggered the latest rise is not stated in the reports. Cash and Finanz und Wirtschaft report at the same time that home ownership in Switzerland keeps getting more expensive. We are watching whether the banks raise their rates further in the coming days.

Vatican · HealthPress ✓ 12 outlets · statements from the Vatican and the doctor

Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer: the lung nodule removed on Tuesday was malignant

Pope Leo XIV underwent lung surgery on Tuesday at the Gemelli hospital in Rome. The Vatican said in the evening that the operation had gone well and that the pope was back in his room (RTS, SRF). It later emerged that the removed nodule was malignant (SRF, Tages-Anzeiger, Le Temps, 24 heures, swissinfo, AP, Politico). According to SRF, the lower lobe of the right lung was removed. His doctor spoke, according to the Tages-Anzeiger, of a good course but a worrying finding. Le Temps cites a lung adenocarcinoma and writes that further examinations are planned; recovery can take three months, according to 24 heures. How the finding affects appointments and travel has not been reported. We are watching whether the Vatican publishes a report on further treatment.

Middle East · EnergyPress ✓ 9 outlets · claims by the warring parties not independently verified

Houthis hit the Aramco headquarters, Saudi-led coalition bombs an oil facility near Sanaa

The Yemeni Houthi militia hit the headquarters of the oil company Saudi Aramco in Dhahran in a missile attack on Tuesday, according to Bloomberg; a fire broke out in part of the complex. The Saudi state agency reports a limited fire with no injuries (via FinancialJuice); Reuters had earlier reported loud explosions nearby. In Riyadh, four people were injured by debris from intercepted missiles, according to swissinfo and Leman bleu. Earlier, the Saudi-led coalition had urged civilians to leave an oil facility near Sanaa and then attacked it (Reuters, Guardian, BBC, 20 Minuten). The Guardian calls it the largest strike so far against Houthi energy facilities. The Houthis threaten, according to the BBC, attacks on Riyadh and Dammam, and according to SRF also on Riyadh airport and Saudi oil facilities.

US President Donald Trump said the Houthis were behaving “very badly” and wanted to push up oil prices before the US midterm elections, probably on behalf of Iran (Reuters, Yahoo, Jerusalem Post, Middle East Eye). He would “never forget” the attacks on Saudi Arabia (Watson, Kurdistan24). SRF writes that Trump is leaving Saudi Arabia in the lurch: before the midterms, gasoline prices are high and ammunition stocks are empty, and he wants to avoid a new front. SRF also reports that Saudi Arabia suspended the warning system before the earlier attacks on Riyadh airport, in which at least 18 people died and more than 300 were injured. The oil price rose overnight into Wednesday, according to Reuters, as traders weigh the supply risk from Saudi Arabia. We are watching whether the Houthis make good on their threats against Riyadh and Dammam.

Ukraine war · SecurityPress ✓ 9 outlets · statements by Zelensky, not independently verified

Missiles hit Kyiv, a power plant is affected: Ukraine received no advance warning from its partners

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Tuesday that Ukraine had received no warning from its partners before a Russian attack on Kyiv with ballistic missiles; five missiles had hit civilian infrastructure (Guardian, UNITED24, Kyiv Post, CNN). In the night into Wednesday, another attack hit an important power plant in the city, again without advance warning, the second night in a row (BBC, Watson, Antiwar.com). Reuters reports two dead; we have no second confirmation of this. The Guardian recalls that the US had earlier threatened to stop sharing intelligence information; the Financial Times writes that Zelensky did not explicitly link the missing warning to this.

Trump declared on Tuesday that the US would continue to give Ukraine intelligence information; Russia would also release diesel for the world market, not for the US (SRF). Blick notes that it is unclear whether the US withheld information. Zelensky and Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko also argued over the city’s winter readiness: Zelensky said, according to SRF, that every Kyivan could see that it was not prepared; Der Spiegel reports the same. Zelensky further said that Moscow had already violated the energy ceasefire announced by Trump (Jerusalem Post, Breakingthenews). We are watching whether the US or other partners explain why no warning came, and whether further power plants are hit.

Switzerland · PoliticsPress ✓ 7 outlets · one poll by 20 Minuten

Vote on November 29: first poll sees No to higher value-added tax for the 13th AHV pension and Yes to fireworks ban

On November 29 Switzerland votes on four bills (La Liberté). The first poll on them has been published by 20 Minuten: the fireworks initiative is heading for a clear Yes, the higher value-added tax for the 13th AHV pension for a clear No. According to 24 heures, 60 percent of respondents would reject the tax increase today. The value-added tax is to rise by 0.4 percentage points (20 Minuten). La Liberté headlines that three of four bills would be rejected. How many people were surveyed and how large the margin of error is, our sources do not say; the poll is a snapshot about six weeks before the date.

On Tuesday the camps launched their campaigns. The Mitte and the left campaign for the higher value-added tax (Blick, 20 Minuten, SRF); a union boss warns, according to 20 Minuten, of a higher retirement age if the vote is No. The committee behind the fireworks initiative puts animals, noise and accidents at the center (SRF, 20 Minuten, RTS); the Federal Council considers the text too far-reaching (24 heures). Finance Minister Karin Keller-Sutter opened the Federal Council’s No campaign against the Mitte’s “Fairness Initiative”: it disadvantages unmarried people, makes taxes more complicated and blows a hole in the federal treasury (Tages-Anzeiger, NZZ). On the War Materiel Act our sources give no figures; 20 Minuten calls the topic a “thriller” in its headline. We are watching how the numbers move once the campaigns get going.
