SVP near 30 percent, SP at its best reading: the SRG SSR barometer a year before the elections

Twelve months before the federal elections, the SVP and the left gain in the poll while the Centre and the FDP lose ground. A snapshot, not a forecast.

The latest election barometer of the SRG SSR puts the SVP at 29.9 percent of voting intentions. The SP would get 19.3 percent, according to Le Temps its best figure in this series of polls. The Centre and the FDP, by contrast, lose support. Le Temps reported the figures on Friday, as did 24 heures and the Tribune de Genève, which both belong to the same publisher. The SRG SSR is the public broadcaster of radio and television in Switzerland; it publishes the barometer about a year before election day in autumn 2027.

What the figures mean, and what they do not: a poll twelve months before an election measures mood, not outcome. Surveys of this kind usually have a margin of error of one to two percentage points, so changes of one point can hardly be told apart from chance. The direction is more striking: the two poles of the party system gain, the middle loses. Nor can national percentages be converted one to one into seats. The 200 seats in the National Council are allocated by proportional representation, canton by canton. Small cantons with few seats therefore work differently from large ones.

In parallel, the debate over the succession in the Federal Council is under way. The Vaud SVP National Councillor Yvan Pahud said in an interview with 24 heures that he will not run: “This is not the right moment.” He had been named as a possible successor to Federal Councillor Parmelin. So far the interview is documented only by 24 heures; no second outlet has confirmed it. Our sources also do not show that Parmelin has announced his resignation; the mention of the name shows above all that the parties are already starting to play personnel games.

It is open whether other institutes measure similar values. A second, independent poll is still to come, and only a pattern across several surveys would support the trend. Until then the rule is: the barometer shows which topics and parties currently have momentum, but it decides nothing. In the coming months the main questions will probably be whether the Centre can stop its losses and how the electorate behaves ahead of the drawing up of the lists. Another point is mobilization: whether voters actually cast their ballots often decides the balance between the camps in Switzerland, and turnout cannot be estimated seriously a year in advance. We will keep reporting as soon as further measurements are available.

Djokovic returns to the Swiss Indoors after 15 years

Novak Djokovic is taking part in the Swiss Indoors in Basel, for the first time in 15 years. That is reported by Watson, 24 heures, the Tribune de Genève and the Quotidien Jurassien. The Serb is now 39 years old. As 24 heures writes, his win over Roger Federer in Basel dates back 17 years; he won the final then, at the Swiss player’s home tournament. The tournament is among the most important indoor events on the continent and is a fixed date in the autumn for Swiss fans. 24 heures asks what is behind the commitment and speaks of a return “to Federer’s turf.” The reason remains interpretation in the reports; no official statement from the player is quoted in our sources. What is clear is only this: for the tournament, the commitment means one of the biggest names in the history of tennis, and for ticket demand that should be a strong signal. Exactly when Djokovic will be on court in Basel is not something we can take from the reports; the tournament traditionally takes place in late autumn. For Basel it is also an emotional subject, because the win over the local hero Federer is remembered to this day. For context: taking part does not automatically mean that the Serb will reach the final; on seeding, opponents and form the sources say nothing.

Paris and Berlin slow the switch to all-electric cars

According to RTS, Paris and Berlin have made official an agreement on the auto industry that slows the transition to all-electric vehicles. Le Temps writes that the two countries are sacrificing part of their climate targets to protect the industry, and speak of a “common approach” meant to preserve jobs. Both outlets describe the step as a compromise between climate policy and jobs. The question of how fast the combustion engine actually disappears is thus being renegotiated.

This matters for Switzerland because suppliers here are closely interwoven with German and French vehicle production. Switzerland is not an EU member, but for new-car emission rules it often adopts the European limits. The reports at hand name no concrete deadlines or quotas for the agreement, so consequences for importers and buyers cannot yet be assessed. We note: two large markets are easing their pace, and that raises the pressure on governments that stick to the existing targets. The timing is also remarkable: in France, presidential elections are due in about six months, and high living costs are politically explosive there. Whether the agreement responds to that is not stated in the reports.

London is said to have offered UBS a move of its headquarters

The British government is said to have proposed to UBS that it move its headquarters from Switzerland to the United Kingdom. Le Temps reports this in the conditional mood. The former British finance minister Rachel Reeves is said to have held talks with the bank’s leadership. This is a single source; a second outlet and a statement from UBS are not in our files. Le Temps names neither the timing nor the form of the alleged talks.

The background is well known: since the takeover of Credit Suisse, Switzerland has been debating stricter capital requirements for the big bank, and it is repeatedly suggested that it might leave the country. The report is therefore plausible but unsubstantiated. Whether there were concrete offers, only soundings or mere rumors remains open. We are watching whether UBS, federal authorities or other outlets react. For Switzerland, jobs, tax revenue and prestige depend on the headquarters. Anyone passing the report on should therefore make clear that so far only one newspaper has reported it, in the conditional. A denial or a confirmation from the bank would clear things up quickly. Until then it remains a report that one should take seriously but not treat as fact.

Lugano turns it around in the Conference League, Davos wins in Lugano

FC Lugano won in the Conference League after trailing against Belgrade; ten outlets report on the turnaround. In ice hockey, Davos can breathe again after a win in Lugano: after trailing in the opening period, HCD turned the game around with three goals in the second period (Blick, Google News). According to Watson, the ZSC again put in a strong performance, and Servette remains the first pursuer. For Swiss football the win in Europe matters because it brings points for the coefficient ranking and boosts confidence. In ice hockey the race for the top places in the league stays tight, even though after a few rounds nothing is decided yet.

At the pumptrack world championships in Beijing, Nadine Aeberhard took the title and Tristan Borel won bronze (24 heures). And in the NHL, thanks to Nico Hischier, the dialect song “Richi” is now also played at the New Jersey Devils (Watson). Further results of the weekend can be found in the league tables of the associations. In addition, 24 heures reports the death of a South African player who was found dead in his hotel on the Île de Ré on the eve of a match. According to the report, the circumstances are not yet clear.
