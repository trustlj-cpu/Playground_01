Trump and Putin agree on Russian diesel exports. Zelensky: “An investment in the war”

After a phone call with Putin, Trump announces that Russia will deliver 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately and significantly more later. In return, Washington eases sanctions. Kyiv protests, and whether prices will fall is an open question.

US President Donald Trump announced a U-turn in Russia policy on Friday after a phone call with Vladimir Putin. On Truth Social he wrote that Russia would deliver 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately, another 500,000 in November and one million tonnes after that; in total it could reach up to three million. Reuters, AP, SRF, NZZ and Blick report this. The figures come from Trump himself; the Kremlin confirmed the agreement, according to Le Monde, but we have not checked the details. The US Treasury Department is temporarily allowing the sale of Russian diesel on the world market under a general license that, according to the Kyiv Independent and The Hindu, runs until April 2027. Russia had earlier stopped its own diesel exports because Ukrainian drones hit refineries and there was a shortage at home (CBC). Behind the U-turn is the price: the US diesel price stands at $6.23 per gallon, according to the automobile club AAA, and in September it hit a record of $6.52. Trump is under pressure to cut the cost of living ahead of the midterm elections in November. Reuters writes, however, that his earlier steps on diesel supply have not yet pushed prices down.

Ukrainian President Zelensky reacted sharply. According to Reuters, he called it an “investment in the war” when the US allows Russia to sell diesel; SRF reports fierce criticism from Kyiv. The objection: the revenue finances Moscow’s army while peace is being discussed. Axios quotes Zelensky as saying the trade is “not fair and not honest”; ANSA writes that he warns Russia will pay for it with more terror against Ukraine. A market strategist says, according to MarketWatch, that the measure may come “too little, too late”. Trump had earlier billed the announcement as a “big announcement” (Reuters). The Washington Post and Newsweek explicitly link it to the aim of lowering prices before the election. CNBC notes that, given the destroyed refinery capacity, the president has few levers left. Three things matter for Switzerland. First, the price: diesel and heating oil follow the world market, but a quick decline is not certain because processing capacity is tight. Second, sanctions: Switzerland generally adopts the EU’s Russia sanctions. We do not know whether it will react to the easing in Washington; we have no statement from the federal government. Third, the franc: the dollar closed at around 0.83 francs. A strong franc dampens the import price of oil, since it is traded in dollars; taxes and levies at the pump are unaffected. At the same time, the Middle East remains a price driver. After the attack on Riyadh airport, which we reported yesterday, Reuters and AP speak of three dead; the European aviation safety agency EASA is extending its warning for Saudi airspace, according to Reuters. WTI closed on Friday at $91.63 a barrel, up 0.15 percent. Today, over the weekend, we are watching whether Moscow puts figures on the volumes, how Kyiv responds, and whether the futures markets price in lower diesel prices on Monday.

Parmelin succession: SVP favorite Friedli will not run

The SVP’s search for a successor to Federal Councillor Guy Parmelin has become more open. Esther Friedli, a member of the Council of States from St. Gallen, said on the sidelines of the Olma on Friday evening that she will not run; SRF, RSI, Blick, 20 Minuten and Leman bleu all report this. Until then she had been seen as the favorite. Her reason, according to reports: she does not want to give up her seat in the Council of States so soon. The decision came as a surprise (20 Minuten). Parmelin announced his resignation for the end of 2026 and himself named three SVP women as possible successors: Friedli, Geneva National Councillor and SVP vice president Céline Amaudruz, and Zurich health director Natalie Rickli. After Friedli’s withdrawal, Amaudruz and Rickli remain in the running. According to 20 Minuten, there are no official candidacies yet; another name that comes up is Pascal Schmid. Blick calls him Christoph Blocher’s favorite, which is a single report. The successor will be elected by the United Federal Assembly, that is, the National Council and the Council of States together, on December 9 in Bern according to 20 Minuten. The SVP holds two of the seven Federal Council seats and wants to keep the one that is becoming vacant. That is not guaranteed: the other parties do not have to adopt the proposal, even if they usually recognize the SVP’s claim to the seat. According to earlier reports, the deadline for candidacies expires on October 23. With Friedli’s withdrawal, the duel between eastern and western Switzerland that SRF had described also falls away. A decision is thus not in sight; the party can still surprise until the end of October.

Wall Street closes higher, SMI gains 1.1 percent. Markets are closed today

US stock markets ended the week on Friday in positive territory. The S&P 500 gained 0.59 percent to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64 percent to 27,366.17 points and the Dow Jones 0.83 percent to 51,654.95 points. The Handelsblatt writes of a recovery in tech stocks after fresh fear of an AI bubble, while Bloomberg speaks of an interest-rate shock spreading beneath the AI-driven rally. The yield on ten-year US government bonds was 5.244 percent. Gold rose 1.51 percent to $4,219.90 an ounce, WTI 0.15 percent to $91.63. AP reports in US regional newspapers speak of a record week.

The Swiss benchmark index SMI closed 1.10 percent higher at 13,787.40 points, after 13,637.00 on Thursday. In the evening the dollar cost around 0.83 francs, 0.11 percent less. Among individual stocks, the airline Delta cut its profit forecast because fuel is getting six billion dollars more expensive, as Reuters and Bloomberg report. Deutsche Telekom shares fell 7 percent after a spectrum deal by SpaceX (Reuters). US consumer sentiment worsened further in October, according to Reuters. The SIX Swiss Exchange is closed today; the next trading day is Monday. That is not a forecast.

World politics · LawPress ✓ 5 outlets · reach of the sanctions not verified

Nobel Peace Prize for Navi Pillay. A few hours later, the US sanctions the criminal court

The Nobel Committee in Oslo has awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to the South African jurist Navanethem “Navi” Pillay, report Reuters, AP, NZZ and RTS. Pillay was a judge at the International Criminal Court (ICC) in The Hague and later UN High Commissioner for Human Rights in Geneva; Le Temps writes that Geneva is celebrating the choice. According to reports, the committee pointed out that the institutions of international law are under pressure.

Only hours later, the US imposed sweeping sanctions on the ICC itself, report Reuters in an exclusive, AP, SRF and RTS. AP describes the move as part of a campaign to break up the court. The prosecution condemned the step and said its work would continue (Reuters). According to media reports, the background is arrest warrants against Israeli politicians. Pillay was shocked, according to Spiegel. For Switzerland this is more than a distant matter: Geneva is home to the UN human rights bodies, and Switzerland is among the states party to the court. Whether Swiss companies or banks are affected by the sanctions is an open question.

EU and China want to halve hybrid car exports. The dispute over electric cars continues

The EU and China have agreed on a “common understanding” on hybrid vehicles, according to Reuters, Le Temps and CNBC. EU Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič said, according to Reuters, that Chinese exports of hybrid and plug-in hybrid cars to the EU should fall by more than half. China’s Ministry of Commerce confirmed an understanding in line with the rules of the World Trade Organization but gave no figure. The mechanism and timetable have not been published; the EU member states still have to deliberate. The background: after the EU’s additional tariffs on pure electric cars, Chinese manufacturers switched to hybrids, which pay only the normal duty of 10 percent. According to the FT, imports rose from around 3,800 to about 50,000 a month.

Talks on pure electric cars continue separately, including on minimum prices. For Switzerland this is of secondary importance, since it does not belong to the EU; but manufacturers and dealers plan with the same prices across Europe. Blick reports that Switzerland is approaching the 50 percent target for the share of electric cars. Fewer cheap hybrids from China could change supply and price pressure. We are watching whether the EU member states approve the understanding and whether Beijing confirms the figure.
