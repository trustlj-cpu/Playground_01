Baume-Schneider campaigns for higher value-added tax. Without a Yes, half the money for the 13th AHV pension is missing

On November 29, Switzerland votes on whether value-added tax for the AHV rises by 0.4 percentage points. The social affairs minister warns of “serious consequences” if the answer is No. The FDP disagrees.

Federal Councillor Elisabeth Baume-Schneider opened the campaign on Monday for the additional funding of the AHV, as Tages-Anzeiger, NZZ and SRF report in agreement. It is about money for a pension that the people have already approved: the 13th AHV pension will be paid out for the first time in December 2026. What is open is who pays for it. Parliament cut back the Federal Council’s proposal. The standard rate of value-added tax is to rise from 8.1 to 8.5 percent, and the special rate for accommodation from 3.8 to 4.0 percent. The reduced rate for food, medicines and other everyday goods stays unchanged. According to the federal government, the 13th pension costs about CHF 4.2 billion a year. The higher value-added tax would bring in about CHF 1.4 billion of that. The Federal Council originally wanted to finance the whole pension; with Parliament’s compromise only about half is covered, as SRF and watson write. When the higher rate would take effect is given differently in the reports, 2028 or 2029; we leave that open until the text of the proposal is available. Because the value-added tax rates are set out in the Federal Constitution, the proposal needs a double majority on November 29: a majority of voters and a majority of the cantons. That makes the arithmetic harder for both camps. The people accepted the 13th pension itself. Whether they will also accept its financing is another question: receiving more money is more popular than paying more tax.

Baume-Schneider argues with the federal government’s figures. Without a Yes, the AHV’s annual deficits would rise to almost CHF 5 billion by 2035. With the increase, the deficit in 2035 would still be around CHF 3 billion. The minister calls the compromise “moderate and socially acceptable.” The Tages-Anzeiger describes the proposal as contested. The FDP rejects it and demands austerity; the party argues that the increase burdens households and small businesses. For opponents, the vote is also a signal for the major AHV reform that the Federal Council is preparing for the period up to 2040. The basic problem remains: the baby-boom generations are retiring, and revenue is growing more slowly than spending. A value-added tax hits everyone, including pensioners themselves. Payroll contributions would hit only the working population and their employers. That is the core of the dispute, and it will be fought until the end of November. Supporters point out that the increase closes only part of the gap and is therefore measured. Opponents counter that a half-financed expansion has already built in the pressure for the next increase. Both sides rely on the same federal figures; they simply draw opposite conclusions from them. Added to this is a political calendar that leaves little calm: at the end of the year the Federal Council loses a member in Guy Parmelin, and the election of his successor falls in the same weeks as the campaign.

Parmelin in India: Swiss companies cite $29 billion. Bern wants an investment protection agreement

Federal President Guy Parmelin began a three-day state visit to India on Monday and met Prime Minister Narendra Modi and President Draupadi Murmu. The $29 billion reported by Tages-Anzeiger and SRF comes from a survey by the Switzerland-India Chamber of Commerce. It adds up investments from the previous year, announced projects and plans through 2030. In the TEPA free trade agreement with India, in force since October 1, 2025, the EFTA states held out the prospect of $100 billion over 15 years; according to Parmelin, Switzerland sees itself on course. An investment protection agreement is now to follow, because in disputes between companies and the Indian state a reliable legal recourse is lacking. Agreements on migration and on the exchange of young professionals were signed. According to the NZZ, it is one of Parmelin’s last major trips: he steps down at the end of the year. Parmelin acknowledged that problems remain to be solved. One issue is intellectual property: India, often called the “world’s generics factory,” also wants to protect its own innovations, while the Swiss pharmaceutical industry insists on strong patent protection. By 2030, according to Parmelin, the EFTA investments are to create 400,000 direct and indirect jobs in India.

Markets · FrancSource ✓ SIX close (AWP), ECB reference rates · Press ✓ 3 outlets

SMI closes 0.31 percent higher. The franc stays strong, the euro falls below $1.12

The Swiss Market Index ended Monday up 0.31 percent at 13,703.53 points, as the closing reports of the AWP news agency record. Investors looked mainly at Europe’s bond markets and at high energy prices. The previous week the benchmark index had fallen sharply; on Thursday it closed at 13,622.85 points, 1.5 percent lower.

In the currency market the euro briefly fell below $1.12 for the first time since May 2025. The ECB set its reference rate at $1.1204 and CHF 0.9311; on Friday it had been $1.1225. French ten-year government bonds yielded 4.94 percent on October 1, the highest level since 2002. The German Dax closed Monday little changed, up 0.09 percent. This implies a dollar rate of about CHF 0.8310. For Swiss exporters that is a double burden: the franc is expensive against both the euro and the dollar. Traders cite distrust of France’s public finances as the reason for the euro’s weakness. The NZZ warns that hedge funds are buying US government bonds on a large scale with borrowed money, which in a crisis could act as an accelerant.

Merz in Kyiv: “Stop this senseless war.” Berlin pledges more than one billion euros

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz traveled to Kyiv by train on Sunday in a surprise visit, accompanied by Economics Minister Katherina Reiche and representatives of the defense and energy industries. According to government sources cited by the dpa agency, more than one billion euros from funds already approved are to go to military aid, plus 350 million euros for repairs to the power grid. The focus is on interceptor drones against Russian attacks. Addressing Vladimir Putin, Merz said, according to SRF: “Stop this senseless war!”

The NZZ calls the trip the right one in a commentary. In Berlin on Monday, the president of the German foreign intelligence service BND warned that Germany risks being drawn into a violent conflict with Russia. SRF reports that Russia’s attacks increasingly target the Ukrainian economy. Shortly after the chancellor’s arrival, a Russian drone hit the roadway of the Nord Bridge over the Dnipro; Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported two injured. At home, Merz drew praise from his own party and criticism from the AfD. For Switzerland, the question of how long the war lasts is above all a question of energy prices and security in Europe.

Lab worker dies in Siberia: US demands answers. A plague case is not confirmed

In Siberia, an employee of a laboratory that researches plague pathogens has died. Tages-Anzeiger, NZZ and SRF report in agreement on a suspected case of plague. The US is demanding information from Moscow. The NZZ writes of rumors about a Russian bioweapons program; the reports cite no evidence for this. SRF sets out what is known and what is not. Little is established as of Monday evening: the death, the woman’s place of work and the demand from Washington. According to the reports available, the Russian authorities have not documented what she died of.

Important for context: an outbreak is not confirmed. Plague today can be treated with antibiotics if it is caught early. The dangerous form is pneumonic plague, because it can be passed from person to person. Plague cases occur naturally in rodents in parts of Siberia and Central Asia, which is why laboratories like this one study the pathogen. A laboratory accident would be something different from a natural case, and both would be something different from weapons research. We are following whether Russia discloses samples and findings and whether the World Health Organization is brought in.
