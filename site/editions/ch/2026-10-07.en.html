The death of a plague researcher in Siberia occupies the WHO in Geneva. Moscow says little

A young scientist at a Russian anti-plague institute has died of severe pneumonia. The World Health Organization offers help and demands information. Donald Trump wants to phone Vladimir Putin.

Reports of a possible plague case in Siberia have been circulating since Monday. On Wednesday the World Health Organization (WHO), based in Geneva, stepped in. According to reports by the NZZ, Tages-Anzeiger and SRF, it is offering Russia technical support and demanding clarification. The person who died was a 28-year-old scientist who worked at a state institute for combating plague diseases in the Irkutsk region. She died in the district hospital in the town of Shelekhov. The Russian epidemic control authority says the death was not caused by a plague pathogen and names pneumonia of unknown origin as the cause. So far this cannot be independently verified. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus asked for details on the cause of the pneumonia, on the pathogen and on media reports about a second employee of the institute who is also said to have fallen ill with pneumonia of unclear cause. WHO epidemiologist Maria Van Kerkhove said the organization does not know the cause of death. A second case is not officially confirmed. Russian state media have largely avoided the subject, according to reports. Two accounts therefore stand opposed: the terse statement from the authority in Moscow and a series of questions it leaves open. Which pathogen caused the pneumonia, whether contacts are being tested and whether the institute has restricted operations is not known.

Washington, too, is demanding transparency. According to the Tages-Anzeiger and SRF, President Trump announced he would phone Kremlin chief Putin over the suspected pneumonic plague. The US had already demanded answers on Monday. The NZZ reported rumors of a Russian bioweapons program; there is no evidence for them. Early media reports suspected that the pathogen had been released accidentally from the lab. That, too, is unconfirmed. Why the case nevertheless deserves attention has to do with the pathogen itself. Plague occurs naturally in wild rodents in parts of Siberia, Central Asia and other regions of the world, and is treated with antibiotics if detected early. Pneumonic plague is the most dangerous form because it can be transmitted from person to person. A lab that researches the pathogen must therefore observe strict safety rules. For Switzerland the question is not only a medical one. The WHO has its seat in Geneva, and the International Health Regulations oblige states to report unusual disease events quickly. Whether Russia shares samples, lab findings and contact lists is the real test. By Wednesday evening no information on this was available. It is worth keeping the levels apart. A natural plague case in a researcher would be tragic, but medically explicable. A lab accident would be a safety problem affecting other labs worldwide. A link to weapons research would be a political scandal. There is as yet no evidence for any of these explanations. The World Health Organization has therefore chosen the only means left to it: asking publicly.

Schmidhalter resigns as president of the Ski World Championships organizing committee. Four months before Crans-Montana, the boss is missing

Claude-Alain Schmidhalter has stepped down as president of the organizing committee of the 2027 Alpine Ski World Championships in Crans-Montana and as vice president of Swiss-Ski. Before that, the championships' board and the Swiss-Ski presidium had provisionally relieved him of his posts, as the NZZ and SRF report. The trigger is an indictment by the Valais public prosecutor's office for fraud and unfaithful business management. The proceedings concern the 62-year-old's business activities, not his roles in sport. Schmidhalter rejects the allegations. He says he is not stepping down because he has anything to answer for, but because the championships need clarity. According to SRF, he must stand trial at the district court on October 27. Sébastien Rey and Diego Züger are leading the championships' board on an interim basis. A few months before the start, the championships are thus left without a president. The sequence of events is notable: first the provisional relief by the bodies, then the resignation, which Schmidhalter presents as his own decision. Who will take over permanently is open. For Crans-Montana it is another blow in a difficult year. The presumption of innocence applies.

SMI closes little changed at 13,808 points. Novartis and Roche support, yields and oil weigh

The Swiss Market Index ended a directionless trading day up 0.14 percent at 13,808.42 points, as “Finanz und Wirtschaft” and the AWP closing report note. The leading index was supported by the heavyweights Novartis and Roche. High bond yields and rising oil prices weighed. Elsewhere in Europe markets fell sharply: Germany's Dax lost 1.35 percent, and the Euro Stoxx 50 dropped to its lowest level since mid-June.

The bond market was the driver. The yield on ten-year US government bonds rose to 5.342 percent, according to Handelsblatt, the highest level since 2002. The European Central Bank set the euro at 1.1177 dollars, after 1.1269 on Tuesday. Toward evening the dollar cost around 0.8326 francs. That the SMI held up better than its neighboring exchanges is due to its makeup: pharma and food are considered defensive in unsettled periods. The International Energy Agency is speeding up the release of oil reserves to dampen prices. For Swiss households the oil price matters mainly for heating oil and at the pump; prices react with a delay of a few days. In the evening the US central bank published the minutes of its last meeting, which according to Handelsblatt show disagreement over the course.

Education · Switzerland · FrancePress ✓ 4 outlets

Student protests: France's prime minister promises better conditions, but no money. In Valais, the police issue threats

In France the student protests continue. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu promised the young people better learning conditions, but no additional money, the Tages-Anzeiger reports. The NZZ writes of a debate about alleged police violence. The government is under pressure because it wants to save billions next year while teachers are in short supply.

In Valais, the cantonal police have responded to calls for student protests and threatened young people with penalties, the Tages-Anzeiger reports. SRF had already reported on Tuesday about criticism of the warning. The debate turns on how far police should warn young people in advance, before anything has even happened. Whether protests will actually take place in Valais was open on Wednesday evening. Valais schools belong to the French-speaking area, and calls on social networks cross the border easily. For teachers and parents the situation is unfamiliar: Switzerland knows student strikes mainly from the climate protests of a few years ago.

Three years after October 7: remembrance in Israel, concern for Gaza

Three years after the Hamas attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, people in Israel and in Europe remembered the victims. UN Secretary-General António Guterres had already demanded the evening before that such atrocities must never happen again, as the Tages-Anzeiger reported. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz assured Israel of his country's support and affirmed the two-state solution. In Berlin, hundreds commemorated the victims. The NZZ tells the story of a woman who stayed one more night in the kibbutz and was murdered, and asks whether Prime Minister Netanyahu bears part of the blame for the failure of that day.

SRF and the Tages-Anzeiger turn their attention to Gaza. Even when no bombs are falling, the war goes on there, writes the Tages-Anzeiger. For Switzerland the conflict remains a matter of diplomacy in Geneva and of humanitarian aid.
