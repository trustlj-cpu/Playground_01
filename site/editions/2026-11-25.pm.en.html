KOSPI plunges 6.16% to close at 4,697.51… ‘Jensen Huang rally’ given back in a day, sell sidecar triggered

The index gave up in one day the 5,000 level it had won back the day before, and New York stocks also fell on the 24th, with the Nasdaq down 1.48% and the S&P 500 down 0.5%

The KOSPI closed on the 25th at 4,697.51, down 308.52 points (6.16%) from the previous session. It gave up in one day the 5,000 level it had won back the day before with a 6.32% surge. When the loss widened to the 4–5% range right after the open, a sell sidecar, which suspends the effect of program sell orders for five minutes, was triggered, and Samsung Electronics and SK hynix also started early trading down 6–7%. Japan’s Nikkei 225 also closed down 2.78% at 57,011.62 the same day. Foreign net selling and the two stocks’ closing losses are not confirmed because the figures differ from tally to tally. With the index sliding from 5,006 to 4,697, anyone holding 1 million won’s worth would have seen a little over 60,000 won disappear in a day.

It began in New York. On the 24th (local time) the Nasdaq closed down 1.48% and the S&P 500 down 0.5%, and Nvidia, which had jumped more than 30% on CEO Jensen Huang’s return, opened lower. The backdrop cited for the decline is profit-taking and reports that the United States is preparing additional tariffs, but these are only interpretations, and the cause is not confirmed. Reports emerged that the United States is preparing tariff increases on major countries including China, the EU, Japan and South Korea, but there is no official announcement and the items and rates are undecided. Huang is said to have apologized to employees for the wrongdoing the day after returning, but the wording differs from report to report. A U.S. congressional hearing is also scheduled, but the dates given conflict. Whether foreign selling continues and whether U.S. chip stocks are pushed lower still in the session on the night of the 25th are not yet known.

Politics · Judiciary✓ 2 outlets · schedule for introducing and voting on the motion unconfirmed; party reactions appear in only some reports

Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok: “Formally proposing a five-party alliance to push impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-de quickly”… People Power Party: “alliance to destroy the judiciary”

Kim Min-seok, leader of the Democratic Party of Korea, formally proposed a five-party alliance at a Supreme Council meeting on the 25th to push quickly for the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-de. The proposal is addressed to the Rebuilding Korea Party, the Progressive Party, the Basic Income Party and the Social Democratic Party, and Kim is also said to have expressed an intention to widen the alliance to civil society. The Democratic Party is said to have made pursuing impeachment party policy after the Supreme Court full bench on the 19th overturned and remanded the case of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo on grounds pointing to acquittal. There are reports that the People Power Party pushed back, calling it an “alliance to destroy the judiciary” rather than an “alliance to overcome the insurrection.” Lawmaker Han Dong-hoon said Han would give up the Assembly seat if the impeachment is upheld, and demanded that lawmakers Seo Young-kyo and Park Sun-won make the same pledge. The dates for introducing and voting on the impeachment motion are not yet fixed, and whether each party issues an official position on the alliance proposal is the next thing to watch.

Society · Traffic accident✓ 1 outlet · number of dead differs between reports and is not confirmed

Trailer overturns at Jillye Junction on Namhae Expressway, then six-vehicle pileup… reports differ on number of dead

At about 4:56 a.m. on the 25th, a trailer overturned in lane 1 at the Jillye Stream Bridge section of Jillye Junction on the Namhae Expressway toward Suncheon, in Jillye-myeon, Gimhae, South Gyeongsang Province, and five passenger cars following behind crashed into it one after another. Early breaking reports said one trailer driver was killed, two occupants of the cars were in cardiac arrest, and three were seriously or moderately injured. Some later reports said the death toll rose, so the number of dead differs between reports.

The cause of the trailer’s overturning is said to be under investigation. Reports said lanes were controlled and traffic backed up toward Suncheon during the cleanup, and an explanation was also given that, at that hour before dawn, the cars behind would have had little time to avoid it. The final numbers of dead and injured and the cause of the overturning have not yet been officially confirmed.

Economy · Outlook✓ 3 outlets · 2027 figures are the forecast of Goldman Sachs alone

Goldman Sachs: “Raising Korea’s growth forecast for next year to 3.2%”… base rate upper bound 3.5%, year-end exchange rate 1,280 won

At a briefing on the ‘2027 Korea Macroeconomic Outlook’ held in Seoul on the 24th, Goldman Sachs said Korea’s economy will grow 3.2% next year. Reports say the figure is higher than the earlier forecast. The semiconductor and artificial intelligence investment boom is lifting exports, but it was also pointed out that domestic demand, centered on private consumption, is feeling only limited warmth. It means that if exporters earn well but household spending does not rise as much, the economy ordinary households feel can differ.

The base rate was projected at an upper bound of 3.5%. The reasoning is said to be that if prices settle in the 2% range in the second half of next year, there is no reason to go above 3.5%. The won-dollar exchange rate at the end of next year was projected at 1,280 won. A lower exchange rate tends to ease import prices and the burden of overseas payments. This is one investment bank’s forecast, and the actual base rate is set by the Monetary Policy Board of the Bank of Korea. The Monetary Policy Board meets on the 26th.

Administration · Sejong relocation✓ 3 outlets · timing for the 13 on hold undecided

Relocation of 31 central government agencies and 5,460 staff to Sejong confirmed… 22 by next year, 9 by 2031, 13 including foreign affairs and defense on hold

On the 24th the Ministry of the Interior and Safety issued a final public notice of the revised central government agency relocation plan, confirming the move of 31 agencies to Sejong. Of the 44 agencies in the Seoul metropolitan area (9,929 staff) that the government designated for relocation last month, 31 (5,460 staff) that are possible under current law were reportedly confirmed first. Twenty-two (1,600 staff) are to move by next year (2027) into leased buildings rented from private owners, and nine (3,860 staff), including the Board of Audit and Inspection and the National Police Agency, by 2031 into newly built government buildings, it is said. There is also a report that the National Human Rights Commission was switched to the new-building group and so pushed back to 2031.

Thirteen agencies (4,469 staff), including the Ministries of Foreign Affairs, Unification, National Defense and Justice, were left out this time because laws must be revised, and are said to be added by later notice after the revision. Sejong Mayor Cho Sang-ho issued a statement of welcome on the 25th and said the city would step in to support the move, including securing candidate sites for leased buildings; there is also a report that the mayor promised to supply 6,000 homes a year. The city asked the government for additional medical, university and broadcasting institutions and for enactment of a special act on the administrative capital. When the 13 agencies will be designated for relocation by notice has not been decided.
