Cross-strait · MilitaryPress ✓ 5 outlets · coordinates and full times per maritime-authority notices

PLA to hold live-fire drills in 5 sea areas for 6 days from the 12th; Wellington Koo condemns “stirring up unease”; China sanctions Paraguayan and Belizean ministers the same day

The announced areas cover the East China Sea, Yellow Sea, Bohai Sea and South China Sea; the one closest to Taiwan is off Zhejiang, and none lies around Taiwan. The Ministry of National Defense says it will “assess the enemy broadly” and keep monitoring.

Several maritime safety administrations across China (the government agencies responsible for the safety of ship navigation) have announced live-fire and other drills in 5 sea areas of the East China Sea, Yellow Sea, Bohai Sea and South China Sea, running 6 days from the 12th to the 17th (Liberty Times). The Japan Times and Straits Times report that the area closest to Taiwan is off China's Zhejiang Province and that none of the announced areas lies around Taiwan. Defense Minister Wellington Koo said on the 12th that the armed forces will “assess the enemy broadly” and keep a close watch, and condemned the drills as an exercise meant to stir up unease (Liberty Times, Straits Times). The Maritime and Port Bureau (the maritime authority under the Ministry of Transportation and Communications) responded that these waters are outside Taiwan's search-and-rescue region and that it assesses no impact on waters around Taiwan (Liberty Times). Live-fire drills are the military practicing with real ammunition. The announcements were issued separately by several local administrations, so outlets' descriptions of the waters may differ slightly; during the announced period, ships and aircraft in the area must stay clear. The basis for coordinates and times is the navigational warnings (notices the maritime authority issues to ships); we have not yet found the full coordinates of the 5 areas, so we do not relay them here. Unlike the large-scale live-fire drills around Taiwan in August 2022, this announcement does not place any area around Taiwan, and there is currently no evidence that the firing targets Taiwan; we did not find the latest counts of PLA aircraft and PLA ships.

Political reactions have different emphases. Mainland Affairs Council Deputy Minister Shen Yu-chung, at a forum on the 12th, condemned the CCP's announcement of drills right after Double Ten as a “hegemonic mindset” and said China “repeatedly becomes a regional troublemaker” (Liberty Times). Chuang Jui-hsiung, caucus whip of the DPP caucus, criticized China for announcing live-fire drills right after President Lai's National Day speech as a “knee-jerk reaction” (Liberty Times). Another line is the maritime gray zone: Reuters earlier reported that the CCP's maritime militia boats appeared in waters off eastern Taiwan for the first time in August; answering legislators on the 12th, Koo said that if militia boats or coast guard vessels cross the line, the Coast Guard Administration is the main force to expel them and enforce the law, with the navy assisting under a support agreement (CNA). The same day, China's foreign ministry announced sanctions on Paraguay's Minister of Information and Communications Technology Villate and Belize's foreign minister Fonseca, saying their remarks about China harmed China's interests; our Ministry of Foreign Affairs condemned this as interference in other countries' sovereignty and a despicable tactic (CNA, PTS, Liberty Times). Scholars told PTS this is an escalation of diplomatic pressure, an attempt to intimidate through lawfare, but it is hard to enforce and the real effect may be limited (PTS). On defense, Koo also said that the military's “Tom Cruise clause” to retain veteran pilots lets pilots who have served 14 years extend their service to age 58, with 72 additional posts now added (PTS); the Executive Yuan will speed up review of the related amendments (Liberty Times). It is important to separate the two: the dates and sea areas in the announcements are fact, while intent is interpretation. Taiwanese officials link it to the National Day speech, but we found no public explanation from the Chinese side for this firing.

Politics · ElectionsPress ✓ 4 outlets · dates are each camp's claims

Taipei mayoral TV debate, first round of talks: both sides agree on cross-examination, but number of debates and first date still open; Shen's camp says Oct. 24 works, Chiang's says Oct. 31 at the earliest

TVBS and SET TV invited the camps of Puma Shen and Chiang Wan-an to a first round of talks on the 12th, which lasted more than an hour; both sides confirmed that cross-examination will be included, but the number of debates and the date of the first one were not settled, and they will talk again at a later date (CNA, Liberty Times, PTS). Shen's campaign office spokesperson Tsai Yi-kai said the sooner the better and that Oct. 24 would work; Shen had originally hoped for 4 debates. Chiang's campaign office said the hosts had explained in the talks that a televised debate needs at least 15 days of preparation and that Oct. 31 is the earliest date, and criticized Shen's side as “fleeing before the battle” (Liberty Times; this is the Liberty Times quoting Chiang's camp, and we found no public statement from the hosts). Separately, the Liberty Times and several other outlets jointly invited the candidates to a debate; Shen has signed on, and Chiang said he will “hold 1 as is customary” (CNA, Liberty Times; the Liberty Times is one of the hosts). The Nov. 28 nine-in-one elections are 46 days away.

World · Russia-UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · scope of the ceasefire is Trump's account, Russia has not confirmed

Trump's “energy ceasefire” is one day old: Kremlin rejects Ukraine's 60-day ceasefire idea, Kyiv says Kharkiv power plant was hit, EU adds nearly 1,600 sanctions

Trump announced on the 11th that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy ceasefire,” effective immediately (Reuters, NBC, Liberty Times). Ukrainian President Zelensky said at the Warsaw Security Forum on the 12th that he had learned of it from the media but is willing to abide by it if Russia cooperates (Liberty Times); in a Telegram post he said Ukraine attacked no Russian energy facilities overnight. But Reuters reports that Zelensky accused Russia of violating the ceasefire, citing an attack on a thermal power plant in Kharkiv, and asked the United States to make sure Moscow complies; AP also reports that Kyiv says Russian attacks on energy facilities are continuing. The Kyiv Post reports that Russian attacks on the 12th killed 1 person in Dnipro, injured 4 in Kyiv and injured 19 in Kharkiv. The Kremlin on the 12th rejected the 60-day ceasefire idea Zelensky proposed so that a presidential election could be held, saying what it wants is a comprehensive peace agreement (Reuters via Straits Times). The EU the same day adopted a list of nearly 1,600 new sanctions on Russia (Reuters). On oil prices, CNA reports that prices fell back on the ceasefire news, offsetting the effect of Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, and Asian stocks were mixed. Whether the Kremlin confirms the scope and duration of the “energy ceasefire,” we had not found as of press time.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · casualty figures cover different scopes in different outlets

Saudi Arabia hit again by missiles and drones after Riyadh airport attack, about 60% of flights canceled; the 10th terminal attack killed 12 and injured 309, Houthis claim responsibility

On the 10th (Saturday) a missile hit the departures terminal at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, killing 12 and injuring 309 (Straits Times, Philippine Star). PTS reports in a roundup that the Houthis attacked Saudi airports and facilities two days in a row, for a total of at least 14 dead and over 300 injured, the deadliest attack in Saudi Arabia in more than a decade and the deadliest inside the kingdom since the U.S.-Iran conflict broke out in late February. Video released by Saudi authorities shows pilgrims queuing for document checks when an explosion went off and ceiling debris fell (Liberty Times, citing AFP). On the 12th, Saudi Arabia said it faced another wave of incoming fire and Riyadh's airport remained temporarily closed; the aviation analytics firm Cirium said about 60% of scheduled flights were expected to be canceled (Straits Times). Diplomatically, Turkey, Pakistan, Egypt and Saudi Arabia issued a joint statement condemning the attacks (Liberty Times); China's foreign ministry condemned them and urged avoiding escalation (Channel NewsAsia); Iran said it was willing to mediate between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia (Straits Times); a Houthi official warned that if the U.S. intervenes the response would be “swift and painful” (Le Figaro). Malaysia advised its citizens to postpone pilgrimages to Saudi Arabia, the Philippines said it does not ban workers from going to Saudi Arabia, and 3 injured people remain in intensive care (Channel NewsAsia, Manila Times, Philippine Star).

Finance · MarketsPress ✓ 4 outlets · U.S. stocks are Friday's (10/9) close, not Monday's

Taiwan stocks edge down 0.02% on reopening after holiday, close at 49,301.96: early plunge of more than 500 points, foreign investors net sellers for a 4th day; Nanya Technology Q3 earnings per share NT$25.4

Taiwan stocks reopened on the 12th after the holiday break, dragged by weakness in the U.S. Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (PTS); the market fell as low as 48,768.82 in early trading, down more than 500 points, then narrowed its losses, turned positive after midday and closed down 11.48 points, or 0.02%, at 49,301.96, on turnover of NT$843.931 billion (CNA, Yahoo News). Strength came from container shipping stocks and financial stocks; TSMC closed down NT$10 (CNA, Yahoo Finance). Foreign investors' net selling came to NT$52.198 billion, their 4th straight day, with Formosa Plastics sold the most, nearly 37,000 lots; the three major institutional investors (foreign and mainland Chinese investors, investment trusts and proprietary traders) combined net sold NT$59.369 billion (CNA). The NT dollar closed in Taipei at 31.895 per U.S. dollar, weaker by NT$0.02, and at one point weakened by more than NT$0.10 intraday, nearing the NT$32 round-number threshold (CNA, Liberty Times); because the close is for now clearly reported only by CNA, the right ear is not shown.

Memory chipmaker Nanya Technology reported on the 12th third-quarter earnings per share of NT$25.4 and a gross margin of 85.7%, both record highs (Liberty Times, ETtoday); general manager Lee Pei-ying said DRAM is still in very short supply, that prices and gross margin should keep rising in Q4, and that it plans to build a 3D wafer packaging and testing plant at Pingtung Science Park, with wafer starts expected in the second half of 2028 (CNA). During the holiday, U.S. stocks closed on Friday (the 9th): the S&P 500 at 7,811.54, up 0.59%; the Nasdaq Composite at 27,366.17, up 0.64%; the Dow at 51,654.95, up 0.83%; West Texas Intermediate crude at US$91.85, up 0.39%; and gold at US$4,216.3, up 1.43% (DailyDrop market data). The U.S. Monday (the 12th) close came after this issue's deadline, so we have not included it. The United States has opened Section 337 investigations into Foxconn, Delta Electronics and Quanta; Delta Electronics on the 12th reported September revenue of NT$65.4 billion, up 14.6% from a year earlier, and said related products are currently unaffected (CNA).
