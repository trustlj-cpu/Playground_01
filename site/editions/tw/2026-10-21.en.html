Steel structure falls at TSMC’s new P4 site in Kaohsiung’s Nanzi; 4 subcontractor workers plunge, reports say 3 dead, Southern Taiwan Science Park Bureau orders work halted

The Southern Taiwan Science Park Bureau said a bolt apparently snapped on the afternoon of the 20th as a crane was hoisting the steel structure, and 4 workers fell from a height; TSMC said the related work has been halted on its own initiative and that it is cooperating with the investigation.

An industrial accident occurred on the afternoon of the 20th at the new P4 construction site in the F22 area of TSMC’s Kaohsiung Nanzi campus. The United Daily News and the China Times reported that a bolt apparently snapped as a crane was hoisting the steel structure, and 4 subcontractor workers who were welding fell with the structure from a height equal to the third floor; the Science Park Bureau’s preliminary investigation points the same way. The accident happened in the third-floor steel-structure work area, all of the injured are subcontractor personnel, and the Bureau said it will send officials for a labor inspection. Casualty figures differ between the papers: the United Daily News reported that the Bureau’s preliminary account that evening was 2 people in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA), 1 seriously injured and 1 slightly injured; ETtoday reported “3 dead, 1 in critical condition,” FTV Finance reported “3 dead, 1 seriously injured,” the Liberty Times’ evening headline was “causing 3 deaths,” and the United Daily News’ evening headline was “Steel beam falls, 3 dead, 1 injured.”

The Bureau has issued a stop-work order, requiring the site to stop all work and the disaster scene not to be disturbed; it will then determine under the Occupational Safety and Health Act whether the businesses and employers involved broke the law before deciding on penalties (United Daily News, China Times). TSMC told CNA and the Liberty Times that the site falls within the scope of contracted work, that the related work has been halted on its own initiative, and that it is cooperating with the relevant agencies’ investigation while urging the subcontractor to handle follow-up matters. PTS reported that 3 of the 4 fallen workers were in OHCA. The Commercial Times and the Liberty Times reported that TSMC said the cause of the accident still has to be clarified and that it will urge and assist the subcontractor and help the families with follow-up matters. The Bureau said it will keep watching the treatment of the injured and help arrange condolence visits to the families of the deceased. All the papers describe the cause as an “apparent” bolt failure; the results of the investigation have not been released.

Politics · JudiciaryPress ✓ 7 outlets · whether Wu Chun-li is a witness or a defendant, the two sides differ

Taitung farm-road graft case spreads: former county magistrate Wu Chun-li taken in at the airport on the 20th, sent to Taitung District Prosecutors Office for further questioning that evening

Former Taitung County Magistrate Wu Chun-li flew into Taitung Fengnian Airport on the morning of the 20th and was taken from the departure lounge by investigators for questioning (PTS, United Daily News, China Times, Mirror Media). Prosecutors and investigators searched his residence, his family office and the service office of his younger sister, Taitung county council speaker Wu Hsiu-hua, the same day. Reports say the case involves the county government’s farm-road widening project, accused of conferring unlawful benefit on the Dongying Resort, in which Wu Hsiu-hua’s family has invested (United Daily News, PTS); Up Media reported that Wu Hsiu-hua is a candidate for Taitung county magistrate; TITV News reported that this is an extension of the investigation into the Zhiben farm-road widening case.

Wu Hsiu-hua’s campaign headquarters said Wu Chun-li was questioned as a witness, while the Liberty Times’ headline reads “Prosecutors: listed as defendant.” The Liberty Times reported that after more than ten hours of questioning, Wu Chun-li was taken to the Taitung District Prosecutors Office at around 9 p.m. for further questioning. The Democratic Progressive Party’s candidate for Taitung county magistrate, Chen Ying, urged Wu Hsiu-hua to “give a full account,” while the Kuomintang caucus said the judiciary cannot enforce the law selectively (Liberty Times). The Liberty Times also reported that Wu Chun-li was stopped at the airport as he was about to fly north, with prosecutors and investigators gathering evidence at his residence at the same time.

First wave of draft-evasion verdicts: all 9 entertainers including Liao Jen-shuai, Ada and Tang Yu-che get suspended sentences, must pay 300,000 to 1.3 million yuan to the public treasury

The New Taipei District Court on the 20th handed down first-instance verdicts for the fourth batch of defendants who turned themselves in in the draft-evasion case. The United Daily News reported that the 9 entertainers and 5 non-celebrity defendants in the self-surrender group were each sentenced to between 6 months and 1 year 10 months in prison, and 13 people received suspended sentences; the Liberty Times’ headline was “Ada, Tang Yu-che and others, 13 people get suspended sentences.” Mirror Daily reported that all 9 entertainers were sentenced to 1 year or more, with suspended sentences of 3 to 4 years.

The Liberty Times reported that the 9 entertainers’ suspended sentences come with payments of 300,000 to 1.3 million yuan to the public treasury, and that they must also perform community service; Storm Media reported that Liao Jen-shuai was sentenced to 1 year 10 months, the heaviest of the group, and CTWANT reported that he must pay 1.3 million yuan to the public treasury; Next Apple News reported that all 9 entertainers received light suspended sentences after pleading guilty; the Liberty Times also reported that Tang Chen-kang received a suspended sentence, will not go to prison, and responded publicly. The papers record individual defendants’ service hours slightly differently, and this is a first-instance verdict that can still be appealed.

Kawaguchiko hotel killing in Japan: 38-year-old Taiwanese woman tourist dies of blood loss from neck stab wound, 27-year-old Taiwanese roommate arrested

A 38-year-old Taiwanese woman on a package tour was found dead in her room at a hotel in Fujikawaguchiko, Yamanashi Prefecture, Japan, on the 19th (Liberty Times, United Daily News). The Liberty Times and CNA reported that Japanese police on the 20th released the forensic autopsy results, confirming that the victim died of excessive blood loss, with at least one deep stab wound to the neck.

The Liberty Times reported that Japanese media have published the identity of the arrested woman. FTV News and the United Daily News reported that the 27-year-old Taiwanese roommate was arrested by Japanese police with a knife in hand; SET News reported that she has reportedly admitted involvement. CNA and the Liberty Times reported that the victim and the arrested woman had each signed up separately for the same tour group and were not traveling together. EBC News reported that Japanese police are still investigating the relationship between the two and what happened.
