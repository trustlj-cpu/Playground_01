In a rare intraday statement, the central bank says the New Taiwan dollar fell 1.37% last week and foreign investors net remitted about US$18 billion in 5 days; it closed at 32.445 yuan on the 19th

The central bank said on the 19th that the New Taiwan dollar’s decline mainly reflects turmoil in global stock markets and foreign investors selling shares and remitting the proceeds out; the currency touched a low of 32.572 yuan that day and closed at 32.445 yuan after central-bank intervention late in the session.

On the 19th the Central Bank issued a press release titled “Explanation of the Recent Depreciation of the New Taiwan Dollar”; the Liberty Times called it a rare statement from the bank issued during trading hours. The Liberty Times reported that the New Taiwan dollar slid last week from 31.895 to 32.338 per U.S. dollar, a decline of 1.37%, which the central bank said mainly reflects turmoil in global stock markets and foreign investors’ net selling of Taiwan shares, with funds remitted out together with dividends received; the bank’s tally shows foreign investors net remitted about US$18 billion in 5 days. Focus Taiwan reported that the central bank said foreign investors net sold US$19.402 billion of Taiwan shares from Oct. 12 to 16, that Taiwan shares fell 5.4% from Oct. 7 to 16, and that the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 15.7% over the same period. CNA reported that the central bank also said the New Taiwan dollar has long been more stable against the U.S. dollar than other major currencies, and that the bank will step in as appropriate depending on foreign-exchange supply and demand.

The New Taiwan dollar kept sliding on the 19th. CNA reported that it broke through the 32.5 mark intraday, touching a low of 32.572 yuan, an 18-month low, and closed at 32.445 yuan after central-bank intervention late in the session, a heavy drop of 1.07 jiao (0.107 yuan); total trading value on the Taipei and Yuantai foreign-exchange markets was US$3.853 billion. The Taipei Times reported that the currency lost 0.33% that day. The Liberty Times reported that the New Taiwan dollar logged seven straight losses, and that even with the bank’s remarks during trading it could not stop the pressure from foreign investors’ continued remittances. CNA had earlier reported that the currency fell a heavy 4.63 jiao (0.463 yuan) last week, its biggest weekly drop in more than a year. Taiwan shares opened low and kept sliding on the 19th, falling as far as 46,170 points intraday, and the New Taiwan dollar weakened in step (CNA, PTS).

Taiwan shares close the 19th at 46,822.08, down 289.18 points, after plunging more than 940 points intraday

Taiwan shares opened low and slid on the 19th. CNA reported that, with the market questioning whether artificial intelligence (AI) investment takes too long to pay back, the TAIEX at one point fell more than 940 points intraday, to 46,170; the OTC index at one point lost more than 3.5%, and electronics shares and the semiconductor index both fell more than 1.5%. The Liberty Times and PTS reported that the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 3.83% last Friday, its eighth straight decline, and Asian shares followed lower on Monday, with South Korean shares opening down 3.16% and below 5,500 points and Japan’s Nikkei opening down 1,493 points and below 64,000. The Liberty Times, under the headline “The battle to defend 46,000,” reported that TSMC led the declines at the open. Focus Taiwan reported that the TAIEX ended at 46,822.08, down 289.18 points, or 0.61%, with an intraday range between 46,170.00 and 46,844.64 and trading value of 825.10 billion yuan; buying of dips in the afternoon narrowed the loss, TSMC closed flat at 2,420 yuan, and foreign investors were net sellers of 76.74 billion yuan. CNA and the Liberty Times reported the same day that the New Taiwan dollar weakened in step and that the central bank issued a statement during trading.

Judiciary · PoliticsPress ✓ 6 outlets · effect on the 17-year first-instance sentence and timing of the appeal ruling unconfirmed

Ko Wen-je appeal trial: Lee Wen-tsung admits breach of trust involving about 2.1 million yuan, Lee Wen-chuan pleads guilty to all charges, Ko Wen-je does not plead guilty

Former Taipei Mayor Ko Wen-je was sentenced to 17 years in prison at first instance in the Core Pacific City and political donations cases and appealed; on the 19th the High Court summoned Lee Wen-tsung, former chairman of the Zhongwang Foundation, and Lee Wen-chuan, former head of Muke Public Relations (Liberty Times). Lee Wen-tsung admitted the breach-of-trust charge, acknowledging that he used about 2.1 million yuan of Zhongwang Foundation funds to pay the salaries of 4 Taiwan People’s Party staffers (Business Today, Up Media); he still denies the charge of public-interest embezzlement, and personally explained why Muke’s profit-and-loss statement was “shredded” (Liberty Times). The Liberty Times also reported that the case involves about 8 million yuan in total paid by the Zhongwang Foundation as salaries for Taiwan People’s Party employees, and that Lee Wen-tsung repeatedly sighed that he had “no choice” as he admitted breach of trust. CNA reported that Ko Wen-je still does not plead guilty on appeal.

Lee Wen-chuan’s attitude differed from the previous hearing. United Daily News reported that on appeal Lee Wen-chuan said she pleads guilty to everything and admitted embezzling 62.34 million yuan in political donations; Mirror News reported that she admitted a link to Ko Wen-je’s political donations. Under the headline “Following in brother Lee Wen-tsung’s footsteps,” the Liberty Times reported that she also asked the judges to allow her to travel abroad to take her 90-year-old mother on a cruise; the judge said the panel would rule after deliberation.

National security · JudiciaryPress ✓ 5 outlets · content of the leaked secrets and accounts of the defendants’ military status differ

High Prosecutors Office indicts retired captain Yu Cheng-yu and 3 others, saying he received and sent virtual currency for Chinese parties to pass on payoffs for leaking secrets

On the 19th the High Prosecutors Office indicted retired captain Yu Cheng-yu, defendants surnamed Lee and Sen, and a coast guard member surnamed Tsai, 4 people in all, under the National Security Act and other charges, and asked the court to impose heavier sentences (Liberty Times, ETtoday). The Liberty Times reported that prosecutors say Yu received virtual currency for people from China, converted it to cash and passed it on as payments for leaking secrets to the service members surnamed Lee and Sen, making a profit of more than 430,000 yuan himself; the Taipei Times reported that Yu had earlier been indicted in a separate leak case, and that this one concerns his role as a financial go-between.

The Liberty Times reported what the indictment says: from March to October 2024, Yu used virtual currency wallets in his name to receive virtual currency paid by Chinese parties, then sold it to a coin dealer for cash and, as instructed, deposited it into the accounts of the defendants surnamed Lee and Tsai, profiting 430,403 yuan; the defendant surnamed Lee received 884,000 yuan, and the defendants surnamed Sen and Tsai together 515,000 yuan; Tsai, the coast guard member, is suspected of providing a bank account to the defendant surnamed Sen to help receive the payments. The report says the defendants surnamed Lee and Sen were detained on application and that Tsai and Yu were released on bail of 100,000 yuan and 50,000 yuan respectively pending summons. The papers differ in describing the defendants’ military status; the headlines of the United Daily News and the China Times both mention taking money for leaking military secrets.

Weather · TyphoonPress ✓ 5 outlets · landfall or northward-turn track and timing of warnings undetermined

Jangmi’s track shifts east again; Weather Administration expects it to come closest to Taiwan Friday and Saturday, with heavy rain in the north and Yilan

Typhoon Jangmi kept strengthening on the 19th and the Weather Administration updated its forecast. The Liberty Times reported that the latest track has again shifted east, with the center passing at some distance from Taiwan and the storm circle brushing land; weather expert Chia Hsin-hsing, after analyzing a thousand tracks, estimated the chance of landfall at about 10%. PTS reported that the Weather Administration expects the storm to be closest to Taiwan from Friday (the 23rd) to Saturday (the 24th), and that the path after its northward turn is still uncertain. The Liberty Times also reported that Jangmi moves on Thursday (the 22nd) to waters south of Japan’s Ryukyus, with its track affected by the strength of the Pacific high. Focus Taiwan reported that the Weather Administration expects the typhoon to turn north early, at around Japan’s Ishigaki Island, and to weaken and move away on Sunday (the 25th).

Warning times and the track still diverge. The Liberty Times reported that a sea typhoon warning could be issued as early as late Wednesday night; PTS reported that the sea warning could come as early as early Thursday morning to the morning, and a land typhoon warning could be issued between Thursday night and early Friday morning. PTS also reported that from Wednesday the northeast monsoon strengthens, with brief rain north of Hsinchu and in Yilan, and that on Friday and Saturday the north, the northeast and central mountain areas should watch for heavy or torrential rain. The Liberty Times reported that the tracks from weather agencies in various countries fall into three camps: landfall in Taiwan, brushing the northeast corner, or heading north off the eastern coast; PTS reported that European and U.S. models show a possibility of the center making landfall on Taiwan’s east. The United Daily News reported that the storm-circle strike probability for Keelung and Yilan is 51%, with 4 other counties and cities above 40%.
