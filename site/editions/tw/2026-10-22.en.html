Strong Typhoon Champi closes in, sea warning issued, land warning as early as around noon today

The Weather Administration expects Champi to come closest to Taiwan on the 23rd, with Yilan, Hualien and the north on alert and about a 60% chance the typhoon strikes northern Taiwan.

The Central Weather Administration issued a sea typhoon warning for Strong Typhoon Champi at 11:30 p.m. on the 21st, Liberty Times reported. The first wave covers the waters off northeastern Taiwan, the waters off southeastern Taiwan (including Orchid Island and Green Island) and the Bashi Channel. The Weather Administration said Champi is expected to come closest to Taiwan on the 23rd and that, if the track holds, a land typhoon warning could come as early as around noon on the 22nd; other reports say the land warning is expected in the afternoon. Liberty Times also reported that the Weather Administration cautioned that Taiwan’s land area is still within Champi’s track “margin of error,” that the typhoon may strengthen and its storm circle widen as it nears Taiwan, and that Friday is when it comes closest. The Weather Administration also noted that Champi’s track has been revised slightly westward from earlier forecasts, so Yilan, Hualien and the north must raise their guard, and the chance of the typhoon striking northern Taiwan remains 60%.

On work and school closures, Liberty Times reported that the Weather Administration assessed that wind and rain in 17 counties and cities meet the threshold for closures. Taipei Times reported that the Weather Administration warned that Friday and Saturday are the two days of strongest impact; although Champi may weaken, officials still warned of the risk of big waves. The Taiwan Typhoon Forum said a force-7 storm circle could cover all of Taiwan and set out when the wind and rain will be fiercest, Liberty Times reported. Because of the typhoon, the ballot-number draw for Hsinchu County candidates in the nine-in-one elections will be moved to 10/27 if work and school closures are declared; the Taiwan Series also announced a revised schedule that depends on whether Taipei City closes work and schools; and the Taitung District Agricultural Research and Extension Station offered farmers ways to protect crops against the typhoon. Whether the land warning is issued and how the alert area is adjusted must wait for the Weather Administration’s latest announcement around noon, and is not yet confirmed.

Huang Yi-hao said to be targeted by Chinese-hired fake car crash plot; 5 indicted; Akio Yaita urges law change

Crosstalk comedian Huang Yi-hao often mocks the Chinese Communist Party and Xi Jinping in his acts, and was earlier intimidated by people acting on Chinese instructions who threw paint and excrement across the border. Liberty Times reported that on the 21st it emerged that the Chinese side had also hired thugs to stage a fake car crash meant to leave Huang “with broken arms and legs,” setting off heated public debate; Liberty Entertainment reported that pro-Taiwan influencer Ba Jiong also said he had been threatened and revealed inside details. Liberty Times reported that Akio Yaita, who has likewise suffered transnational repression, urged Blue-White lawmakers to amend the law and close the loopholes. Taipei Times reported that 5 people have been indicted over this China-linked attack case; Huang thanked prosecutors and police for the investigation and called on Kuomintang legislative caucus whip Fu Kun-chi to support the related legislation. The reports do not say how far the “broken arms and legs” claim has been verified, nor whether the 5 indicted are part of the same case as that claim.

SpaceX earnings lift AI confidence; U.S. stocks close higher, S&P 500 up 0.58%

CNA reported that SpaceX, the space-exploration company under Elon Musk, released results after the U.S. market closed on the 20th: revenue for the quarter ended Sept. 30 beat expectations and more than doubled from a year earlier, driven by surging artificial intelligence (AI) and Starlink satellite businesses. A CNA roundup of wire reports from London on the 21st said SpaceX posted its first quarterly profit since going public, and major European stock markets closed higher on the 21st.

CNA also reported that Super Micro Computer released quarterly results as well, which together with SpaceX lifted investors’ confidence in AI-related stocks, and the main U.S. indexes closed higher on the 21st; market data show the S&P 500 closed at 7,757.17 on the 21st, up 0.58%. On the previous day the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed up more than 500 points, as money rotated into industrial stocks and international oil prices fell back. Bloomberg reported that SpaceX and Tesla are discussing a potential merger, which Musk supports.

Trump says end of Russia–Ukraine war may be closest yet; Putin agrees to resume trilateral negotiations next month

U.S. President Donald Trump told reporters at the White House on the 22nd that he believes the end of the Russia–Ukraine war may be closer than at any point so far, and thanked Russian President Vladimir Putin for releasing a detained American citizen, AFP reported. U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff wrote on social media that his talks with Putin were “constructive,” but that “important work” remains to be done.

A Kremlin official said Putin agreed on the 21st to resume “trilateral negotiations” with Washington and Kyiv next month, which could lead to the first such talks since February. The Kremlin also said a three-way meeting among Putin, Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping is “being prepared,” and that Putin raised it when he met Witkoff, AFP and Bloomberg reported; Liberty Times ran the story under the headline “November U.S.–Russia–China three-way summit takes shape.” Reuters reported that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky wants a new round of talks with Russia and the United States in early November.

PHP hit by two critical flaws, both scored 9.2; cyber agency urges agencies and companies to patch quickly

The Administration for Cyber Security under the Ministry of Digital Affairs said on the 22nd that the security team behind PHP, an open-source server-side scripting language, has released an emergency security update fixing two critical flaws, each with a severity score of 9.2, which could let an attacker execute arbitrary code from outside without authentication, CNA reported. The Administration urged agencies and companies to finish patching as quickly as possible.

Liberty Times reported that after the PHP flaws were disclosed, website operators rushed to protect themselves, and government websites in Europe and elsewhere went offline on the 22nd to guard against hacking; by one count, about 75% of websites whose programming language can be identified use PHP. The report says the affected range covers a wide swath of sites built with PHP, and the reports do not mention whether any Taiwanese websites have been breached.
