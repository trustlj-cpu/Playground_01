Weather · TyphoonPress ✓ 5 outlets · whether the typhoon’s center makes landfall is undecided; reports differ on the timing of work and school closures in Taitung County proper and Kinmen County

Champi land warning widened to 19 counties and cities, Taiwan Railway halts all reserved-seat trains on the 23rd, many counties and cities declare typhoon days off

PTS reports Champi has weakened to a moderate typhoon, and the Central Weather Administration widened the land typhoon warning area to 19 counties and cities this evening; Liberty Times reports Taiwan Railway has announced that all reserved-seat trains will stop on the 23rd, while High Speed Rail runs normally on all lines.

PTS reported that Typhoon Champi weakened to a moderate typhoon on the evening of the 22nd, and that the Central Weather Administration widened the land typhoon warning area to 19 counties and cities on Taiwan proper. The storm circle is expected to cover all of Taiwan on the morning of the 23rd, and the force-10 wind radius may sweep the northeastern corner of Yilan; the typhoon’s center is expected to come closest to Taiwan from late on the night of the 23rd to the early morning of the 24th, the Administration does not rule out landfall, and the northern mountains and Yilan must guard against extremely heavy rain or worse on the 23rd. Liberty Times reported that at 11 p.m. on the 22nd Champi’s maximum wind speed near the center fell from 45 to 43 meters per second, that rain will be heaviest from the 23rd to the early morning of the 24th, and that overall rain will not ease until the 25th. Focus Taiwan reported that, in an earlier list, Taiwan proper except Taitung County, plus Penghu and Matsu, had already announced work and school closures for the 23rd, while in Taitung County only Green Island and Orchid Island closed.

Taipei Times reported on the 23rd that as of 9 p.m. on the 22nd, every county and city except Kinmen County had announced work and school closures for the 23rd. Liberty Times reported that Taiwan Railway announced on the evening of the 22nd that all reserved-seat trains would stop on the 23rd; among non-reserved trains, the Yilan Line, North-Link Line and Coastal Line would stop, and the other lines would run flexibly depending on wind and rain, while High Speed Rail would run normally on all lines. Election work was also affected: CNA reported that the Central Election Commission said the ballot-number draw for candidates in the nine-in-one elections, originally set for the 23rd at county and city election commissions, would be moved to the 27th if a local government declares a work stoppage because of the typhoon; Liberty Times reported that the election commissions of Taichung, Tainan, Miaoli, Yunlin, Changhua, Hsinchu City and Chiayi City had already announced that the draw would be moved to October 27.

Finance · Stocks and currencyPress ✓ 5 outlets · when foreign selling pressure will ease and how the next trading day will open are unconfirmed

TAIEX closes at 46,041.43 on the 22nd, down a sharp 846.34 points; market closed for 1 day on the 23rd because of the typhoon

CNA reported that the TAIEX swung by more than 1,000 points on the 22nd and closed down 846.34 points at 46,041.43, with financial stocks holding up the market; TSMC fell 35 yuan, and the index barely held the 46,000 mark. ETtoday Finance Cloud, under the headline “TAIEX swings 1,290 points, barely holds 46K,” reported that TSMC closed at 2,385 yuan and that the index at one point rose more than 444 points to top 47,300 before turning to a sell-off late in the session. CNA also reported that foreign investors again engaged in net selling of Taiwan stocks of more than 80 billion yuan, and the New Taiwan dollar weakened by 2 fen to close at 32.508 yuan. The typhoon also affected market operations. CNA and Liberty Times reported a one-day market closure for the Taipei stock market on the 23rd; Liberty Times’ headline read “Taipei City declares typhoon day off; stock market closed for a day tomorrow.” ETtoday Finance Cloud reported that both the stock and foreign-exchange markets would be closed on the 23rd. Bloomberg reported that this market, worth about $5 trillion, would close on Friday as Champi approaches; Taisounds reported that settlement of cash and shares on the closure day would be postponed.

Finance · U.S. stocksPress ✓ 5 outlets · reports differ on the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index’s closing decline; whether AI spending doubts will continue is unconfirmed

On the 22nd the Nasdaq Composite plunges 2.6%, its worst single day since June, and Meta shares fall about 7%

Liberty Times reported that most major U.S. indexes closed lower on the 22nd; the technology-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6% at the close, its worst single-day performance since June, mainly because doubts about artificial intelligence (AI) company spending resurfaced while international oil prices jumped on rising geopolitical tension. In its early-session report, Liberty Times said the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index at one point plunged more than 5%. Bloomberg TV reported under the headline “Meta Slides 7% as AI Spending Fuels Credit Fears”; Reuters reported that Meta’s credit risk gauge hit a record high after its earnings, and covered this selling under the headline “Steep bill for AI buildout punishes Meta, other tech favorites.”

CNBC reported that the S&P 500 closed at 7,663.43, down 1.21%, with Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) down more than 8% and Nvidia down about 4%. CNA reported that major European markets also closed lower on the 22nd, mainly because of mixed corporate earnings, higher oil prices and rising French government bond yields; London’s FTSE 100 slipped 27.85 points, or 0.26%, to close at 10,725.03.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 5 outlets · actual supply losses from the attacks and the Saudi-led coalition’s next response are unconfirmed

Houthis step up attacks on Saudi Arabia, Brent crude tops $110, U.S. orders some diplomats to leave

CNA and Liberty Times reported that international oil prices jumped on the 22nd, with Brent crude reaching a five-month high, mainly because Saudi Arabia was attacked by Yemen’s rebel “Youth Movement” (Houthi), raising market fears that supply from this key energy exporter could be disrupted further. Bloomberg TV reported that the United States ordered some diplomats to leave Saudi Arabia and that Brent briefly topped $110; the network also reported that Saudi Aramco evacuated its headquarters and that Saudi Arabia is on alert for possible further attacks.

Al Jazeera reported that Saudi Arabia’s Civil Aviation Authority said King Fahd International Airport in Dammam was attacked on the evening of the 21st, killing 2 people and wounding 8, and that airport operations have resumed; the Houthis claimed to have launched multiple attacks within 48 hours, including on the Ministry of Defense in Riyadh and Aramco facilities in Yanbu. PTS reported that the fighting continues to spill over: the United States has ordered the evacuation of some diplomats and family members stationed in Saudi Arabia, and Britain announced it would send additional Royal Air Force “Typhoon” fighters for defensive air patrols to help intercept missiles and drones.

World · Public healthPress ✓ 5 outlets · the route of infection and the virus’s actual role in the death are still under investigation

France reports world’s first human infection with Shamonda virus, patient has died, World Health Organization says wider spread unlikely

CNA reported that France’s health and agriculture ministries said on the 22nd that the country has seen the world’s first human case of Shamonda virus infection, and that the patient died earlier this month; complications of the nervous system that the patient developed before death are very likely linked to the virus, and the investigation is continuing. CNA noted that the patient’s immune function was severely impaired, and that the patient died at a hospital in Lyon in early October. Liberty Times reported that the virus had previously circulated only among livestock, and French authorities stressed that there is no risk of infection for the general public at this stage. BBC and The Guardian both reported under the headline “France detects Shamonda virus in a human for the first time.”

CNA reported that authorities suspect the patient was bitten by a virus-carrying midge in eastern France; the virus is spread mainly by biting midges of the genus Culicoides and usually infects livestock such as cattle. The World Health Organization said this is an isolated case and unlikely to lead to further spread of the virus (reported by Reuters).
