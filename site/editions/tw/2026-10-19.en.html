Weather · TyphoonPress ✓ 7 outlets · northward turn or westward track after the 22nd undetermined

Jangmi, a severe typhoon, forecast to peak in intensity today; Central Weather Administration says a sea warning could come as early as Wednesday night

Multiple outlets report that Jangmi is forecast to reach peak intensity on the 19th, that the Weather Administration says a sea typhoon warning could be issued as early as the evening of the 21st, and that after the 22nd both a northward turn and a more westerly track remain possible.

Typhoon Jangmi was already being called a severe typhoon on the 18th in reports by the United Daily News, China Times, ETtoday, Liberty Times and SET News. CNA reported on the 16th that the Weather Administration had said Jangmi was still a tropical storm, expected to strengthen into a typhoon within 24 hours, with its track still uncertain; it was then over waters east of Guam. Focus Taiwan reported on the 17th that Jangmi was about 2,980 km east-southeast of Taiwan’s southern tip, moving west at 22 km/h, and that the Weather Administration said Jangmi could approach or cross Taiwan next week. The Liberty Times and SET News, citing forecasters, said Jangmi was forecast to reach its first intensity peak on the 19th (today); SET News reported the forecast under the headline “high chance of hitting Taiwan.” The United Daily News, China Times and ETtoday reported that the Weather Administration said a sea typhoon warning could be issued as early as the evening of the 21st (Wednesday), and earlier if the typhoon speeds up; ETtoday had already reported this statement on the 17th under the headline “Typhoon Jangmi accelerates,” while the China Times also reported the rain hot spots published by the Weather Administration; the Taipei Times, the Liberty Times’ English edition, ran the same statement on the 19th. The United Daily News also reported that the most noticeable impact would come on Friday and Saturday, the 23rd and 24th, with rain across Taiwan and localized heavy to torrential rain in the north, Yilan and Hualien.

The track is where the reports diverge most. Focus Taiwan, citing a Weather Administration forecaster, said Jangmi was expected to reach waters south of the Ryukyus around the 21st and to lie east of Taiwan around the 22nd (Thursday), after which it may gradually turn north. The Weather Administration explained that the track depends on the Pacific high: if the high weakens markedly, the typhoon will make its northward turn earlier; if it strengthens, the typhoon will turn closer to Taiwan and could even cross it. The Liberty Times reported two possibilities at present: a northward turn off the east coast, or a more westerly pass over Taiwan, even landfall. Two other reports separately cited weather experts, one saying the chance of a northward turn is low, the other that forecasts are gradually leaning toward landfall. CNA also reported that on the weekend of the 24th and 25th, the northeast monsoon or the typhoon’s outer circulation could bring more rain to the north and east. The Philippines’ Philstar reported that Jangmi reached super typhoon strength when it entered the Philippine area of responsibility and was expected to weaken gradually afterward. As of the Taipei Times report published on the 19th, the Weather Administration had not yet issued a sea or land typhoon warning.

Society · Health insurancePress ✓ 3 outlets · implementing rules, start date and funding unclear

Senior Citizens Welfare Act amendment passes third reading; central government to pay full health insurance premiums for eligible people 65 and over

On the 16th the Legislative Yuan passed on third reading an amendment to Article 22 of the Senior Citizens Welfare Act: for people aged 65 and over, or the person legally obliged to support them, whose combined comprehensive income over the most recent year does not reach the filing threshold, or whose comprehensive income tax rate is below 20%, health insurance premiums will instead be fully subsidized by the central competent authority. Min Pao, the Liberty Times and SET News reported that the vote was 60 to 39, that the amendment was led by the Taiwan People’s Party caucus, and that the Kuomintang and the People’s Party worked together to push it; a motion for reconsideration put forward afterward failed, with 39 in favor and 60 against. The subsidy’s recipients, items and criteria are to be set separately by the central competent authority. Minister of Health and Welfare Shih Chung-liang voiced opposition, saying the core of national health insurance is ability to pay and mutual help, and that the subsidy would still have to be borne by other groups or by tax revenue; he estimated that if it fell on people aged 15 to 64, the average per person would rise by more than 3,000 yuan a year, and if it were funded from tax revenue, the average per household would rise by about 8,000 yuan a year (Min Pao, Liberty Times). Seven medical groups including the Taiwan Hospital Association issued a joint statement opposing it, saying there had been no financial projection or impact assessment. Premier Cho Jung-tai criticized Legislative Yuan Speaker Han Kuo-yu for letting opposition lawmakers take the lead in an unconstitutional move; the Executive Yuan said it would take countermeasures that are constitutional.

Politics · ElectionPress ✓ 5 outlets · campaign staff list and duties not yet announced

Lu Shiow-yen confirms she will chair Chiang Wan-an’s campaign headquarters; team to announce key staff at 2 p.m. today

Taichung Mayor Lu Shiow-yen (盧秀燕), speaking to reporters on the 18th in Hsinchu City while attending the opening of Kao Hung-an’s (高虹安) campaign headquarters, confirmed that three days earlier she had agreed to serve as chair of the campaign headquarters of Taipei Mayor Chiang Wan-an (蔣萬安). The United Daily News reported that Chiang Wan-an personally confirmed the arrangement the same day. ETtoday and the United Daily News reported that Chiang Wan-an’s campaign headquarters would hold a press conference at 2 p.m. on the 19th (today) to announce that Lu Shiow-yen would serve as chair and to unveil the campaign team’s key staff list. World Journal reported the appointment under the headline “Drawing on her appeal to centrist voters.”

Lu Shiow-yen had already served since Aug. 1 as chair of the campaign headquarters of Chiang Chi-chen (江啟臣), the Kuomintang’s candidate for Taichung mayor; CNA reported that she said at the time Chiang Chi-chen was the best person to succeed her. Chiang Wan-an is seeking re-election, and his opponent is Democratic Progressive Party candidate Puma Shen (沈伯洋). ETtoday’s headline framed the 19th press conference as Lu Shiow-yen and Chiang Wan-an joining forces against the “ocean current”; CNA reported that since Oct. 11 Shen’s camp has been holding a vehicle sweep called “Taipei Grand Ocean Current.”

World · EuropePress ✓ 3 outlets · cause of the violence and casualty details unclear

Anti-high-prices march in France turns violent; Paris police: 855 arrested in Greater Paris

French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said that Saturday the 17th’s protests against high prices turned into violent clashes in many places; the AP reported about 550,000 people took part nationwide. Reports differ on the number arrested nationwide: the AP reported at least 1,800, and Euronews reported that Nuñez said more than 2,000; Paris police said 855 were arrested in the Greater Paris area (ABC News, relaying the AP and Euronews). On injuries, the AP reported Nuñez said more than 500 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters were hurt, while Euronews reported more than 600, the latter a provisional figure.

Nuñez wrote on X that protest is a basic right, but deliberately and collectively using violence to create disorder is not. Euronews reported that shops were looted in Paris, Marseille, Bordeaux, Nice and Strasbourg, and that Limoges’ city hall was set on fire; Nuñez attributed the violence to thousands of far-left activists.

Finance · EnergyPress ✓ 4 outlets · Legislative Yuan review outcome and electricity rate changes undecided

Executive Yuan’s 180.9 billion yuan supplementary budget awaits review; Ministry of Economic Affairs, citing the Middle East conflict, urges support to steady energy prices

The Executive Yuan on Oct. 1 approved a supplementary budget totaling 180.935 billion yuan, including a subsidy top-up of 101.431 billion yuan for CPC and 71.104 billion yuan for Taipower, plus 8.4 billion yuan for related oil companies; TTV News and Newtalk reported that Premier Cho Jung-tai urged the Legislative Yuan to pass it on third reading by the end of November. The Liberty Times and United Daily News reported that the Ministry of Economic Affairs said international oil and natural gas prices are far above the estimates used when the Year 115 (Republic of China calendar) budget was drawn up last August, and that without a supplementary budget CPC and Taipower would struggle to keep playing their role in stabilizing prices.

On the 18th the Ministry of Economic Affairs again urged all sides to back the supplementary budget, saying that besides arranging oil and gas shipping schedules and fuel dispatch, CPC and Taipower also serve as the front-line “breakwater” against price increases (Liberty Times, United Daily News). The ministry said that compared with before the war, Brent crude has risen 66%, while Taiwan’s gasoline prices are up 14% and diesel 12%, and Japan and South Korea have also taken subsidy measures; Taipower decided at both its April and October rate reviews this year not to raise electricity rates for now, and residential natural gas and bottled gas prices are frozen through year-end, with electricity rates to be adjusted or not depending on the Legislative Yuan’s review of the supplementary budget.
