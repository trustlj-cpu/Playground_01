Watercare takes $6 million from nearly 44,000 accounts, 18 days early, in a billing-system error

The Auckland water provider apologised on Wednesday and blamed an upgrade to its billing system. Consumer NZ says affected customers should ask for a refund and claim back any bank fees the early payment caused.

Watercare, which supplies Auckland’s drinking water and treats its wastewater, apologised on Wednesday 14 October after payments were taken early from the bank accounts of 43,835 customers, according to the New Zealand Herald, RNZ, 1News and Stuff. Stuff put the total at $6.14 million; the other three rounded it to $6 million. 1News reported that the money left the accounts 18 days before it was due. RNZ and the Herald both put the error down to an upgrade of Watercare’s billing system, which brought payments forward. Watercare told the Herald and 1News that it is “deeply sorry”.

For Watercare the sum is modest. For the households involved, it is the timing that bites. Spread across the accounts it comes to about $140 each on average, which is DailyDrop’s arithmetic rather than a figure any outlet gave, and individual amounts will differ with each customer’s bill. A debit that lands more than two weeks early can leave an account short on days when other bills are due, and an overdrawn account usually costs money. That is why Stuff, quoting Consumer NZ, reported that affected customers should seek refunds and reimbursement for any bank fees or other losses caused by the error. The coverage checked does not say how or when Watercare will return the money, whether it has already done so, or whether it will cover those fees itself. Those are the three questions to put to the utility, and they are the ones that decide whether an apology is enough.

Nation · Public servicePress ✓ 4 outlets · vacancy split from one outlet

New ministry MCERT, created in July, proposes cutting 400 roles in its first restructure

MCERT, the ministry formed in July, met staff on Wednesday 14 October and put forward a restructure that would remove 400 roles, RNZ, 1News, Stuff and the New Zealand Herald reported. RNZ and 1News describe it as a new mega-ministry, and Stuff’s headline calls them public service cuts. The Herald adds a detail that changes how the number reads: of the 400 roles proposed to go, 365 are vacant. That figure comes from one outlet, and if it holds, about 35 filled positions are affected, which is DailyDrop’s arithmetic. A vacant role is a post that exists on paper but has no one in it, so cutting it saves money without making anyone redundant, though it can still mean work goes undone. A proposal is also not a decision. Restructures of this kind are normally put to staff for comment before the final plan is confirmed, but the coverage checked does not set out MCERT’s timetable, and DailyDrop has not seen the ministry’s full name, which services the cuts would touch, or any reaction from the parties in an election campaign.

New poll has National and Labour level, with Opportunity still holding the balance of power

A new 1News-Verian poll found the two big parties tied, the New Zealand Herald reported, and 1News’s own headline said Chris Hipkins and Christopher Luxon are now neck and neck. RNZ’s headline said Opportunity is still in the kingmaker seat, and 1News said the party is on track for 10 MPs. Stuff put its support at 8% and reported Luxon dismissing it again, saying of the party “no idea what they stand for”. DailyDrop has not confirmed the party-vote percentages for National and Labour from two outlets, so it does not print them. What the headlines agree on is the arithmetic: with the two largest parties level, a party of about 8% decides who can form a government.

Elsewhere in the campaign, the Herald reported that Opportunity came under fire at the leaders’ debate, where its Qiulae Wong and the Greens’ Chlöe Swarbrick clashed, and the Waikato Times reported Wong saying she would call the party with the most seats first, even if it won fewer votes. Labour also published its economic plan. The Herald says it includes an Elevate fund drawing up to $50m from the Super Fund; RNZ describes the pledge as up to $50m a year for a venture capital fund. Those two readings differ, and DailyDrop has not seen the plan itself. The Herald’s Richard Prebble argued in a column that neither National nor Labour has said what it would cut.

World · VaticanPress ✓ 9 outlets · prognosis not stated

Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer, the Vatican says, and is recovering after surgery

The Vatican announced on Tuesday 13 October, Rome time, that Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer, after surgery to remove part of his right lung, AP, Reuters, the BBC, 1News and Stuff reported. The 71-year-old Pope had gone into Rome’s Gemelli hospital that day for an operation on a lung nodule, Reuters said, and the BBC described a procedure lasting about 90 minutes. NBC News and the Livemint site report that the diagnosis is adenocarcinoma, a form of non-small cell lung cancer. DailyDrop has not seen a stage or a treatment plan in the coverage checked, so the outlook is not confirmed.

The news since has been steadier. The Vatican said the Pope spent a peaceful night, Reuters reported, and the Guardian, the BBC and AP all reported that his doctors say recovery is progressing as expected, with the Pope walking in his room and eating normally. AP added that a planned trip to Latin America is now in question. Leo, a US cardinal, was elected in May 2025, and Reuters wrote that the shape of his papacy is now more uncertain.

World · EconomyPress ✓ 6 outlets · rate-hike odds per Reuters

US inflation runs hotter than forecast on fuel costs, as a container ship sinks in the Strait of Hormuz

US consumer prices rose 3.7% in the year to September, above the 3.6% economists expected, per the Liberty Times, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Wednesday 14 October, according to Taiwan’s Central News Agency, the Liberty Times, Reuters and Yonhap. Costlier petrol drove the rise, and Reuters said core CPI was firmer too. Reuters reported that the hotter reading dented Wall Street shares on Wednesday while the dollar and bond yields rose, as investors raised the chance of a Fed rate increase later this month. The Straits Times, carrying Reuters, said it also raises the odds of another increase before year-end, and Brazil’s Valor Econômico and Japan’s Jiji reported that Citi now expects a 0.25-point rise in December. Gold fell by more than 1%, Reuters said. Wednesday’s Wall Street session had not finished when this edition was put together, so no close is given.

The fuel link runs through the Gulf. A Singapore-flagged container ship, the GFS Pride, was hit by a projectile on Tuesday and sank near the Strait of Hormuz, the BBC reported, with all 21 crew evacuated safely. Singapore’s maritime authority said two of its registered ships were struck and no one was hurt. The Guardian reported a second vessel was reportedly sinking and described the sharpest rise in Iranian attacks on shipping since the war began, and Reuters, citing officials, said Iran is shifting tactics to step up such attacks before the US vote. Reuters also reported oil steady after earlier gains. Pump prices in New Zealand follow world oil and the exchange rate, so both stories matter here: dearer oil feeds petrol and diesel, and a Fed that raises rates can firm the US dollar against the kiwi.
