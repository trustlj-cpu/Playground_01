National calls Labour's fiscal plan "not credible" as Labour insists it has "sufficient headroom"

The row is over pay equity: National puts the cost of restoring it at about $11 billion, Treasury's costing is reported at about $13 billion, and Labour will not name a figure.

The argument over Labour's fiscal plan carried into Monday 5 October, according to 1News, The Spinoff and RNZ's election live blog. National finance spokesperson Nicola Willis called the plan "not credible" and said Labour was avoiding fronting up to the cost of its promises. She also said some former Labour finance ministers would be ashamed of it. Labour finance spokesperson Barbara Edmonds said the plan had "sufficient headroom" and that she stood by it.

Edmonds said Treasury's costing of pay equity claims, put at about $13 billion and rising, did not mean that would be the final bill, and said she was confident the plan could settle the claims. National says restoring the old regime would cost $11 billion and leave an $8.5 billion hole. Chris Hipkins said the plan was responsible and paid for every promise. The Spinoff reported that Labour's path back to surplus matches the government's forecasts, with spending brought down to 30.4% of GDP. The figures are the parties' own and have not been independently audited.

Greens promise to remove Auckland level crossings, and a car crosses the tracks as a train approaches

Green co-leaders Marama Davidson and Chlöe Swarbrick used an event in Morningside, Auckland, on Monday to pledge funding to remove level crossings on the Western Line, according to 1News and RNZ. As they spoke, a car drove over the tracks with the lights flashing and the barrier down while a train approached. Swarbrick said it showed why the crossings needed to go.

Davidson said there had been almost 70 crashes and hundreds of near misses involving vehicles and pedestrians at Auckland level crossings over the past decade. The announcement follows the party's $4 billion passenger rail package from Saturday, which includes an overnight Auckland-Wellington train and the return of the Southerner between Christchurch and Dunedin, according to 1News.

Two people critical after a stabbing in Glendene, West Auckland

Two people were taken to hospital in a critical condition after an incident at a property in Glendene, West Auckland, on Sunday night, according to 1News. A second 1News report said a baby was among three people injured. The NZ Herald, as relayed in the day's news roundups, reported three injured, two of them critically, after a stabbing.

Early reports differed on the number of people hurt, and the figure of three is the later one. DailyDrop has seen the details in 1News reporting only and has not seen a police account of what happened, so it does not describe the circumstances. Police inquiries were continuing when the reports were published.

Police name the three who died in Papatoetoe and say the inquiry will take time

Police named the man and two children who died at a home on Cambridge Terrace, Papatoetoe, on Monday afternoon, according to a New Zealand Police statement and 1News. They were a 40-year-old man and children aged 6 and 9. They died on Thursday 1 October. DailyDrop is not repeating the names at the family's request for privacy.

Detective Inspector Karen Bright said the investigation was continuing on behalf of the Coroner and would take time, and that police were giving the family wrap-around support. The scene was handed back to the family on Saturday. This is a development on the earlier report; the deaths had not been described as a homicide. Help is available by calling or texting 1737.
