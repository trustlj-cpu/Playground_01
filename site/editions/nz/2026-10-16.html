Missing Hawke’s Bay student is found by an Air Force helicopter and winched to safety

RNZ said he had left the group after a fall in which he hit his head. His condition has not been confirmed from two outlets.

A student who went missing after a school hike in the Ruahine Forest Park was found and lifted out by helicopter on Wednesday 14 October, according to 1News, the New Zealand Herald, Stuff and RNZ. 1News published footage of the moment he was winched to safety. The Herald carried pictures released by the Air Force from the rescue, and Stuff reported that he shouted “help me” as the crew spotted him in a river valley.

RNZ reported that the student had separated from his group and hit his head in a fall. None of the headlines checked gave his condition, how long he had been missing or which hospital took him, and DailyDrop has not confirmed those details. Searches like this one are run by police with help from volunteers and the defence force; the Air Force supplies aircraft that can reach steep bush where ground teams cannot.

Greens vow to reverse National’s $2.4b public service cuts at a cost of $3.17b

The Greens promised on Wednesday 14 October to undo the $2.4 billion of public service cuts made by National, and put the cost of doing so at $3.17 billion, according to RNZ and the New Zealand Herald’s live election coverage. The Herald’s live blog also reported that the Greens would be open to funding the Mongrel Mob to address drug addiction if elected.

The headlines checked do not say over how many years the $3.17 billion would be spent or how the party would pay for it, and DailyDrop has not confirmed either. Election day is 7 November, so the policy joins a long list of costed promises voters are weighing.

Brian Tamaki to appear in court on charges of inciting racial disharmony

Destiny Church leader Brian Tamaki is due to appear in court charged with inciting racial disharmony, 1News and RNZ reported on Wednesday 15 October. RNZ’s headline said he is accused of “allegedly inciting” disharmony, and the case sits under a section of the Human Rights Act that makes it an offence to publish or distribute material that is threatening, abusive or insulting and likely to stir up hostility against a group because of its colour, race or ethnic or national origins.

The headlines checked do not give the court date, the plea or the specific words or actions at issue, and DailyDrop has not confirmed them. A first appearance is normally short and deals with procedure such as name suppression, remand and the next date. Every charge is an allegation until it is proved.

Tech selloff deepens as CoreWeave’s debt worries spread, and a hot US inflation reading rattles Asia

Asian markets sold off on Thursday after a hotter-than-expected US inflation reading, Reuters reported, and the slide in artificial-intelligence stocks carried on in Europe and the US. Reuters said US chip stocks were set to lose about $2 trillion in value in the latest rout, and that CoreWeave shares and bonds fell further as debt fears grew. Bloomberg reported on Thursday that stocks fell while Treasury yields dropped as worries over CoreWeave weighed on tech.

Reuters said US consumer prices accelerated in September on costlier petrol and that producer prices rose by the most in four and a half years; Bloomberg described the producer-price rise as the biggest since 2022. The US dollar retreated from an 18-month high as Federal Reserve officials played down hikes. DailyDrop could not confirm Wednesday’s closing levels from two sources, so none are quoted.

Greens’ Marama Davidson stands by a visit to the Mongrel Mob as Hipkins attacks the Greens and Te Pāti Māori

Greens co-leader Marama Davidson “doubled down” on Wednesday 14 October amid outrage over a visit to the Mongrel Mob, RNZ reported, and the New Zealand Herald reported that the Greens are open to funding the gang to address drug addiction if elected. Labour leader Chris Hipkins criticised the Greens and Te Pāti Māori after the meeting, according to Newstalk ZB and rova. The Herald also asked whether the Mob’s methamphetamine treatment programme worked.

The headlines checked do not give the amount of any funding or the exact terms of the Greens’ proposal, and DailyDrop has not confirmed them. The question for voters is whether paying a gang to run treatment is a pragmatic way to reach people others cannot, or a reward for a group that has harmed communities.
