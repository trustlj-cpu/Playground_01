Navi Pillay wins the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize for work on accountability for war crimes

The 85-year-old South African former judge led a United Nations inquiry into Gaza whose findings Israel rejected. The prize will be presented in Oslo on 10 December.

The Norwegian Nobel Committee announced on Friday 9 October that the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize goes to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, according to Khaleej Times, PBS NewsHour and the Nobel Prize website. The committee chair, Jørgen Watne Frydnes, said the prize recognised her efforts to promote peace and international law, including work on accountability for war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide. Pillay, who is 85, was born in Durban under apartheid.

Reports link the award to her leadership of a United Nations inquiry into Gaza, whose findings Israel rejected, and some tie the timing to new United States sanctions on the International Criminal Court. UN Secretary-General António Guterres praised the choice as a tribute to those who uphold international law, according to one report. The prize is presented in Oslo on 10 December.

Trump says Russia will supply diesel to the US and the world and eases sanctions to let it happen

President Donald Trump said on Friday 9 October, after a phone call with Vladimir Putin, that Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel, then 500,000 tons in November and a million after that, according to MercoPress, St. Louis Public Radio (NPR), The National and ORF. He added a further three million tons depending on the state of Russian refineries. The US Treasury issued a temporary licence for Russian-origin diesel, reported to run until April 2027, a partial easing of sanctions.

The White House did not say who would pay for the fuel or when it would arrive. Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, called it "a weak decision by strong partners", and some US lawmakers criticised it. The move was framed as easing prices before the November midterm elections. For New Zealand, which imports all its refined fuel, the effect on pump prices would depend on how much extra diesel reaches Asian refineries and markets.

Westpac lifts its inflation forecasts and expects the Reserve Bank to pause in October, then raise rates in December

Westpac's October forecast update raised its short-term inflation forecasts to reflect higher energy prices and a lower exchange rate, and cut its 2026 house price forecast from a small rise to a 1.4% fall, according to Westpac IQ. It expects the Reserve Bank to pause at its October review and lift the Official Cash Rate to 3% in the December Monetary Policy Statement. Interest.co.nz and RNZ reported the update, with a headline figure of annual inflation reaching 4.2% by the end of 2026.

Westpac's weekly note on 9 October described a higher inflation picture alongside growth risks. RNZ summarised the bank as seeing a raft of negatives for the near-term outlook, naming high fuel prices, higher interest rates, a weak New Zealand dollar and high unemployment. The bank's forecasts are its own opinion, and DailyDrop has seen only Westpac's notes, not the interest.co.nz or RNZ articles.

NZX 50 rises 0.4% to 13,749 on Friday, ending the week higher

The S&P/NZX 50 index rose 58 points, or 0.4%, to 13,749 on Friday 9 October and gained about 0.5% over the week, according to Trading Economics. Delegat gained 3.7%, a2 Milk 2.2%, Gentrack 2.2%, Freightways 2.0%, Mainfreight 1.0% and Fisher & Paykel Healthcare 0.6%. Interest.co.nz's 3pm snapshot had the index flat and up 0.1% on the week, so the move came in the closing stages.

The index was about 0.9% lower over the month, interest.co.nz said, and well below the August record of about 14,069 cited by Trading Economics. Hipkins warns a vote for Opportunity is "giving your vote away" as Luxon promises cheap loans for water storage

On Friday 9 October Labour leader Chris Hipkins told supporters that voting for the Opportunity Party would be "giving your vote away", according to Stuff's live coverage and RNZ's account of the campaign day. National leader Christopher Luxon announced a policy of low-cost loans for farm water storage at Feilding's Rural Day, the same coverage reported.

Both lines target the swing party at the centre of the polls: Labour is warning its voters against drifting to Opportunity, while National is courting rural voters. DailyDrop saw the live-blog summaries only, not the full speeches, and does not have costings for the water storage loans.
