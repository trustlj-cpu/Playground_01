National releases its fiscal plan: more health money, no tax cuts next term, and a hint of cuts only after 2029

Finance spokesperson Nicola Willis said the books must be back in surplus first. The party’s main new promise is four years of funding for health cost pressures, which the New Zealand Herald says is more than Labour has offered.

National released its fiscal plan on Monday 12 October, with leader Christopher Luxon and finance spokesperson Nicola Willis speaking to media in Auckland, according to RNZ, the New Zealand Herald, Stuff and 1News. The message was restraint. Willis confirmed there would be no tax cuts in the next term, saying “now is not the time”, and RNZ reported that National hinted at cuts only after 2029, once the books are back in the black. The Herald reported that the main new commitment is four years of funding for health “cost pressures”, the yearly rise in wages, drugs and equipment that hospitals and clinics must absorb, and that National has promised more of it than Labour. Funding cost pressures mainly keeps existing services whole as prices rise, rather than adding new ones, though DailyDrop has not seen the detail of what is included. Stuff’s headline put the plan’s headroom at $7.5 billion, meaning the room left for new spending once existing commitments are met. That figure comes from one outlet, and DailyDrop has not seen the full document. RNZ also reported that Willis described the plan as National’s superannuation policy: the party keeps its intention to raise the retirement age but did not say when. Earlier in the day RNZ’s live coverage reported Luxon rejecting “sugar hit” economics.

National and its partners ACT and NZ First have governed together since the 2023 election, while Labour, led by Chris Hipkins, would need partners of its own. A fiscal plan is a party’s pre-election account of how it would run the government’s money: how much it expects to borrow, how much room it has for new promises, and when the budget returns to surplus, which is what “in the black” means: the government takes in more than it spends. With the election on 7 November and New Zealand’s MMP system rarely handing one party a majority, the numbers matter to all three governing parties. ACT’s David Seymour set out his party’s fiscal plan on Sunday, and Newstalk ZB reported that he pledged to raise the NZ Super age. The caution is easier to understand beside the rest of Monday’s news: petrol hit a record average of $3.60 a litre and the kiwi dollar fell to a multi-year low, both of which squeeze any government’s room to promise relief (see the fuel story on this page). DailyDrop has not seen a Labour response to the plan in the coverage checked, and Labour’s own costings are the other half of the comparison. Watch whether Labour and the Greens publish their numbers this week, and whether independent economists test National’s headroom against the Treasury’s forecasts.

Peters and Seymour both say Opportunity will not make it into Parliament, as the campaign enters its third week

The campaign entered its third week on Monday with two veterans of the right, NZ First’s Winston Peters and ACT’s David Seymour, dismissing the Opportunity Party. Stuff reported that both said they do not believe Opportunity will win seats, even though most polls show it above the 5% party-vote threshold, the line a party must cross to enter Parliament unless it also wins an electorate seat. In RNZ’s live coverage Peters, who has spent half a century in politics, said staying on was “not a matter of enjoyment”, and told RNZ that living in New Zealand today is “not as attractive as it used to be”. RNZ’s analysis added that he says neither of the two Chrises, Labour’s Hipkins or National’s Luxon, will be calling the shots after the election. 1News’s live blog also had Peters discussing the fireworks ban, abortion and the polls. Seymour set out ACT’s fiscal plan on Sunday, RNZ’s live coverage carried a Greens promise on jobs whose detail DailyDrop has not seen, and RNZ’s explainer counts 17 parties competing this year. DailyDrop reported yesterday that Luxon has ruled out a deal with Opportunity, which could leave it holding the balance of power if it gets in.

Riyadh’s airport is hit again by Houthi attacks as oil chiefs pull out of a Riyadh energy congress

Saudi Arabia’s King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh was struck again on Sunday 11 October, a day after the Saturday strike that Saudi authorities said killed 12 people. 1News reported that the latest hit was on the terminals serving domestic flights, and FinancialJuice, relaying Saudi civil aviation, reported one dead and 11 wounded. The BBC put the total at 13 dead across attacks on the airport and another facility, called it a significant escalation, and reported that a Saudi minister vowed a “serious response”. Reuters described the skies over the capital as largely empty, and FinancialJuice reported that Saudi Arabia extended the suspension of operations at the airport.

The Houthis, the armed group in Yemen backed by Iran, claimed the attack. They also said they struck Dammam airport and Aramco facilities in the east, a claim DailyDrop has not been able to confirm, while the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile and Bloomberg reported that new missiles were intercepted. Turkey, Pakistan and Egypt joined Saudi Arabia in condemning the attacks, Reuters reported. BP and other oil chiefs pulled out of the World Petroleum Council’s Riyadh congress, the Financial Times and Reuters reported, and Philippine Airlines suspended Riyadh flights until 29 November, Philstar reported.

Petrol hits a record $3.60 a litre as the kiwi dollar sinks to a multi-year low

Petrol has reached a record at the pump in New Zealand. Gaspy, a fuel-price app, puts the average price of 91-octane petrol at $3.60 a litre and diesel at about $3.23, according to RNZ and the New Zealand Herald, which both asked who is profiting from the rise. Newsroom reported that the kiwi dollar has hit a multi-year low while petrol surges towards $4 a litre, a combination it said adds pressure to the cost of living and tests the Reserve Bank’s monetary policy path.

DailyDrop could not confirm Monday’s NZD/USD level or the NZX 50 close from two sources, so both ears show a dash. The pressure is global: Reuters reported the US dollar staying near an 18-month high as the Middle East conflict and US inflation data stayed in focus. A weaker kiwi makes imported fuel dearer in New Zealand dollars even when the oil price in US dollars does not move.

Man charged with two murders in Christchurch appears in court

A man charged over a double killing in Christchurch appeared in court on Monday 12 October. RNZ named him as Rodney Smith and reported that he is accused of murdering Glen Allan Tukaki and Nigel John Robinson, and also faces charges of attempted murder and grievous bodily harm. The New Zealand Herald described the accused as a 58-year-old man appearing on murder charges, and Stuff and 1News also reported the appearance.

The headlines checked do not say when the killings happened or what led to them, and DailyDrop has not confirmed the court’s next steps, such as bail, remand or a next appearance. A first appearance is usually brief and deals with those matters rather than the evidence. Every charge is an allegation until it is proved in court.
