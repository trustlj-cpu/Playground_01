Man, 58, to face two murder charges after two people die in Christchurch's Phillipstown

Police were called to an address on Olliviers Road shortly after midnight on Saturday. Two people died at the scene and two others are in hospital with serious injuries.

A 58-year-old man is due in Christchurch District Court on Monday charged with two counts of murder, one of attempted murder and one of wounding with intent, according to 1News and New Zealand Police. Officers were called to the Olliviers Road address in Phillipstown shortly after midnight on Saturday 10 October. Two people died at the scene and two others were taken to hospital with serious injuries.

Police said earlier that two people were in custody assisting with inquiries. They believe this was an isolated incident and are not looking for anyone else. Residents should expect more police presence while scene examinations continue. Anyone with information or CCTV footage is asked to call 105 and quote file number 261010/6589. Police have not named the dead. A charge is not a finding of guilt.

World · Natural hazardsPress ✓ 4 outlets · magnitudes revised by USGS

A magnitude 7.6 earthquake strikes off southern Panama, followed by a second quake and aftershocks

A strong earthquake struck near the southern coast of Panama on Friday 9 October at a depth of about 10 km, according to the US Geological Survey, as reported by Reuters via The Star, the Associated Press, Khaleej Times and the Jamaica Observer. The USGS first put the magnitude at 8.0, then revised it to 7.6 and later to 7.7. AP described a second quake of magnitude 6.6 and a series of aftershocks. A tsunami warning was reported, and Tocumen International Airport temporarily suspended operations.

Reports on damage differ. AP described collapsing buildings near the epicentre, while Khaleej Times reported no immediate reports of damage. DailyDrop has not found a casualty figure it can confirm from two outlets, and no New Zealand impact was reported in the sources it saw.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 3 outlets · casualty figures per Saudi civil aviation authorities

Houthi attacks on Saudi airports leave three dead and more than 30 injured, Saudi authorities say

Saudi civil aviation authorities said three people were killed and more than 30 injured after two Saudi airports were targeted, according to Gulf News, Khaleej Times and the Associated Press via ABC News. Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen claimed responsibility for the attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, saying a ballistic missile caused damage but giving no evidence. A witness reported a large blast at the airport on Thursday, a day after the kingdom said one person had been killed.

The attacks have disrupted travel through one of the Gulf's hubs. Several airlines suspended flights to Riyadh, and flight trackers described the airport as closed again on Thursday afternoon. For New Zealanders, the effect is mainly on oil prices and on travel plans that route through the Gulf. The Houthi claims and the damage are unverified.

Sport · FootballPress ✓ 4 outlets · suspension reported, no final ruling

Ronaldo faces a provisional suspension after walking out of Portugal's camp

Cristiano Ronaldo left Portugal's camp in Copenhagen before the match against Denmark after a disagreement with coach Jorge Jesus over his role, according to Football Transfers, ARY News, EWN and Arise. Reports citing The Mirror said Portuguese Football Federation rules automatically triggered a provisional suspension. The federation's disciplinary council will decide the length, which could run from one to six months, and a ban of more than a month would rule him out of the next two Nations League matches, against Denmark and Wales.

Ronaldo issued a lengthy statement accepting responsibility for leaving and saying he should be punished by the federation, and made clear he is not retiring from international football. The federation said only that he had left and its preparations continued with 24 players. DailyDrop has seen no final ruling.

NZX 50 ends the week at 13,749; gold jumps 1.4% in New York as local markets close for the weekend

The S&P/NZX 50 index closed Friday 9 October at 13,749, up 0.4%, and gained about 0.5% over the week, according to Trading Economics. Interest.co.nz's 3pm snapshot had the index flat and up 0.1% on the week, and about 0.9% lower over the month. The market is closed until Monday.
