NZ First puts newcomers above four sitting MPs on its party list, with Winston Peters at the top

Former National minister Alfred Ngaro is fifth, ahead of caucus members who are now ranked 10th to 13th. The party needs a high poll result to bring them back.

NZ First released its party list for the 7 November election on Wednesday 7 October, according to 1News, interest.co.nz, the Otago Daily Times and The Spinoff. Winston Peters is first, followed by Shane Jones, Casey Costello and Mark Patterson. Alfred Ngaro, a former National minister, is fifth. Michael Laws, Harete Hipango-Brownlie, Craig Stobo and Taine Randell fill places six to nine. Interest.co.nz describes Stobo as a former chair of the Financial Markets Authority and Randell as a retired All Black.

Four current MPs, Jenny Marcroft, Andy Foster, David Wilson and Jamie Arbuckle, are ranked 10th to 13th. On the 2023 list Marcroft was fifth, Arbuckle sixth and Foster seventh. 1News said they would need NZ First to poll at about 10% or more to keep their seats. The former minister Ron Mark is 16th, which one report called surprisingly low; Peters said he expected Mark to be elected.

Willis and Edmonds agree on supermarket prices but not on how to pay for promises

National's Nicola Willis and Labour's Barbara Edmonds went head to head on RNZ's Morning Report on Wednesday, according to RNZ's campaign-trail coverage. Both said supermarket prices were a priority. They disagreed over where the money for their election promises would come from, repeating the argument of recent days over Labour's fiscal plan.

Edmonds said Labour was offering a package of solutions to help with the cost of living. RNZ's round-up of the day also listed fresh policy announcements from other parties, including a pledge from ACT about the tax break for donations. DailyDrop has seen RNZ's summaries only, not the full interview.

Greens' KiwiMart plan would force the supermarket duopoly to sell off at least 120 stores

The Greens' publicly owned supermarket chain, KiwiMart, announced on 6 September, remained part of the debate on Wednesday as National and Labour both called grocery prices a priority, according to 1News, interest.co.nz, Waatea News and Supermarket News. The policy would require Foodstuffs and Woolworths to divest at least 120 stores and some distribution capacity into public ownership, with the Commerce Commission choosing which stores based on local market share.

Reports put the cost at about $1.3 billion for the stores and a further $1.5 billion to set up KiwiMart, roughly $2.8 billion in all, although 1News describes the $1.5 billion as covering distribution centres. Co-leader Chlöe Swarbrick said two companies take about a million dollars a day in excess profit from shopping baskets. The package also includes a ban on excessive pricing. DailyDrop has seen no independent analysis of the likely effect on prices.

What NZ First needs to bring its four demoted MPs back

Jenny Marcroft, Andy Foster, David Wilson and Jamie Arbuckle sit at 10th to 13th on the NZ First list, according to 1News, interest.co.nz and the Otago Daily Times. Under MMP a list MP is elected only if the party wins enough of the party vote. 1News reported they would need NZ First to poll at 10% or higher to be returned.

The ranking is a change from 2023, when Marcroft was fifth. The party has not said publicly whether the order was set by Peters or by a panel, and DailyDrop did not find that in the reports. Newcomers including Ngaro, Laws, Hipango-Brownlie, Stobo and Randell occupy the places above the four.
