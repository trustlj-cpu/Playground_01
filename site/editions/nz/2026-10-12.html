Ardern backs Hipkins and Labour in a campaign video, four weeks before the 7 November election

The former prime minister, who lives in Australia, asked supporters to donate to Labour. A panel of undecided voters gave the first leaders' debate to Chris Hipkins, and two polls put the centre-left bloc narrowly ahead.

Dame Jacinda Ardern ended her silence on the 2026 campaign on Sunday 11 October with a video on her own website in which she backs Labour leader Chris Hipkins and asks supporters to donate, according to Bloomberg, the New Zealand Herald, Newstalk ZB, The Post and The Straits Times. Ardern, who resigned as prime minister in January 2023 and now lives in Australia, called Hipkins an old colleague and a friend. The same video was emailed to Labour supporters, so it works as a fundraising appeal rather than a campaign-trail appearance. She had not been involved in the campaign publicly until now, the reports say. Money is where Labour is weakest. Reports on the video note that the party has declared only $890,000 in donations above $20,000 this year, against $2.3 million for National and $2.4 million for ACT. Labour usually does better with small donations, which do not have to be declared straight away, so the declared totals understate what it raises from small donors. Ardern left office more than three years ago after five and a half years as prime minister, and her intervention tests how much weight an overseas-based former leader still carries. Hipkins, who succeeded her, led Labour into the 2023 election, which National won, and National now governs with ACT and NZ First.

The endorsement comes four weeks before the 7 November election. After the first televised leaders' debate on Tuesday 6 October, a panel of 100 undecided voters favoured Hipkins over National leader Christopher Luxon by 49 to 24, according to 1News and Stuff. Hipkins has called himself prime minister in waiting, and Luxon said before the debate that he was looking forward to it. Two polls, from TVNZ and RNZ, have the centre-left bloc narrowly ahead, a gap attributed to Luxon ruling out any deal with the Opportunity party, which could end up holding the balance of power. Because New Zealand votes under MMP, one party almost never wins a majority, and coalition talks are expected after polling day. Luxon's main pitch is no new taxes; Labour proposes a capital gains tax on investment property and businesses, and Hipkins has pressed Luxon on whether existing taxes would rise, pointing to a National fuel tax proposal. Hipkins has also promised to reverse the government's rule that voters must be enrolled two weeks before election day, which the Electoral Commission estimates could see about 55,000 votes disallowed. Inside the coalition, NZ First leader Shane Jones has publicly warned Luxon over National's fisheries plan, Stuff's live coverage showed. The 7 November date was set in January, and overseas voting opens on 21 October, so a large share of ballots will be cast before election day itself. DailyDrop has not found a poll taken since the debate.

Houthi missile strike on Riyadh's airport kills 12 and injures 309; Saudi coalition vows a response

A missile strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday 10 October killed 12 people and injured 309, Saudi authorities said, according to AP, Reuters, Politico and Asharq Al-Awsat. Saudi Arabia extended the airport's closure on Sunday. Houthi strikes on Thursday had already killed three people and injured more than 30 at two Saudi airports, Saudi civil aviation authorities said, and Lufthansa Group suspended Riyadh flights through 16 October. The Saudi-led coalition blamed the Houthis, called the attack a war crime and promised a firm response. Reuters reported that the Houthis renewed their warning to airlines and travellers against using Saudi airports, and that Saudi shares fell.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was deeply shocked, and governments including Canada, Italy, India and Pakistan condemned the attack. DailyDrop has seen no detail of what form the coalition's response would take. President Donald Trump called it "a terrible thing"; DailyDrop has seen no announced US military response. Le Monde described it as the deadliest Houthi attack on Riyadh in ten years. For New Zealand the effect is indirect but real: the Gulf supplies much of the world's oil, New Zealand imports all of its refined fuel, and Gulf hubs carry many flights between Australasia and Europe. West Texas Intermediate closed on Friday at US$91.85 a barrel, before the attack.

All Blacks beat the Wallabies 50-19 at Eden Park in the first Bledisloe Cup Test

The All Blacks beat Australia 50-19 in the first Bledisloe Cup Test at Eden Park in Auckland on Saturday 10 October, according to ABC News, Stuff, 1News, RugbyPass and Planet Rugby. New Zealand led only 17-14 at halftime, then scored five tries to one after the break. Reports put the match total at seven tries, and Damian McKenzie kicked 15 points. It was the first defeat for Wallabies coach Les Kiss, whose side had been unbeaten in its first five Tests. Australia has not beaten the All Blacks at Eden Park since 1986. Centre Len Ikitau failed a head injury assessment in the first half.

The second Test is in Sydney on Saturday 17 October, and Kiss said his team would "return fire", RugbyPass and Planet Rugby reported. The Straits Times and CNA reported that four All Blacks have been ruled out of that match. Sources differ on the streak, with one counting 23 straight Bledisloe Cup wins for New Zealand and another a 24-year drought for Australia. DailyDrop has not confirmed which figure is right.

World · Ukraine warPress ✓ 5 outlets · Trump's and Zelenskyy's words as quoted

Trump says Ukraine needs a new president; Zelenskyy offers to stop refinery strikes if Russia stops hitting energy sites

President Donald Trump said on Saturday 10 October that it was "time for Ukraine to get a new president", after Volodymyr Zelenskyy called his Russian diesel deal "a weak decision by strong partners", according to the BBC, Reuters, the Evening Standard and Rappler. Trump also told Zelenskyy to stop hitting Russian refineries. Elon Musk backed him, saying Zelenskyy "has gone too far", Kyiv Post reported, and the Kremlin welcomed the idea of a Ukrainian election. Reports put the toll from Russian strikes on Ukrainian cities that day at between 15 and 21.

On Sunday Zelenskyy said Ukraine would stop striking Russian refineries if Russia stops attacking Ukraine's energy infrastructure, and that any de-escalation must be mutual and backed by real guarantees, according to Reuters, The Straits Times, Kyiv Post and Ukrainska Pravda. Wes Streeting told the BBC that the UK does not support the diesel deal. For New Zealand, which imports all of its refined fuel, the question is whether extra Russian diesel eases prices in Asia, where it buys much of its supply.

World · Gulf shippingPress ✓ 5 outlets · mine claim unverified

US jet disables a cargo ship trying to run the Iran blockade in the Gulf of Oman

US Central Command said a fighter jet struck the stern of the Panama-flagged cargo ship Ocean Molica on Saturday 10 October, completely disabling its propulsion, after the crew ignored repeated warnings while leaving an Iranian port, according to Reuters, Al Jazeera, Asharq Al-Awsat, The Straits Times and JNS. Washington has imposed a naval blockade on Iran, and the strike was presented as enforcing it.

Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed that an oil tanker exploded after hitting a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, and UKMTO reported an outbound tanker hit by an unidentified projectile there, with its crew safe. Both reports came through the FinancialJuice news wire and the mine claim is unverified. The UAE said its coast guard rescued 22 people after a fire on an oil tanker in the Gulf. DailyDrop cannot confirm whether the incidents are linked. West Texas Intermediate closed Friday at US$91.85, up 0.39%, and gold at US$4,216.3, up 1.43%.
