Labour sets out its fiscal plan, promising a surplus by 2028/29 as National claims an $8.5b hole

The plan includes $2.5 billion over four years for a $4-an-hour rise for 65,000 care and support workers. Labour says every promise is costed; National says restoring pay equity is not.

Labour released its fiscal plan on Sunday 4 October, five weeks before the 7 November election, according to 1News, the Otago Daily Times, RNZ, Newstalk ZB and Stuff. The plan aims for an operating surplus in 2028/29. It also includes about $2.5 billion over four years for an interim pay rise of $4 an hour for roughly 65,000 care and support workers. Chris Hipkins described that as about half of what unions have sought, and not a final deal.

National finance spokesperson Nicola Willis said the plan leaves a gap of about $8.5 billion, because it puts no figure on restoring the old pay equity regime. National puts that cost at $11 billion, citing a Treasury estimate. Labour says it does not want to undermine negotiations by naming a number, and that it has about $10.5 billion of unallocated funding. The $8.5 billion figure is National's calculation. Labour has not accepted it and has not published its own.

Politics · TransportPress ✓ 4 outlets · costings per Parliamentary Library

Greens pledge free buses and trains for children and students, and a $2 cap on adult fares

The Green Party launched its public transport policy on Sunday, according to 1News, the Otago Daily Times, interest.co.nz and RNZ. Buses and trains would be free for children, students, Community Service Card holders and Total Mobility users, and other adults would pay no more than $2 a journey. Reports differ on the exact age cut-offs for free travel, and the policy document does not say whether ferries are covered.

The Parliamentary Library costed the fare changes at $262 million in 2027/28, rising to $381 million by 2030/31, and the Greens say their tax policy would pay for it. The party also set aside up to $13.65 billion for light rail or bus rapid transit in Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch, a ceiling it calls indicative. Co-leader Chlöe Swarbrick tied the policy to fuel costs.

National promises $1.75b for the first stage of Auckland's Mill Road, with construction from late 2028

National said on Sunday 4 October that a re-elected government would put $1.75 billion towards the first stage of the Mill Road corridor, from Manukau to Alfriston, according to 1News, RNZ and Newstalk ZB. The money would sit in a tagged contingency in Budget 2028, with construction starting in late 2028. A further $100 million would go to protecting the route for stages two and three, from Alfriston to Drury.

The full corridor would be a four-lane, 21.5 km road running alongside State Highway 1. National's document cites NZTA figures of $3.10 in benefits for every dollar spent if the road is not tolled, or $2.20 if it is. Whether it would be tolled is unresolved, and no date was given for stages two and three. Transport spokesperson Chris Bishop said roads in the south had not kept pace with growth.

Police continue inquiry into the deaths of a man and two children in a Papatoetoe home

Police are investigating the deaths of three members of one family at a house on Cambridge Terrace in Papatoetoe, South Auckland, according to 1News, New Zealand Police and Bernama. Officers were called at about 6.40pm on Thursday 1 October and found one person dead, then two more inside. Police said the three were a man and two children, and that they were not looking for anyone else.

The inquiry is being run on behalf of the Coroner, and police have not described the deaths as a homicide. The scene examination was finished and the house was handed back to the family on Saturday morning. Police said they do not believe there is any risk to the wider public. They said answers would take time. If this story is distressing, free counselling is available by calling or texting 1737.

Markets · New ZealandPress ✓ 5 outlets · levels per NBR and Bloomberg

NZX 50 slides 0.9% on Friday to 13,680 and the kiwi sits near US56 cents, on course for a sixth weekly loss

The S&P/NZX 50 index fell 130.02 points, or 0.9%, to 13,680.49 on Friday 2 October, according to the National Business Review. That left it down about 1% for the week. Interest.co.nz's 3pm snapshot showed a fall of about 1.0%. Serko had the biggest weekly drop among large companies at 8.3%, followed by Ryman Healthcare at 7.7% and Summerset at 6.3%.

The New Zealand dollar traded around 56 US cents in Asia on Friday, set for a sixth weekly loss, according to Bloomberg, with a firm US dollar, higher oil and election risk cited. After the US jobs report it lifted to about 56.1 cents, Mitrade reported, and FXStreet said it had earlier slid below 56 cents for the first time since November 2025. DailyDrop could not find an official closing rate for the pair.
