National pledges random drug tests for Jobseeker recipients, starting with 10,000 a year

A second failed test would trigger a clinical assessment, and the party also plans lower rates for long-term recipients from April 2028. National puts the testing cost at about $1.5 million a year.

National announced on Thursday 8 October, at an event in Paraparaumu, that it would introduce random drug testing for people receiving the Jobseeker benefit, according to 1News, the Otago Daily Times and RNZ. The party said it would start with 10,000 tests in the first year and expand over time. The Ministry of Social Development would record a first failed test. A second would trigger a clinical alcohol and drug assessment, and anyone who then failed to meet their obligations could face the normal sanctions, which can include cuts to the benefit.

National expects the testing to cost about $1.5 million a year, met from existing ministry budgets. The package also includes lower payments for long-term recipients, reported as about $48 a week for a single person without children and about $24 a week for those with dependent children, at current rates. One report says the lower rate would apply from 1 April 2028 for people who have been on Jobseeker Support for two years. Reports credit the social development spokesperson, Louise Upston, with the announcement, while a 1News live page was titled around Christopher Luxon announcing it.

Oil climbs as record tanker attacks cut shipping through Hormuz

Oil prices climbed in early trading on Thursday 8 October as attacks on tankers cut shipping through the Strait of Hormuz to a two-month low, according to CNN, CNBC and DTN. Reports said nine tankers had been attacked in and around the strait over the past week, and Brent traded above US$100 a barrel. Houthi militants also fired ballistic missiles at Saudi Arabia's capital, Riyadh, CNBC reported.

How far prices ended the day will be clear only after the US session closes later today. For New Zealand the link is the pump: the country imports all its refined fuel, and the New Zealand dollar has been weak. DailyDrop has not seen a New Zealand pump price for this week.

Construction begins on the Waikare Gorge bridge, billed as the country's highest road bridge

Work began on Thursday on a new bridge across the Waikare Gorge on State Highway 2 between Napier and Wairoa, according to 1News and the Otago Daily Times. It is described as the country's highest road bridge, about 65 metres above the river. It replaces a temporary one-lane Bailey bridge that has been in place since Cyclone Gabrielle destroyed the old bridge in 2023.

Reports differ on the design: 1News and the ODT described a bridge of about 260 metres, with the ODT calling it a truss bridge, while earlier NZTA material described an arch bridge just over 160 metres long. The project also includes a 4 km realignment of the road, and construction is expected to take about four years, possibly less. Funding was confirmed through the National Land Transport Programme in February 2025.

Walter Peak fire near Queenstown is contained after burning for three days

A large vegetation fire at Walter Peak, near Queenstown, was declared fully contained on Thursday after burning for three days, according to 1News. The fire was estimated to have burned about 1350 hectares. Fire and Emergency New Zealand said firefighters had demobilised and the fireground had been handed back to the landowners, with some areas likely to smoulder for days.

DailyDrop has seen 1News reporting only. The cause of the fire and any damage to property were not reported in the material it saw.

The first leaders' debate reached more than a million viewers, 1News says

TVNZ's 1News said the leaders' debate between Christopher Luxon and Chris Hipkins on Tuesday reached more than a million viewers, in its news bulletin summary for Thursday. The debate was the only head-to-head between the two before the 7 November election.

The audience figure is TVNZ's own and DailyDrop has not seen an independent ratings source. It does not say how many people watched the whole 90 minutes. Polling by RNZ-Reid Research and 1News-Verian suggests the race remains close, with Opportunity as the swing party.
