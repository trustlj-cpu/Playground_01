Nobel Peace Prize goes to South Africa's Navi Pillay. Hours later, the U.S. sanctioned the International Criminal Court

On the day a prize went to defend international legal bodies, the U.S. Treasury put one of those bodies on its sanctions list. South Korean stocks were off yesterday for the Hangeul Day trading holiday

On the 9th (local time), the Norwegian Nobel Committee announced, at 6 p.m. Korea time, that this year's Nobel Peace Prize goes to Navi Pillay (85), a South African jurist. She is also reported to be of Indian descent (Mint, Hindustan Times). She served as UN High Commissioner for Human Rights and as a judge of the International Criminal Court (ICC), and is now an ad hoc judge of the International Court of Justice (ICJ) (Yonhap, Reuters). According to The Washington Post, the committee cited her work in strengthening international legal institutions. Yonhap reported that the committee also criticized the U.S. sanctions on the ICC. It is the second year in a row that a woman has won. Pillay said she dedicated the prize to survivors of international crimes, and joked that if the applause went on, she would have to share the prize with President Trump. South African President Cyril Ramaphosa welcomed it as “a tribute to courage.” It is the first South African laureate since Mandela, 33 years ago. The UN also congratulated her, calling it a signal for human rights (Handelsblatt).

Reactions split at once. Israel's Foreign Ministry condemned the award, saying “Goebbels would cheer” (Yonhap, Kyunghyang Shinmun). President Trump has openly said he wants this prize (Yonhap). Forbes reported that Trump reposted a message calling the laureate “someone who hates America and Israel.” The same day, the U.S. Treasury announced it had put the ICC itself on its sanctions list (Reuters exclusive, NHK). In August the U.S. had sanctioned ICC President Tomoko Akane; now it has widened the pressure to the whole institution. Russia also demanded that the Netherlands hand over Akane and others (NHK, Japan Times). Der Spiegel noted that Pillay has criticized Israel's actions in the Gaza Strip, and Mexico's Expansión called it an award that Trump and Netanyahu would find uncomfortable. Japan's Prime Minister Takaichi voiced deep concern, saying it was incompatible with Japan's position (Asahi). With a former judge winning the prize on the day her court came under sanctions, the two events are read together. But whether the timing of the sanctions was linked to the award, and what exactly the sanctions cover, has not yet been confirmed. South Korea is an ICC member, so this is not someone else's problem. This weekend, the reactions of the ICC and European governments are the thing to watch.

International · SecurityPress ✓ 4 outlets · the attacker is per the Houthis' claim

Houthis' attack on Riyadh airport kills 3; another ship is hit in Hormuz

Saudi civil aviation authorities said three people died at Riyadh airport after an attack by the Houthis (Reuters, AP). The Houthis claimed responsibility; Saudi Arabia has not itself named who carried out the attack. Yonhap reported that the dead include a Saudia captain. Earlier, on the 7th, Saudi Arabia announced that three people died in attacks on the Riyadh and Abha airports; whether the two tallies refer to the same incident has not been confirmed. Lufthansa and Indian airlines suspended Riyadh routes, and the EU aviation authority widened its Saudi airspace warning (Reuters). The U.S. Treasury sanctioned Turkish, UAE and Indian companies involved in illicit trade in Iranian petroleum products (Yonhap). In the Strait of Hormuz, a ship was hit by a projectile on the 9th, the UK Maritime Trade Operations said, and the Revolutionary Guards said they would punish ships that break the shipping lane (Yonhap). Oil fell in Asian trading after President Trump spoke of negotiations with Iran (Reuters). On the 8th, New York WTI rose 3.6% to the $91 range. New York stocks opened higher on Friday as oil fell (Yonhap), but trading was still under way as this paper went to press, so we could not include the close.

International Criminal CourtInternational law

The ICC. A permanent court that tries individuals for war crimes and crimes against humanity, located in The Hague, the Netherlands. It is a different body from the International Court of Justice, which handles disputes between states.

Industry · SemiconductorsPress ✓ 6 outlets · remarks are Chairman Chey's answers on site

SK's Chey Tae-won visits Honam chip site for the first time: “As fast as possible, at two-thirds the scale of Yongin”

SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won visited the Gwangju military airport, the planned site of the Honam semiconductor cluster, for the first time on the afternoon of the 9th. After touring the site with Jeonnam-Gwangju Mayor Min Hyung-bae and SK hynix CEO Kwak Noh-jung, he said, “Once conditions allow us to build, we will build at the fastest speed possible” (Yonhap, Dong-A Ilbo, SBS). The site covers about 2.48 million pyeong (over 8.18 million m²). He also said he would build the fab first even if power and water supply are delayed (Dong-A Ilbo, Korea Economic Daily).

He said the plan is for a scale of “about two-thirds of Yongin at present,” which could grow if there is more land and demand (Kyunghyang Shinmun, ZDNet Korea). Some call it unusual for a group chairman to come to the site in person and speak about speed. But the groundbreaking date and the investment amount have not yet been announced, and power and water support, along with designation as a national industrial complex, remains up to the government and local authorities.

Trade · IndustryPress ✓ 5 outlets · the size of the cut is the EU's explanation

China agrees with EU to cut hybrid vehicle exports; EU: “Roughly halved over four years”

On the 9th, China said it had reached agreement with the EU in trade talks on hybrid vehicles, and is also said to have agreed to ease export licensing for rare earths (Yonhap). The EU explained that the deal could cut Chinese hybrid vehicle exports to the EU by roughly half over four years (The New York Times, Reuters). The two sides agreed to keep negotiating the electric-vehicle dispute.

The wording differs. China called it an ‘agreement’; Reuters and the Financial Times wrote ‘understanding.’ How the cuts, in effect a voluntary export restraint, will be made, and how far rare-earth licensing will be relaxed, has not been made public. For Korean carmakers that run into Chinese hybrids in Europe, it is news that could change the competitive landscape, but the impact can only be gauged once the details emerge.

Cyber · FinancePress ✓ 5 outlets · the suspect's involvement is a security firm's analysis

Korean financial-sector hack: signs of links to Chinese AI tool and DDoS group; police expand investigation team to 43

Police recently expanded their investigation team on hacks of financial institutions to 43 people (Yonhap, Kyunghyang Shinmun). Security firm Logpresso analyzed that the person named as a suspect appears to have been active in a Chinese DDoS-for-hire group (Chosun Ilbo, Yonhap). According to Reuters, the developer of ARTEX, a Chinese AI security-testing tool reportedly used in the hacks, said it would take the source code private and stop updates (Dong-A Ilbo).

Reuters reported that nine domestic banks and two large churches are investigating whether an AI tool was used. In Japan, too, Daiwa Securities, SoftBank, Lawson and others have suffered successive cyber incidents. Yonhap pointed out that of 18 overseas-origin hacks since 2024, the attacker could not be identified in 16. Whether the named person is the actual attacker has not yet been confirmed. Customers are advised to turn on two-step verification in banking apps.
