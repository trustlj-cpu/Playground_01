News · Road haulage✓ 4 outlets · not confirmed whether the government will issue a back-to-work order

The Strike Guarantee Authority asks for a back-to-work order over the blockades by Tir trucks, while the government earmarks 105 million for road haulage

ANSA, AGI, Il Fatto Quotidiano and TGCom24 report the Authority’s request, the decree on soaring diesel prices and airports in the South running short of jet fuel.

ANSA and AGI report that on October 22 the Strike Guarantee Authority asked for a back-to-work order for road haulage: according to ANSA, “widespread and persistent blockades prolong critical conditions.” Also on October 22 ANSA writes that the government has chosen a hard line and that the Tir truck blockades have been suspended in Sicily and Calabria, while in the rest of the South the protests continued. Il Fatto Quotidiano reports that the Council of Ministers approved a decree with support for soaring diesel prices: 105 million for road haulage, 60 for agriculture and 10 for fishing.

TGCom24 reports that airports in the South have run dry of jet fuel, with the advice “Get supplies elsewhere,” and publishes the list of airports at risk. The day before, on October 21, ANSA had reported on the meeting at Palazzo Chigi with Giorgia Meloni, at which the Viminale guaranteed supplies of basic necessities, and on the Council of Ministers called for a decree on Tir trucks and agriculture. On that same October 21 Il Fatto Quotidiano gathered the voice of the truckers stopped in Naples: “We are protesting against the high cost of living, we are fighting for everyone.”

World · Borders✓ 4 outlets · end date of the checks not confirmed

Italy and Spain do not extend the border checks born of the Ceuta crisis, after a phone call between Piantedosi and Grande-Marlaska

ANSA reports that the agreement between Rome and Madrid drops the Schengen checks at the borders with Italy, after a phone conversation between Interior Minister Matteo Piantedosi and his Spanish counterpart Fernando Grande-Marlaska. AGI headlines the Italy-Spain understanding with the stop to border checks and the reopening of the borders, while La Repubblica writes that the two countries agree to stop the suspension of Schengen. Il Fatto Quotidiano links the decision to the tensions over the events in Ceuta and speaks of checks suspended at the borders between Italy and Spain. The four outlets published the news on the morning of October 22.

Politics · Security✓ 4 outlets · not confirmed when the troops will begin operating

Mattarella signs the security decree and Salvini announces 500 paratroopers in Milan ready to step in with Lince and drones

ANSA and Rai News report that on October 22 President of the Republic Sergio Mattarella signed the security decree. ANSA reports the words of Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini: the troops in Milan will be able to step in with Lince and drones. AGI headlines “500 paratroopers with Lince and drones in Milan” and also quotes Justice Minister Carlo Nordio, who says: “We need more juvenile prisons.” Il Fatto Quotidiano writes that the 500 paratroopers arriving in Milan for public order will be “ready to step in with the Lince and the drones.” In the same hours ANSA reports the judges’ decisions on the Milan clashes: three minors in prison and 21 split between house arrest and community placement, at the end of the last 24 validation hearings on the arrests. In another headline ANSA speaks of tension in the government over the arrival of the troops.

World · Ukraine✓ 3 outlets · no date confirmed for the three-way summit

Putin opens the door to a summit with Trump and Zelensky but calls the territories of eastern Ukraine “historically Russian lands,” while the raids continue

ANSA reports that Vladimir Putin has opened the door to a summit with Donald Trump and Volodymyr Zelensky (“we are discussing it”) and said that Russia and Ukraine can reach an agreement. According to ANSA the Russian president called the territories of eastern Ukraine “historically Russian lands” and said that trilateral talks should cover security guarantees for Moscow too, as well as broader questions of European security. The day before, on October 21, ANSA had reported Trump’s words: “perhaps we are closer than ever to the end of the war in Ukraine.” Il Fatto Quotidiano writes that from Moscow comes the answer “The discussion is under way” on the summit between Putin, Zelensky and Trump, and that meanwhile Russian raids continue. In ANSA’s summary Zelensky speaks of three waves of ballistic missiles. La Stampa headlines that Putin and Zelensky are ready to negotiate and that there is an OK to the trilateral in mid-November with the US.

Economy · Markets✓ 3 outlets · not confirmed whether oil exceeds 100 dollars

Piazza Affari closes at -2.54% with European stock markets amid fears over artificial intelligence and oil heading toward 100 dollars, and the BTP-Bund spread closes at 124 points

ANSA reports that on October 22 the Milan stock exchange closed sharply lower, with the FTSE MIB at -2.54% at 48,383.66 points, in line with the other European stock markets: Paris -1.39%, Frankfurt -1.07% and London -0.26%. ANSA attributes the fall to fears over artificial intelligence and to oil, with WTI heading toward 100 dollars a barrel; in Amsterdam TTF gas rises 3.9% above 90 euros per megawatt hour. ANSA also writes that diesel on the motorway reaches 2.3 euros a litre.

The spread between BTPs and Bunds closed at 124 points, according to ANSA, after touching 127. La Stampa headlines European stock markets plunging, with Milan at -2%, oil soaring and the spread at 127. Il Fatto Quotidiano writes that yields on French ten-year bonds are at their highest since 2002, with the differential against Bunds at 170 points, and that the Italian spread is widening too.
