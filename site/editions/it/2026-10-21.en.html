Council of Ministers approves the demonstrations decree: a new offence of violent participation with sentences of two to five years, and preventive detention applies to minors too

Meloni claims the principle “you break it, you pay”; the rule giving judicial police powers to soldiers is dropped after objections from the Quirinale.

The Council of Ministers on Tuesday, October 20 approved a decree-law on security and juvenile crime after the street disorder in Milan and other cities, report LaPresse, ANSA and DIRE. Giorgia Meloni, in the video released at the end of the meeting, said: “The offence of violent participation in a demonstration is created” and confirmed preventive detention for minors as well, according to LaPresse and DIRE. ANSA writes that the new offence carries sentences of two to five years in prison and a fine of up to 20,000 euros and that the decree consists of eight articles. Il Messaggero quotes the premier’s line: “Unacceptable violence, let no one downplay it. You break it, you pay”; according to Stampa Parlamento, if the offender is a minor, the parents answer for it. Also according to Stampa Parlamento and Italpress, the detention of minors makes immediately operational a rule already approved by Parliament that presumes the capacity to understand and to will of young people aged 14 to 18, unless proven otherwise, and LaPresse indicates a duration of up to 24 hours.

The rule giving judicial police functions to soldiers, backed by Defense Minister Guido Crosetto, was dropped after objections from the Quirinale, write LaPresse, Il Fatto Quotidiano and Quotidiano.net, which headlines that the Colle “rejects” those powers. LaPresse adds that 500 more soldiers remain in Milan until the end of the year. In the afternoon Il Fatto Quotidiano had written that the detention of minors was about to drop out of the text after discussions with the Colle; in the evening video Meloni instead confirmed it. Giuseppe Tiani, of the police union Siap, had rejected during the day “this authoritarian twist” concerning soldiers with police powers, reports Il Fatto Quotidiano. TGCom24 publishes a summary of the measures with preventive detention, minors held criminally liable and the new offence. The decree-law enters into force on publication in the Gazzetta Ufficiale and must be converted by Parliament within 60 days.

Economy · Digital services✓ 7 outlets · cause of the outage not uniformly confirmed

A Google Cloud outage in London knocks out Revolut and disrupts other British banks for hours

Google reported on Tuesday, October 20 network failures at “several” cloud services in Europe; the problem involves the group’s data center in London, ANSA reports. Revolut is among the services affected: according to Quartz, citing the Wall Street Journal, card payments, bank transfers, withdrawals, currency exchanges and share trading went out of service, and the digital bank stated that customers’ money and data are safe. Quartz writes that Lloyds said its app was accessible while some web services had problems; Protos reports outage reports at HSBC and Bank of Scotland on Downdetector as well. AP headlines that the outage hit British banks, and RSI writes that the problems also reached Switzerland. On the cause the accounts do not coincide: Protos points to an electrical event in an availability zone of Google Cloud’s London region, while Quartz writes that Google did not give a cause. Perfil reports that the outage lasted 7 hours and 52 minutes and affected services in the United Kingdom, Germany, Spain and France. In the evening NU.nl wrote that all Revolut services had been restored. The association Adusbef, according to ANSA, demands immediate compensation for users and speaks of legal action if no answers are given, calling Tuesday’s the second anomalous case in a month.

Economy · Air transport✓ 4 outlets · actual size of the increases cannot be verified

O’Leary (Ryanair) forecasts the biggest rise in European airfares ever: up to 20% more in 2027

Michael O’Leary, chief executive of Ryanair, on Tuesday, October 20 forecast a record increase in airfares in Europe next year, up to 20% more in 2027, report ANSA, Il Sole 24 Ore and the Guardian. ANSA links the increase to the war between the United States and Iran and to costs that airlines pass on to passengers. The Guardian writes that the conflict in the Middle East pushes up the cost of jet fuel and that many airlines are not hedged against these rises. BFMTV quotes O’Leary’s line “You are going to see the biggest rise in airfares in history” and indicates a range of increases between 10% and 20%, attributed to the surge in kerosene, the end of the contracts that protect airlines from price rises and the reduction in the number of flights. Il Sole 24 Ore headlines “record airfare increases, up to 20% more in 2027”, in line with ANSA. The Guardian, which speaks of an increase of up to a maximum of 20%, describes the forecast as a ceiling and not as a firm figure for every route.

Economy · Steel✓ 5 outlets · date of the United Sections’ decision not known

Ex-Ilva: the Court of Cassation’s Prosecutor General’s Office opposes stopping the hot area and the United Sections reserve their decision

The civil United Sections of the Court of Cassation on Tuesday, October 20 examined the companies’ appeals against the Milan Court of Appeal decree imposing a stop to the hot area of the Taranto plant, and reserved their decision, report Rai News and Quotidiano di Puglia. The Prosecutor General’s Office, through Advocate General Birritteri, said no to the stop and asked for a balance between protecting health and production, write ANSA and La Stampa. ANSA reports his words: “The danger of asbestos is beyond dispute but the Milan Court of Appeal infers the danger and does not establish it.” The decree, according to Affaritaliani’s reconstructions, requires the blast furnaces to be shut down by October and was upheld by the Milan Court of Appeal, which rejected the request for a suspension. Affaritaliani also writes that, while the Court of Cassation reserves its decision, talks are under way at the ministry on cassa integrazione for 8,622 workers. Quotidiano di Puglia and La Stampa report that the case concerns the appeals of Ilva and Acciaierie d’Italia, while the citizens of Taranto support the Milan ruling.

World · Ukraine✓ 6 outlets · responsibility and duration of the slowdown not clarified

The Wall Street Journal: Musk ordered the slowing of the military version of Starlink used by Ukraine; Hegseth says Washington has not withdrawn access

Elon Musk last week ordered the slowing of the military version of the Starlink satellite service that Ukraine relies on, the Wall Street Journal reports on Tuesday, October 20, citing US officials and people familiar with the matter, according to Reuters, CNBC and ANSA. ANSA reports that Musk “authorized it and discussed it with Trump.” La Stampa writes that the slowdown made some offensive drones unusable, and Zeit Online reports that the SpaceX founder reportedly approved the limit and discussed the matter with the American president.

The Hill indicates that the slowed service is Starshield, the military version of Starlink, and that the shutdown has damaged Ukraine’s ability to respond to Russian attacks. According to The Hill, Republican senator Thom Tillis asked Musk to explain the loss of data speed that Kyiv needs for military operations. According to Reuters, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said on Tuesday that Washington has not withdrawn Ukraine’s access to Starlink, while pointing to the terms of service that Kyiv must respect.
