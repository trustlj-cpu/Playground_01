News · VaticanPress ✓ 12 outlets · date of the operation not announced

The Pope: “I have a nodule on my lung, I have decided to have surgery.” The announcement at the Angelus, Italy rallies around Leo XIV

On Sunday Leo XIV told the faithful that doctors have found a nodule on his lung and that he will have surgery in the coming days. The date has not been announced: today details are expected from the Holy See and the Gemelli Polyclinic.

On Sunday, October 11, Pope Leo XIV told the faithful at the Angelus that doctors have found a nodule on his lung and that he has decided to have surgery “in the coming days” (ANSA, Gazzetta dello Sport, RaiNews, Reuters). The date of the operation has not been announced: NPR writes that the surgery is expected this week, and some foreign outlets, such as Channels TV, speak of a small nodule at an early stage. Tgcom24 observes that no pontiff had described his own illness in such detail before the faithful, and recalls that John Paul II announced at the Angelus one of his hospital stays. The Pope’s doctor is Luigi Carbone, who has already assisted Pope Francis and accompanies him on trips (ANSA, Tgcom24). Tgcom24 recalls the precedents of John Paul II and Paul VI, both of whom had operations, and Il Fatto Quotidiano retraces how, from Pius XI to today, the way the Vatican talks about the health of Popes has changed.

Reactions came within a few hours. The President of the Republic Sergio Mattarella wrote that “the Italian people are close to you” (la Repubblica); prime minister Giorgia Meloni said she is “close to the Holy Father” and “heartened by his serenity” (Tgcom24, ANSA). Messages of closeness also came from Cardinal Matteo Zuppi, president of the Italian Episcopal Conference (Cei), and from the Italian Islamic community, while the friars of Assisi called for prayers for the Pope (Il Fatto Quotidiano, Tgcom24, ANSA). Tgcom24 speaks of bipartisan closeness from politics, from the Government to the opposition. The news was immediately picked up by international outlets too, from Reuters to NBC News and the Guardian.

ANSA and la Repubblica explained how a lung nodule is discovered and in which cases the decision to operate is made; a pulmonologist told Il Fatto Quotidiano that “if it can be operated on, that is good news.” No source speaks of a diagnosis. According to Tgcom24 the Gemelli Polyclinic in Rome has prepared a mini apartment to receive the Pope, but neither the hospital nor the Holy See has confirmed it. It is not clear how the agenda will change: the Buenos Aires Times recalls that the operation comes about a month before a planned visit, while La Stampa links it to a trip and to appointments at the top of the Vatican. Today attention turns to the date of the operation, to a possible medical bulletin from the Holy See Press Office and to the first changes in the Pope’s audiences. Three questions remain that the Vatican will have to answer: in which hospital he will be operated on, with what kind of procedure and with what recovery times. As far as the sources we have read show, the Holy See has not yet released a medical bulletin, and DailyDrop does not speculate about the Pope’s state of health.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 8 outlets · toll of the second attack from the Saudi civil aviation authority

Riyadh, new attack on the airport the day after the massacre: one dead and 11 injured. Dammam hit too

On Sunday a second attack hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, the day after the Houthi missile that on Saturday had killed 12 people and injured 309 (Reuters, AP, Washington Post). According to the Saudi civil aviation authority the new raid caused one death and 11 injuries, with a fire in a terminal and an explosion heard in the city centre as well (ANSA, Tgcom24, FinancialJuice, Livemint). In the evening the airport of Dammam, on the Persian Gulf, was also targeted (Asharq Al-Awsat, Handelsblatt, HLN), while the Saudi-led coalition says it shot down a drone in the eastern province. The Houthis claim a series of raids on the airport and on Saudi energy facilities and renew their warning to airlines and travellers: do not use the Kingdom’s airports (ANSA, Reuters). Houthi TV reports an attack by Saudi fighter jets on Sanaa airport. Giorgia Meloni condemned the attacks, the European Union called them a “terrifying act of terrorism” and Donald Trump judged them unacceptable (ANSA, Tgcom24). The British embassy advises against non-essential travel to Jeddah, Dammam and all Saudi airports except Medina (FinancialJuice, citing its post on X); the foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia and Iran have spoken, and Bloomberg writes that oil is rising.

World · Ukraine and energyPress ✓ 9 outlets · the truce is confirmed by neither Kyiv nor Moscow

Trump announces an “energy truce” between Russia and Ukraine: Kyiv knew nothing about it, Moscow does not confirm and hits a power plant

On Sunday Donald Trump announced that Russia and Ukraine have agreed an “energy truce” with immediate effect, warning: “Don’t break it” (Reuters, USA Today, CNBC, Livemint). Volodymyr Zelensky replied that he knew nothing about it (“news to me,” NBC), that he was waiting for details from the United States and that he was ready to support the truce; shortly afterwards he said that a power plant in Kharkiv had just been hit (Ukrainska Pravda, ANSA, ABC, The Hill). La Stampa writes that Moscow denies the agreement and that the trip to Russia by the American envoys Witkoff and Kushner has been suspended; Axios reports that Russia does not accept the truce (a report from a single outlet). British prime minister Andy Burnham and Canadian prime minister Mark Carney back the idea, while in the United States Republican criticism of the diesel deal with Putin is growing: one senator argues it could be illegal (Reuters, The Hill). The day before, Trump had asked Kyiv to change president; Zelensky replied that “it is up to Ukrainians to choose their own leader” (ANSA).

Sport · F1, Serie A and MotoGPPress ✓ 6 outlets · results from ANSA, Gazzetta and agencies

Verstappen wins in Singapore, Leclerc 2nd and Antonelli 3rd: first world-title match point at Austin. In Serie A Lazio and Inter lead

Max Verstappen won the Singapore Grand Prix for the first time, ahead of Charles Leclerc; third was Kimi Antonelli, who at Austin could become world champion as early as October 25, according to the Gazzetta dello Sport: an Italian has not won the world title in 73 years (ANSA, la Repubblica, Il Fatto Quotidiano). Lewis Hamilton lost the podium after failing to follow the call to pit, a mistake that, according to Fred Vasseur, cost Ferrari a double podium finish; he apologized after feeling unwell (The Straits Times, FMT, ANSA). In Serie A Lazio beat Monza 3-1 and draw level with Inter at the top; Roma drew 0-0 at Como with Malen sent off, Sassuolo-Milan and Cagliari-Juventus both ended 1-1 (with Spalletti and Pisacane sent off) and Bologna won 2-1 at Lecce in the first league match (ANSA, la Repubblica). In MotoGP, in Indonesia, Marco Bezzecchi won on the Aprilia and Jorge Martín is back at the top of the World Championship (ANSA, BBC, Al Jazeera, L’Équipe).

Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 5 outlets · closes of Friday, October 9

On Friday Piazza Affari +0.91% at 49,746 and Wall Street higher. Today the exchanges reopen with Riyadh and oil under watch, and the extraordinary Mps board meeting

Stock markets are closed over the weekend: the latest data are those of Friday, October 9. The Ftse Mib, the Piazza Affari index, closed at 49,746 points, up 0.91%. On Wall Street the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83% to 51,654.95. WTI crude closed at 91.85 dollars a barrel (+0.39%) and gold at 4,216.3 dollars an ounce (+1.43%). Bloomberg speaks of a rally ahead of the quarterly earnings season.

In recent hours Bloomberg writes that oil is rising because of the attacks in the Middle East and that the dollar is strengthening slightly. Today in Italy the board of directors of Mps meets in extraordinary session after Intesa’s moves, ahead of the shareholders’ meeting (ANSA, Il Sole 24 ORE, la Repubblica): the sources we have read do not yet explain what those moves are. On the industrial front, in Paris Stellantis presented 60 products and 9 concept cars, including an electric Citroën 2 CV (ANSA, Il Sole 24 ORE); ANSA also writes that the European Union will limit imports of Chinese hybrid cars to 350,000 a year from December 1.
