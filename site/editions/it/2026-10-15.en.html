News · VaticanPress ✓ 6 outlets · the lymph node results are not yet known

Leo XIV undergoes lung surgery: it was an adenocarcinoma, the right lower lobe removed. The post-operative course is regular

The operation at the Gemelli lasted about three hours in the operating room, with a minimally invasive technique and no complications. On Wednesday the Pope got up, ate and began respiratory physiotherapy. To know whether further treatment is needed, we have to wait for the lymph node examination.

On Sunday, October 11, at the Angelus, Leo XIV had announced that he would undergo surgery (la Repubblica); Cardinal Pietro Parolin, Secretary of State, had called the news “a bolt from the blue” (Il Fatto Quotidiano). On Tuesday, October 13, the Pope was operated on at the Gemelli Polyclinic in Rome. In the evening the doctors said that the lung nodule announced on Sunday was a lung adenocarcinoma: a malignant tumor (Tgcom24, la Repubblica, La Stampa, Il Fatto Quotidiano). The surgeons removed the right lower lobe of the lung by video-assisted thoracoscopy, a minimally invasive procedure carried out through small incisions (La Stampa, Tgcom24). The Pope spent about three hours in the operating room, about ninety minutes of which for the operation itself. Reports speak of a successful operation with no complications (la Repubblica, Il Fatto Quotidiano, Tgcom24). The team consisted of seven doctors and was led by Stefano Margaritora, director of Thoracic Surgery at the Gemelli and an expert in minimally invasive techniques (ANSA).

On Wednesday the Holy See reported a quiet night (ANSA, RaiNews, Tgcom24, La Stampa). In the afternoon the Vatican Press Office spoke of a regular post-operative course: Leo XIV got up, ate and began respiratory physiotherapy, and “his mood is good” (ANSA, Tgcom24). According to Il Fatto Quotidiano he also walked around the room and concelebrated Mass in a hall of the hospital. An expert interviewed by ANSA recalls that the first 24-48 hours remain the most delicate. The Pope’s brother, Louis Prevost, said he had received a message in which Leo said he was doing well (ANSA).

The open question is the lymph nodes. Luca Voltolini, a thoracic surgeon at Careggi, in Florence, who is not treating the Pope, explained to la Repubblica that the histological examination takes about two weeks and that only afterwards will it be decided which therapies to adopt; the hospital stay, he added, normally lasts four or five days. La Stampa headlines on “fears over the lymph nodes” and on the uncertainty about further treatment. The Holy See has communicated neither a discharge date nor a treatment plan. Cardinal Sturla, in Uruguay, does not rule out a postponement of the Pope’s visit (ANSA).

La Repubblica writes that the test results were delivered by hand to the Pope’s team, without passing through computers, to protect them from theft. ANSA notes that the Holy See chose to name the illness, a change of pace in Vatican communication. Wishes for a recovery have arrived from many capitals: from Chile to Mexico, from Peru to Colombia (ANSA), and from Donald Trump (la Repubblica). What we do not know: the outcome of the lymph node examination, any later treatment and when Leo XIV will be able to resume his engagements. DailyDrop is following today’s bulletins.

Politics · GovernmentPress ✓ 9 outlets · partisan statements

Meloni in the Senate: the US-Russia deal on diesel is “risky and legitimizes Moscow.” On the EU budget: “We sign only when it is right for Italy”

On Wednesday morning Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni delivered her statement to the Senate ahead of the European Council of October 15 and 16, repeated in the Chamber of Deputies in the afternoon (ANSA, Sky TG24, Il Fatto Quotidiano). The most discussed passage concerns the agreement between the United States and Russia on diesel exports, announced by Donald Trump. For Meloni it is a “very risky” choice that “legitimizes Moscow” while it bombs Ukraine, and its economic benefits are doubtful (ANSA, Tgcom24, la Repubblica, Reuters). Moscow replied that it is “none of” its “business” (Il Messaggero, Sky TG24). On the EU multiannual budget the prime minister said that Italy will sign only when the deal is fair and does not accept cuts to the Common Agricultural Policy and cohesion (ANSA); according to ANSA, the Élysée rules out a deal without substantial resources for Europe. On energy, “the greatest burden” for households and businesses, she called for a coordinated and rapid response (ANSA, Teleborsa, RaiNews). She repeated that support for Kyiv does not change and that, if France, Germany and the United Kingdom want to negotiate for Ukraine, an official mandate would be needed (ANSA). In the Chamber she attacked the M5S over the new electoral law: “They insult me, but under the new rules Conte would never have reached Palazzo Chigi” (Corriere Roma, Il Fatto Quotidiano). She blamed the high fuel prices on the war with Iran (HuffPost Italia, Tgcom24). She rejects the accusation of leading a government of “warmongers” and assures that defense funds will not be taken away from healthcare (ANSA, Tgcom24). The Parliamentary Budget Office warns, however, that meeting NATO commitments will require cuts or other measures (Il Fatto Quotidiano).

News · RomePress ✓ 8 outlets · the two minors are reported to the authorities, not tried

Rome, the minicar attacks: two boys aged 16 and 17 identified, they reportedly confessed

On Wednesday carabinieri and local police identified the two youths who on October 4 and 5 allegedly attacked at least six passers-by between Monteverde and Testaccio aboard a black minicar. They are 16 and 17 and have been reported to the authorities; the car was seized overnight (ANSA, la Repubblica, Sky TG24, Tgcom24, RaiNews, AGI, La Stampa). The most seriously injured victim is the actor Davide Iacopini, with a guarded prognosis (RaiNews, la Repubblica); the number of attacks varies between outlets, six for ANSA and Il Fatto Quotidiano, eight for RaiNews. Before the public prosecutor the two reportedly admitted the attacks, speaking of a game and of boredom: “We did it for fun, we were bored” (ANSA, Tgcom24, Il Fatto Quotidiano). The charge is attempted murder (RaiNews, La Stampa). Investigators got to them through surveillance cameras and a sticker with a cobra on the hood, which a mechanic recognized (la Repubblica, La Stampa, Il Fatto Quotidiano). One of the two is the son of a carabiniere and is the owner of the vehicle (ANSA, RaiNews). La Repubblica writes that the parents are in shock and that the two reportedly went back to school as if nothing had happened. The same day the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport announced a crackdown on modified minicars, with personalized license plates (ANSA, Tgcom24).

World · UkrainePress ✓ 9 outlets · the “energy truce” is not holding

Ukraine, another night of raids on energy: 600,000 people without power. Kyiv denounces limits on Starlink, Zelensky goes to London

In the night between Tuesday and Wednesday Russia hit Ukrainian energy infrastructure for the second night in a row, despite the “energy truce” announced on Sunday by Donald Trump (Il Fatto Quotidiano, Tgcom24, ANSA). The toll is at least six dead and blackouts in Kyiv, Kharkiv and Sumy (ANSA). President Volodymyr Zelensky speaks of at least 600,000 people without electricity and of a “lack of respect” toward the United States never seen before (la Repubblica, Guardian); Moscow says it hit six power plants (ANSA). Kyiv claims that the allies did not warn it (Reuters, BBC, Corriere della Sera) and that SpaceX has limited the Starlink terminals used by the army, blocking some missions (Reuters, Guardian, la Repubblica); it has asked the US, SpaceX and NATO to clarify. Meanwhile Zelensky is in London, where he sees King Charles III and Prime Minister Burnham, before meeting the leaders of the Twenty-Seven (ANSA, la Repubblica). The EU has unlocked 2.8 billion in military aid (ANSA, Il Messaggero). On the opposing side, Russian Deputy Prime Minister Novak said that the first diesel shipments are under way (FinancialJuice, citing Tass). Il Fatto Quotidiano reports that Trump reportedly said that Ukraine needs a new president: we did not find this in other sources. Semafor, picked up by ANSA, writes that Trump is reportedly furious and is considering cutting off intelligence with Kyiv, while the CIA director is reportedly opposed: an unconfirmed report.

Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 7 outlets · closes of Wednesday, October 14

US inflation at 3.7% sends Wall Street down, Milan closes lower. Today Tokyo sheds more than 4%

On Wednesday US consumer prices for September rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% on the year, against the 3.6% expected; petrol was up 9.1% (Bloomberg, Reuters, la Repubblica). After the data Wall Street closed lower, dragged down by chip and artificial intelligence stocks, and the S&P 500 ended its longest calm stretch since 2020 (Bloomberg, Financial Times, la Repubblica). Bets on further rate hikes by the Federal Reserve have increased; according to FinancialJuice, the figure was judged “discouraging” by Fed official Goolsbee and worse than hoped by Barkin. We do not have a closing level confirmed by two sources for the US indexes.

In Milan the Ftse Mib closed down 0.61%, at 49,753 points according to ANSA; Borsa Italiana speaks of a decline weighed down by banks and carmakers. In Europe London gained 0.7%, Paris 0.58% and Frankfurt 0.15% (ANSA). The Btp-Bund spread rose to 108 points, with the yield on the Italian ten-year at 4.54% (ANSA). Brent was trading around $106 in the morning (ANSA). Overnight the selling reached Asia: Tokyo lost more than 4% (ANSA), Taiwan more than 2% (FinancialJuice), and Reuters and the Financial Times speak of a global decline in tech stocks. In Piazza Affari, Avio and Campari shone, Prysmian lost (ANSA).
