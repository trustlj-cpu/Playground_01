Nobel Peace Prize to Navi Pillay. Hours later, the United States sanctions the International Criminal Court

The European Union calls the measures “deplorable” and Italy reaffirms its support for the Court; Israel and Trump attack the prize. Today, Saturday, attention turns to the responses of European governments.

On Friday the Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Peace Prize to the South African jurist Navanethem “Navi” Pillay, 85, “for her commitment to peace and international law” (ABC News, Reuters, AP). Raised under apartheid, Pillay was a judge of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and of the International Criminal Court, then UN High Commissioner for Human Rights from 2008 to 2014 (Le Monde, CBS News). She recently chaired the United Nations commission of inquiry that spoke of “genocide” in Gaza (Le Monde). She told German television that the Nuremberg trials inspired her and that the prize shows how important criminal justice is (Tagesschau). Reactions were harsh: Israel called the choice “grotesque” and an official said that “Goebbels applauds from hell” (ANSA, Deutschlandfunk), while Donald Trump judged it “disgraceful” (ANSA).

A few hours later Washington struck the very institution Pillay has served. The United States imposed sanctions on the International Criminal Court as a whole, Reuters reports in an exclusive, followed by AP and the Guardian. According to the Washington Post the measures aim to cut the Court off from the global financial system and threaten punishment for countries that do business with it. Secretary of State Marco Rubio spoke of a “rogue tribunal” (ANSA). The Court’s president, Tomoko Akane, replied that the institution “firmly rejects” the sanctions, an “attack on the rule of law” (The Hill). The European Union called them “deplorable” and said it was ready to safeguard the Court’s work (ANSA, Euronews); Italy confirmed its confidence in the Court and its opposition to the sanctions (ANSA, Il Resto del Carlino, la Repubblica).

A joint statement by the foreign ministries of Canada, Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom reaffirmed support for the Court (GOV.UK). Pillay said she was shocked by the sanctions but declared that she believes “firmly” in the tribunal (Der Spiegel). The Court is based in The Hague and tries people accused of genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes, when it has jurisdiction; it is not a UN body and has no police force of its own. For this reason its strength depends on the states that carry out arrest warrants and, now, also on the banks that keep working with it. DailyDrop has not yet seen the text of the American order and cannot say which accounts, salaries or suppliers are affected: for now the details come from the news agencies. To watch: whether European governments will use instruments to protect the Court and whether Washington will extend the measures to those who cooperate with The Hague.

World · Energy and UkrainePress ✓ 9 outlets · volumes from Trump’s announcement alone

Trump announces Russian diesel for the US and suspends sanctions. Zelensky: “An investment in the war”

After a phone call with Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social that Russia will supply “immediately” 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the United States and the world market, and further quantities in November (Reuters, CBS News, NOS). The US Treasury Department has granted a temporary authorization to sell Russian diesel: ANSA writes that the sanctions are suspended until April and that Putin confirmed the understanding. The New York Times recalls that Moscow has an export ban still in force, and the Washington Post notes that the volumes are small compared with American demand. Trump spoke of “millions of tonnes” in the coming months: for now that is only his indication. The move eases pressure on Moscow in order to curb prices before the midterm elections (AP). Volodymyr Zelensky replied that it is “an investment in the war” and that Putin will repay it “with more terror” (Reuters, ANSA). Meanwhile in Miami, Ukrainians and Europeans are working on a truce proposal (ANSA).

Mattarella signs the electoral law. Now the debate shifts to appeals and the date of the vote

President of the Republic Sergio Mattarella promulgated the new electoral law on Friday, after the Chamber gave it final approval on Thursday (ANSA, RaiNews, la Repubblica, Il Sole 24 ORE, Il Fatto Quotidiano). Promulgation is the act by which the Head of State signs a law, which takes effect once it is published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale. The papers call it “Stabilicum” or, ironically, “Melonellum” (RaiNews): in short, a proportional system with a strong majority bonus and closed lists. Sky TG24 leads on possible appeals to the Constitutional Court and on the critical points. The date of the elections remains open: on Thursday Fazzolari floated June, while Tajani preferred the end of the legislature. DailyDrop has not yet found an official statement from the Quirinale on the text.

Panama, magnitude 7.6 earthquake: collapses near the epicenter, tsunami alert later lifted

On Friday afternoon an earthquake of magnitude 7.6, according to the US Geological Survey (USGS) cited by Reuters and ANSA, struck southern Panama at a depth of about ten kilometers; some outlets, including NBC News, SCMP and Le Temps, report 7.7. The epicenter is near Tonosí (El Mundo), in a sparsely populated area of Herrera province (NOS). A second shock of magnitude 6.6 followed, with further aftershocks (SCMP, G1). There were collapses near the epicenter (SCMP) and a hotel in Penonomé collapsed, according to Tgcom24; RSI reports various damage and injuries. In the evening ANSA reported that there was no news of victims and that the United States ruled out a tsunami risk, while Die Zeit writes that Panama has lifted the alert. The toll remains provisional.

Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 6 outlets · Wall Street closes from final data

Milan closes at +0.91% at 49,746 points. Spread falls to 109, Tata raises its bid for Iveco

On Friday the Milan stock exchange closed up 0.91% at 49,746 points, with Fincantieri, Amplifon and Banca Generali standing out and STM down (ANSA). Europe closed higher: Frankfurt +1.13%, London +1.06%, Paris +0.95%, with investors looking to next week’s quarterly earnings (ANSA). The spread, meaning the yield difference between Italian and German government bonds, fell to 109 basis points and the yield on the ten-year BTP to 4.57% (ANSA). Gas in Amsterdam rose 1.8% to €80.33 per megawatt-hour (ANSA). On Wall Street the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83% to 51,654.95. The ten-year Treasury yields 5.244%, WTI closed at $91.63 (+0.15%) and gold at $4,219.9 (+1.51%).

Bloomberg notes, however, that beneath the rally in the stock markets, driven by artificial intelligence, a retreat by investors is widening, because of high oil prices and the high cost of money. On the corporate front Tata Motors raised its takeover bid for Iveco by 30 cents, to €14.40 per share, for a maximum outlay of €3.91 billion; Iveco’s board of directors welcomed the higher offer (ANSA, Il Sole 24 ORE). SpaceX’s deal to buy radio frequencies for mobile telephony sent telecom stocks lower, with Deutsche Telekom down 7% (Reuters). Today, Saturday, the Milan exchange is closed and reopens on Monday. In the coming week the spotlight is on US inflation (FinancialJuice) and on the start of quarterly earnings.
