World · Ukraine and energyPress ✓ 9 outlets · death toll differs by source

Trump: “It’s time for a new president for Ukraine.” Moscow applauds, Europe and London stay with Zelensky

The day after the diesel deal with Putin, the US president attacks Zelensky and asks Kyiv to change leader. Today, Sunday, attention turns to contacts among Ukrainians, Europeans and Americans after the Ukrainian delegation left the Miami talks early.

On Saturday Donald Trump said that “it is time for a new president for Ukraine”: Kyiv, he explained, must find a leader “able to reach a deal” (ANSA, Il Sole 24 ORE, RaiNews, BBC). Volodymyr Zelensky, he added, “wants to create problems for the world” and must stop hitting Russian refineries; according to FinancialJuice, Trump also asked Ukraine to hold elections. The words come after the diesel deal: on Friday, at the end of a phone call with Vladimir Putin, Trump had announced that Russia will supply “immediately” 300,000 tonnes of diesel and that the United States is easing sanctions on Moscow (Reuters, AP, ANSA). Zelensky had called it “an investment in the war” (Reuters). The Russian government then announced a partial lifting of the ban on exporting diesel (FinancialJuice), while Germany reiterated that it is keeping the sanctions (Reuters).

Moscow applauded: the Russian negotiator Kirill Dmitriev called Trump’s position “iconic and historic” (ANSA, EADaily) and the Kremlin spoke of “much understanding” on Putin’s part for some American proposals on de-escalation, that is, on reducing tensions (ANSA). Kyiv replied that statements from abroad “confirm that Zelensky is doing his job well” (ANSA). British prime minister Andy Burnham expressed “full solidarity” with Zelensky and will meet him in person in the coming days (ANSA, British government). The High Representative of the European Union for foreign policy, Kaja Kallas, said that stopping the sanctions on Russian diesel “finances Moscow’s war,” and Brussels reiterated that it will continue to reduce energy dependence on Russia (ANSA). Meanwhile in Miami Americans, Ukrainians and Europeans were working on a truce proposal: the Ukrainian delegation left the meetings early and La Stampa reports its anger (“they treated us like fools”).

On the ground, Russia bombed Zaporizhzhia, a city in south-eastern Ukraine. The toll is not clear-cut: 12 dead according to AGI, 17 according to Tgcom24, up to 20 or 21 for other outlets, including three children (ANSA, Guardian, The Straits Times). Zelensky spoke of a “thank you” from Putin for the lifting of sanctions (ANSA, Il Sole 24 ORE). On the price of diesel the effects are uncertain: Bloomberg and several analysts say the deal is unlikely to bring American prices down, while in Italy, ANSA writes, diesel is falling and the idea of sliding excise duties, that is, a tax that adjusts to prices, has receded. DailyDrop has not seen the text of the American authorization and cannot say what volumes are planned beyond the first cargo; Washington’s threat to cut off intelligence with Kyiv is reported for now by a single agency and is in the “Unverified” box. To watch: the meeting between Burnham and Zelensky and the European Union’s response.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 8 outlets · provisional toll from the Saudi authority

Houthi missile hits Riyadh airport: at least 12 dead and 309 injured. Trump does not rule out joining Saudi strikes

On Saturday a missile launched by Yemen’s Houthi rebels hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. The Saudi civil aviation authority reports at least 12 dead, four Saudis and eight foreigners including an American citizen, and at least 309 injured (ANSA, AP, la Repubblica, New York Times); early tallies counted 5 and then about 50 injured, so the numbers may still rise. The airport was closed and the embassies of the United States and the United Kingdom advised their nationals to avoid the area (ANSA, FinancialJuice). The Saudi-led coalition attributed the attack to the Houthis, called it a “war crime” and said it had intercepted another missile at al-Kharj; a projectile fell near Dammam airport (ANSA, FinancialJuice). Donald Trump called the attack “a terrible thing” and said the United States could join the Saudi strikes: “we’ll decide” (ANSA, La Stampa). In the background is the conflict with Iran: US Central Command said it had disabled a cargo ship trying to run the naval blockade on Iran in the Gulf of Oman (Centcom, via FinancialJuice). The organizers of the big Saudi summits say the conferences are going ahead (Reuters).

News · Bad weatherPress ✓ 4 outlets · Sunday bulletin from MeteoWeb

Bad weather in the South: streams overflow in Lipari, landslides around Reggio Calabria. Civil Protection alert for six Regions today as well

On Saturday cloudbursts and flooding hit the South (ANSA, la Repubblica). In Lipari, in the Aeolian Islands, streams overflowed and mud invaded streets and houses (ANSA, Il Giornale di Lipari); in the Reggio Calabria area a “cloudburst” caused landslides and damage, especially in Montebello Jonico (ANSA). In Bari water entered the Basilica of San Nicola (ANSA) and in the Salento schools were closed, including those in Lecce, because of the orange alert (la Repubblica, City of Lecce). On the Maiella, in Abruzzo, mountain rescue and firefighters intervened for five hikers in difficulty (ANSA). For today, Sunday the 11th, the Civil Protection reports an alert for strong thunderstorms, intense rain and risk of flooding in six Regions (MeteoWeb); DailyDrop does not list them because it read only the headline of the bulletin. Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani expressed solidarity with the residents of Lipari on social media (Il Giornale di Lipari).

Sport · Serie A and cyclingPress ✓ 6 outlets · results from ANSA and Gazzetta

Inter and Napoli win 3-0, Nerazzurri alone at the top. Seixas, 20, wins the Giro di Lombardia

In the sixth round of Serie A, Inter beat Parma 3-0 at San Siro with goals from Sucic, Calhanoglu from a penalty and Thuram, and move alone to the top of the table (ANSA, Corriere della Sera, La Gazzetta dello Sport, La Stampa). Napoli beat Frosinone 3-0 at the Maradona: a brace from De Bruyne, the first from a penalty, and a goal from Lang (ANSA, La Gazzetta dello Sport); there was controversy over the penalty, awarded for a foul on Højlund (Sportmediaset, La Gazzetta dello Sport). In cycling the Frenchman Paul Seixas, 20 years and 16 days old, won the Giro di Lombardia and is the youngest ever: third was the Italian Giulio Ciccone, behind Mas (ANSA, La Gazzetta dello Sport, Reuters). Off the field, Massini is the new president of the AIA, the referees’ association: he won the runoff with 461 votes out of 912 (ANSA); Cristiano Ronaldo has been provisionally suspended from the Portugal national team after leaving the training camp (Reuters, Il Sole 24 ORE).

Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 6 outlets · Friday closes from final data

On Friday Piazza Affari +0.91% at 49,746; Wall Street ends the week higher. On Monday the exchanges reopen with Riyadh and oil in the spotlight

Stock markets are closed over the weekend: the latest data are those of Friday, October 9. Piazza Affari closed up 0.91% at 49,746 points (ANSA); the spread, the difference in yield between the BTP and German government bonds, was at 109 basis points and the ten-year BTP at 4.57%, while gas in Amsterdam rose 1.8% to 80.33 euros per megawatt-hour (ANSA). On Wall Street the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow Jones 0.83% to 51,654.95. The ten-year Treasury yields 5.244% (+1.3 basis points), WTI, the benchmark American oil, closed at 91.85 dollars a barrel (+0.39%) and gold at 4,216.3 dollars an ounce (+1.43%). The euro is worth 1.1206 dollars (−0.09%).

Bloomberg observes that strong corporate results are supporting American stocks and that debt issuance linked to artificial intelligence continues despite rising yields, with SpaceX and Broadcom preparing big deals. According to the Financial Times, Reuters reports, Nvidia is in talks to invest more in Reflection AI, an American AI start-up, or to buy it; the companies have not confirmed. The weekend brings two factors for the markets. Analysts cited by ANSA recall that oil is weighed down by the Middle East more than by the lifting of sanctions on Russian diesel, and on Saturday the attack on Riyadh arrived. Then there is Hurricane Isaias, which made landfall in Florida: ANSA reports four dead and Bloomberg writes that it has halted almost three quarters of oil production in the Gulf of Mexico; the Department of Energy approved, according to FinancialJuice, an exchange of up to 4 million barrels from the strategic reserve to ease the disruption. On Monday the 12th in the US it is Columbus Day; in the coming week the spotlight is on American inflation and the start of quarterly earnings (FinancialJuice).
