Wall Street falls: OpenAI’s numbers weigh on tech stocks, 10-year Treasury holds at 5.23%

Nasdaq −1.25%, S&P 500 −0.47%, Dow Jones +0.10%. Oil rises by more than 3% and the Fed talks of further hikes. Milan reopens on Friday morning (Oct. 9) from Thursday’s −1.35%.

On Thursday Wall Street closed mixed, with tech stocks under pressure: the S&P 500 lost 0.47% to 7,765.36 points, the Nasdaq 1.25% to 27,193.34, while the Dow Jones gained 0.10% to 51,231.64. Reuters described a session weighed down by rising yields and oil, which have revived fears about inflation. Bloomberg tied the slump in chip makers to OpenAI’s numbers: according to the Financial Times the company’s annualized revenue would be about $20 billion below what had previously been indicated, and ANSA, citing the paper, speaks of 50 billion, below expectations. DailyDrop found no direct confirmation from the company: the figures remain attributed to the Financial Times.

The point is that OpenAI is the customer on which much of the spending on chips and data centers depends. If its revenue grows less than expected, it becomes harder to justify the debt that the big tech groups are piling up to build computing capacity: Bloomberg spoke of an “AI debt race” that operators are repricing. In the same session the Fed made its voice heard. Christopher Waller said more rate hikes are needed, though with “flexibility” on the pace (Reuters); Alberto Musalem, president of the St. Louis Fed, indicated that rates should rise over the next six to nine months (Bloomberg). The ten-year Treasury yield closed at 5.231%, 4.6 basis points lower than the previous day, after the 30-year bond auction drew solid demand (Reuters); the 30-year fixed US mortgage rate touched 7.40%, a level that, Reuters notes, still holds back demand for homes. The auction’s allotment yield, 5.618% against 5.308% at the previous one, shows how much the market demands to lend to the government over the long term (FinancialJuice).

On oil, WTI gained 3.6% and settled around $91.5 (ANSA: 91.49); Brent closed at $104.28, up 4.07%, according to FinancialJuice. Gold closed at about $4,157 (+0.2%). Among other financial news, American Express was fined $350 million for an inadequate anti-money-laundering program (Reuters, Financial Times). The price increases are tied to the tensions in the Gulf, covered alongside this story. In Europe the day had already gone badly: Piazza Affari closed at −1.35% at 49,298 points, Frankfurt at −1.2%, with oil pushing the indexes down and government bonds under strain (ANSA). The spread between BTPs and Bunds, however, fell to 111 basis points. Christine Lagarde explained to the Eurogroup that the rise in yields depends on global factors. On Friday (Oct. 9) the question is whether Europe will reopen following Wall Street or whether relief on government bonds will prevail. The signal to watch remains the Treasury: a ten-year yield stably above 5% makes mortgages and debt more expensive for everyone, in Italy too.

Gulf: Houthis hit Riyadh, Iran threatens Hormuz. Trump: no attack before the vote

The Houthis claimed a new attack on Riyadh’s airport, and Reuters reported smoke visible on an aircraft at the airport. Lufthansa and Indian carriers have suspended flights to the Saudi capital (Reuters). Turkey is weighing defensive support for Saudi Arabia but rules out an offensive role; Syria is considering help in the war in Yemen (Reuters). Minister Guido Crosetto specified that there is “no new mission” for Italy in Arabia, only a redeployment (ANSA). US production in the Gulf of Mexico is partly halted by Hurricane Isaias, which is approaching the coast (Reuters), and this adds to the supply shock. On the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters writes that Iran threatens to block other routes and that the risk of attacks on tankers is growing; ANSA speaks of passage “in a trickle.” Washington has imposed new sanctions on Iran’s shadow fleet, while Shell has partly restarted the Pearl plant in Qatar (Reuters). Putin, receiving Iranian president Pezeshkian, assured him of Russian support (FinancialJuice, single source). Donald Trump said the United States will not attack Iran before the November elections (Reuters, Financial Times, ANSA, RaiNews). The New York Times instead writes of a Pentagon plan for three days of raids: there is only one source and the government denies it, so we keep it among the unverified rumors.

Former Ilva: hot area shuts down from Oct. 9. Unions: national strike from Monday

Acciaierie d’Italia has started the shutdown of Taranto’s hot area, in effect from Oct. 9, and the cassa integrazione procedures, ANSA and RaiNews report. The group also announced the temporary stoppage of the Genoa and Novi Ligure plants. The unions responded by calling a 24-hour national strike at all sites starting Monday (ANSA). Undersecretary Alfredo Mantovano announced a decree-law with protections for workers for the next Council of Ministers. The sticking point remains the plant’s future: the blast furnace shutdown opens months of uncertainty for thousands of families, for the supplier network and for those who depend on Italian steel.

World · United StatesPress ✓ 5 outlets · execution date from Military Times and NBC

Fort Hood: Hasan’s firing squad execution to be livestreamed. Set for December 3

The Pentagon said that the execution by firing squad of Nidal Malik Hasan, the former Army major who killed 13 people at Fort Hood in 2009, will be livestreamed, report Reuters, AP, ANSA, the Financial Times and la Repubblica. The date set is December 3, at Fort Hood, Texas, according to Military Times and NBC News; the order was approved by Trump on October 2. It would be the first US military execution in over sixty years (Death Penalty Information Center). Hasan can still file appeals in the federal civilian courts, which could delay the date. We have not yet found details on the technical arrangements for the broadcast.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 4 outlets · raid figures from Reuters and ANSA

Ukraine: Rubio speaks of stalemate, negotiators in Miami on Oct. 9. 33 dead in Kramatorsk

Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the war in Ukraine as “at a dangerous standstill”; the Kremlin replied that it agrees with the assessment (Reuters). On Friday US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner meet a Ukrainian delegation in Miami, trying to turn ideas into a single proposal: security guarantees and a reconstruction plan are on the table, and advisers from the United Kingdom, France and Germany are also invited (Reuters, via Internazionale; TASS). For Kyiv there will be the head of the presidential office Kyrylo Budanov, Rustem Umerov and David Arakhamia.

On the ground, a Russian raid hit two buses in Kramatorsk, in eastern Ukraine, with at least 33 dead (Reuters, ANSA). Ukraine says it has hit Russia’s largest refinery, while drones damaged Yandex’s data center: the company does not know whether it can restore it (Reuters). The picture is that of a war without breakthroughs, in which cities continue to pay the highest price.
