Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon: “Stock gambling for the local elections with the National Pension”… demands apology from President Lee Jae-myung

In a Facebook post on the 8th about the stock plunge, Mayor Oh criticized the government for using the National Pension as seed money to expand the game. No official reaction from the presidential office has been confirmed.

Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon posted on Facebook on the 8th and asked the government to answer for the recent plunge in the stock market. Mayor Oh claimed that the government used the National Pension as seed money to expand the game and opened the way to inflate the stakes with high-risk leveraged products, and demanded that President Lee Jae-myung personally apologize to individual investors and come up with remedies. The KOSPI fell 6.02% on the 6th alone, closing at 5,059.69. Leveraged products are financial products built to track twice the daily move of an index or a stock, so gains and losses both double.

Mayor Oh took issue with two things: the National Pension’s decision on May 28 to raise its target weight for domestic stocks from 14.9% to 20.8%, and the listing on May 27 of products that track twice the daily moves of Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. Mayor Oh argued that the two decisions coincided six days before the local elections. Mayor Oh also said that circuit breakers (a system that briefly halts trading when prices plunge) were triggered 11 times on the KOSPI market this year, 9 of them after the local elections. A demand was added to disclose who led the weight increase and the product listings and why they were rushed. The government has explained that the move is meant to bring the asset weight in line with the target, reflecting that the actual share of domestic stocks held has risen. The presidential office’s official reaction has not been confirmed, and no official conclusion on the cause of the plunge has come out.

Cheong Wa Dae National Situation Office head replaced… Kim Jung-woo resigns, Kim Tae-geun, secretary for local autonomy development, tapped as successor

Kim Jung-woo, head of the Cheong Wa Dae National Situation Office, announced the resignation on SNS on the 8th, writing, “I am leaving Cheong Wa Dae, where graduating is harder than enrolling.” A former Ministry of Economy and Finance official, Kim Jung-woo served in the 20th National Assembly and as head of the Public Procurement Service, and in the last presidential election was senior vice chair of the Digital Asset Committee in candidate Lee Jae-myung’s election campaign committee. The tenure was about 1 year and 4 months. Kim Tae-geun, secretary for local autonomy development, has been tapped as successor. Kim Tae-geun is a figure who did civic activism in Ulsan, served as the first chair of the Ulsan Autonomous Police Committee and secretary general of Ulsan Citizens’ Solidarity, and joined Cheong Wa Dae about four and a half months ago. The National Situation Office is the unit that reports domestic and foreign developments to the president around the clock. This change followed President Lee Jae-myung’s naming of Choi In-ho, former president of the Housing & Urban Guarantee Corporation, as successor to the senior secretary for political affairs. No successor has yet been decided for the post of secretary for local autonomy development left vacant by Kim Tae-geun’s move. In the post, Kim Jung-woo said efforts for the success of the People’s Sovereignty Government would continue, and mentioned nothing else as a reason for resigning.

Labor disputes fall this year but lost working days jump 45.2%… calls for profit sharing spread

The Korea Employers Federation (KEF) said on the 8th that as of the end of September this year, labor disputes numbered 88, fewer than a year earlier, but lost working days rose 45.2% to 489,190 from 336,800 last year. Lost working days is the total of days not worked because of strikes and the like, so even when the number of cases falls, it rises if strikes are long and large. The KEF pointed out that strikes in major manufacturing such as autos and shipbuilding widened the loss. Hyundai Motor settled its wage and collective bargaining talks on Aug. 31, but suffered production disruption from strikes in the meantime.

Calls for profit sharing, asking that the source of performance bonuses be set at a fixed share of profit, are also spreading. The Hyundai Motor union is reported to be demanding 30% of net profit, the HD Hyundai Heavy Industries union at least 30% of operating profit, and the HL Mando union 15% of operating profit. The KEF said that refusal of overtime and special-shift work and partial strikes continue at HL Mando, raising concern about disruption of the supply chain, including subcontractors.
