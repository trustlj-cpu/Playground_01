KOSPI plunges 3.5% to close at 5,533.77… Kosdaq also down 3.6%

A day after a rebound, another drop of more than 3%, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix dragging the index down

Yonhap reported that the KOSPI opened on the 22nd down 1.14% at 5,670.11, widened its losses in the afternoon and ended the regular session down a sharp 3.5%. The close tallied by the Korea Exchange is 5,533.77. Korea Economic Daily reported that as the index slid toward the 5,600 level during the session, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, known together as ‘Samjeon-Nix,’ led the decline, and Yonhap, SBS and Korea Economic Daily all reported that the Kosdaq also fell 3.6%. A drop of more than 3% is not a first for the KOSPI this month. SBS Biz reported that on the 12th the index also fell 3.65% to close at 6,384, which means the index level has dropped by more than 800 points in ten days. That morning the Bank of Korea’s Monetary Policy Board held the base rate at 3.00%, and Governor Shin Hyun-song’s press conference followed in the afternoon.

Overseas factors piled on. Bloomberg TV reported that Asian stocks fell across the board the same day, led by tech shares, and that Chinese stocks fell by the most since the so-called ‘Liberation Day.’ Bloomberg also reported that as attacks by Yemen’s Houthi rebels on Saudi Arabia continue, the United States has ordered some diplomats to leave Saudi Arabia and Brent crude has topped $110 a barrel. In the U.S., earnings reports showed Meta raising its AI investment outlook and Tesla cutting its capital spending outlook. In its closing broadcast the same day, Sampro TV’s ‘Closing Bell Live’ diagnosed a ‘supply-demand gap,’ rather than earnings, as what widened the drop. Governor Shin said at the press conference that “consumption is temporarily reduced by stock-price adjustments,” Yonhap and Korea Economic Daily reported.

President Lee: “Giving up command and supervision of SCIA is grounds for impeachment”… opposition retorts “declaration of command over investigations”

Yonhap, Chosun Ilbo and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that President Lee Jae-myung posted on X on the 22nd that “the president’s command and supervision of state affairs is both an authority and a responsibility” and that “giving up command and supervision of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency (SCIA) violates the Constitution and the Government Organization Act and is grounds for impeachment.” Chosun Ilbo reported that Lee also used the phrase ‘dereliction of duty.’ The dispute began over whether heads of investigative and prosecuting bodies, such as the SCIA chief, may sit in on Cabinet meetings. Dong-A Ilbo reported that the day before, Lee quoted an answer from Google’s Gemini to rebut the argument against their attendance as ‘sophistry,’ and on the 22nd he wrote again, “I did not quote it because I do not know.” Yonhap reported that conservative opposition parties including the People Power Party criticized this as a ‘declaration of command over investigations,’ and Korea Economic Daily reported that former leader Han Dong-hoon fired back, “exactly the opposite.”

BOK holds base rate at 3.00% in unanimous decision… catching its breath after two hikes

Yonhap reported that the Bank of Korea’s Monetary Policy Board held the base rate at 3.00% on the 22nd, and SBS reported that all 7 board members voted to hold. Kyunghyang Shinmun and Dong-A Ilbo reported that the decision is a ‘breather’ after two straight hikes. According to Yonhap, the board said, “The effects of the hikes need to be reviewed.” Yonhap reported that BOK Governor Shin Hyun-song said at the press conference, “We need to continue the tightening stance,” and that he would check third-quarter GDP and October prices further. Korea Economic Daily reported that Shin said the won is still weaker than in past years on an effective exchange rate basis and that the recent fall in the exchange rate is a process of normalization. Shin said the inflation rate would approach the 2% target around the year after next, and that the oil price ceiling system becomes a fiscal problem in the long run.

Kim Yo-jong: “New response means on mission standby along entire border… loudspeakers on standby within days too”

Yonhap reported that Kim Yo-jong, head of the General Affairs Department of North Korea’s Workers’ Party, said in a statement on the 22nd that “new response means will go on mission standby along the entire border,” and that loudspeakers aimed at the South will also go on standby within days. Chosun Ilbo reported that this raises the pitch a notch from Kim’s warning the day before that “loudspeakers will be turned on this month,” and added that the day marked one month since the landmine provocation in the Demilitarized Zone. Dong-A Ilbo pointed to a setup in which Chairman Kim Jong-un stays silent while Kim Yo-jong takes the offensive. Dong-A Ilbo also reported that Kim Yo-jong attacked Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Byung-joo as “a minnow.” Kyunghyang Shinmun and Korea Economic Daily also carried the statement as breaking news.

HD Hyundai Heavy names 93,000 m³ world’s largest ammonia-powered ship… delivery in November

Yonhap and Dong-A Ilbo reported that HD Hyundai Heavy Industries held a naming ceremony on the 22nd at its Ulsan shipyard for the ‘Terra Clove,’ a 93,000 m³ ammonia-powered ship, the largest of its kind in the world. According to ZDNet Korea, the vessel is a gas carrier ordered by Singapore shipping company Eastern Pacific Shipping (EPS), has a propulsion system that can run on both conventional marine fuel and ammonia, and will be delivered to the owner in November. ZDNet Korea likened the ship’s length to the height of a 77-story apartment building. Kyunghyang Shinmun explained that ‘Terra’ means Earth in Latin.

Dong-A Ilbo reported that Lee Sang-kyun, HD Hyundai Heavy’s executive vice chairman, Cyril Ducau, EPS chief executive, and officials of the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA) attended the ceremony. Korea Economic Daily had reported that in April HD Hyundai Heavy floated the world’s first mid-sized ammonia-powered gas carrier, of 46,000 m³. Because ammonia emits no carbon dioxide when burned, it is cited as a fuel for meeting the decarbonization rules of the International Maritime Organization (IMO).
