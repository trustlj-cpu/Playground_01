Deaths and injuries after major fire at CFB Wainwright in Alberta, Carney and Defence Minister say

A fire that began in the base's training area on Saturday afternoon spread in high winds; officials have confirmed fatalities but released no toll or cause.

A major fire that began inside the training area of Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, east of Edmonton, has caused deaths and injuries, Prime Minister Mark Carney and Defence Minister David McGuinty said on Saturday, October 17. National Defence said the fire ignited at about 4 p.m. local time, and Wainwright RCMP said officers were called just after 4 p.m. to what early reports described as a fire at a training area. In a social-media post, Mr. Carney wrote that a "serious incident creating a major fire" had occurred at the base and that he was working with Alberta Premier Danielle Smith, according to La Presse, Radio-Canada and CBC. Mr. McGuinty confirmed fatalities and injuries but gave no figures, CBC and CTV News reported, and CP24 quoted him describing the situation as active and delicate. The Canadian Armed Forces said the numbers of dead and injured would not be made clear until next of kin had been informed. Mr. McGuinty did not say what caused the blaze or whether weapons or ammunition were involved. CTV News reported an explosion and a fire, while officials have so far spoken only of a fire. Ms. Smith called the fire "significant and tragic" and said it affected military personnel.

The fire spread quickly. The Municipal District of Wainwright said the blaze moved rapidly because of Saturday's strong winds, and CBC and Global News reported that it originated on the base and spread into the surrounding area; CNN reported that wind carried flames beyond the base fence. TVA Nouvelles described a large fire raging in the area of the base. No evacuation order was issued, according to CTV News and CNN. Authorities asked residents to avoid the incident area, keep roads clear and stay away from the Wainwright Health Centre except for critical medical needs. By about 11 p.m., the municipal district said on social media that the fire was being held and that property and critical infrastructure had been protected, CNN reported. The Globe and Mail reported that no details on injuries, fatalities or cause had been released when emergency crews first responded. The Government of Canada posted a notice overnight confirming that the fire ignited within the training area at approximately 4 p.m. local time on October 17. Reports published by Sunday morning did not include an official count of casualties, an official cause or a statement on how many people were on the training grounds.

Alberta heads into Monday's 10-question referendum after Carney and Smith's final push

Albertans vote on Monday, October 19, on a 10-question referendum, the Globe and Mail and Yahoo News Canada reported. On Question 10, voters choose between Option 1, to remain in Canada, and Option 2, to begin the process toward a second, binding referendum on separation, according to the Globe and Mail; Radio-Canada wrote that Albertans will learn Monday whether steps begin toward a referendum on independence. The other nine questions range from constitutional issues to immigration, Global News reported. Elections Alberta said 703,914 Albertans voted in the first four days of advance polling, CTV News reported (it gave 703,824 for the same period), and Yahoo News Canada also carried the figure of more than 700,000. Mr. Carney wrapped up a two-day tour of the province in Edmonton, Global News reported, and told a rally there that the stakes are high, CBC reported. Ms. Smith joined him in Red Deer on Friday, October 16, the Globe and Mail reported, and has said she voted for Alberta to remain in Canada. Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre told Albertans not to turn their backs on the country, La Presse reported.

Politics · British Columbia✓ 4 outlets · results are preliminary

Pete Fry wins Vancouver mayoralty and Doug McCallum returns in Surrey in B.C. municipal elections

British Columbians voted in municipal elections on Saturday, October 17. CBC News projected that Pete Fry, a Green Party member, defeated incumbent Mayor Ken Sim and will be the first Green mayor in Vancouver's history. In Surrey, CBC, Global News and Daily Hive reported that Doug McCallum defeated incumbent Mayor Brenda Locke and will return to the mayor's office; CBC described it as his fifth term as mayor, while CTV News headlined it a third stint in the city's top job. Global News reported that Ms. Locke conceded to Mr. McCallum. CBC said new mayors were elected in the province's largest cities as voters opted for change. In Prince George, mayor-elect Garth Frizzell said the next four years will be significant, citing an acute care tower, a mental health and addictions facility and new mine projects, CBC reported. The counts reported by outlets are unofficial, and official results had not been confirmed in the reports available on Sunday morning.

Macklem says Bank of Canada will sharply raise its inflation forecast as oil prices stay high

Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said on Saturday, October 17, that the bank expects to raise its quarterly inflation forecast significantly this month because of persistently high crude oil and petroleum product prices, Reuters and the Globe and Mail reported. Reuters said he cited the Middle East conflict and refinery damage as reasons the risk that inflation broadens and becomes more persistent has gone up. He framed the question as whether inflation would come back toward the target on its own or whether the bank would have to raise interest rates, according to Reuters. The new forecasts will accompany the bank's rate decision on October 28, and the policy rate stands at 2.25 per cent, CTV News and other outlets reported. Statistics Canada's September inflation data and the bank's business surveys are due Monday, the Globe and Mail reported. Le Devoir and Radio-Canada reported that Mr. Macklem also warned that global resilience is being severely tested and that high energy prices will push up inflation in Canada.

Protests over living costs sweep France, with clashes, fires and hundreds of arrests

Demonstrations over rising prices and public services took place across France on Saturday, October 17, with clashes, fires and looting reported by CNN, ABC News, Euronews and the Globe and Mail. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said about 550,000 people marched in 110 demonstrations and about 400 people were arrested, while organizers, including the CGT union and student groups, said about 800,000 took part. Protesters cited higher gasoline, energy and food prices. The CGT called for higher wages and pensions, lower prices and protection of public services. Local officials said at least 107 law enforcement officers were injured, and the city hall in Limoges was set on fire. CBC reported that a young protester lost a hand in Saint-Étienne. President Emmanuel Macron vowed to prosecute and punish those who took part in violence. La Presse reported that hundreds of people gathered in downtown Montréal in solidarity with the French demonstrations, and Radio-Canada reported dozens of arrests across France in the first rallies on Saturday morning.
