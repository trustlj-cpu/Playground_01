Four Canadian Armed Forces members killed and 39 hospitalized after fire at CFB Wainwright during live-fire training

Defence officials confirmed four deaths after earlier reports of seven, and Alberta's premier says at least 19 of the injured are in critical condition.

A fire on Saturday 17 October at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta killed four members of the Canadian Armed Forces and injured dozens more, CBC News, CBS News, CNN, Time, Al Jazeera and Global News reported. CBC said soldiers were taking part in a training exercise with live munitions when the blaze broke out, and Al Jazeera described it as a grass fire. Global News and CBC reported that sources first told them seven people had died, before defence officials confirmed four deaths. Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed the loss of four service members, La Presse reported, and the Canadian Armed Forces said in a statement that it was with profound sadness that it confirmed the deaths.

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith said on Sunday that 39 patients were still in hospital, at least 19 of them in critical condition, and that patients had been moved from the base to four hospitals across the province, according to Al Jazeera and CBC. Some patients have severe burns and could face weeks or months of intensive treatment, the reports said. Defence Minister David McGuinty described the situation as very active and delicate and said the RCMP is involved, Al Jazeera reported. The Department of National Defence said there were no indications of terrorist activity, and CBC reported that officials do not believe outside interference or damage to oil and gas infrastructure caused the fire. Time reported that the mayor of Wainwright said the fire crossed the base fence and was brought under control by firefighters. Carney asked for a detailed briefing, and CBC reported that an inquiry is expected to take at least a month.

Alberta votes on ten referendum questions today after nearly 1.4 million ballots were cast in advance

Albertans vote today, Monday 19 October, on ten referendum questions, with advance and special ballots already above 1.3 million, Global News and Yahoo News Canada reported. Global News said Elections Alberta counted 1,356,873 votes between 13 and 17 October, against 809,988 at the same point in the 2023 provincial election. CTV News reported on 16 October that the total had passed one million, about one third of Alberta's roughly three million eligible voters, and CP24 said more than 1,400 polling stations will be open from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. today, with results likely in the early hours of 20 October. Questions one to nine are yes-or-no questions on immigration and constitutional matters, and question ten deals with separation, Global News and CTV News reported. Global News said Premier Danielle Smith has set a winning threshold only for the separation question, and that the government's own orders state none of the ten results are binding. An Ipsos poll of 801 Albertans conducted exclusively for Global News from 13 to 16 October, with a margin of error of 4.2 percentage points, found about seven in 10 plan to vote to remain in Canada. Ipsos said support for a binding separation referendum rose three points since September to 23 per cent, that five per cent are undecided and that two per cent say they will not vote.

Mathieu Lévesque named interim leader of the Coalition avenir Québec, a party with no seats in the National Assembly

The Coalition avenir Québec named Mathieu Lévesque its interim leader over the weekend, Radio-Canada, La Presse and Les Affaires reported. Radio-Canada said he will be paid a salary of $200,000 a year by the party. Lévesque was the member for Chapleau from 2018, lost the seat in the 5 October election, and had briefly been minister responsible for youth and minister delegate for the regions in the Fréchette government, according to La Presse and Radio-Canada. He succeeds Christine Fréchette, who resigned on 6 October, the day after the party elected no members, The Globe and Mail and CTV News reported.

In a party statement reported by La Presse, Lévesque said he sees the CAQ as the real third way in Quebec politics and called for an internal review of the party. Radio-Canada reported that former premier François Legault praised the choice on X, that Fréchette called him the right person to lead the party, and that Parti Québécois leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, the premier-designate, congratulated him. The National Assembly sits again this week, with the Liberal members to be sworn in today and Québec solidaire members tomorrow, Radio-Canada reported.

Doug McCallum wins Surrey mayor's race, defeating incumbent Brenda Locke after she concedes

Doug McCallum won the Surrey, B.C., mayoral election held on Saturday 17 October, defeating incumbent Brenda Locke, the Surrey Now-Leader, Daily Hive, CP24 and Global News reported. Global News said Locke conceded in a speech before the polls had closed, telling supporters the voters had spoken. Preliminary results cited by the Now-Leader and CP24 gave McCallum about 42,200 votes, or 31.8 per cent, and Locke about 30,800, with Mike Starchuk of Imagine Surrey third and Linda Annis of Surrey First fourth; the two outlets differ slightly on exact counts. Turnout was 37.35 per cent, with 134,437 ballots cast, according to the reports.

The Now-Leader reported that McCallum, 82, previously served as mayor from 1996 to 2005 and from 2018 to 2022, and that this is his fifth term. CTV projected that his Safe Surrey Coalition will hold a majority on council, a count the Now-Leader and Daily Hive also described as a decisive win for the slate. CP24's recap of the B.C. municipal elections also reported a change of mayor in Vancouver. McCallum is due to take office on 2 November 2026, according to the Now-Leader.

Canada · Business✓ 3 outlets · how long the sector slump will last is not known

Canadian telecom shares slump this year while the S&P/TSX Composite is up about 11 per cent

Canada's telecom stocks are in a slump even as the S&P/TSX Composite Index has risen about 11 per cent this year, The Globe and Mail reported on 18 October. The newspaper said the five-company telecom index is down more than 16 per cent in 2026, with BCE down about 11 per cent, Rogers down about 13 per cent and Telus down about 33 per cent since January. The Globe cited rising bond yields, which make dividend stocks less appealing next to safer assets, and said Bell, Rogers and Telus together carry nearly $100-billion of long-term debt, so higher borrowing costs are a concern. An analyst quoted by the Globe also pointed to possible satellite competition for cellphone service.

The Globe's markets coverage reported that telecom shares fell sharply after SpaceX bought a U.S. low-band spectrum portfolio, with BCE down more than 5 per cent that day, although the deal does not directly affect Canada. BBN Times reported that Telus shares fell more than 11 per cent in late July after a second-quarter loss and a reset of its quarterly dividend that cut the annualized payout by 55 per cent. Bloomberg reported in April that shares of the biggest Canadian telecoms fell after TD Cowen cut BCE, Telus and Rogers to hold from buy, with analysts citing price wars and slower subscriber growth.
