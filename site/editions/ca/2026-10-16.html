Carney takes a last-minute unity pitch to Calgary as Alberta’s referendum looms

The prime minister told supporters Thursday that unity is not uniformity, and urged Albertans to vote “No to the Nine.”

Prime Minister Mark Carney made a late push in Calgary on Thursday, October 15, urging Albertans to keep their province inside Confederation. CBC reported that he told voters to choose “No to the Nine” in the referendum, while La Presse and Le Devoir described a warm welcome from a friendly crowd and a campaign sprint in the final stretch.

Carney was not alone in making the pitch. CBC and Google News Canada headlines reported that Opposition Leader Pierre Poilievre also brought a pro-unity message to Alberta the same day, an unusual moment of shared purpose between the two main federal leaders. Neither man, according to those reports, was arguing for sameness: Le Devoir quoted the prime minister as saying that unity is not uniformity, a line aimed at Albertans who feel Ottawa does not listen.

The stakes are visible in the numbers Le Devoir cited: a little more than one Albertan in five, it reported, could be tempted to start a process toward independence. That figure comes from a single outlet’s summary and we have not confirmed the underlying poll, its sample or its dates, so treat it as indicative rather than settled. What is clear from several outlets is that federalist leaders see the vote as close enough to warrant a personal visit in the closing days.

For readers elsewhere in the country, the practical question is what happens after the ballots are counted. A strong result for staying would likely ease pressure on Ottawa, though grievances over energy policy and federal-provincial relations would remain. A weaker one would open a long and contested conversation about what the next step even looks like. The Alberta government and the federal government have not, in the reporting we reviewed, laid out an agreed procedure for that second case. We will track the campaign’s final days and the results as they are confirmed.

Three dead, at least 10 injured after two transit buses catch fire in northeast Calgary

Three people are dead and at least ten others were injured after two Calgary Transit buses appeared to catch fire in the city’s northeast on Thursday, Global News and Google News Canada headlines reported. CBC separately confirmed that police were responding to a fatal bus fire in the same area, and TVA Nouvelles reported at least three deaths. The sources we reviewed do not say what started the fire, how the two buses came to be involved, or whether the dead and injured were passengers, drivers or bystanders. Those are the questions investigators and the city will now be asked. Until police or the transit authority release more, treat claims about a mechanical fault, a collision or anything else as unverified. Readers in Calgary should expect service disruptions in the northeast and follow the city’s transit alerts. We will update as official details arrive.

Oregon elementary school shooting leaves three children and a teacher dead

A shooting at an elementary school in Bend, Oregon, on Thursday left three children and one teacher dead, CBC reported, citing local police. Google News Canada’s world feed carried the same account. Earlier in the evening, Le Devoir and TVA had reported “several victims” and at least four dead, a reminder that first counts often change.

Le Devoir and Google News reported that when officers arrived, the gunman had already been shot dead. We have not seen a confirmed motive or details of how the shooter gained access, and those remain unverified.

The shooting is likely to feed the US debate over gun laws and school security ahead of the midterms, now less than three weeks away. Canadian readers with family in Oregon should rely on local authorities for updates.

Politics · Québec✓ 2 outlets · figures from La Presse

Parti Québécois sworn in at the National Assembly: from three seats survivors to 59 members

The Parti Québécois MNAs were sworn in Thursday after the October 5 election, La Presse reported, noting that the party had three survivors from a crushing defeat in 2022 and now has 59 members. Premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon closed with a cry of “vive le Québec libre,” according to La Presse and X posts from Québec political accounts.

Radio-Canada reported that former premier Pauline Marois and Jean Royer will train the party’s rookies at the National Assembly, while La Presse profiled 20 of the new MNAs to watch. Taken together, the stories point to a government-in-waiting working quickly to turn a large and inexperienced caucus into a functioning one.

The timing matters nationally. With Alberta’s referendum days away, a sovereigntist government taking office in Québec gives federalists a second front. Neither outlet we reviewed tied the two directly, so that connection is our reading, not theirs.

Economy · Energy and trade✓ 3 outlets · amounts as announced

Ottawa to announce $100 million for Nova Scotia offshore wind, and a Canada-EU working paper surfaces

The federal government is set to announce $100 million toward offshore wind projects in Nova Scotia, according to the Globe and Mail, CBC and Google News Canada. CBC reported that $5 million of that is earmarked for measuring wind speeds and ocean conditions ahead of a bid call planned for winter. The remaining breakdown was not detailed in the summaries we reviewed.

Separately, Le Devoir and Radio-Canada reported on a working document outlining a closer Canada-EU relationship, including cooperation on digital security, critical minerals and even aerial refuelling tankers. It is a draft, not an agreement, and the outlets did not say when or whether it would be signed.

On markets, the S&P/TSX Composite closed Thursday at 36,137.10, up 1.55 per cent, and the S&P 500 at 7,744.06, up 0.33 per cent, from our quote table; the figures come from a single data source.

Elsewhere, Friday’s Lotto Max draw is a record $90 million, the REM wants $156 million from its insurers, the Honoré-Mercier Bridge gets emergency repairs this weekend, and CBC reports that prediction markets have taken bets on Ontario municipal races.
