Politics · AlbertaPress ✓ 7 outlets · Mr. Carney's schedule is his own announcement

Alberta votes early as former rivals Notley and Kenney share a stage. Carney will rally in Calgary and Edmonton

Advance polls opened Tuesday at about 310 stations for the October 19 separation referendum, with early voting through Saturday. The prime minister has accepted invitations to two Forever Canadian rallies.

Albertans began voting early on Tuesday in the referendum on whether the province should stay in Canada. About 310 advance polling stations opened across Alberta, according to Global News, The Globe and Mail and CBC, and advance voting runs for five days, through Saturday, ahead of the vote itself on Monday, October 19. The ballot is narrower than the headlines suggest. As CBC's explainer lays out, voters are asked whether Alberta should remain part of Canada or whether the legal process required for separation discussions should begin, with another referendum still to come. A vote for the second option would open a negotiation; it would not take Alberta out of Canada overnight. Alberta NDP Leader Naheed Nenshi urged people to vote as the first stations opened, The Globe and Mail reported. The most unusual scene of the day was political. Rachel Notley, the former NDP premier, shared a stage with her longtime opponent Jason Kenney, the former United Conservative premier, to make the case for staying, CBC, The Globe and Mail and Global News reported. Mr. Kenney also said, according to CTV News, Global News and Castanet, that the United Conservative Party must choose its federalist roots and expel separatists once the vote is over, a pointed message to the governing party led by Premier Danielle Smith.

Prime Minister Mark Carney, who grew up in Alberta, called Canada "worth fighting for" in his Thanksgiving message, Global News reported. On Tuesday evening he said he had accepted invitations to two Forever Canadian rallies, in Calgary on Thursday and in Edmonton on Friday, CBC reported, and Global News and Castanet carried the same announcement. Forever Canadian is the campaign urging Albertans to vote to remain. Smaller disputes show how closely the machinery is being watched. CBC reported that Elections Alberta told registered third-party advertisers they cannot appoint scrutineers, the observers who watch voting and counting, and that campaigners on both the federalist and separatist sides objected; we saw this only in CBC. CBC also carried a Brexit scholar, Ian Cooper, who after attending a separatist meeting saw parallels with Britain's exit from the European Union, and Radio-Canada noted that, unlike in Quebec, Alberta's separatist movement has no political party of its own. The Globe and Mail published an analysis of the risk of a mass exodus from Alberta if it went independent, which is one outlet's argument, not a forecast. In Quebec, where Paul St-Pierre Plamondon is preparing to lead a Parti Québécois government, La Presse called the Alberta debate strange, and The Globe and Mail reported that Quebec businesses are bracing for a renewed sovereignty debate. We saw no new poll in the reports we read, so we make no prediction. After Monday, the numbers that matter are turnout and the margin: Mr. Kenney has himself warned in recent days that a low turnout could favour separatists.

Economy · OntarioPress ✓ 6 outlets · the 58% figure is the company's own

Stelco rejects Ottawa's call to halt layoffs, citing a 58% fall in U.S. exports. NDP leaders urge Carney to take it over

Stelco's president answered Industry Minister Mélanie Joly's demand that the Hamilton layoffs be halted with a refusal, CBC reported on Tuesday, while CTV News and Radio-Canada carried the company's reply. The company, owned by the Ohio steelmaker Cleveland-Cliffs, said its steel exports to the United States have fallen 58% and pointed to foreign imports, according to BNN Bloomberg and Radio-Canada. Those are the company's own numbers, and we did not see them checked independently. Ontario NDP Leader Marit Stiles said public ownership is emerging as the only way to save roughly 350 jobs, Global News reported, and the National Post said federal NDP Leader Avi Lewis asked Ottawa to nationalize the company.

Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre asked "Did you do your job?" and said the Liberal government should have protected Stelco's workers, the National Post reported. A BNN Bloomberg headline said the Cleveland-Cliffs chief executive is prepared to fight Canada in court. Ottawa has threatened to ask a court to enforce the job pledges made when it approved the 2024 takeover; we saw no filing. The layoffs are part of Stelco's plan to idle its Hamilton operations and concentrate production at Lake Erie Works in Nanticoke, as reported earlier this week, and Ms. Joly's demand followed the five-business-day deadline she set on October 5. We saw no reply from Ottawa on what it will do next.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 8 outlets · tolls and launch warnings are disputed

Russian missiles hit a Kyiv power plant with no warning to Ukraine. Twelve U.S. senators urge Trump to drop the diesel deal

A Russian ballistic-missile strike on Kyiv on Tuesday night hit the Darnytsia heat and power plant and other civilian sites, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine received no warning from its partners, The Guardian, Reuters, Radio-Canada and Le Devoir reported. The Guardian linked the complaint to a recent U.S. threat to halt intelligence sharing; Reuters said Mr. Trump insists the United States is still sharing intelligence with Ukraine. Reuters and The Guardian also reported that SpaceX has restricted Starlink service in Ukraine, which a presidential aide said disrupts military missions. Tolls differ by outlet and by hour: Reuters had two dead in Kyiv on Tuesday, CBC three killed across Ukraine overnight with dozens of power facilities damaged, and The Kyiv Independent four dead and 25 hurt. Earlier on Tuesday Reuters had noted that Russia hit no energy targets for the first time since Mr. Trump announced his "energy ceasefire".

In Washington, 12 senators, six of them Republicans, told Mr. Trump to rescind the diesel deal with Russia, CBC and Reuters reported, and a Reuters report on growing Republican resistance carried the line "gift to our adversary". Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni called the deal a "very risky choice", Reuters said. Reuters also reported the International Energy Agency saying diesel shortages could last well into 2027. There is a Canadian thread: The Globe and Mail and Radio-Canada reported that Dominion Dynamics, a Canadian firm, will help build uncrewed ground vehicles for Ukraine, and the National Post's Tasha Kheiriddin argued in a column that Mr. Carney is struggling to decide whether to back Mr. Trump on Russia. That is her opinion, not a report.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · the fire is the Saudi Energy Ministry's statement

A Houthi missile strike sets fire to part of Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran

An administrative building belonging to Saudi Aramco in Dhahran was attacked, causing a limited fire, the Saudi Energy Ministry said on Wednesday, CBC reported. The Globe and Mail, citing Bloomberg, said the company's headquarters was hit during a Houthi missile attack on Tuesday, and Reuters reported loud booms near the site. AP said residents of Riyadh reported new explosions. FinancialJuice relayed a Houthi statement claiming strikes on Aramco in Dhahran, King Khalid Airport and an Aramco refinery in Riyadh, and a coalition claim of intercepting a missile aimed at Riyadh; both come from a single outlet.

Mr. Trump said the Houthis are trying to drive up oil prices before the U.S. midterm elections, Reuters reported. Crude rose as traders weighed Saudi supply risk and the flow of Russian diesel, Reuters said, and Reuters also reported that Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks on shipping before the U.S. vote, citing officials. For Canadians the channels are fuel prices and the energy sector. We did not see a casualty figure for the Aramco fire. FinancialJuice also relayed Oman saying its talks with Iran on the Strait of Hormuz have concluded but that finalizing them depends on U.S.-Iran talks, and the International Energy Agency's forecast that world oil demand will fall by 2.6 million barrels a day this year because of the Iran war's disruption and soaring prices. Both come from that single outlet.

Markets · WorldPress ✓ 7 outlets · no confirmed TSX or Wall Street closing levels in our sources

Chip selloff and mixed bank results weigh on Wall Street. IMF lifts its growth forecast but warns on bond yields

Tuesday's U.S. session was shaped by a deepening chip selloff, with Reuters reporting that chip stocks extended their slide on a China memory-chip report and worries about AI spending; the rout had begun in Asia, where AP said South Korea's Kospi sank more than 5%. The big banks opened earnings season with mixed results. JPMorgan posted a rare profit miss and its shares slipped, while Citigroup's profit jumped about 40%, Goldman Sachs beat estimates and Wells Fargo's profit rose, according to Reuters and The Globe and Mail. Bank of America was fined more than US$1.2 billion by U.S. authorities over failures to police suspicious transactions, Reuters, Bloomberg and the Financial Times reported. We could not confirm Tuesday's closing levels for the S&P/TSX Composite, the Canadian dollar, Wall Street or oil from two sources, so we print none.

The International Monetary Fund slightly raised its forecast for world growth at its Bangkok meetings but warned of an AI-related market correction, government debt and war, CBC and Reuters reported. Its financial stability report named a surge in government bond yields as the biggest emerging risk, Bloomberg said, and Global News used the word "disorderly". On Wednesday in Europe, ASML raised its outlook and said it cannot meet AI-driven demand in 2027 and 2028, Reuters reported. U.S. consumer price figures for September are due today, and Reuters' preview expected high gasoline prices to push inflation up.
