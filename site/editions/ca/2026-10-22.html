Carney and Ford launch $9-billion action plan for Ontario workers and industry

The prime minister and the Ontario premier announced joint spending on steel, autos and mining on Wednesday.

Prime Minister Mark Carney and Ontario Premier Doug Ford launched a joint action plan on Wednesday aimed at protecting workers from tariffs and strengthening Ontario industries, according to reports by Le Devoir and the National Post. Le Devoir reported that the package is valued at $9 billion and includes the creation of a new action fund for the steel sector. The National Post reported that the prime minister touted billions of dollars in joint investments in steel, autos and mining as the way to protect Ontario's economy. Reports published before the event had described it as an announcement of support for workers amid the trade war, with the two leaders appearing together to present the measures.

National Post columnist Scott Stinson wrote that Carney 'sings from the Doug Ford songbook' as he announces $9B for Ontario industry, a headline that frames the prime minister as echoing the premier's approach. The three sectors named in the coverage, steel, autos and mining, are the ones the plan is said to target, and the steel action fund is the one new instrument described by name. Le Devoir's account presents the plan as a response to the tariffs themselves, while the National Post's account emphasizes the joint nature of the investments between Ottawa and Queen's Park. How the $9 billion is divided between the two governments, over what period it will be spent and which workers or companies qualify has not been confirmed.

U.S. Embassy accuses Canadian politicians of election meddling; Carney rejects the claim

The U.S. Embassy in Ottawa accused Canadian politicians on Wednesday of interfering in American elections, a charge reported by CBC, The Globe and Mail, Radio-Canada and La Presse. The Globe and Mail reported that the embassy released a list of quotes which it says indicate 'plans to influence American politics.' CBC reported that the embassy said the federal government is brushing off concerns about 'potential interference' in the midterms. La Presse and Radio-Canada reported that Prime Minister Mark Carney rejected the accusations launched by the Trump administration, which La Presse described as a new twist in the tumultuous relationship between Ottawa and Washington. Separately, CBC reported that Canadians are being caught up in a U.S. voter fraud crackdown under which, since late last year, federal prosecutors have charged dozens of non-citizens, many of them longtime U.S. permanent residents. That story ran under a headline pairing the crackdown with the embassy's accusation of election interference by Canada. Which politicians are quoted on the embassy's list and what remedy, if any, Washington is seeking have not been confirmed.

St-Pierre Plamondon names 24-member cabinet, with a minister for independence

Quebec Premier Paul St-Pierre Plamondon unveiled his cabinet on Wednesday, with 10 women and 14 men, 24 ministers in all, Le Devoir reported. La Presse reported that he named Catherine Gentilcore deputy premier, making her the number two in the government, and described Girard and Paquet as superministers. Le Devoir and La Presse reported that Gentilcore is the minister charged with 'accéder à l'indépendance', a first in Quebec, and that she says she will have time to lead the file on top of her other responsibilities. Pascal Bérubé takes over the Culture ministry with a mandate to make Quebec culture more attractive to young people, according to Le Devoir.

La Presse reported that the premier gave his ministers many mandates at the swearing-in. TVA Nouvelles reported that St-Pierre Plamondon is promising three weeks of vacation for all Quebecers. La Presse also reported that a petition is calling for the resignation of Ruba Ghazal, who defended herself on Wednesday against the charge of having displayed the keffiyeh in the Red Room (Salon rouge) of the National Assembly. Radio-Canada ran a profile of the new ministers.

Carney won't rule out Quebec-style immigration deal for Alberta as Smith rules out second referendum

Prime Minister Mark Carney will not rule out giving Alberta a Quebec-style immigration deal, Global News reported on Wednesday. The story followed comments by Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre on Tuesday that the Liberals have lost control of immigration. Quebec's arrangement with Ottawa gives the province a larger role in immigration than other provinces have, which is the model referred to in the headline. No terms of an Alberta agreement were reported.

Headlines this week also reported that Alberta's Smith said separation is now a dead issue and ruled out a second referendum, and that she vows to push for more Alberta autonomy. Together, the reports place the immigration question inside Alberta's wider push for autonomy within Canada. Whether Ottawa and Alberta have begun talks on immigration has not been confirmed.

Electronic Arts to shut down Edmonton's BioWare after more than 30 years

Video game publisher Electronic Arts is shutting down the popular Edmonton-based game development studio BioWare after more than 30 years, CBC reported early Thursday. CBC described BioWare as a well-known Edmonton studio and said EA, its publisher, is the company closing it. The closure ends a run of more than three decades for the studio in the Alberta capital, according to the report.

The CBC report was also carried in Canadian headline listings on Thursday morning. How many employees are affected, what happens to the studio's current projects and when the closure takes effect have not been confirmed.
