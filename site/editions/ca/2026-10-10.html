Canada lost 68,000 jobs in September and unemployment rose to 6.5%. Traders trimmed bets on a Bank of Canada rate hike

Quebec accounted for 49,000 of the losses. The loonie slipped, yet the TSX still jumped 1.5% on Friday.

The Canadian economy shed 68,000 jobs in September and the unemployment rate rose to 6.5%, according to reports on Friday's Labour Force Survey from Reuters, CBC, BNN Bloomberg, The Globe and Mail, Radio-Canada, La Presse and the Financial Post. Reuters called the contraction a surprise and said the jobless rate inched up. The Globe and Mail and La Presse both used the word "again" in their headlines, which points to earlier weakness; we did not verify the figures for previous months. We also did not see the report's detail on industries, age groups or wages, so we cannot say where outside Quebec the losses fell. The survey is a sample of households, so a monthly change carries a margin of error, and economists often look at several months together before calling a trend.

The provinces were uneven. TVA Nouvelles and Le Journal de Montréal reported that Quebec lost 49,000 jobs, which would be roughly three-quarters of the national drop and gives the incoming Parti Québécois government, which won the Oct. 5 election, a weaker labour market than the one it campaigned in (we saw no PQ reaction), Elsewhere, the Telegraph-Journal said unemployment in New Brunswick rose again. Markets read the report as a reason to doubt higher borrowing costs. Bloomberg's headline said the Canadian dollar weakened as soft jobs data dimmed bets on higher rates, and FinancialJuice, citing swap market data, said the odds of a Bank of Canada rate hike in October fell to 27% from 40%. That percentage comes from one source, so treat it as indicative. The loonie closed near 70.2 US cents, with USD/CAD at 1.4254, up 0.2%, in DailyDrop's quote feed. Stocks went the other way: the S&P/TSX Composite Index closed up 1.48% at 35,664.62, a gain of more than 500 points, Yahoo Finance Canada reported, as U.S. markets also rose. The outlets we read did not explain the size of the move; one plausible reading is that investors saw the data as lowering the odds of higher rates, not as the start of a deeper downturn, but that is our inference. Global News separately reported that the Bank of Canada says an aging population and low immigration are "reshaping" the economy; we saw only the headline, not the remarks. Ottawa's own Friday messaging pointed to business investment: ministers Metlege Diab and Lightbound promoted the new Productivity Mega Deduction in separate releases, which the government says will help firms invest, grow and create jobs. Another release said Ottawa is helping Saskatchewan businesses adapt amid what it called unjustified U.S. tariffs, and Minister Sidhu is to lead a Team Canada trade mission to India. The headlines of those releases did not mention the jobs figures. Monday is Thanksgiving Day, so the TSX is closed and the next full read on the market comes on Tuesday.

Navi Pillay wins the Nobel Peace Prize. Hours later Washington sanctions the International Criminal Court

The Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Peace Prize on Friday to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, a South African human-rights lawyer and former international judge, AP, Reuters, NPR, USA Today and The Washington Post reported. Hours later the Trump administration imposed new sanctions on the International Criminal Court, Reuters, AP and Politico reported. The court's prosecutor condemned them and said its work will continue, and French-language reports quoted the court as calling them an attack on the rule of law. A day earlier the Canadian poet and classicist Anne Carson won the Nobel Prize in Literature, Reuters, AP and The New York Times reported. AP described the move as part of a drive to dismantle the tribunal. We did not see the full list of people and bodies named.

Canada joined seven other countries in a joint statement of foreign ministers backing the court, according to Global Affairs Canada and the British government; the others are Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom. We have not seen the text. Reuters ran a roundup of reactions to the sanctions, and we have not seen every government's statement. Mr. Trump said Norway has an "indelible stain" for not awarding him the prize, Reuters and NBC News reported.

Trump says Russia will ship diesel to the U.S., easing pressure on Moscow. Kyiv calls it an "investment in war"

President Donald Trump announced on Friday that Russia will supply diesel to the United States and global markets, Reuters, AP, The New York Times, NBC News and CBS News reported. Reuters put the first supply at 300,000 tons, "immediately", while CBS News headlined "millions of tons"; the quantities are not reconciled. AP and NPR framed the deal as a relaxing of pressure on Moscow to ease fuel prices before the midterm elections. Reuters reported that Mr. Trump's earlier moves to boost diesel supply had not yet lowered prices.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said letting Russia sell diesel to the U.S. is an "investment in war", Reuters reported. Reuters also said U.S., Ukrainian and European negotiators met in Miami as the deal was announced, and that the Kremlin said Vladimir Putin, in a call with Mr. Trump, downplayed an immediate resumption of Ukraine talks. Delta Air Lines cut its profit forecast because of a US$6-billion surge in fuel costs, Reuters reported. We found no Canadian pump-price figures and no reaction from Ottawa.

Quebec: outgoing CAQ minister is dropped from cabinet for attending the PQ victory party. A tribunal sets aside the CAQ's strike-limit law

Mathieu Lacombe, the outgoing culture minister, was removed from the Council of Ministers after he attended the Parti Québécois election-night party, Radio-Canada, TVA Nouvelles, Le Journal de Montréal and Le Devoir reported. TVA added that he lost his government limousine, and Mr. Lacombe has apologised. The PQ won the Oct. 5 vote with 59 of 127 seats, five short of a majority, as earlier editions reported. Radio-Canada also reported a first "constructive" call between PQ leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon and Prime Minister Mark Carney.

Separately, Radio-Canada reported that a tribunal declared the CAQ's Law 14, which limits the right to strike, "inoperative" in a case involving the CPE Le Jardin de Robi, a subsidized daycare, and called it a union victory. It is the only outlet we saw on the ruling, and we do not know whether the government will appeal or how far the decision reaches beyond this case; that is not yet confirmed. The Globe and Mail wrote that the incoming PQ is weighing friends and foes across the aisle, since a minority government needs opposition votes.

Markets · CanadaPress ✓ 5 outlets · DailyDrop quote feed for TSX and USD/CAD

The TSX gained 1.5% and Wall Street rose on Friday. Gold climbed 1.4% and WTI edged up to US$91.85

The S&P/TSX Composite Index closed at 35,664.62 on Friday, up 1.48%, according to DailyDrop's quote feed, and Yahoo Finance Canada put the rise at more than 500 points. On Wall Street the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow 0.83% to 51,654.95. The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 5.244%. West Texas Intermediate settled at US$91.85, up 0.39%, and gold rose 1.43% to US$4,216.3. USD/CAD finished at 1.4254, up 0.2%, as the Canadian dollar weakened after the jobs report.

Reuters reported that U.S. consumer sentiment deteriorated further in October, and that AI-related companies are expected to drive most of the third-quarter U.S. earnings gains. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found a bipartisan majority of Americans blaming Mr. Trump's policies for rising costs. Bloomberg said Wall Street rallied ahead of next week's earnings. Canadian markets are closed on Monday for Thanksgiving, while U.S. stock markets are open.
