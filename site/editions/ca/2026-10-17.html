Carney and Smith share a stage as Alberta's referendum campaign enters its final stretch

The prime minister and the Alberta premier made a joint appeal for a united Canada on Friday, while the Conservative leader urged Albertans not to turn their backs on the country.

Prime Minister Mark Carney and Alberta Premier Danielle Smith appeared together on Friday, October 16, to promote a united Canada, according to reports from La Presse and Le Devoir. Le Devoir described the event as a last appeal to Albertans to vote to keep the province inside the country. The pairing is notable because the two leaders are not natural allies on most files, and the report framed the joint message as a deliberate show of common cause ahead of the vote. The same day, Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre said he understands the disillusionment of separatists in the province but urged Albertans not to turn their backs on Canada, La Presse reported. His remarks add a third federal voice to the final days of the campaign, alongside the prime minister and the premier. CBC published an explainer breaking down the constitutional questions Albertans are being asked, and Quebec radio commentary on QUB radio warned that Mr. Carney cannot rest on his laurels.

Le Devoir also reported that the campaign has been marked by fears of foreign interference and of manipulation using artificial intelligence. It said the distrust is partly explained by the high number of advance votes and mail-in ballots expected before polling day. We have not seen independent confirmation of any specific interference incident, so that part of the story is, for now, a concern reported by one outlet rather than an established fact. What is established is the timing and the tone. Three of the country's best-known political figures spent Friday making the same argument, and they did so in public, with cameras present. What the vote will mean for the Constitution, for equalization and for pipeline politics depends on the exact wording of the questions and on turnout, neither of which we can confirm from the reports reviewed this morning. We will update when results or official turnout figures are published.

Nova Scotia to host a large data centre for Anthropic's Claude, CBC reports

CBC reported on Friday evening, October 16, that Nova Scotia will host a massive data centre to power Anthropic's AI assistant Claude. This is a single original report. We have not seen a provincial statement, a company statement, an investment figure or a construction timeline, and we are not going to guess at them. A data centre of this kind is a building full of servers that needs a large and steady supply of electricity and cooling water or air. That is why the choice of province matters: Nova Scotia's grid mix, its transmission capacity and its power rates will shape what is practical. Residents and municipalities usually learn the details through permit filings and utility regulator hearings, which tend to come after the announcement. Readers in Halifax and Cape Breton may want to watch for notices from the provincial government and the utility board in the coming days.

Hitachi announces $479-million plan for a transformer insulation factory in London, Ont.

Hitachi announced on Friday, October 16, a $479-million plan to build a factory in London, Ont., Global News reported. The 21,000-square-metre facility would be the first in Canada to produce pressboard, a material made from paper that is used as insulation in a transformer. A transformer steps voltage up or down on the power grid, and utilities across North America have reported long waits for new units.

The reports we reviewed did not give a construction start date, an opening year, a headcount or a government funding figure, so we are not repeating any. The dollar amount is the company's own figure as carried by the outlets, and we have not seen an independent estimate. For London and the surrounding region, a manufacturing investment of this size would be one of the larger recent announcements, and local officials typically follow with details on jobs and training within days. Readers working in skilled trades or electrical supply may want to watch for hiring notices.

Montreal police suspend officers over allegations the mayor calls very serious

The Service de police de la Ville de Montréal (SPVM) has suspended police officers after allegations that Montreal's mayor described as very serious, Le Devoir reported on Friday, October 16. Radio-Canada first reported that a 32-year-old officer was suspended over allegations he urinated on homeless people while in uniform. A later report said two officers were suspended. According to La Presse, one of the two officers from neighbourhood station 21 allegedly urinated on a person in a state of homelessness.

The accounts differ on the count, which is why we say one or two. The allegations have not been tested in court, and we have seen no statement from the officers or their union. A suspension is an administrative step, not a finding of guilt, and the SPVM and the province's police ethics and investigation bodies have separate roles. Readers who want to follow the case should look for the force's own statement and for any announcement about an independent investigation.

Calgary police investigate fiery city bus crash that killed three passengers

Calgary police are working to determine the cause of a fiery city bus crash that killed three passengers and injured 16 others, Global News reported on Friday, October 16. The reports describe the vehicle as burning after the collision, but we have not seen the time, the location, the condition of the injured or a statement on whether another vehicle was involved. We are not filling those gaps. In the coming days, readers should expect updates from the Calgary Police Service, Calgary Transit and possibly the provincial agencies that investigate serious collisions. Families of the dead and injured are the first priority for those updates; names are normally released only after relatives are notified. If you were a passenger or a witness, police generally ask you to contact them directly rather than post video publicly. We will carry confirmed details when they are published, and we will say clearly if the cause remains unconfirmed.

A Delta, B.C., crash involving a double-decker bus closed Highway 99. A paddleboarder missing for 23 days was found in Okanagan Lake. Global News reported a legal challenge to Stelco layoffs, and Manitoba declared its measles outbreak over. Prosecutors said they will seek a dangerous offender designation for Daniel Senecal, and an accountant testified in the Crypto King fraud case. In Washington, Donald Trump signed an executive order pausing a Russia sanctions package. On the markets, the TSX closed at 36,176.55, up 0.11 per cent, while the S&P 500 fell 1.09 per cent. Next Tuesday, NASA will brief on spacewalks by Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk.
