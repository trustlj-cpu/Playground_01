Quebec · TransportPress ✓ 4 outlets · the corrosion reports come from one outlet

A cracked steel beam closes a lane of the Honoré-Mercier Bridge, and Quebec waives a toll to ease the jam

Maintenance crews found the flaw on Tuesday. No end date has been given, and local leaders are asking the next government to build a new crossing.

Maintenance work on the Honoré-Mercier Bridge, which carries traffic between Montréal and the South Shore, turned up a crack in a steel beam on Tuesday, Le Devoir reported. One lane has been closed since then “for an indefinite period,” according to La Presse and TVA Nouvelles, and TVA said it is the left lane. On Wednesday TVA described kilometres of congestion, and Le Devoir reported that half of the lanes were still closed. The two descriptions can both be right if the affected direction has two lanes, but we did not see a lane count, so we give both. Late on Wednesday the Transport Ministry announced that drivers on Highway 30 heading toward Montréal will not pay the toll between 5:30 a.m. and 9:30 a.m. starting Thursday, La Presse and TVA reported. La Presse said the measure is meant to soften the effect of the closed lane on the roads. The reports we read mention only the morning window, and we did not see how long the exemption will last or a date for reopening the lane.

The closure has revived a long-running argument over the state of the structure. La Presse reported that elected officials and unions say the sudden shutdown shows how run-down a bridge nearly a century old has become, and that some want the next government to make a new crossing to the South Shore a priority. The mayor of Châteauguay told TVA Nouvelles and the Journal de Montréal that residents are “fed up” (« On est écœurés ») and asked for a new bridge. Early Thursday the Journal de Montréal reported that ministry reports describe corrosion eating away at the bridge, and it ran a piece showing the crack. We have only that outlet for the corrosion findings, and none of the reports we read says corrosion caused this crack. We also did not see a repair plan or a cost.

In DailyDrop’s reading, the timing matters as much as the steel. Quebec is between governments: the outgoing Coalition Avenir Québec cabinet held its final cabinet meeting, La Presse reported on Wednesday, so decisions on repairs and on any new crossing will largely fall to the incoming one. The toll waiver is a modest tool: it nudges some drivers onto another route, but it does not add a lane, and nothing we read suggests it will cover the evening return. For commuters the practical points are the free-toll window and slow mornings until the lane reopens. The facts to look for next are the repair estimate, a reopening date and what the ministry’s inspection reports say about this beam.

Politics · AlbertaPress ✓ 7 outlets · ballot count from Global News

Alberta’s referendum draws 174,000 ballots on day one of advance polls. Premier Smith says she voted to stay

Albertans cast about 174,000 ballots on the first day of advance polling, Global News reported, and CTV News said mayors welcomed the turnout. Premier Danielle Smith said in a social-media video that she voted to stay in Canada and urged support for the other nine questions on the ballot, according to Global News, Castanet, Radio-Canada, TVA Nouvelles and the Journal de Montréal. Voting day is Monday. The Globe and Mail described the nine as policies that would upend immigration rules and require reopening the Constitution. CBC reported that the premier put the immigration and constitutional questions on the ballot as easy winners, but that officials in her party worry she could lose some of them. CBC also reported that three former Alberta premiers joined to urge a “no” on the immigration questions.

One campaigner quoted by Global News warned that a lead in the polls breeds complacency. The Globe and Mail cited a survey indicating that hundreds of doctors would leave if Alberta became independent, and the Financial Post quoted law professor Andrew Leach saying the ninth question could embolden B.C. to block a West Coast pipeline; each of those is one outlet. Turnout, not the premier’s own ballot, is the number to watch.

Crime · TorontoPress ✓ 7 outlets · counts of ammunition differ between outlets

Three Canadian Armed Forces reservists are charged in the kidnapping of a crypto exchange owner. Stolen military ammunition was recovered

Toronto police announced on Wednesday that a third member of the Canadian Armed Forces, along with four other people, has been arrested and charged in a kidnapping investigation that earlier led to the arrest of two reservists, CBC reported. CTV News and CP24 said the abduction was aimed at the victim’s cryptocurrency assets, and La Presse described the victim as the owner of a cryptocurrency exchange platform. Global News reported that two of the soldiers, Sachintha Fernando, 22, and James Moreau, 29, were arrested on Aug. 11 and face a total of 49 charges, including kidnapping for ransom and forcible confinement. Police Chief Myron Demkiw said “no one is above the law,” the National Post reported. The Globe and Mail said the case began as an abduction inquiry and widened as police found firearms, ammunition and illicit drugs, and the new charges come more than two months after the first arrests.

Investigators also recovered ammunition allegedly stolen from the military. The Globe and Mail put the amount at 2,500 rounds, about half of them blanks, according to police; La Presse and Radio-Canada also reported that stolen military ammunition was recovered, but La Presse spoke of hundreds of cartridges, which does not match the Globe’s figure, so we treat the count as unsettled. We did not see how the ammunition left military control or what the Armed Forces will do about the three members.

Politics · Canada–U.S.Press ✓ 7 outlets · the accusation is the ambassador’s own statement

The U.S. ambassador tells Canadian politicians to “keep your nose out of American politics.” Joly says Canada would never interfere

U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra said American officials are “closely watching” Canadian politicians for signs of interference in next month’s midterm elections, CBC and the Globe and Mail reported; the Globe said he spoke on a podcast. The National Post reported that he pointed to an Ontario government anti-tariff television ad that aired last year, and to comments by Industry Minister Mélanie Joly, as things that could be viewed as interference. The Toronto Star and CTV News carried his line that Canadians should “keep your nose out of American politics.” Ms. Joly replied that Canada would never interfere in U.S. elections, the Globe reported; the Globe also quoted her as saying the world will not be safer even if Mr. Trump leaves office, a remark we have from that outlet alone.

The same day, CTV News and TVA Nouvelles reported that Mr. Trump said Canada would become the 51st state, “because that’s the only way it works.” The National Post quoted trade analysts who say Ottawa may have to move first to restart talks with Washington, and Global News reported Ontario Premier Doug Ford saying Republicans might “pull the carpet” from under Mr. Trump if they lose the midterms; each of those is one outlet. The reports we read describe an ad and some remarks, not any specific act of interference.

Markets · U.S.Press ✓ 7 outlets · index levels and the TSX close not confirmed by two sources

Wall Street has its worst day since July as U.S. inflation reaches 3.7% and chip stocks tumble

U.S. inflation rose to 3.7% in September, hotter than expected, according to CNN, BNN Bloomberg, The Guardian, Radio-Canada and Global News, with gasoline a main driver, CNBC reported. U.S. stocks then had their worst day since July, BNN Bloomberg reported, and Bloomberg said the S&P 500 ended its longest stretch of calm since 2020. Chip shares fell even after ASML raised its sales outlook and said demand would exceed its capacity through 2028, The Globe and Mail and Barron’s reported. Bank of America slid on a $1.21-billion fine tied to anti-money laundering shortcomings, Bloomberg Television and the Globe said. We could not confirm Wednesday’s index closing levels from two sources, so we give none, and the S&P/TSX Composite and USD/CAD are blank for the same reason.

The selloff spread to Asia, Reuters and Bloomberg reported, and Reuters said Nasdaq futures were down about 1% early Thursday. Fed officials sent mixed signals on a rate hike: one told Reuters that hikes are likely needed but not urgently, and another that there is time to “wait and see.” The dollar eased from an 18-month high, Reuters said, and oil fell as supply worries eased, Reuters and Bloomberg reported. In Canada, the Globe and Mail asked why fixed mortgage rates have jumped even though the Bank of Canada has not moved; the usual channel is bond yields, and the IMF warned this week of spillovers from rising yields, Reuters reported.
