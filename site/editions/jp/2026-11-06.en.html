Russia Puts Four JT Subsidiaries Under Temporary Control; Putin Signs Decree, Japan to Protest

The subsidiaries that run Japan Tobacco’s business in Russia are covered. Ownership remains, but management effectively passes into the hands of Russian companies.

On November 5, Russian President Putin signed a presidential decree placing four Russian subsidiaries of the Japan Tobacco (JT) group temporarily under the control of Russian companies. Those covered include “JTI Donskoy Tabak,” which runs the Russian business of JT’s overseas subsidiary JT International (JTI), and the group’s four factories in Russia also come under the control of Russian companies. Ownership remains with the companies, but the authority to manage is effectively lost. The measure of entrusting foreign companies’ assets to domestic firms under the name of “temporary management” was applied in September to Nestlé of Switzerland and Auchan of France; this is the first time it has been applied to a major tobacco company.

The Japanese government plans to protest to Russia. As of the night of the 5th, no official explanation had been made public of why Russia took the measure. JT shares were sold down to the limit-down level in off-exchange trading after regular exchange trading had ended. How long the control will last and whether the assets will be returned are unclear, and the focus is on the outlook for the business JT has continued in Russia.

Government to Decide Emergency Measures on Unauthorized Access as Early as the 6th: About 10 Billion Yen From Reserve Fund, “Cyber Rescue Team” to Be Created

The government will decide an emergency package of measures against the string of unauthorized access incidents as early as the 6th. It plans to spend about 10 billion yen from this fiscal year’s reserve fund to improve police systems and for other purposes, and will set up a new “Cyber Rescue Team.” It has asked businesses to keep carrying out vulnerability assessments that also use AI. The Commissioner General of the National Police Agency has called for strategic implementation, including neutralization measures against attacks. Behind this is a chain of damage. In the unauthorized access to the email delivery service “Arara,” leaks, or the risk of them, were announced of 220,000 email addresses at Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, up to 830,000 at Matsui Securities and more than 620,000 at Monteroza, and Mos Food Services, Workman and IIJ also announced leaks. Iris Ohyama announced that personal information on about 7.7 million people may have leaked from its online shopping site, and unauthorized access was also confirmed at the safety-confirmation system of the Fire and Disaster Management Agency of the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications.

Docomo Outage Hits 5G SA Subscribers Nationwide: Cause Under Investigation, Separate Leak of Up to About 300,000 Records Feared

NTT Docomo announced on November 5 that voice calls, data communications and SMS were hard to use across the country. Services of budget carriers that rent Docomo’s lines (MVNO) are also affected. The outage is limited to subscribers of “5G SA,” which communicates using only dedicated 5G equipment, and in some cases service works if a device is switched to 4G and restarted. Docomo is investigating the cause and the extent of the impact.

On the morning of the same day, Docomo urgently suspended “My docomo” and its online shop services, saying it was highly likely that its own servers had been subject to unauthorized access. Customer information of up to about 300,000 records may have leaked. Docomo says it is checking the relationship between the outage and the unauthorized access. For urgent procedures, it is urging people to consult a store.

Kozo Tashima Announces Run for FIFA President: First Candidate From Japan, Challenging Incumbent Infantino

Kozo Tashima, honorary president of the Japan Football Association, held a press conference in Tokyo on November 5 and announced an intention to run in the election for president of FIFA (the international football federation) planned for next year. It is the first candidacy from Japan in history. President Gianni Infantino, whose many decisions on the World Cup have drawn criticism, has indicated an intention to run in the next election, so Tashima will stand as a challenger to the incumbent.

At the press conference, Tashima said, “Whether it has self-cleansing ability is being put to the test,” and, “We have to convey sincerity, soundness and integrity to the world.” The date of the presidential election, and whether anyone else will declare a run, had not been made clear as of the 5th.

Board of Audit Flags 250 Cases of Wasted Tax Money and More, 144.5 Billion Yen in Total; Finds Supreme Court’s 1.4 Billion Yen Improper Too

The Board of Audit compiled its audit report on the settlement of accounts for the last fiscal year on November 5. Waste of the nation’s tax money and improper accounting came to 250 cases totaling 144.5 billion yen, of which 211 cases violated accounting laws and regulations. They include excessive payment of subsidies by government offices. As for the Supreme Court, the board flagged 1.4 billion yen as improper because it violated accounting laws and regulations.

The report also touched on the response to the nuclear accident, showing that the amount spent so far is 13.9 trillion yen, which has reached 60% of the total the government had assumed. For Tokyo Electric Power, it conveys concern about a harsh management environment burdened with decommissioning and compensation.
