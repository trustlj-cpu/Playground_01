Shooting Near Paris Leaves 1 Dead; Macron Calls It a “Turning Point,” and About 70,000 Police Guard the Nationwide Demonstrations of the 24th

On the 23rd, a man fired from a car near Paris and one person died. In France, protest demonstrations were held across the country again on the 24th.

NHK reported that a series of shootings in the suburbs of Paris left one person dead and five injured, and that the authorities are investigating it as a terrorist attack. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that a man fired a gun indiscriminately from a car, leaving six people dead or injured, and that President Macron said “the victims were targeted because of their religion and the color of their skin.” Jiji Press reported that the president described the incident as a “turning point,” signaling alarm. 47NEWS reported in a news flash that protesters and riot police clashed at the demonstration in Paris. As of the morning of the 24th, NHK had reported that a sense of wariness was spreading through the streets of Paris ahead of another protest.

TV Asahi (ANN) reported that about 70,000 police officers and others were mobilized across the country for the nationwide demonstrations on the 24th, that marches in Paris followed routes that avoided commercial districts, and that the Louvre Museum was closed for safety. According to Interior Minister Nuñez, 135 people were detained nationwide at a stage when no major incident had occurred. According to ANN, in Lyon and elsewhere police used tear gas to disperse people who had gathered in areas where entry was banned. Asahi Shimbun noted that the demonstration on the 17th drew 550,000 people, more than had been forecast in advance, with riots and looting in places, and reported that on the 24th the authorities also restricted gatherings and citizens are pushing back. In the early hours of the 25th the paper reported “Paris Again Clashes With Police; ‘More Than 500 Rioters’ at the End Point of a Large-Scale Demonstration.” The motive for the shooting and the full extent of the damage from the clashes on the night of the 24th have not been confirmed in what has been reported.

Japan Series Game 1: SoftBank Win Opener in Extra 11th Inning; Yudai Shoji Delivers the Decisive Hit

NHK reported that Game 1 of the professional baseball Japan Series was played at Yokohama Stadium on the 24th, and that SoftBank edged DeNA in extra innings to claim the opening win. The player who delivered the decisive timely hit was Yudai Shoji. Nikkan Sports headlined its story “Wins an Epic 11-Inning Battle, Opens With a Win on the Road to a Second Straight Japan Series Title,” and Chunichi Sports described the series as a rematch with DeNA for the first time in two years and reported that the score was 3-1. According to Chunichi Sports, Shoji was the MVP of the Climax Series Final Stage and is from Yokohama. After the game he said, “Before I knew it I had hit it, and before I knew it I was on second base.” d menu News reported that DeNA’s Shugo Maki hit a tying home run, bringing his total to five home runs in seven postseason games. Chunichi Sports reported that starter Naoyuki Uwasawa held DeNA scoreless for seven innings, and that in the 11th inning Shoji’s go-ahead double and a sacrifice fly by pinch hitter Tatsuru Yanagimachi added two runs. Nishi Sports WEB OTTO! reported that SoftBank manager Hiroki Kokubo, looking back on his decision not to send a pinch hitter for Uwasawa in the 7th inning with one out and a runner on third, said, “I agonized over that one like crazy,” and that he had been thinking, “If we win, it will be 1-0.” Jiji Press reported the announced starters for Game 2, to be played on the 25th.

GMO Internet Asks Government for Activation of “Active Cyber Defense”; Government Side Cautious

NHK reported on the 24th that GMO Internet had asked the government to apply “active cyber defense” over an unauthorized access problem. ITmedia NEWS reported that the request concerned an attack on the FX-use VPS of “Onamae.com” and that the company had sought activation. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that GMO, having suffered the unauthorized access, urgently asked the government to apply it. Jiji Press also reported in a brief that the company had applied for its use. Mainichi Shimbun reported that if activated, it would be the first case. TBS NEWS DIG reported that the company will pay a flat 5,000 yen to all users and that it will not comply with monetary demands from the attackers.

On how the government side is taking it, Asahi Shimbun reported that “the government side is cautious,” and Yomiuri Shimbun reported that a government official said, “It is not a system under which we decide to activate it upon request.” Whether and when it will be activated and how far the source of the attack has been identified have not been indicated in what has been reported.

Kim Jong Un, During China Visit: “Thanks to the Heroes Who Stood Up to America”; Some See Move to Probe Resumption of U.S.-North Korea Dialogue

Mainichi Shimbun reported that North Korean General Secretary Kim Jong Un, visiting China, said “thanks to the heroes who stood up to America” on a milestone anniversary of the Korean War. 47NEWS reported in a news flash that Kim said he would carry on the “historic friendship” with China. Asahi Shimbun reported that there is a view linking Kim’s visit to Dandong, in northeastern China, to the future of U.S.-North Korea relations. Jiji Press distributed an article titled “Surprise Visit, China and North Korea’s Calculations Align, With Eye on ‘Trump.’”

On the aim of the visit, Mainichi Shimbun, citing South Korean media, conveyed a view that the purpose of the trip is a “positive signal toward a U.S.-North Korea summit.” Asahi Shimbun, taking it together with provocative remarks by Kim Yo Jong, introduced a view that it is a move to probe the resumption of U.S.-North Korea dialogue. United Daily News (Taiwan) reported an analysis that, following the lightning visit, the aim is to urge Beijing to carry out sooner the promises made when Xi Jinping visited North Korea. The content of China’s official announcement and whether a meeting with Premier Li Qiang took place have not been confirmed in what has been reported.

Aichi-Nagoya Asian Para Games Close: Japan’s Gold Medals a Record 64, 186 in Total

NHK reported that the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Para Games, the largest multi-sport event for para sports in Asia and the first held in Japan, reached their closing ceremony on the 24th, ending seven days of intense competition. Jiji Press reported that Japan’s gold medals reached a record 64, and Mainichi Shimbun reported the Japanese delegation’s total medal count as 186. NHK reported that in the final of the boccia mixed pairs on the last day, Kazuki Takahashi and Ayaka Sekine beat a Singapore pair 4-2 to win gold.

Mainichi Shimbun reported that Tokito Oda, who won in wheelchair tennis, said, “It was the most fun week.” Asahi Shimbun looked back on the Games in a piece titled “The Flame Goes Out Quietly in Nagoya.” Nikkei reported that while Japan’s gold medals were a record, empty seats were conspicuous at the venues. The attendance at the closing ceremony and the final ticket sales figures have not been confirmed in what has been reported.
