Israeli President Entrusts Opposition Leader Eisenkot With Forming Government; Rival to Prime Minister Netanyahu

On the night of the 7th, President Herzog entrusted Eisenkot, leader of the opposition party that came second in the general election, with building a new government. The deadline is 28 days, and the focus is whether a majority of 61 seats can be secured.

Israeli President Herzog announced on the night of the 7th (local time) that the opposition leader Eisenkot is being entrusted with the task of trying to form a government. Here, forming a government means the work by which a prime minister candidate builds a coalition framework able to win majority support in parliament. Eisenkot leads the opposition party that came second in the recent general election. In the consultations in which the president heard from each party, lawmakers’ recommendation of Eisenkot outnumbered that of Prime Minister Netanyahu. Those recommending the prime minister were the ruling Likud and other right-wing and religious right-wing parties, while those backing Eisenkot were the opposition parties and the Arab parties. The ultra-Orthodox parties recommended neither.

The period for forming a government is 28 days, and it can be extended by 14 days if necessary. If it fails, the president entrusts the task to someone else. The parliament has 120 seats and a majority is 61, but neither camp reaches that on its own. President Herzog had indicated that, although Eisenkot was clearly ahead in the recommendations, it is not known whether a government can be formed because the parties are refusing to cooperate with one another. Likud’s Kish had warned the president not to let a government that relies on Arab parties be formed. The entrusting does not mean the premiership has been decided, and coalition talks will now begin in earnest.

Quake of Upper 5 in Seismic Intensity Hits Aichi; 19 to 24 Injured by Tallies, First Upper 5 in the Prefecture in 29 Years

In the earthquake at around 1:04 a.m. on the 8th with its epicenter in western Shizuoka Prefecture (magnitude 5.7), injuries have occurred mainly in Aichi Prefecture, and have been confirmed in Toyota, Tokoname and elsewhere. The number of injured ranges from 19 to 24 depending on the tally, and the final number has not been determined. It is the first time in 29 years that a seismic intensity of upper 5 has been recorded in Aichi Prefecture, since the 1997 earthquake in eastern Aichi. The Japan Meteorological Agency continues to urge caution for about the next week against earthquakes with shaking of similar strength. The extent of injuries, building damage and the outlook for restoring roads and railways could not be confirmed as of the evening of the 8th.

On the 7th, U.S. President Trump, in a post on the president’s own social media, publicly asked Dana White, chief executive of the mixed martial arts organization UFC, to take the job of White House press secretary, appealing, “Please accept.” The press secretary is the post that conveys the president’s remarks and the administration’s policy, at news conferences and elsewhere, at home and abroad. No announcement on the procedure or timing of an appointment has come from the White House either.

Trump Calls Democrats’ Impeachment Moves “Blackmail” in Post, Orders Justice Department to Investigate

On the 7th, U.S. President Trump claimed in a post on social media that Democrats were applying pressure by dangling impeachment over the attorney general and others, and called the move “blackmail.” Impeachment is a procedure in which the legislature decides whether to hold senior government officials accountable and dismiss them. In the post, the president wrote, in essence, that there was no conduct worthy of impeachment and that the Democrats too admit there is no reason for it, and then directed the Justice Department to investigate Democratic lawmakers and others.

The range of lawmakers to be investigated and how the Justice Department will act have not been confirmed. The specific remarks by the Democratic side said to have dangled impeachment are also not shown within the post. The post came after the midterm elections, and the confrontation with the administration in situations where the Democrats hold power in Congress is the focus going forward.

Fluorescent Lamp Manufacturing Ban Nears at End of 2027; Decided Under Minamata Convention, LED Adoption Rate About 60%

The manufacture of fluorescent lamps will be banned internationally by the end of 2027. At the 2023 conference of the parties to the Minamata Convention, it was decided to end the manufacture, export and import of fluorescent lamps for general lighting by the end of 2027. Fluorescent lamps use mercury, and the Minamata Convention is an international treaty to prevent health damage and environmental pollution caused by mercury. The timing differs by type, and some, such as the bulb-shaped ones, have been subject to regulation since earlier. Even after the ban, it remains possible to use and sell fluorescent lamps and stock already on hand.

The switch to LED is advancing, and according to a survey by the Japan Lighting Manufacturers Association, the LED adoption rate as of July 2024 was about 60%. Panasonic announced in October 2024 that it will end production of fluorescent lamps by the end of September 2027. Replacing the remaining roughly 40% of fixtures is the challenge, and there have also been fire accidents caused by swapping LED lamps into fixtures made for fluorescent lamps.
