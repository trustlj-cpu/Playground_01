Bahrain's Interior Ministry Says Iranian Attack Set Fire to Civilian Industrial Facility, No Injuries; Revolutionary Guard Claims It Targeted U.S. Military Radar

Bahrain announced a fire at a civilian facility and no injuries, while Iran claims an attack on military targets; the accounts conflict.

Bahrain's Interior Ministry announced that an Iranian attack caused a fire at a civilian industrial facility in the early hours of the 22nd (Japan time). Local civil defense authorities are working to bring the fire under control; according to provisional information there was physical damage but no injuries. The country's Defense Force also explained that its air defense units intercepted multiple aerial attacks by Iran, that debris from the interceptions caused physical damage, and that there were no casualties. After announcing that the alert had been lifted, the Interior Ministry again urged caution over the possibility of new threats.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard, for its part, claims it attacked two U.S. military bases in Bahrain and targeted two Patriot (an air defense system that operates interceptor missiles) radars, Iranian media report. The Revolutionary Guard is an elite military organization set up separately from Iran's regular army. The place Bahrain calls a “civilian industrial facility” and the military targets Iran cites do not match, and it has not been confirmed which facility was actually damaged. Whether the U.S. military suffered damage, and whether the attacks will continue, have also not been confirmed.

Emergency Vehicles Can Now Pass in Kitashiobara, Fukushima; Some Previously Isolated Hotel Guests Reported to Start Heading Home

In Kitashiobara Village, Fukushima Prefecture, wet snow since the 20th brought down trees one after another, and about 18 km of National Route 459 between Inawashiro Town and Kitashiobara Village was closed, leaving residents and hotel guests isolated. As of the afternoon of the 21st, 514 households with 948 people were reported isolated, and by the morning of the 22nd the figure was said to be a little over 500 households. Emergency vehicles have since become able to pass, some hotel guests have begun heading home, and food and water are reported to have been delivered by disaster-prevention helicopter. The prefecture resumed snow-clearing work from the morning of the 22nd.

Isolation refers to a state in which roads become unusable and it is hard to travel to and from the outside or to transport supplies. Some headlines say the isolation has been resolved, but the extent to which it has been resolved, and how many people remain isolated, have not been confirmed. When the national road's closure will be lifted, the effects on daily life such as power outages and water cuts, and the number of hotel guests have also not been confirmed.

Tokyo Regional Taxation Bureau Reported to Have Audited More Than 90 Crypto Asset Users Last Fiscal Year, Flagging Over 3.9 Billion Yen in Unreported Income

In tax audits of crypto assets (virtual currency) users, the Tokyo Regional Taxation Bureau is reported, according to people familiar with the matter, to have examined more than 90 people last fiscal year and flagged a combined total of over 3.9 billion yen in unreported income. With users growing year by year, the tax authorities are said to plan to strengthen their audit setup. Worked out per person, that comes to an average of unreported income in the 40-million-yen range.

Unreported income refers to a state in which income that should have been declared was not, and profits from trading crypto assets are in principle treated as taxable income. However, these figures rest on accounts from anonymous people familiar with the matter, and no official announcement from the Regional Taxation Bureau has been confirmed. The amount of additional tax, the breakdown of those audited and the specifics of the strengthened setup have also not been confirmed.
