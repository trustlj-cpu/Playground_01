Trump Replaces Chief of Staff Wiles With Miller; No Official Reason Given for the Departure

President Trump said on the 6th, U.S. time, that Chief of Staff Wiles will leave the post and that Deputy Chief of Staff Miller will be appointed as successor. No official reason has been given for the departure.

U.S. President Trump announced on the 6th, U.S. time, that Susan Wiles, the White House chief of staff, will step down and that Stephen Miller, the deputy chief of staff, will be appointed as successor. The chief of staff is the closest aide to the president, managing the president’s schedule, appointments and coordination with the departments, and Wiles has held the post since last year, when the second-term administration began. Republicans suffered a heavy defeat in the midterm elections on Nov. 3.

Miller, the successor, is known as a hard-liner who has led the line of tightly restricting the acceptance of immigrants. Until now Miller has served concurrently as deputy chief of staff for policy and as the presidential assistant for homeland security. It cannot be confirmed that the reason for the departure was explained by the president’s side, and whether the change is a personnel move to hold someone responsible for the election defeat has also not been shown officially. The chief of staff is a post that does not require confirmation by the Senate and can be replaced at the president’s discretion. Wiles’s next step, and how the people around the president will change under Miller, had not been made clear as of the 6th.

Tesla’s Musk to Scale Back Cybertruck Production in Stages, Increase Output of Self-Driving Taxis

Tesla CEO Elon Musk said on the 6th that production of the Cybertruck, an electric pickup truck, in Texas will be scaled back in stages. As the reason, Musk explained that the factory’s capacity will be redirected to increasing production of the Cybercab, a driverless self-driving taxi. The Cybertruck drew attention for its angular stainless-steel body, and deliveries began in 2023. The reason Musk gave is increased production of self-driving taxis. The specific timing of the scale-back and annual sales figures could not be confirmed. The timing and volume of the start of Cybercab production have also not been stated.

Iranian Official: “U.S. Must Take Concrete Action Before Talks Resume”; Trump to Decide Soon on Further Strikes

An Iranian government official said on the 6th that the United States must first take concrete action before diplomatic negotiations with it can resume, stressing a stance of no compromise. Earlier, President Trump had indicated that a decision on whether to carry out further attacks on Iran would be made soon. The United States attacked Iran this year, and whether things move toward resumed talks or resumed attacks now stands at a fork in the road.

Qatar is attempting mediation, and its prime minister said the United States and Iran cannot reach a “perfect deal” and should accept a good one. The prime minister also made remarks to the effect that further clashes cannot be endured. The substance of the talks via Qatar, including the handling of the nuclear program, could not be confirmed. The official’s remarks did not move from the position that the United States should act first. Whether a deal is near could not be confirmed as of the 6th.

U.S. Treasury Secretary and China’s Vice Premier He Reportedly Hold Phone Call, Discuss Implementation of Trade “Truce” Ahead of Summit

According to one report, U.S. Treasury Secretary Bessent held a phone call on the 6th with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng. Ahead of a summit between President Trump and President Xi Jinping, they reportedly discussed the implementation of the “truce” struck in the U.S.-China trade friction, among other matters. The truce was decided at last October’s summit as a one-year framework and originally expired on the 10th of this month. At ministerial-level talks in September, the two sides agreed to extend the deadline by two months, to Jan. 10, 2027.

The details of the phone call and the date of the summit could not be confirmed. The truce expires on Jan. 10, 2027, and how the two countries will treat this framework by then has not been decided.

Joshua Wong Sentenced to 10 Years, 10 Countries Condemn in Statement, Single Report Says; Not Yet Confirmed Elsewhere

According to one report, a Hong Kong court sentenced Joshua Wong, a prominent Hong Kong pro-democracy activist, to 10 years in prison. The same report says that 10 countries, including Japan, the United States and European nations, condemned the sentence and issued a statement calling for “an immediate end to the unjust pursuit of those who have been charged.” No other confirmation has been obtained. Wong is known as a student leader of the large-scale 2014 pro-democracy protests, the “Umbrella Movement.”

The date of the sentence, the charges and the full text of the countries’ statement could not be confirmed. How the Hong Kong and Chinese governments responded to the statement has also not been stated.
