Lawyer Atsumi Harasawa Wins Yokohama Mayoral Race for the First Time, Beating Six Rivals Including Former Mayor Yamanaka, Who Resigned Over Power Harassment

The snap mayoral election was held on the 18th. Turnout was 45.56%, up 3.92 points from the previous election.

Atsumi Harasawa (59), a lawyer and political newcomer with no party affiliation, won the Yokohama mayoral election held on the 18th to choose a successor to former Mayor Takeharu Yamanaka (54), who resigned after being found to have engaged in power harassment of staff, defeating six rivals including Mr. Yamanaka, who ran again (Tokyo Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun, Kanagawa Shimbun). Seven candidates ran, and six of them, all but the Japanese Communist Party's candidate, had no party affiliation (Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun, distributed by Kyodo News). Mr. Harasawa had the endorsement of the local chapters of the Liberal Democratic Party, Nippon Ishin no Kai and the Democratic Party for the People, and the support of the Komeito city assembly caucus (Tokyo Shimbun, Kyodo News). According to Tokyo Shimbun, Mr. Yamanaka had been found in a third-party investigation to have engaged in seven kinds of conduct, including “control of staff backed by personnel authority” and “remarks denying people's character,” and resigned in September. Asahi Shimbun reported that Atsushi Fukuyama, endorsed by Sanseito, was certain to finish second and ahead of Mr. Yamanaka and the others. The early returns on tvk also had Mr. Fukuyama in second place behind Mr. Harasawa.

According to Kanagawa Shimbun, the turnout announced by the city election commission was 45.56%, up 3.92 points from the previous election. The paper said voters' reaction to the power harassment issue was harsh and that Mr. Yamanaka failed to win re-election. According to Mainichi Shimbun, after his defeat became certain Mr. Yamanaka said it was “a harsh reception from the citizens.” On hearing he had won, Mr. Harasawa thanked supporters, saying, “For me, with no record of achievement,” and Jiji Press reported that he indicated he would start work “right away.” In a Kyodo News phone survey before the official campaign began, Mr. Harasawa had the edge, with Mr. Yamanaka and Kazuma Nakatani, a former member of the House of Representatives backed by the Constitutional Democratic Party's prefectural chapter, in pursuit. Mainichi Shimbun reported that Mr. Harasawa drew wide support from supporters of the LDP, Ishin and the DPP and from unaffiliated voters. According to Kyodo News, Mr. Harasawa is the woman lawyer the LDP had been working to field; after saying she would give it “positive consideration” when approached, she announced her candidacy on September 10 (Akita Sakigake Shimpo, Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun).

Asian Para Games, Held in Japan for the First Time, Open in Nagoya; Emperor Attends Opening Ceremony

The Aichi-Nagoya Asian Para Games opened on the 18th, with the opening ceremony held from 6 p.m. at Paloma Mizuho Stadium in Nagoya (Jiji Press, TV Aichi). It is the first time the Asian Para Games have been held in Japan, and according to Jiji Press 45 countries and regions are taking part, with competition continuing through the 24th. The Emperor arrived at JR Nagoya Station by Shinkansen at about 11:40 a.m. and was greeted by Governor Hideaki Omura and others (CBC TV). Prince and Princess Akishino also attended the opening ceremony (TV Aichi). There are 18 sports, and the venues span Aichi and Shizuoka prefectures. Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun (distributed by Kyodo News) reported that about 280 athletes from the Japanese team are expected to compete in all 18 sports. Jiji Press reported that many empty seats were conspicuous at the opening ceremony. Nakagawa, who served as flag-bearer, said, “It tightened me up” (Jiji Press). The Emperor is scheduled to watch Para table tennis in Toyota on the 19th (CBC TV). Asahi Shimbun touched on the “power of change” that Para sports bring and reported the opening as the start of a festival toward realizing an inclusive society. At a news conference before the opening, the head of the Japanese delegation said he wanted to speed up social change (Kyodo News).

Hanshin Weighs Allowing Teruaki Sato to Move to Major League Baseball via Posting; Applications Start November 1

Hanshin is considering allowing infielder Teruaki Sato, who wants to move to Major League Baseball this offseason, to use the posting system, Sports Hochi and Nikkan Sports reported. According to Sports Hochi, the club regards Mr. Sato as an indispensable part of its lineup but also understands his dream, and is expected to make a final decision soon. Nikkan Sports reported that, after the team's elimination in the Climax Series, it will enter full-scale talks. Satoshi Shimamura, the club general manager, said on the 17th, “I have no comment as of today,” and on the 18th, on the question of allowing it, “I will refrain from commenting,” though he did not deny it (Sports Hochi, Tokyo Sports). Posting applications open on November 1 (Sports Hochi). Sports Hochi reports that Mr. Sato became the ninth Triple Crown winner in history this season.

550,000 Rally Across France as Some Turn Violent; City Hall in Central Limoges Nearly Burned Down

On the 17th in France there were nationwide demonstrations demanding measures against high prices and better conditions in education, and 550,000 people took part, according to the Interior Ministry (Yomiuri Shimbun, Nikkei, FNN). According to Nikkei, it was the largest since high school students and others began their protest movement in September by blockading schools. Destruction and looting also took place under cover of the demonstrations, and in Limoges in the central part of the country, rioters set fire to the city hall and most of the building burned (Yomiuri Shimbun, Nikkei). Jiji Press reported that arson and break-ins also occurred in Lyon and Marseille, that nearly 1,500 retail stores were attacked across the country, and that the damage is expected to reach several hundred million euros. According to Jiji Press, about 1,800 people had been arrested by the 18th, and more than 500 police officers and firefighters were injured.

Very Strong Typhoon No. 30, With Maximum Gusts of 70 Meters, Heads for South of Okinawa; Sakishima to See Heavy Seas From the 22nd

Very strong Typhoon No. 30 (Champi) moved west near the Mariana Islands on the 18th (Weathernews, RBC Ryukyu Broadcasting). RBC Ryukyu Broadcasting, citing the Japan Meteorological Agency, reported that the central pressure at 6 p.m. on the 18th was 950 hectopascals and the maximum gust was 70 meters. According to the agency, the typhoon is expected to reach south of Okinawa around the 23rd, and the Sakishima region will see heavy seas from around the 22nd to the 23rd, with a risk of warning-level storm surge in the Yaeyama region (RBC Ryukyu Broadcasting). Weathernews reported that most conventional physical models show a westward course, while AI-based forecasts still include a calculation that moves north through the East China Sea. Chugoku Broadcasting reported that one of the agency's forecast models shows a route toward the continent. According to the same station, as of the 16th this model had shown a route approaching Honshu from the vicinity of Okinawa.
