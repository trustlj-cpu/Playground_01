Lawyer Atsumi Harasawa Wins Yokohama Mayoral Race for the First Time, Beating Six Rivals Including Former Mayor Yamanaka, Who Resigned Over Power Harassment

The re-run mayoral election was held on the 18th. Turnout was 45.56%, up 3.92 points from the previous election.

Former Mayor Takeharu Yamanaka (54) resigned after being found to have subjected staff to power harassment. The Yokohama mayoral election to choose his successor was held on the 18th, and Atsumi Harasawa (59), an independent newcomer and lawyer, won for the first time, beating six rivals including Yamanaka, who ran again (Tokyo Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun, Kanagawa Shimbun). Seven candidates ran, and six of them, all but the Communist Party’s candidate, were independents (Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun, distributed by Kyodo News). Harasawa had been endorsed by the local chapters of the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP), Nippon Ishin no Kai and the Democratic Party for the People (DPP), and supported by the Komeito city assembly caucus (Tokyo Shimbun, Kyodo News). According to Tokyo Shimbun, Yamanaka had been found in a third-party investigation to have committed seven items, including “control of staff backed by personnel authority” and “remarks denying personal character,” and resigned in September. The Asahi Shimbun reported that Atsushi Fukuyama, endorsed by Sanseito, was certain to be the runner-up, ahead of Yamanaka and the others. In tvk’s vote-count flash, too, Fukuyama was second behind Harasawa.

According to Kanagawa Shimbun, turnout announced by the city election administration commission was 45.56%, up 3.92 points from the previous election. The paper reported that voters’ reaction to the power harassment issue was harsh and that Yamanaka was not re-elected. According to Mainichi Shimbun, after his defeat became certain Yamanaka said, “the citizens’ harsh response.” On learning that she was certain to win, Harasawa thanked her supporters, saying, “to me, who has no record of achievement,” and Jiji Press reported that she indicated she would get to work “right away.” In a Kyodo News telephone poll before the official start of the campaign, Harasawa held the advantage, with Yamanaka and Kazuma Nakatani, a former House of Representatives member backed by the Constitutional Democratic Party’s prefectural chapter, in pursuit. Mainichi Shimbun reported that Harasawa drew broad support from supporters of the LDP, Ishin and the DPP and from unaffiliated voters. According to Kyodo News, Harasawa is a female lawyer whom the LDP had been working to field; she replied that she would consider it “positively” when approached, and announced her candidacy on Sept. 10 (Akita Sakigake Shimpo, Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun).

Asian Para Games, Held in Japan for the First Time, Open in Nagoya; Emperor Attends Opening Ceremony

The Aichi-Nagoya Asian Para Games opened on the 18th, and the opening ceremony began at 6 p.m. at Paloma Mizuho Stadium in Nagoya (Jiji Press, TV Aichi). It is the first time the Asian Para Games have been held in Japan; according to Jiji Press, 45 countries and regions are taking part, and the competition continues through the 24th. The Emperor arrived at JR Nagoya Station by Shinkansen at about 11:40 a.m. and was greeted by Gov. Hideaki Omura and others (CBC TV). Prince and Princess Akishino also attended the opening ceremony (TV Aichi). There are 18 sports, and the venues span Aichi and Shizuoka Prefectures. Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun (distributed by Kyodo News) reported that the Japanese team plans to field about 280 athletes in all 18 sports. Jiji Press reported that empty seats were conspicuous in the audience at the opening ceremony. Flag bearer Nakagawa said, “It sharpened my focus” (Jiji Press). The Emperor is scheduled to watch para table tennis in Toyota on the 19th (CBC TV). The Asahi Shimbun, touching on the “power of change” that para sports bring, reported the opening as a festival aimed at realizing an inclusive society. The head of the Japanese delegation said at a pre-games press conference that he wants to accelerate social change (Kyodo News).

Hanshin Considering Allowing Teruaki Sato to Move to the Majors via Posting; Applications Open Nov. 1

Sports Hochi and Nikkan Sports reported that Hanshin is leaning toward allowing infielder Teruaki Sato, who wants to move to the major leagues this offseason, to use the posting system. According to Sports Hochi, the club regards Sato as indispensable but is said to understand his feelings about his dream as well, and is expected to make a final decision soon. Nikkan Sports reported that, following the elimination in the Climax Series, the club will enter full-scale talks. Satoshi Shimamura, the club’s operations chief, said on the 17th, “I have no comment as of today,” and on the 18th, while saying “I will refrain from commenting” on whether the move would be allowed, did not deny it (Sports Hochi, Tokyo Sports). Posting applications begin on Nov. 1 (Sports Hochi). Sports Hochi reports that Sato became the ninth Triple Crown winner in history this season.

550,000 Demonstrate Across France; Some Turn Violent, and City Hall in Central Limoges Is Nearly Burned Down

On the 17th in France, a nationwide demonstration demanding measures against high prices and improvements in the education environment took place, and 550,000 people took part, according to the Interior Ministry (Yomiuri Shimbun, Nikkei, FNN). According to Nikkei, it was the largest since high school students and others began a protest movement in September by blockading schools. Destruction and looting also took place under cover of the demonstration, and in Limoges in the central part of the country, rioters set fire to the city hall and most of the building burned (Yomiuri Shimbun, Nikkei). Jiji Press reported that arson and break-ins also occurred in Lyon and Marseille, that nearly 1,500 retail stores were attacked nationwide, and that the damage is expected to reach several hundred million euros. According to Jiji Press, about 1,800 people had been arrested as of the 18th, and more than 500 police and firefighters were injured.

Very Strong Typhoon No. 30, With Maximum Gusts of 70 Meters, Heads for South of Okinawa; Sakishima to See Heavy Seas From the 22nd

Very strong Typhoon No. 30 (Champi) moved west near the Mariana Islands on the 18th (Weathernews, RBC Ryukyu Broadcasting). RBC Ryukyu Broadcasting, citing the Japan Meteorological Agency, reported that the central pressure at 6 p.m. on the 18th was 950 hectopascals and that the maximum gust was 70 meters. According to the Japan Meteorological Agency, the typhoon is expected to reach south of Okinawa around the 23rd, and the Sakishima region will see heavy seas from around the 22nd to the 23rd, with a risk of warning-level storm surge in the Yaeyama region (RBC Ryukyu Broadcasting). Weathernews reported that while most conventional physical models show a westward course, some AI-based forecasts still include a calculation of it moving north through the East China Sea. Chugoku Broadcasting reported that one of the Japan Meteorological Agency’s forecast models shows a route toward the continent. According to the station, as of the 16th this model showed a route approaching Honshu from near Okinawa.
