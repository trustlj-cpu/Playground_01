Censure Motion Against Education Minister Seki Reported Voted Down in Upper House by Narrow Margin, 120 For and 124 Against; Prime Minister Takaichi Plans to Keep Seki in Post

The House of Councillors on the 27th voted down a censure motion against Education Minister Yoshihiro Seki at a plenary session. The tally is reported as 120 for and 124 against. Prime Minister Takaichi plans to keep Seki in the post

The House of Councillors on the 27th (Fri.) put to a vote at a plenary session the censure motion against Education Minister Yoshihiro Seki that the opposition side had submitted on the 26th. It was voted down by a majority of the ruling parties and others against it, and the tally is reported as 120 for and 124 against, a margin of four votes. A censure motion is an expression of the House of Councillors' will to hold a minister responsible; even if passed, it creates no legal obligation to resign. However, there was a view that passage would affect deliberation of bills in the House of Councillors, and as of the 26th a forecast that votes for and against would be evenly split, producing a tie, had also emerged. The origin is the problem of Seki holding a political fundraising party in a members' office building of the Diet, in violation of House of Representatives internal rules. Before the vote, Seki denied resigning and said, "I want to fulfill my responsibility."

With the ruling parties and others voting against, the motion was rejected. Prime Minister Takaichi plans to keep Seki in the cabinet. After the vote, Seki did not respond to reporters' calls. In what form Seki will go on explaining the party problem had not yet been shown as of the evening of the 27th. What approach the opposition side will take next in the Diet has not been confirmed either.

Tokyo's 23 Wards Consumer Prices for November Up 2.9% Excluding Fresh Food, Above 2% for a Third Straight Month

The consumer price index (flash) for November for Tokyo's 23 wards, released on the 27th, was 2.9% higher than in the same month a year earlier for the overall index excluding fresh food, whose prices swing widely with the weather. The rate of increase topped 2% for a third straight month, and electricity and gas charges pushed it up. The consumer price index puts into numbers the movement in the prices of the goods and services that households buy. Because the figures for Tokyo's 23 wards come out ahead of the national results, they serve as a clue for grasping the national trend in prices early. This figure is also material for reading the Bank of Japan's monetary policy. The BOJ raised its policy rate in September from about 1.0% to about 1.25% and left it unchanged in October. The next Monetary Policy Meeting is on Dec. 17–18, and on the 26th, Policy Board member Naoki Tamura said that the policy rate needs to be raised early to the neutral rate. The neutral rate is the level of interest rate that neither heats nor cools the economy. With price growth staying above 2%, the focus is on the decision on an additional rate hike at the December meeting.

Land Minister Ibayashi on Corporate Donation, Defense Minister Koizumi on Unrecorded Donation, Minister Kiuchi on Fundraising Party: Reports on Ministers' Political Funds Come One After Another

On the 27th, it was reported that Land Minister Ibayashi had received a donation during an election period from a company that has a contract with the government. The Public Offices Election Act (Election Act) prohibits companies that have contracts with the government, such as for public works, from making donations in connection with elections. Ibayashi denies any illegality. Ibayashi also held a political fundraising party in October, and had explained, "It was prepared from before I took office, and I believe it does not conflict." Defense Minister Koizumi was reported on the 26th to have left unrecorded in the political funds report a donation of 3.8 million yen between a party branch that Koizumi heads and an organization of the Yokosuka mayor. Koizumi on the 27th denied a violation of the Election Act.

Minister Kiuchi said on the 27th, regarding having held a political fundraising party, that it "does not violate the ministerial code." The ministerial code is an understanding that in principle bars ministers from holding political fundraising parties while in office. Earlier that same morning of the 27th, a censure motion over the party that Education Minister Seki held in the members' office building had just been rejected in the House of Councillors. Whether moves will emerge to look closely into the three ministers' explanations and the circumstances of the donation had not been confirmed as of the evening of the 27th.

"Secondary Capital" to Be Designated in Autumn 2027 and Begin Operating in Spring 2028, as Government Settles Basic Approach and Planned Schedule

On the 27th, the government announced a basic approach and planned schedule for a "secondary capital" that would take over capital functions in the event of a large-scale disaster. The plan envisages designating several prefectures in autumn 2027 and starting operation in spring 2028. The secondary capital is a concept of preparing, in normal times, a base that can supplement core government and economic functions when Tokyo's functions are halted by a disaster.

The designation is made by the state, and several prefectures are expected to be chosen. Which prefectures will be designated, and the requirements for designation, the conditions for administrative structure and the costs, are not shown in what has been published and are not yet decided.

Astronaut Recruitment to Begin Around April 2027, a Few to Be Hired, No Requirement on Education or Field of Expertise

The Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology and others announced on the 27th that recruitment of new astronauts will begin around April 2027. The plan is to hire a few people, and educational background and field of expertise are not required. After a selection examination, results will be announced around autumn 2028. It is the first recruitment of Japanese astronauts in five years, and it aims to develop people to take part in lunar exploration.

Recruitment has not yet started, and it is not possible to apply at present. Detailed conditions such as eligibility and the content of the examination must await future announcements. A selection period of about a year and a half from the start of recruitment to the announcement of results is envisaged. Because field of expertise is not required, the door is open not only to researchers and engineers but also to people in occupations that have had no connection with space until now.
