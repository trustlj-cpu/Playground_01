Woman Killed at Naha Hotel: 20-Year-Old U.S. Marine Lance Corporal Indicted for Robbery-Murder; Reports Say He Returned to Base After the Crime

The Naha District Public Prosecutors Office on the 25th indicted a U.S. Marine lance corporal over the killing of a woman at a hotel in Naha on October 3.

Mainichi Shimbun reported on the 25th that the Naha District Public Prosecutors Office had indicted a U.S. serviceman for robbery-murder on suspicion of killing a woman at a hotel in Naha and taking her backpack. Jiji Press also ran a news flash under the title “U.S. Serviceman Indicted for Robbery-Murder; Woman’s Body at Naha Hotel.” Yomiuri Shimbun reported that the person indicted was a member of the U.S. Marine Corps and that at his arrest he had denied the suspicion, saying, “I don’t know anything about it.” Kyodo News reported that the defendant is Devin Ballard (20), a lance corporal assigned to Futenma Air Station, and that according to the indictment, on the 3rd he killed the woman by wrapping a telephone-handset cord around her neck and took a backpack containing a wallet. The cause of death was reportedly suffocation. According to Jiji Press, the case began with the discovery of a woman’s body at a hotel in Naha on October 3, and Okinawa prefectural police had arrested the defendant on the 4th.

Asahi Shimbun reported, citing investigative sources, that the defendant is believed to have returned to the base after the crime, changed clothes and gone out again. Kyodo News reported that he returned to the base once after the crime and was found by prefectural police as he went out again, and that his statements have shifted from an outright denial at the time of arrest to ones partly hinting at involvement. Kyodo News also reported that the deputy chief prosecutor of the Naha District Public Prosecutors Office commented that the office will work to prove the case on the basis of law and evidence at the coming public trial. Asahi Shimbun reported the same day that a shop owner in Koza, speaking of the U.S. serviceman caught on a security camera, said angrily, “Nothing has changed.” The English-language Japan Times also reported in English that a U.S. Marine had been indicted in the Okinawa robbery-murder case. The motive and the schedule of the public trial have not been confirmed in what has been reported.

SoftBank Wins a Second Straight 10-Inning Game, 4-3, on Yamakawa’s Tying Homer and Yuto Kawamura’s Winning Solo Shot, Opening the Series With Two Wins

Jiji Press reported that Game 2 of the Japan Series in pro baseball was played on the 25th and that SoftBank came from behind to beat DeNA 4-3 and open the series with two straight wins. According to Baseball Channel, DeNA took the lead at one point, but in the 9th SoftBank pinch hitter Hotaka Yamakawa hit a tying home run, and in the 10th Yuto Kawamura, who had entered as a pinch runner, hit a winning solo home run off DeNA pitcher Hiromu Ise. Nikkan Sports reported that Kawamura had no home runs this season and that a winning home run in a first at-bat of the Series was the second since 1990. Asahi Shimbun reported that both Kawamura and manager Hiroki Kokubo took the winning shot as “unbelievable.” Mainichi Shimbun reported that Yamakawa hit a ball out of the park after advice from “the King,” and that DeNA manager Aikawa said of the relievers who gave up the homers, “How can you blame them?” Sankei Shimbun reported that SoftBank has won five straight in the postseason without a loss and that Game 3 will be played on the 27th at SoftBank’s home ballpark.

Typhoon No. 30 Becomes an Extratropical Cyclone on the Night of the 25th; With a Weather Front, Heavy Rain Possible From Western Japan to Kanto Through the 26th, Evacuation for the Elderly and Others Issued in Kumamoto and Elsewhere

NHK reported, citing Japan Meteorological Agency observations, that Typhoon No. 30 turned into an extratropical cyclone over the East China Sea at 9 p.m. on the 25th. The broadcaster reported that “evacuation for the elderly and others” was issued for Kumamoto City and Nagato, Yamaguchi Prefecture, and that at a fishing port in Minamishimabara, Nagasaki Prefecture, a fisherman in his 70s was found sunk in the sea and died; it said police believe he had gone to check on his boat in preparation for the typhoon and are investigating. Kyoto Shimbun reported that warning-level heavy rain is possible in the Kinki region from the 25th to the 26th, with two peaks of strong rain, and that a heavy rain warning was issued for Osaka City.

FNN Prime Online reported that, because of the weather front and the extratropical cyclone that the typhoon turned into, heavy rain is possible in Kanagawa and Chiba prefectures as well from the 25th to the 27th, and that caution is needed against landslides, flooding of low-lying areas and flooded roads. Asahi Shimbun urged vigilance against warning-level heavy rain in western Japan, and tenki.jp reported that storm-force winds are possible in Hokkaido on the 28th.

Society · EarthquakePress ✓ 5 outlets · whether there was damage unconfirmed

M7.0 Deep-Focus Earthquake Off the West of the Ogasawara Islands; Seismic Intensity 3 in Ogasawara, Shaking Also Felt in Tohoku in an Anomalous Seismic Zone, No Tsunami Concern

NHK and Jiji Press reported in news flashes on the 25th that an earthquake registering seismic intensity 3 occurred in Ogasawara Village. Weathernews reported that a magnitude 7.0 deep-focus earthquake occurred off the west of the Ogasawara Islands, that there is no tsunami concern, and that an anomalous seismic zone was observed, with shaking recorded even in Tohoku, far from the epicenter. tenki.jp of the Japan Weather Association reported that it occurred at about 11:10 a.m. at a focal depth of about 470 kilometers.

Chiba Nippo reported that shaking was also felt in Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, Chiba, Shizuoka and elsewhere, that the maximum seismic intensity was 3, and that the population of the areas reached by the shaking comes to 34.53 million. tenki.jp reported seismic intensity 2 in Chiyoda Ward, Tokyo. tenki.jp reported that there is no tsunami concern. Injuries and damage to buildings have not been confirmed in what has been reported.

Hyogo Prefectural Assembly By-Election: Former Vice Governor Katayama Loses; Naha Mayor Chinen Wins Re-election; Shizukuishi, Iwate, Gets the Prefecture’s First Woman Town Head

Jiji Press reported that Yasutaka Katayama, a former vice governor who had been a close aide to Governor Motohiko Saito, lost in the Hyogo Prefectural Assembly by-election. Before voting and counting, Asahi Shimbun had reported that the by-election was a contest between the former vice governor and a former city assembly member from the LDP camp, and Kyodo News reported that Asuka Shimazu (38), a former Takasago city assembly member endorsed by the LDP prefectural chapter, won. Mainichi Shimbun and NHK reported that incumbent Satoru Chinen was all but certain to win re-election in the Naha mayoral race, and Mainichi Shimbun said he had the backing of Governor Koja. Jiji Press and NHK reported that incumbent Yaichi Nakahara was all but certain to win a third term in the Niigata mayoral race, defeating two newcomers.

Asahi Shimbun reported that in the town mayoral race in Shizukuishi, Iwate Prefecture, Hakuseru (ハクセル) defeated the incumbent to win a first term, producing the first woman local chief executive in the prefecture, and that eight prefectures nationwide still have “zero” women local chief executives, with Tokyo having the largest number and share. The paper also reported that in the town mayoral race in Nakai, Kanagawa Prefecture, incumbent Yuji Tomura won re-election over a newcomer by a margin of 309 votes.
