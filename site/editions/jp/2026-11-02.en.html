GMO President to Hold First Press Conference on the 2nd to Explain Attack on Investor Cloud; Another Group Service Also Leaked Data on 45,030 People

The course of the attack and the extent of the damage have not yet been officially explained. At another GMO group service, the information of employees of client companies could be obtained from outside for about 9 years.

GMO Internet Group President Masatoshi Kumagai will hold a press conference on the 2nd, the first since the attack on its investor-oriented cloud service came to light. The company has not yet officially explained the method of attack, the extent of the damage or who carried it out. The possible involvement of a North Korea-linked hacker group is being speculated about, but neither GMO nor the government has acknowledged it, and the basis for the claim has not been made clear. On October 24, after transactions that no one could account for were found, the company made an urgent request to the government for active cyber defense. It is a mechanism under which the government can detect signs of a serious attack in advance and neutralize the servers the attack comes from.

Another GMO group service has also revealed lapses in information management. In a service provided by group company GMO GlobalSign that manages employee IDs and passwords, the personal information of a total of 45,030 people at 2,492 client companies leaked. The cause was a configuration error, and the information could be obtained from outside for about 9 years. It is a separate matter from the attack on the investor cloud, and it has not been confirmed whether the information was actually misused.

Consumption Tax Cut Bill Back in the House of Representatives This Week; Ruling Parties Aim to Clear It on the 6th, Opposition Wants Prime Minister to Attend

A bill to cut the consumption tax on food and beverages from 8% to 1% will be debated in the House of Representatives again this week. The government adopted the bill by Cabinet decision on October 9; it would apply for 2 years from April 2027. The funding needed for the cut is put at about ¥10 trillion over the 2 years, but concrete arrangements to cover it have been put off. The ruling parties, calling it “the best measure against rising prices,” want to hold talks toward a vote on the bill and aim for House passage on the 6th. The opposition has repeatedly demanded that Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi appear before the committee and answer questions, and whether the prime minister attends is the focus. Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) Policy Research Council chair Kobayashi said on the 1st that the party intends to approach the opposition to get the bill enacted. There is caution within the party as well: former Tax Commission chair Miyazawa holds the position of “benefit payments rather than a tax cut,” but supports the bill itself. Both ruling and opposition camps are also watching Komeito’s response, and it is being speculated that the House of Representatives and the House of Councillors could split on approval.

Disaster Management Agency Launches on the 2nd, Headed by the Prime Minister and Handling Everything From Normal Times to Recovery

The Disaster Management Agency, the command center for disaster response, was launched on November 2. It is an organization directly under the Cabinet, set up by reorganizing the disaster management bureaus of the Cabinet Office, and the prime minister serves as its head. It handles everything in one line, from pre-disaster prevention to reduce damage, through the response after a disaster occurs, to recovery and reconstruction. A disaster management minister, who holds recommendation authority to demand improvements from other ministries, oversees operations, and Reconstruction Minister Hosono is expected to double as the first holder of the post.

Before the launch, Hosono said, “We will make Japan’s disaster management new, we will rebuild it,” and had said an “urgent request” would be issued to the public and others. The budget request for fiscal 2027 totals ¥65.1 billion, about 3 times the initial fiscal 2026 budget of ¥21.7 billion for the disaster management bureaus and others. Of this, ¥22.5 billion is a new category supporting the development and practical use of disaster-prevention technology by the private sector. The final budget amount will be decided in the upcoming budget compilation.

Iran Holds Talks With Mediator Qatar Again on the 1st; Maneuvering to Gauge U.S. Reaction Continues

Iran held talks with the mediating country Qatar again on November 1. Iran’s foreign minister has said a delegation visited Qatar. As the view spreads that the United States may resume attacks on Iran, Iran is thought to be sounding out the U.S. reaction indirectly through Qatar. The focus is whether indirect talks between the U.S. and Iran will resume, but the content and schedule of the talks have not been made public.

The fighting that began in February has not ended, with the claims over opening the Strait of Hormuz still in conflict. On September 22, the U.S. and Iran held about 2 hours of indirect talks mediated by Qatar. As conditions for resuming negotiations, Iran has cited an end to the U.S. military’s blockade of ports and the release of frozen assets. The Supreme Leader’s office said on October 31 that it had instructed the military to respond without geographic limits if the U.S. resumes attacks, and attention is on whether tensions will rise before and after the U.S. midterm elections on the 3rd.

Miyu Yamashita Wins Maybank Championship Again; 5th U.S. Tour Win, First Repeat at an Event by a Japanese Player in 38 Years

Women’s golfer Miyu Yamashita won the Maybank Championship, a U.S. tour event held in Malaysia, on November 1, winning the tournament for the second year in a row. Starting the final round alone in the lead, Yamashita held a 2-stroke lead with 3 holes to play and pulled away to win. It is Yamashita’s 5th career win on the U.S. tour and 3rd of this season.

It is the first time in 38 years, since Ayako Okamoto, that a Japanese player has won the same U.S. tour event in consecutive years. The week before, Yuri Yoshida won, and Japanese players took the U.S. tour two weeks in a row. Winning the same event two years running takes both familiarity with the course and the steadiness not to falter in the final round.
