Personal Data of About 5.4 Million Records Leaks, Including Digital Edition Members of a Business Daily, Through Unauthorized Access to a Contractor's Marketing Service

The major newspaper company that runs the business daily's digital edition announced on the 27th that personal information on about 5.4 million records, covering members and others, had leaked through unauthorized access to the marketing service “KARTE,” used by a contractor. It explained that credit card information is not included, and misuse has not been confirmed

The major newspaper company that runs the business daily's digital edition announced on the 27th that personal information on about 5.4 million records of digital edition members and others had leaked through unauthorized access to its contractor's marketing support service “KARTE.” The affected include, besides digital edition members, people who registered the company's ID for home newspaper delivery or for subscriptions to specialist media. What leaked is said to be email addresses and phone numbers converted into a form that cannot be restored to the original string, along with sex and year and month of birth. The company explains that credit card information and passwords are not included, and as of the 27th no misuse of the information or secondary damage had been confirmed. Names and addresses are not listed among the leaked items. Still, the fact remains that information close to contact details has gone outside, and the company plans to contact the affected users individually.

The sequence is as follows. The service provider is said to have detected the unauthorized access on the 13th, and the newspaper company received a report on the 15th. It disclosed the possibility of a leak on the 17th, then analyzed the data it was given and confirmed about 5.4 million records on the 27th. KARTE is used by many companies to analyze customer behavior, and it is reported that up to about 9.44 million customer records also leaked at the airline Peach Aviation. The effect has spread to users of other companies through the same service. The overall picture of the damage, including other companies, was still not settled as of the 27th.

Operators of High-Performance AI to Face a Safety Obligation and Penalties for Defying Orders, Outline of AI Law Revision Bill Comes to Light

The outline of the government's draft revision of the AI law came to light on the 27th. It would impose a safety obligation on businesses that develop and provide high-performance AI and have them report serious accidents and the like to the state. The state would be able to issue a recommendation or an order to businesses, and penalties would be imposed for violating an order. The current AI law was enacted in May 2025 but had no penalty provisions. Behind the review is growing concern over cyberattacks that misuse AI and over AI “runaway,” in which AI acts in ways people did not intend. A Cabinet Office working group presented on the 11th a draft built on four pillars: obligation, reporting to the state, recommendation and order, and this outline carries on that flow. Which performance level of AI will be covered has not yet been decided. An order is a means to be issued to a business that does not follow a recommendation, and penalties are positioned to back its effectiveness. The government is reported to aim to submit the revision bill to the ordinary Diet session in 2027. It is also reported to plan to present the overall picture of the system review by the end of the year, and details are yet to be worked out. The severity of penalties and the conditions for applying them were not clear as of the 27th.

Accident Involving More Than 10 Vehicles on Prefectural Road in Shimizu, Shizuoka Prefecture, 10 Taken to Hospital, Car Driven by Man in His 60s Suspected of Hitting Vehicles One After Another

Around 3 p.m. on the 27th, an accident involving more than 10 vehicles, including passenger cars, occurred on a prefectural road in Tamagawa, Shimizu Town, Shizuoka Prefecture. According to police, a passenger car driven by a man in his 60s hit vehicles in its own lane and in the oncoming lane one after another. Ten men and women from their teens to their 70s were taken to hospital, all believed to have minor injuries. Just before the accident there was a 110 emergency call saying “there is a runaway car,” and the area near the scene was closed to traffic for a time.

The number of vehicles involved is reported variously as more than 10, 11 and 12. The sequence of the accident, the physical condition of the man who was driving and the cause had not been made clear as of the night of the 27th. The Numazu police station has jurisdiction and is investigating the circumstances of the accident. “Runaway” is the expression of the person who called, and police have not announced a cause.

Figure Skating Grand Prix Final: Malinin Leads Men's SP With an All-Time World Best of 114.81 Points, Mone Chiba Tops Women at 77.12

The Japan leg, the final event of the Grand Prix series, opened on the 27th at Keio Arena TOKYO in Tokyo. In the men's short program, Ilia Malinin of the United States scored 114.81 points and took the lead, the highest score ever recorded in this event worldwide. Shun Sato of Japan stood second with 96.78 points, Riku Nakata was 10th and Nozomu Yoshioka 11th. The gap between first and second is about 18 points.

In the women's short program, Mone Chiba, who won the Cup of China, took the lead with 77.12 points. Without breaking down under the tension, the Japanese women are reported to have moved closer to winning every event of this season's Grand Prix series. Mako Yamashita was fifth and Yuna Aoki sixth. The free skate that decides the standings takes place on the 28th. In ice dance, the “Utamasa” pair withdrew from the event after Morita, who fell the day before, injured a ligament in the left knee.

Defense Ministry Releases New AI Guidelines, Five Principles Including “Humans Hold Judgment and Responsibility,” Also Writes In AI Sovereignty to Avoid Dependence on Specific Technologies

On the 27th, the Defense Ministry reviewed its basic policy on the use of AI and released new guidelines, “Defense AI Vision.” The pillars are five principles, and at the center is the idea that “humans hold judgment and responsibility.” How much to leave to AI is set in advance, and the system is designed so that humans can intervene where necessary. AI agents are given only the minimum necessary authority, and human confirmation is required for important operations.

The other pillar is AI sovereignty. It is the idea of not relying excessively on specific technologies or businesses so that Japan does not lose the initiative, and the Defense Ministry put forward “Japan will not lose the initiative.” At a press conference on the 27th, Defense Minister Koizumi explained that it had been made clear that humans hold judgment and responsibility. The ministry also restated its existing position that it has no intention of developing fully autonomous lethal weapons that operate beyond human involvement. To prevent automation bias, the uncritical belief in AI's suggestions, the policy is to thoroughly carry out education and training. Concrete procedures for an emergency, such as how to verify target information shown by US-side AI, have not been shown.
