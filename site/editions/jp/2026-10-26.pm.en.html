Former Prime Minister Yasuo Fukuda Dies at 90; Heart Failure on the 22nd; In Office About a Year as Premier, Worked to Improve Japan-China Ties

NHK reported on the 26th that former Prime Minister Yasuo Fukuda died on the 22nd of this month.

NHK reported that former Prime Minister Yasuo Fukuda died on the 22nd of this month, at the age of 90. Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that the cause of death was heart failure and that he died at a hospital in Tokyo. According to the paper, Mr. Fukuda became prime minister in 2007 and served about a year, decided on the creation of the Consumer Affairs Agency and advanced legislation to strengthen the management of public documents. His father was former Prime Minister Takeo Fukuda; after graduating from Waseda University he worked at Maruzen Petroleum (now Cosmo Energy Holdings), it said. Asahi Shimbun reported that Mr. Fukuda was the 91st prime minister, was elected to the House of Representatives 7 times, served for a long time as chief cabinet secretary under the Koizumi government, and as prime minister advanced measures against global warming, among other things. Mainichi Shimbun reported that his term as prime minister was one year and that he chaired the Lake Toya Summit.

Asahi Shimbun reported that Mr. Fukuda worked to improve Japan-China relations even after he retired from politics, and it also touched on his search for a “grand coalition.” TBS NEWS DIG reported that Chinese media flashed news of Mr. Fukuda’s death, and that Mr. Fukuda, whose family had been involved with China across two generations, urged the deepening of the “mutually beneficial strategic relationship” and the importance of private-sector exchanges. The network also reported that Prime Minister Takaichi said in a statement, “I cannot help but feel sorrow.” Mainichi Shimbun reported that Prime Minister Takaichi said, “Remembering his great achievements,” and paid a condolence call on Mr. Fukuda’s bereaved family.

Four-Title Women’s Champion Tomoka Nishiyama Announces She Will Exercise Her Right to Become a Professional; to Reach 4-dan April 1, 2027, the First Woman Professional

NHK reported that Tomoka Nishiyama (31), a four-title women’s champion in shogi, held a press conference on the 26th and announced her intention to exercise her right to become a professional player. The first woman professional in history will be born. Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that Hakurei title holder Nishiyama, who had won the women’s Hakurei title 5 times and had gained the right to professional qualification under a new system introduced last year, said, “I decided to exercise the right,” and will become 4-dan (professional) effective April 1, 2027. ABEMA TIMES reported that at the press conference, held at the Shogi Hall in Shibuya Ward, Tokyo, she indicated her intention to exercise the qualification to enter as a free class professional, earned by winning the Hakurei title 5 times in total. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that Ms. Nishiyama, the four-title women’s champion, said, “I want to play stronger opponents.” Mainichi Shimbun reported that her teacher, 7-dan Hirofumi Ito, rejoiced, saying, “It was a dream for both of us,” and also ran an article on how the playing environment will change by holding both professional and women’s status. Jiji Press reported that Women’s 6-dan Takojima, the first women’s shogi player, said, “She worked hard.”

Lula Wins Brazil’s Presidential Election, Heading for a Fourth Term; Japan’s Government Congratulates on Re-election

Asahi Shimbun reported that in the runoff of Brazil’s presidential election, incumbent Lula was re-elected and takes a stance of keeping his distance from the U.S. Trump administration. The British paper The Guardian reported that the 80-year-old left-wing President Lula defeated the right-wing Flávio Bolsonaro, weeks after narrowly losing the first round, and won a fourth term. The Japan Times reported that Lula won a historic fourth term in a dramatic comeback, halting the string of right-wing election victories that had continued in Latin America.

Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that voting was extended by 2 hours. South Korea’s Chosun Ilbo, citing the AP and others, reported that the defeated Flávio Bolsonaro said he could not accept the result and indicated he would seek to have the election annulled. The iJAMP portal reported that the Japanese government expressed congratulations on President Lula’s re-election.

GMO Chief Reveals Problem Was “Flagged Internally Beforehand”; Over Unauthorized Access, Government to Hold Cyber Ministerial Meeting the Evening of the 26th

ITmedia reported that Masatoshi Kumagai, chief of GMO Internet Group, revealed that an in-house white-hat hacker had flagged the problem beforehand but that countermeasures were delayed. According to the same article, the reason the problem could not be resolved was that the company proceeded carefully while weighing the impact on service availability and priority against other matters to be dealt with. The article reported that criticism that this is “shifting the blame” has also appeared on social media. NHK reported that Mr. Kumagai said on his blog, regarding his request that the government apply active cyber defense, “I would like them to consider its applicability in light of the impact on people’s lives.”

Mainichi Shimbun reported that the government will hold a ministerial meeting on cybersecurity the evening of the 26th, and in a separate article dated the 25th reported that the government would “decide for itself” whether to invoke the active cyber defense that GMO asked to have applied. Reuters reported that the chief cabinet secretary said the meeting would be held at 6 p.m., and Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that Prime Minister Takaichi is expected to order the drafting of emergency measures. The Japan Times reported that GMO disclosed unauthorized access to servers for automated FX trading and asked the government for active cyber defense.

No Progress in Talks on Releasing Japanese Journalist Detained in Russia; Government Keeps Pressing, Russia Does Not Change Its Stance

The Japan Times reported that there is no sign of progress in talks over the Japanese journalist detained in Russia, that the Japanese government intends to keep asking the Russian side for a resolution, but that Russia has not softened its stance. The Philippines’ Inquirer.net reported that a journalist rendered as “Yuki Imai” was detained by the FSB on October 14 in Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk on Russia’s Far Eastern island of Sakhalin, and that he is suspected of espionage.

Inquirer.net reported that officials of Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs visited Moscow and tried to make contact with him. On the 24th, Toyo Keizai Online took up the detention of a Hokkaido Shimbun journalist by Russian authorities on Sakhalin, positioned it as a grave problem shaking press freedom and trust between states, and argued that what is asked of the Japanese government is to ensure the journalist’s safety and his early return home.
