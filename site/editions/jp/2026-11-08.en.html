Quake of Upper 5 in Seismic Intensity Hits Aichi Prefecture; M5.7 Centered in Western Shizuoka, No Tsunami Concern

A quake centered in western Shizuoka Prefecture struck at about 1:04 a.m. on the 8th, and seismic intensity upper 5 was recorded in Chita, Aichi Prefecture. There is no tsunami concern, and the Japan Meteorological Agency is urging people to watch for quakes with similar shaking over the next week or so.

A quake with its epicenter in western Shizuoka Prefecture struck at about 1:04 a.m. on the 8th, and shaking of upper 5 was recorded in Chita, Aichi Prefecture. The magnitude, which indicates the size of the quake, was 5.7, and lower 5 was also recorded in Hamamatsu, Shizuoka Prefecture. The seismic intensity scale ranks the strength of shaking at a given place, and upper 5 is shaking in which it is difficult to walk without holding on to something. The Japan Meteorological Agency said there is no tsunami concern and held a press conference from 3:05 a.m. on the 8th. It is urging people to watch for quakes with shaking of up to about upper 5 over the next week or so.

At 1:07 a.m. the government set up a liaison office at the Crisis Management Center of the Prime Minister's Office and is confirming the damage. According to the Aichi Prefectural Police, five roads, the Minamichita Road, the Chita Peninsula Road, the Chita Odan Road, the Chubu Airport Connecting Road and the Nagoya Seto Road, were closed along their entire length in both directions. In Toyohashi, Aichi Prefecture, where seismic intensity 4 was recorded, the glass of a car dealership's showroom window broke badly. According to JR Central, as of 2:30 a.m. the sleeper limited express “Sunrise Seto/Izumo” was stopped between Takatsuka and Maisaka stations in Hamamatsu for a safety check, with passengers waiting on board. The full picture of human casualties, such as the number of injured, and the outlook for restoring roads and railways could not be confirmed as of the morning of the 8th.

Shooting in Central Square of Krakow, Poland Leaves 6 Dead and 15 Injured

On the afternoon of the 7th, a man in a military uniform fired indiscriminately from a car in the central market square of Krakow, a city in southern Poland, killing 6 people and injuring 15. According to the authorities, the suspect was shot by a police officer, taken into custody and taken to a hospital. The death toll rose from an initial 5 to 6. Defense Minister Kosiniak-Kamysz called it “an act of terrorism” and urged people not to spread unconfirmed information. A spokesperson for President Nawrocki said the president has begun procedures to make the 8th and 9th a two-day period of national mourning. Prime Minister Tusk is also heading to the site. The motive and the suspect's identity and background have not been officially given by the police or prosecutors.

Russia Puts Three JT Subsidiaries Under Temporary Management; Senior Foreign Ministry Official Protests and Demands Withdrawal

Russian President Putin signed a presidential decree on the 6th placing three local companies in Russia of the Japan Tobacco (JT) group under the temporary management of Russian companies. Similar action was taken on the 5th for production sites in the country, and this is an addition to that. In temporary management, the running of a company is entrusted to another organization for a set period.

A senior official of Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs visited the Russian Foreign Ministry on the 7th, protested the measure and demanded its withdrawal. It has not been confirmed how the Russian side explains the grounds for the measure, how long the management will continue, or what will happen to ownership of the shares.

New Zealand General Election: Ruling Party and Largest Opposition Party Nearly Even, Coalition Outlook Unclear

Voting in New Zealand's general election took place on the 7th, and the count is under way. In the preliminary count, Prime Minister Luxon's center-right National Party and the largest opposition party, Labour, are nearly tied in votes, with the ruling party slightly ahead. Neither has enough seats to form a government on its own, and the seats of minor parties are expected to decide the outcome. A newly emerged party is also expected to win seats.

Prime Minister Luxon said the result is a close race and not to be taken for granted, while indicating that there is a path to remaining in government. Because coalition talks will be needed, it may take time before the lineup of the new government is settled. Whether there will be a change of government could not be foreseen as of the 8th.

Pro Bowler Ritsuko Nakayama Dies at 84; Driving Force of the Bowling Boom

Pro bowler Ritsuko Nakayama, who led the bowling boom of the 1970s, has died at 84. News of the death was reported on the 7th. Known by the nickname “Sawayaka Ritsuko-san,” Nakayama was active as a member of the first class of women's professional bowlers.

Nakayama also served as president of the association and worked to develop the sport. Nakayama is remembered by many people as a figure who spread bowling across Japan. It has not been confirmed that the date and cause of death or the funeral schedule had been made public as of the night of the 7th.
