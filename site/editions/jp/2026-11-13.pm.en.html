Worldwide Microsoft 365 Outage Spreads to Government Information Systems, Causing Confusion in Kasumigaseki

An outage that began in the early hours of the 13th, Japan time, left users unable to connect to email and other services. Government information-system infrastructure was also affected.

A large-scale outage hit U.S. Microsoft’s business software “Microsoft 365” from the early hours of the 13th, Japan time. Users could not connect to Exchange Online, the email service, could not read mail in Outlook, and several other services, including Teams, were also affected. The company announced the outage at around 3 a.m. Japan time on the 13th (around 10 a.m. on the 12th, U.S. Pacific time), and users in Japan appear to have been included. According to the explanation being reported, the cause was that many servers failed automatic health checks and stopped responding, and a recent configuration change may have played a part. Health checks are a mechanism by which machines regularly verify that a server is running normally; a configuration change is work that alters settings or the contents of software. When email does not arrive, contact with business partners and internal approval procedures come to a halt.

The impact reached government offices as well. The Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary said on the 13th that the Microsoft outage had caused a failure in the government’s information-system infrastructure, and work in Kasumigaseki was thrown into confusion. As of the morning of the 13th, some services had been restored, and the company is pressing ahead with measures including the introduction of automatic recovery. However, it has not been confirmed when all services will be back to normal, or which government operations were halted and to what extent.

Prime Minister Takaichi to Visit the Philippines and China for Four Days From the 16th, to Attend ASEAN and APEC Leaders’ Meetings

On the 13th, the government announced that Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi will visit the Philippines and China on a four-day schedule starting next week on the 16th. In the Philippines, the prime minister will attend leaders’ meetings related to ASEAN (the Association of Southeast Asian Nations); in China, the APEC (Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation) leaders’ meeting. A multilateral meeting is a place where a leader meets the heads of many countries at once, and bilateral talks are often arranged alongside it. Visits to Japan by the leaders of Peru and Chile are reported to be planned as well, but the timing and the content of the talks have not been confirmed. Whom the prime minister will meet there, the agenda, and the details of the schedule in China have not been given within the scope of the government’s announcement.

Government Decides to Spend Over ¥650 Billion From Contingency Reserve, Including Winter Electricity and Gas Support for Three Months From Next Month

On the 13th, the government formally decided to spend a little over ¥650 billion from this fiscal year’s contingency reserve to fund support for electricity and gas bills for three months from next month, among other items. The plan to spend had been reported on the 12th. A contingency reserve is money set aside in advance for spending that could not be foreseen when the budget was drawn up, such as disasters or sudden economic measures.

How much of the roughly ¥650 billion is for electricity and gas support and how much for other costs, and by how much each household’s bill will fall, have not been confirmed within the scope of this decision. Winter is a time when heating use rises and utility costs tend to pile up.

Review of Co-Payments for Those 70 and Over to Consider Widening the 30% Group, Working-Level Officials of LDP and Ishin Say

With a view to reviewing the co-payment on medical costs for people aged 70 and over, working-level officials of the Liberal Democratic Party and Nippon Ishin no Kai will examine, among other things, widening the range of people who have working-age-level income and pay 30%, and will organize the specific points at issue. A co-payment is the share of medical costs that patients pay themselves at a hospital or pharmacy; people aged 70 and over pay 10% to 30% depending on income. Working-age-level income is a category for older people who earn about as much as the working-age generation.

The talks are at the stage of organizing points at issue, and how far to widen the group paying 30%, and from when to change it, have not been decided. Widening the group has entered the list of items under consideration, but its implementation has not been decided.

¥52 Billion for the Kumamoto Earthquake Reconstruction Fund; Prime Minister Takaichi Announces Funding Through Special Local Allocation Tax

On the 13th, Prime Minister Takaichi disclosed a plan to provide ¥52 billion through the special local allocation tax as funding for a reconstruction fund to be created by Kumamoto Prefecture, for recovery and reconstruction of the areas hit by the Kumamoto earthquake. The prime minister is also reported to have issued instructions to the disaster management minister. A reconstruction fund is money that a local government sets aside so that it can use it relatively freely for rebuilding disaster victims’ lives and for local reconstruction projects. The special local allocation tax is the part of the local allocation tax that the national government distributes to local governments that is given out to meet special fiscal needs, such as disasters.

The size of the fund, its operating framework, and when the ¥52 billion will be paid to the prefecture have not been confirmed within the scope of announcements so far. The specific content of the instructions to the disaster management minister has not been made clear either.
