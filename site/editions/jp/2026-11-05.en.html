U.S. Midterms: Republicans Certain to Lose Majority in Both Chambers; Democrats Set to Regain Control

The Dow rose by more than 1,200 dollars at one point; Japan’s government is watching for a shift in diplomatic course

The U.S. midterm elections were held on November 3, and it is now certain that the ruling Republican Party will lose its majority in both the Senate and the House of Representatives of the U.S. Congress. In the count, which began on the morning of the 4th, Japan time, the opposition Democratic Party is on track to regain majority control of both chambers. Midterm elections are held exactly halfway through a president’s four-year term; all House seats and some Senate seats are up. For President Trump, it is a harsh verdict on the administration. On the New York stock market on the 4th, buying led, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose by more than 1,000 dollars from the previous close just after trading opened. The gain topped 1,200 dollars at one point, to a level not seen in about two months. The market welcomed the large Democratic win.

Within the Japanese government, there is a view that President Trump, with a little over two years left in office, may seek results in foreign affairs, where Congress constrains least, and the government is watching for any change in its diplomatic course. Professor Mieko Nakabayashi of Waseda University, an expert on American politics, pointed out that Japan needs to build strong ties with Democratic leaders as well. If the majority in both chambers belongs to the opposition, the president will find it hard to move budgets and bills without opposition cooperation. The Nikkei average closed on the 4th at 62,188.63 yen, down 1.42% from the previous day, and trading on the Tokyo market on the 5th, following the sharp rise in U.S. stocks, is the next focus. The final Senate seat count must await further progress in the count.

“Shodo” Calligraphy Headed for Intangible Cultural Heritage Status: UNESCO Evaluation Body Recommends, Japan’s 24th

UNESCO’s evaluation body on the 5th put together a recommendation that “Shodo” (calligraphy), which Japan has sought to have registered, be “inscribed” on the Intangible Cultural Heritage list. The Agency for Cultural Affairs made this known. If it is decided as recommended, it will be the 24th Intangible Cultural Heritage in Japan. Formal registration is expected soon. The evaluation body’s recommendation comes at the stage before the decision on whether to register. Shodo is the practice of writing characters with a brush and ink, and what drew notice is its familiarity as a brush culture in daily life, such as school lessons, New Year’s cards and the first calligraphy of the year. With typing on digital devices now so common, some see it as a chance to reconsider “writing by hand.” The date of formal registration has not been confirmed in this reporting. The figure of 24 in Japan is the running total if this entry is added to those registered so far. Registration does not change Shodo itself, but whether interest grows in classes at schools and in communities and in exhibitions will be the point to watch.

More Than 15 Million Member Records Leak From “Asoview”: Email Addresses and Booking History

More than 15 million member records have leaked from “Asoview,” a reservation service for leisure facilities, it was reported on the night of the 3rd. What leaked includes email addresses and the history of leisure-facility reservations. As for whether credit card numbers are included, there is no confirmed information at this point. The cause and period of the leak and details of how members will be contacted have not been made clear in what has been reported. If a registered email address falls into outside hands, phishing emails may arrive that pose as the company and lead people to fake sites.

As leaks of personal information continue, “alert fatigue” among recipients is also being pointed out. In a survey conducted in November 2025 by a company in the cloud business, 79.2% of people said they had received a suspicious email in the past year, and among those aged 60 and over the figure reached 92.0%. The basic precaution is not to open URLs in the message body without checking the sender and the link destination. It is best to wait for the company’s official announcement and guidance on contact points, and confirm through them.

Level 4 Sediment Disaster Warnings for Izu Islands and Chiba Prefecture; Heavy Rain Possible Until Around Noon

The Japan Meteorological Agency issued a Level 4 sediment disaster danger warning for Mikurajima Village in the Izu Islands, Tokyo, at 4:33 a.m. on the 5th. The same warning was issued for Kozushima Village at 1:33 a.m. and for Minamiboso City, Chiba Prefecture, at 11:44 p.m. on the 4th. Saying the danger of sediment disasters has become very high, the agency is urging strict vigilance. Warm, moist air around a typhoon and other factors have put developed rain clouds over the Izu Islands and Chiba Prefecture. In the Kanto region and the Izu Islands, very heavy rain is possible until around noon on the 5th.

The agency asks people to check evacuation information issued by local governments and to act early. A Level 4 sediment disaster danger warning shows that the situation is one where a sediment disaster could occur at any moment. Because warnings were issued one after another from night into early morning, conditions in each area are changing moment by moment. Future rainfall and the outlook for lifting the warnings have not been confirmed in this reporting. Decide on evacuation by following the latest announcements from the agency and local governments.

Trump Announces End of Aid to Israel by Year’s End: 4 Billion Dollars a Year

President Trump said in a U.S. television interview on the 3rd that annual aid of 4 billion dollars (about 630 billion yen) to Israel would be ended by the end of this year. The remarks also included: “Why do we have to put up with the attacks of a madman?” The aid has been a major issue in the midterm campaign as well. The remark came as the Republican Party struggled, and attention is on whether the election had an influence.

The announcement took the form of a remark in the president’s interview, and the specific procedures for ending the aid, coordination with Congress and details of timing have not been made clear. Now that it is certain Republicans will fall short of a majority in both chambers, how Congress will take part in the administration’s foreign policy is also a focus. The reaction of the Israeli government and moves in the U.S. Congress have not yet been reported. We need to see how a specific policy is presented before the end of the year.
