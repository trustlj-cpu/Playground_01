Prime Minister Takaichi Makes Privately Funded Tamagushi Offering at Yasukuni Shrine's Autumn Grand Festival but Skips a Visit; About 70 Lawmakers, Including Transport Minister Ibayashi, Pay Respects

Before and after taking office, the prime minister has kept skipping visits at the grand festivals and on the anniversary of the end of the war; this makes three in a row.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, as president of the Liberal Democratic Party, made a privately funded tamagushi offering on the 19th to mark the autumn grand festival that had been under way at Yasukuni Shrine in Kudankita, Tokyo. According to NHK and Kyodo News, the offering was delivered on the prime minister's behalf by the LDP's General Council chair Hiroshi Kajiyama, who told reporters, “I took charge of delivering her sentiments as a tamagushi offering.” Yomiuri Shimbun reported that two days earlier, on the 17th, the prime minister had sent a masakaki offering in her name as prime minister. The prime minister herself did not visit during the festival, and NHK reported that the decision was to forgo a visit. Nikkan Sports reported that consideration for China and South Korea was involved, and South Korea's JoongAng Ilbo reported that the prime minister, who upholds the Murayama Statement, skipped a visit for the third time in a row. According to Yomiuri Shimbun, the tamagushi offering was made as LDP president rather than as prime minister, and the name was kept separate from that on the masakaki. According to NHK, Mr. Kajiyama, who delivered the offering, also visited the shrine himself. Asahi Shimbun and Nikkei also reported that the prime minister made a tamagushi offering. According to Kyodo News, the prime minister also sent a masakaki offering to the spring grand festival in April this year and made a privately funded tamagushi offering then.

Among cabinet ministers, Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism Minister Tatsunori Ibayashi visited. According to Jiji Press and NHK, Mr. Ibayashi went as a member of the cross-party league of lawmakers “Minna de Yasukuni Jinja ni Sanpai suru Kokkai Giin no Kai” (the Diet Members' Group to Visit Yasukuni Shrine Together), and Kyodo News said it was the second visit by a minister in the Takaichi cabinet during a festival period, following Growth Strategy Minister Minoru Kiuchi. According to NHK, about 70 members of the league visited. 47NEWS and NHK reported that LDP election strategy committee chair Yasutoshi Nishimura also visited, and Kyodo News reported a visit by Haruko Arimura, chair of the upper house Budget Committee. Sankei Shimbun described the league's members heading together into the grounds on the morning of the 19th. According to Kyodo News, the prime minister also skipped visits at the autumn grand festival in October last year, just before taking office, at this year's spring festival, and on the anniversary of the end of the war in August. ABEMA and others flashed news of the tamagushi offering, and Jiji Press reported Mr. Ibayashi's visit and the prime minister's offering side by side.

On Funding the Consumption Tax Cut, Prime Minister Says “I Do Not Rule Out Issuing Government Bonds,” Later Posts “Without Relying on Special Deficit Bonds”; Bill Goes to Lower House Debate on the 20th

In Diet deliberations on the 19th, Prime Minister Takaichi said of the funding for the consumption tax cut on food items, “I will control the amount of government bond issuance appropriately. I do not rule out issuing government bonds,” Jiji Press and Asahi Shimbun reported. According to Yomiuri Shimbun and NHK, the prime minister also indicated she would review both spending and revenue. Asahi Shimbun reported that the prime minister then posted on social media, in connection with her remarks at the Budget Committee, that the funding would be arranged “without relying on special deficit bonds.” According to NHK, the Diet also pressed her on matters concerning former Agriculture Minister Yana and Education Minister Seki. The bill would cut the tax rate on food and beverages from 8% to 1% for two years only, and the ruling parties proposed that after the lower house plenary session on the 20th, deliberations begin at the Financial Affairs Committee on the 21st. NHK reported that the opposition is calling for full deliberations, including the prime minister's attendance at the committee. According to Asahi Shimbun, LDP acting secretary-general Koichi Hagiuda indicated that talks with the opposition on the bill would be held “at the lower house stage.” NHK reported that in the upper house as well, the Diet affairs committee chairs of the LDP and the Constitutional Democratic Party discussed how to proceed with deliberations.

About 1.26 Million Records at Hankyu Hanshin Department Stores, a Total of About 9 Million at Email Delivery Firm Yumirlink: Data Leak Announcements Come One After Another

Companies announced personal data leaks one after another on the 19th. According to Nippon TV and Yahoo! News, there was unauthorized access to three sites operated by Hankyu Hanshin Department Stores, and personal data on about 1.26 million customers may have leaked. According to NHK and 47NEWS, Yumirlink, which provides email delivery services for companies and local governments, also suffered unauthorized access to its systems and announced that a total of about 9 million records, including email addresses, may have flowed outside. According to ITmedia, Sourcenext explained that an intruder broke into an API with access to the decryption key for encrypted passwords, and that passwords were leaked in plain text. Japan Post Holdings also told NHK that there had been an unauthorized login to the dedicated site for its own points, that information on about 250 members may have been viewed, and that information leaked from other companies may have been used.

Accident Involving Four Vehicles, Including a Bus and a Passenger Car, at Intersection in Fujisawa; 14 Taken to Hospital, One in Cardiopulmonary Arrest

An accident involving a bus, a passenger car and other vehicles occurred at an intersection in Fujisawa, Kanagawa Prefecture, on the 19th, and according to Asahi Shimbun and Jiji Press 14 people were taken to hospitals, one of whom went into cardiopulmonary arrest. According to Yomiuri Shimbun, a passenger car collided with two oncoming cars, kept going straight, and then hit a large tour bus. TBS reported that it is believed to have collided with the bus at the intersection. The number of vehicles caught up was given as four by Asahi Shimbun and TV Asahi and as three by Jiji Press and Nippon TV, and the number taken away was 13 in the first reports from Nippon TV and Fuji TV. NHK reported that a little over 10 people were taken to hospitals and one was unconscious. The local Kanagawa Shimbun (Kanaloco) also reported that 10-odd people were taken by ambulance.

Mars Moons Probe “MMX” Launched on H3 Rocket No. 10 From Tanegashima Space Center

Japan's Martian moons probe “MMX” was launched shortly before 5 a.m. on the 20th from the Tanegashima Space Center in Kagoshima Prefecture aboard H3 rocket No. 10, NHK reported. According to NHK, the probe was scheduled to separate from the rocket about 1 hour 11 minutes after liftoff. JAXA had announced in August that launch would be at 4:41 a.m. on the 20th (Kyodo News). According to Kyodo News, No. 10 is the “2-4 configuration,” the most powerful version of the H3, and the probe is to enter orbit around Mars in about a year, collect sand from the moon Phobos and bring it back to Earth in fiscal 2031. The return craft is to come back near Earth, drop a capsule holding the samples, and the capsule will be recovered in Australia. Yomiuri Shimbun and Sankei Shimbun had reported on the rocket's move to the launch pad before liftoff.
