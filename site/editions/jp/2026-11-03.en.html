Disaster Management Agency Launches With Urgent Request That the Public Stock at Least 3 Days of Water and Food; Disaster Management Minister Hosono: “Act in Earnest”

The Disaster Management Agency, formed by reorganizing the Cabinet Office’s disaster management bureaus, launched on the 2nd and on its first day issued an urgent request asking the public, local governments and ministries to prepare. Its staffing quota is 352.

The Disaster Management Agency launched on November 2. With the prime minister at its head, Disaster Management Minister Goshi Hosono oversees operations, and its staffing quota of 352 is a large increase from the 220 of the disaster management bureaus that had been in the Cabinet Office. One organization will handle not only the response after an earthquake or eruption but also pre-disaster prevention, such as making buildings earthquake-resistant and improving conditions in evacuation shelters, all the way through to recovery and reconstruction. At the launch ceremony, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said it should be “a strong command center” and stressed, “We will protect the lives of the people.”

What it issued on its first day was the “urgent request.” It asks the public to secure furniture and make homes earthquake-resistant, and urges them to stock water and food for at least 3 days, and ideally a week. It asks ministries to check their business continuity plans and local governments to improve conditions in evacuation shelters and to take measures for stranded commuters, and it also includes strengthening the system under which prefectures support municipalities. It also mentions that the agency may use its recommendation authority to demand improvements if efforts are insufficient. Hosono placed a Nankai Trough earthquake and an eruption of Mount Fuji among the national-crisis-level disasters. The focus from here is how far ministries and local governments put this request into practice.

GMO Unauthorized Access: Metropolitan Police Set Up Investigation Headquarters With About 70 Officers, Including the Public Security Bureau; Company Says Intrusion Was on August 5

In the unauthorized access at GMO Internet Group, the Metropolitan Police Department has set up an investigation headquarters staffed by about 70 people, including the Public Security Bureau. With possible violations of the Unauthorized Computer Access Law and other offenses in view, it is analyzing the materials GMO has provided and questioning those involved. At the press conference on the evening of the 2nd, the focus had been on an apology and a cut in executive pay, but on the same day the investigation into the incident itself began and the timing of the intrusion was explained. According to the company, the attacker broke into its systems on August 5 and stole administrator privileges that allow control of the entire system. Malicious programs were found in “Onamae.com Desktop Cloud” on October 20, so 2 and a half months passed between the intrusion and its discovery. The attacker demanded about ¥8 billion, but GMO’s policy is not to comply, and there has been no contact from the attacker since the 23rd of last month. President Masatoshi Kumagai said, “We cannot say,” about the analysis results and said the data had been reported to the relevant authorities. Neither the investigators nor the company has said who the attacker is.

Personal Data Leaks Announced One After Another: Nojima About 7 Million Records, NetMile Up to 5.49 Million People, Roadside Assistance Firm Up to 5.5 Million Records

On the 2nd, announcements of personal data leaks through unauthorized access came one after another. The largest was Nojima, where the names, addresses, phone numbers, login IDs and encrypted passwords of about 7 million members of its online shopping site “Nojima Online” and its used-smartphone buyback site went outside the company. It detected the intrusion on the 1st and confirmed the leak the same day. It says it does not hold credit card information on either site, so none was affected. The two sites are suspended, and no date has been set for resuming them.

NetMile, a point-exchange service, announced up to about 5.49 million people’s data, and Prestige International, which runs roadside assistance, announced that up to about 5.5 million records may have leaked from a group company’s system. Marui Group said that the server of a contractor it had entrusted with managing digital corporate bonds was attacked and that data on up to about 9,000 people leaked. On the same day the 2nd, Suntory Holdings also announced a leak involving about 100,000 people, and cases where a contractor’s or affiliate’s system was the way in stand out. Each company says it is still checking which data has been misused and to what extent.

U.S. Midterms: Voting Opens on the 3rd, Japan Time; All 435 House Seats and One-Third of the Senate Up for Election

Voting in the U.S. midterm elections takes place on the 3rd, U.S. time, and begins from the night of the 3rd in Japan. Coming at the midpoint of President Trump’s term, all 435 House seats and about one-third of the Senate seats are up for election. The president finished the last rally in the Midwest and appealed to voters to vote “as if I were on the ballot.” On the Democratic side, former Vice President Harris is calling on people to “turn anger into action.” Discontent over rising prices is feeding into criticism of the president, and the president also went to Iowa, a closely contested state.

In Michigan, a Democratic lawmaker switched overnight to backing the Republicans. Voting deadlines differ by state, and the first states close in turn from around 8 a.m. on the 4th, Japan time. Because the 3rd is Culture Day in Japan and the Tokyo stock market is closed, the first market reaction to the results will not be visible in Japan until trading on the 4th. Which party holds the majority in the Senate and the House, and whether that changes, will affect how the Trump administration’s policies get through. The count may take time in some states.

Specified Skilled Worker Intake Cap: Minister Onoda Says “Reduce It as AI Advances,” Negative on Expansion at Press Conference on the 2nd

Kimi Onoda, who is in charge of policy on foreign nationals, said at a press conference on November 2, regarding the intake cap for Specified Skilled Worker foreign nationals: “If we do what needs to be done, such as the development of AI and improved treatment, the cap should be reduced, let alone expanded.” Specified Skilled Worker is a residence status for accepting foreign nationals to work in fields such as nursing care and construction where labor is short, and the state estimates an intake cap for each field.

The government’s projected intake, Specified Skilled Worker and Employment for Skill Development combined, comes to about 1.23 million people. The remark can be read as intended to put a brake on the trend of continually raising this cap, but it does not say in which fields, or by how much, AI would replace labor. How to reconcile labor shortages on the ground with a review of the cap has been left to future debate. The full text of the remark and its context have not been confirmed.
