Prime Minister Takaichi Arranging to Propose Creation of an “Asia Version of the IEA” at the 17th Summit, One Outlet Reports; Rules for Coordinated Release of Oil Reserves Under Study

The idea is to create a new framework with Southeast Asia, where energy shortages have arisen amid the Middle East situation, that decides in advance how oil reserves are to be released. Among Southeast Asian companies, too, talks begin on a mechanism for priority purchase in emergencies.

Prime Minister Takaichi has reportedly begun arranging to propose, at the summit with Southeast Asian countries and others to be held on the 17th, the creation of a new framework to study matters such as rules for the coordinated release of oil reserves. Coordinated release means several countries acting in step to put oil held in national reserves onto the market. The International Energy Agency (IEA) is an organization created so that member countries pool their reserves when oil supply is cut off, and this plan is the Asian counterpart. It is not something the government has formally announced, and the countries that would join the framework and the concrete method of sharing reserves have not been confirmed.

Behind this is an energy shortage in Southeast Asia, caused by the Middle East situation, particularly the situation surrounding Iran. On the 14th, it was also decided that concrete talks would begin among Southeast Asian companies toward introducing a mechanism that lets them buy oil products first in an emergency. Two movements, reserves shared between countries and priority purchase by companies, now stand side by side, preparing for the same shortage. But both are at the stage of arrangement or talks, and when and how much would move has not been confirmed.

Japan National Soccer Team Beats Brazil 3-2 in Singapore; Takefusa Kubo and Keito Nakamura Score in First Half

Japan's national soccer team played Brazil in a friendly in Singapore on the night of the 14th and won 3-2. Takefusa Kubo scored the opener in the 15th minute of the first half, and Keito Nakamura added a second. Brazil also scored once, and the first half ended 2-1 at the break. It is Japan's 2nd career win over Brazil, and the 1st was last October. The starting lineup announced before the match was a back four, with Shuto Nakano at full-back and Nakamura, Kubo and Doan lined up in the second line. The course of the second half, the scorer of Japan's third goal and the details of Brazil's two goals have not been confirmed.

49 Years Since the Abduction of Megumi Yokota on the 15th; Family Calls for a Japan-North Korea Summit at Niigata Rally

Megumi Yokota was abducted to North Korea in November 1977 in Niigata, when in the first year of junior high school. Ahead of the 49th anniversary on the 15th, a large rally praying for the return of the victims was held in Niigata, and the family, including mother Sakie, appealed for a Japan-North Korea summit to be held toward a rescue as soon as possible. The abduction issue is the matter of North Korea having taken Japanese people away as a state, and among the victims recognized by the Japanese government, some have not been able to return.

North Korea explains that Megumi died, but the Japanese side does not accept this explanation and demands their return on the premise that they are alive. The victims' families are aging. No announcement that a date for a Japan-North Korea summit has been set has been confirmed.

U.S. and China Hold First Online Meeting of “Trade Committee” to Consider Tariff Cuts

The United States and China held, in online form, the first meeting of a “trade committee” set up to consider matters such as cutting tariffs. The U.S. side stated in a statement that the Office of the Trade Representative and the Treasury Department held the first official meeting. Next week, the leaders of the two countries are scheduled to meet on the occasion of APEC (Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation), to be held in China.

The items to be covered, the size of the cuts and the timing of implementation have not been confirmed. A U.S. government official is said to have stated that no announcement about the trade committee is expected at the meeting with President Xi Jinping, but this comes from one outlet and has not been confirmed in an official document.

U.S. Air Force Captain Arrested in Okinawa on Suspicion of Drunk Driving, One Outlet Reports; One Week After Japan and U.S. Agree on Measures to Prevent Recurrence

In Okinawa, a U.S. Air Force captain was arrested on suspicion of drunk driving. The Japanese and U.S. governments had only about a week earlier agreed on measures to prevent recurrence of incidents involving U.S. military personnel. This arrest came right after that agreement.

Details such as the agency that made the arrest, the date and place of the arrest, and whether alcohol was detected or at what level have not been confirmed. The captain's name and unit have not been made public either. The concrete content of the agreed measures has not been confirmed. How incidents caused by U.S. military personnel in Japan are handled has been tied to the debate over the Japan-U.S. Status of Forces Agreement.
