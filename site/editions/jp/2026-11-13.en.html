Nvidia CEO Huang Resigns, Kress Named Interim CEO, One Night After Reports of Removal Preparations

A day after reports that the board was preparing to remove the CEO, the company announced the resignation. A permanent CEO will be sought from inside and outside the company.

Nvidia, the U.S. chip giant, announced on the 12th, local time, that Jensen Huang had immediately stepped down as president and chief executive officer (CEO) and as a director. According to a filing (8-K) with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while remaining CFO, and the board will choose a permanent CEO from candidates inside and outside the company. Harvey Jones became board chair, a post the company is creating for the first time. Until the day before, the matter had been at the stage of reports that the board was preparing to remove the CEO. What is new this time is that the preparations have turned into an announced resignation and the procedure for a successor has begun.

Before the resignation, it had been reported that Huang was in a close relationship with a woman said to be a collaborator of a Chinese intelligence agency. No confirmation by the company or by authorities of the woman's position or the substance of the relationship can be found. Nor can any explanation the company gave for the resignation be confirmed. U.S. President Trump posted on social media that Huang “has done wonderful work” and that the only possible successor is Apple's Tim Cook. However, Cook's name does not appear in the company's announcement. What to watch next is when, and whom, the board picks as permanent CEO.

Muji Kept 1.09 Million Card Numbers From a Tie-Up It Ended in July 2021; President Apologizes at Press Conference

Ryohin Keikaku, which runs Muji, held a press conference on the 12th to apologize over the unauthorized access. The card numbers of MUJI Card members may have been read from outside in all digits, across about 1.09 million records. According to the company, it used credit card numbers to identify customers until July 2021, and kept some of the information without deleting it even after changing its system. It acknowledged this as a lapse in operations. Those covered joined before July 2021, and the data include the card number, member ID and date of birth. The cardholder name, expiration date and security code are said not to be included. Of the roughly 1.09 million records, about 345,000 are cards still valid today; the rest are expired or canceled. The company says it has confirmed no damage from misuse of the cards. This is the company's account, and no outside verification can be confirmed. At the press conference, a policy of having the company bear the fee for reissuing cards was also presented. The first announcement, on October 30, had said that “card information is not included.” A later investigation revealed that card numbers were held, and the content was revised on the 4th of this month.

Aso Rebukes Ishiba and Others Who Left Before the Vote on Food Consumption Tax Cut: “The LDP Faces Things United”

A related bill to reduce the consumption tax on food was passed by a majority of the ruling parties and others at the House of Representatives plenary session on the 6th and sent to the House of Councillors. Just before the vote, four LDP members left: former Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba, Gen Funada, Yuko Obuchi and Yoshihisa Furukawa. Obuchi explained that doubts about funding had not been resolved, saying, “I agonized until the last moment.” Secretary-General Suzuki on the 9th said the four's conduct “cannot be overlooked” and imposed a reprimand. The ruling bloc is in the minority in the House of Councillors, and the outcome of the vote is hard to read.

On the 12th, Vice President Taro Aso rebuked the four, saying that the LDP is a party that faces what the party has decided united, and called for tighter discipline. It is reported that Aso also said the disciplinary action was “not action meant to instill fear.” As for the content of the tax rate, reports say 1%, while the text submitted to the House of Representatives contains a temporary special provision that sets the tax rate on food and drink at zero, and the two do not match. Which is accurate has not been confirmed. No date has yet been announced for deliberation in the House of Councillors.

Japanese Reporter Held in Russia: Newspaper Publishes Reporter's Name for the First Time, No Prospect of Release

Regarding a reporter for a regional newspaper in Hokkaido who was taken into custody by Russian security authorities on suspicion of espionage, the newspaper said on the 12th that it was disclosing the real name, reporter Imai, for the first time in its own reporting. As its reason, the company cited the fact that there is still no prospect of release, and explained that it needs to appeal to society under the real name. The reporter was taken into custody in October in Sakhalin Oblast in Russia's Far East, reportedly the day before the scheduled departure date. A Moscow court approved detention of about two months.

Russia's Federal Security Service claims the reporter had been taking instructions from a Japanese intelligence agency. This is the Russian side's claim, with no independent confirmation. The newspaper explained that it is a news organization independent of the government, and Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi also denied that any such fact exists. The Japanese government is asking the Russian side, through diplomatic channels, for prompt provision of information and humanitarian consideration. Because detention has been set at about two months, what happens afterward is the next focus.

Yen Briefly Hits the 160-Yen Range per Dollar, Weakest Since Early September; Vice Finance Minister for International Affairs Issues Warning

In New York foreign exchange trading on the 12th, selling of the yen and buying of the dollar advanced, and the yen briefly fell to the 160-yen range per dollar. It is the weakest level in about two months, since early September. In Tokyo too, the yen was already trading in the upper 159-yen range and at one point came close to 160 yen. What is new is that, from the 159-yen range reported the previous day, it has gone as far as the 160-yen mark. At 160 yen to the dollar, a 100-dollar purchase works out to 16,000 yen, a level that tends to affect the price of imports.

The vice finance minister for international affairs said on the 12th, “It is too early to forget the memory of summer,” cautioning the market. It was also reported that the remark hinted at yen-buying intervention. However, whether yen-buying intervention was actually carried out has not been confirmed. As for what drove the yen's fall, no explanation attributable to an identifiable speaker has been confirmed, so we do not state one here.
