Lower House Seat Cut of 31 in Proportional Tier: LDP, Ishin and Democratic Party for the People Line Up; Three Parties Head to Talks on Drafting a Bill

Ishin and the Democratic Party for the People have said they favor the plan, shown by a working-level LDP official, to cut 31 seats from the proportional tier.

Ruling and opposition parties met on the 28th for talks on the seat reduction in the House of Representatives, and Nippon Ishin no Kai and the Democratic Party for the People expressed their intention to back moving ahead with consideration on the basis of the plan, shown by a working-level LDP official, to cut 31 seats in proportional representation. NHK reported that the LDP called on the parties to “draft a bill as three parties.” Asahi Shimbun reported under the headline “Lower House Seat Cut of 31: LDP, Ishin and Democratic Party for the People in Favor; Three Parties Head to Talks on Drafting a Bill,” and also reported that the support of Ishin and the Democratic Party for the People made it possible to pass the bill in the current Diet session. Jiji Press flashed that Ishin had decided to accept the cut of 31 seats, and the Yahoo! News Topics headline read “Lower House Proportional Seats: LDP, Ishin and DPP Agree on 31-Seat Cut Plan.”

The Democratic Party for the People had decided to back the plan on the morning of the same day. According to NHK, the reason was that, although the cut differs from the size the party had argued for, a review of the electoral system that it had also been seeking was added. Mainichi Shimbun reported that the Democratic Party for the People would back the LDP’s Lower House electoral reform plan. The previous day, Asahi Shimbun reported that the LDP had proposed a “unified list” and “block consolidation” for Lower House proportional representation and that the Democratic Party for the People had given a favorable assessment, and in another article it put the words “Our position was included” in a headline. The Japan Times reported that the plan includes introducing a system in which multiple parties jointly compile proportional representation lists. Yomiuri Shimbun had reported that if Ishin signs on, a bill related to the seat reduction could pass in the current Diet session.

GMO Follow-Up: Domain of Attacker’s Site Posting Personal Information Confirmed Suspended; Metropolitan Police Investigating

On the 28th, GMO Internet Group announced that, as a result of responding in cooperation with the businesses concerned over the unauthorized access, it had confirmed that the domain of a site on which personal information believed to relate to users had been posted was suspended. NHK and ITmedia NEWS reported this. According to ITmedia, the company said that if a similar site is newly opened in future, it will immediately act toward a takedown (removal) as soon as it is confirmed. Mainichi Shimbun reported that the attacker’s site that had carried personal information was removed, and TBS NEWS DIG reported that the Metropolitan Police Department is investigating on suspicion of violating the Unauthorized Computer Access Law, among other things. ITmedia explained that on the 27th a statement believed to be from the attacker and data said to be user information were published online, and that the problem had entered a new stage. Mainichi Shimbun explained in a Q&A, among other things, that the attackers say they will publish customer information every day. Asahi Shimbun had reported that, on the published “customers’ personal information,” the company called on people not to spread it and was preparing legal action. Bloomberg, citing Tominaga of Central Tanshi FX, said that foreign exchange trading volume among individual investors has held up, and that some investors are closing accounts. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that GMO’s sales have grown eightfold in 20 years.

Agriculture Minister Takebe Says “Confirming the Details of the Facts” on Reports of “Double-Counting” of Campaign Costs

Newly appointed Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries Minister Takebe said in the Diet on the 28th, “We are confirming the details of the facts,” NHK reported, on partial reports of suspected double-counting, in the income and expenditure report of both the campaign office and the support group, of the cost of campaign activities for past House of Representatives elections. NHK also reported that on the preceding day, the 27th, at the House of Representatives Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries Committee, answering partial reports of suspicion that the campaign office had paid remuneration to a photographer registered as a “campaign worker” in the 2024 general election, Takebe said a request had been made to correct the report.

Jiji Press reported on the 27th that Takebe explained to the Agriculture and Forestry Committee that the report of campaign spending had been “corrected” and a transfer request deleted. Mainichi Shimbun reported that Takebe had notified the Hokkaido election commission of a correction and deletion as a “clerical error,” and said both were responses to a weekly magazine’s report. Shukan Bunshun’s table of contents for its November 5 issue says that Takebe had set up a slush fund of 30 million yen in the general election. NHK focused on the point that, in the Diet on the 28th, Takebe said the details of the double-counting suspicion are being confirmed.

Three Months After Kumamoto Quake, Over 1,000 Remain in Shelters; Authorities Cannot Grasp Full Scope of Home-Based Evacuation

Three months have passed, as of the 28th, since the Kumamoto earthquake in July this year. NHK reported that life as evacuees has dragged on in the stricken area, with more than 1,000 people still staying in shelters and many others living in damaged homes, and that with winter coming, securing housing and continued support for those in home-based evacuation are the challenges. Mainichi Shimbun reported under the headline “‘Home-Based Evacuation’ Still Continues Three Months On; Authorities Also Fail to Grasp Full Scope.” In another article, NHK reported that a woman who continues to live in a “barn” beside her completely destroyed home said, “It’s not just us. People we know are living in storerooms too.”

Nishinippon Shimbun reported that Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara said at a press conference on the 28th that more than 1,000 people had moved into temporary housing, and explained that “efforts are steady.” au Web Portal distributed the same content. NHK reported that people living in damaged homes in the stricken area have various circumstances.

SK Hynix Operating Profit Up 7.3 Times in July–September for Record High; Generative-AI Demand Expands

South Korean chip giant SK Hynix announced results for the three months through September on the 28th, and NHK reported that operating profit was 7.3 times that of the same period a year earlier, a record for a quarterly result. NHK cited the spread of generative AI and the resulting expansion in semiconductor demand as the background. Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported under the headline “SK Hynix Net Profit Triples in July–September; AI High-Performance Chips Soar,” and Yahoo! News reported that it announced an additional shareholder returns program of about 60 trillion won (about 7 trillion yen). Business+IT reported that July–September sales topped 100 trillion won for the first time and the operating profit margin reached 80%. Yonhap News Agency reported operating profit of 83.3338 trillion won and sales of 104.6 trillion won. TBS NEWS DIG reported that four major memory makers, including SK and Kioxia, are reporting results one after another, a point at which the outlook for the AI industry is being gauged.

Bloomberg TV reported that SK Hynix’s better-than-expected profit added fuel to the rebound in AI-related stocks. In a separate report, BigGo Finance reported that Elon Musk forecast that memory demand for robots would exceed that of all other apps in the world combined.
