Nvidia Board Preparing to Remove CEO Huang, a U.S. Paper Reports Citing Sources; Company Announces Nothing

The report is based on people with knowledge of the matter; there is no announcement that the company has decided to remove the CEO. The board had announced on the 8th that it would begin an independent investigation.

The board of U.S. chip giant Nvidia is preparing to remove founder and chief executive officer (CEO) Jensen Huang, a leading U.S. newspaper reported on the 11th, citing people with knowledge of the matter. The report rests on anonymous sources, and the company has announced nothing about it. There has been no announcement that the company has decided to remove the CEO, and no information has been confirmed that Huang has signaled an intention to resign. On the 8th, following reports that Huang had been targeted by an operation of a foreign intelligence service, the company’s board had announced that a committee of independent directors, with the help of outside lawyers, would conduct an “independent investigation.” At that time it said there was no evidence that national security or company secrets had been compromised, and also expressed confidence in Huang and the management team. Huang pledged to cooperate with the investigation.

A leading Republican member of the U.S. Senate Banking Committee was reported to have called on Huang to resign. In after-hours trading, meaning the buying and selling of shares after regular trading hours have ended, Nvidia shares fell. The company sits at the center of the world’s AI chip business, and its share price is a concern for the whole market. What decision the board will actually make, and when it will be made public, has not been made clear. The result of the independent investigation has not yet been shown either.

U.S. Midterms: Democrats Projected to Take Majority in Both Houses, U.S. Media Say; Official Results Not Yet Final, Mississippi Senate Seat Possibly Their First in 44 Years

In the U.S. midterm elections held this month, the opposition Democratic Party is projected to take a majority in both the Senate and the House of Representatives of the U.S. Congress, U.S. media reported. This is a news-organization projection, not an officially certified result; certification goes through state procedures and has not yet been completed. In the Senate race in the southern state of Mississippi, whose outcome was the focus to the very end, Democratic newcomer Scott Colom is projected to be elected, U.S. media reported on the 12th. If the projection holds, it would be the first time in 44 years that the Democrats hold a Senate seat in that state, and the first time in about 150 years that a Black senator emerges, it is said. The count was an unusually close contest, and “projected to be elected” is the judgment of a news organization. The Republican Party was reported to have chosen a new leadership team in Congress. The White House has criticized the Democrats’ readiness to pursue investigations of the administration in Congress as an abuse of power. President Trump is scheduled to head to Asia next week to attend the ASEAN summit and the APEC summit, the first major diplomatic stage since the election. The final number of seats will not be settled until official certifications are all in.

Yen Slides to the Upper 159s per Dollar as Vice Finance Minister Hints at Possible Intervention, With 160 Within Reach

On the 12th, the yen slid to the upper 159s per dollar. At a level just short of 160, regarded as a key threshold, the Vice Finance Minister for International Affairs said, “It is still too early to forget the memory of the summer.” The remark was reported as hinting at the possibility of currency intervention to prop up the yen. Currency intervention is the buying and selling of currencies on the foreign exchange market by the government and the Bank of Japan to smooth out sudden moves; yen-buying intervention, which means selling dollars and buying yen, is a means of supporting the yen’s value. Yen-buying intervention was also carried out this summer.

No announcement that the Ministry of Finance has actually intervened this time has been confirmed. Whether the yen will break through 160, and at what level the authorities will act, have not been made clear. It is customary for the authorities not to disclose the level for intervention in advance. As the weak yen deepens, burdens on households and companies tend to grow through higher prices of imported raw materials and energy.

Docomo’s 5G Outage Recurs After the 5th; Restored in About 9 Hours, No Unauthorized Access Confirmed

On the 11th, NTT Docomo suffered an outage in its “5G SA” service, a communication method dedicated to 5G, making voice calls and data communications hard to connect nationwide. There was also a failure in the same service on the 5th of this month, making this the second. The outage was restored in about 9 hours, and the company announced that service had been fully restored. As on the 5th, the cause is reported to be “congestion” at switching equipment that relays communications. Congestion is a state in which communications concentrate in one place and the equipment cannot process them all, making connections hard.

No attack from outside, such as unauthorized access, has been confirmed. The number of users affected, the details of how the congestion arose, and specific measures to prevent a recurrence have not been made clear.

Avian Influenza at a Poultry Farm in Kameoka, Kyoto, the First Confirmed This Season; About 200,000 Birds to Be Culled

It became known on the 12th that a “highly pathogenic” avian influenza virus (H5 type), which is highly contagious and has a high death rate, was detected at a poultry farm in Kameoka, Kyoto Prefecture. On the 11th, 14 chickens were found dead on the farm, and the prefecture had been proceeding with detailed testing. A simple test came back positive, and this is the first confirmation this season.

The prefecture plans to cull the roughly 200,000 chickens kept at this farm. To prevent the spread of infection, movement restrictions on chickens and eggs are normally imposed around the farm. How the virus got into the farm is not yet known.
