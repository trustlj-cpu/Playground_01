Bank of Japan Judges 2% Price Goal Reached, Holds Policy Rate at 1.25%

It acknowledged that the “underlying inflation rate” has effectively reached the target, but passed on another rate hike. The pace of its ETF sales will be doubled.

At its Monetary Policy Meeting on October 30, the Bank of Japan decided to hold its policy rate at around 1.25%. At the same time, it judged that the “underlying inflation rate,” which shows the trend in prices, has effectively reached the 2% target. It is the first time in more than 13 years since it set the 2% price target in 2013 that the Bank has acknowledged reaching it. To gauge the effect of last month’s rate hike, it passed on a further increase this time. Its outlook for growth has been revised up.

It will also speed up the disposal of the exchange-traded funds (ETFs) it holds. Annual sales will rise to about 860 billion yen, and the estimated time to sell everything will shorten from 112 years to 43 years. At a press conference, Governor Kazuo Ueda said, “It does not amount to declaring victory,” describing reaching 2% as “a milestone” while also touching on the path for future rate hikes. The Nikkei average that day fell back on losses in U.S. tech stocks, closing at 63,290.11 yen, down 2.12%. The next focus is the timing of another rate hike, but the Bank has not given a specific time. How the votes at the meeting split also cannot be confirmed at this point.

Muji App May Have Leaked Up to 27.26 Million Customer Records

Ryohin Keikaku announced on October 30 that unauthorized access to its official “MUJI app” may have leaked up to 27.26 million records of customer information. The company said, “We deeply apologize.” The items that may have leaked, when and how the intrusion happened, and whether credit card information is included cannot be confirmed. Other companies made announcements the same day as well. Matsuya Foods disclosed a possible leak of more than 2.37 million records, the “World” group 1.2 million, Nihon Kotsu about 290,000 people and Yakult Honsha about 40,000 records. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs has announced that 3,600 email addresses and names, including those of people other than staff, may have leaked. Since the start of this month, leaks of personal information through unauthorized access have surged, and a tally putting the damage at 260 million people has appeared, but its scope and method cannot be confirmed.

Worries Over AI Investment Flare Again, Nasdaq Drops as Much as 3%; Nikkei Average Falls 2.12%

On the Tokyo stock market on the 30th, the Nikkei average fell back for the first time in four trading days, closing at 63,290.11 yen, down 2.12% from the previous day. During trading, the decline at one point exceeded 1,900 yen. In the United States the day before, after-hours shares of Amazon and others fell, and a drop in Intel’s share price added to anxiety over semiconductors. Concern that the huge capital spending on AI would not pay for itself, or “overinvestment,” flared again, and semiconductor-related stocks were sold. Ibiden hit limit-down, and its fall eased after the Bank of Japan’s announcement.

The worry spread to New York. In the U.S. market on the 30th, sell orders swelled for AI and semiconductor-related stocks, and the Nasdaq index, heavily weighted toward tech stocks, fell more than 3% at one point. Meanwhile, U.S. real GDP for July–September, released the same day, rose 3.4%, a faster pace than in the previous quarter, supported by consumer spending and AI-related investment. The economic figures remain strong while only stocks are being sold, and the final closing level of U.S. stocks cannot be confirmed.

Female Student Stabbed at Tokyo Metropolitan Roppongi High School; Classmate Arrested on Suspicion of Attempted Murder

Around 1:05 p.m. on October 30, a staff member at Tokyo Metropolitan Roppongi High School in Minato Ward, Tokyo, called 110 to report that “a student has been stabbed with a blade.” When officers from the Metropolitan Police Department’s Azabu Police Station rushed to the scene, they found a 16-year-old female student who had been stabbed in the library. She is conscious and is said not to be in a life-threatening condition. Another female student believed to have done the stabbing was arrested at the scene as a flagrant offender on suspicion of attempted murder. The school was in the middle of its cultural festival that day.

The stabbed student suffered wounds in several places, including the side and back. The weapon is believed to be a kitchen knife. There is information that the arrested student has said, in effect, “I thought I would kill the other person and die myself,” but it cannot be confirmed from multiple sources. The Metropolitan Police Department is investigating the relationship between the two, the motive and how the blade was brought into the school.

Kioxia’s Interim Net Profit Jumps 37-Fold to 2.19 Trillion Yen

Semiconductor giant Kioxia Holdings announced consolidated results for April–September on October 30. Its net profit was more than 2.19 trillion yen, 37 times the same period a year earlier. Demand tied to AI grew strongly, and demand for NAND flash memory, which stores data, swelled. It will pay an interim dividend for the first time. The company has reached an “effectively debt-free” state, with more cash on hand than debt, and the market’s attention is also turning to stronger shareholder returns.

For October–December, it expects net profit of 1.36 trillion yen, 15 times the same period a year earlier. But that level is only a slight increase from the previous quarter, and the share price plunged in after-hours trading following the earnings release. The same day, semiconductor stocks were sold in the Tokyo market on wariness about AI investment, and views are split on the strength of demand and the durability of growth.
