SpaceX Catches Its Giant Spaceship at the Launch Pad for the First Time

An attempt to catch a vehicle that has finished its spaceflight with an arm on the launch pad has succeeded for the first time. The technology for full reusability, aimed at the Moon and Mars, has moved a step forward.

In a flight test of its large spaceship, SpaceX has for the first time succeeded in catching, at the launch pad, a vehicle that came back from a spaceflight. After atmospheric reentry, the vehicle was caught in midair by a huge arm built into the pad at the launch site. A large booster, the lower stage of the rocket, was recovered the same way in October 2024, but this is the first time the ship that flew in space has been caught. It has no landing legs; it returns by being grabbed directly by a structure waiting on the ground.

The aim is full reusability: flying a vehicle again and again at short intervals rather than throwing it away. If a ship can be returned to the pad, serviced, loaded again and launched, cost and preparation time can be cut sharply. The company has its eye on crewed flights to the Moon and Mars, and this success clears one of the mountains of technology that stand as a precondition. On the other hand, how much damage the caught vehicle has taken, and how much servicing is needed before the next flight, have not yet been made clear. The date of the next flight has not been given either.

Zelensky Says U.S. Floated Donbas Withdrawal in Exchange for NATO Membership; No Confirmation From U.S. Side

Ukraine's President Zelensky has disclosed that the U.S. side asked whether Ukraine could withdraw its forces from the eastern Donbas region on condition of joining the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO). It is said to be the first time that a proposal linking membership to territorial cession has come up at peace talks. On the same day, Zelensky also said a U.S. envoy had told the president that President Putin had agreed to European participation in the next round of negotiations. In March this year, too, Zelensky had said that the United States was linking security guarantees to withdrawal from Donbas. Russia has long demanded withdrawal from the whole of Donbas. The Ukrainian side has kept a stance of not accepting a plan to hand over territory, but a proposal in exchange for NATO membership, a long-held wish, would be received differently at home and abroad. Whether the United States formally presented the plan and how Russia responded have not been confirmed, and there is no prospect of an agreement.

Leaders of U.S., China and Russia to Meet in Shenzhen, China, on the 19th; Russian Presidential Administration Announces

U.S. President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Putin will meet on the 19th in Shenzhen, in southern China, Russia's Presidential Administration has said. The meeting is planned to coincide with the leaders' meeting of Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC). It would be the first time the leaders of the three countries have come together, and the agenda is said to be nuclear arms reduction and the Iran situation. A senior U.S. government official is reported to have explained that the Ukraine issue will not be taken up.

There were reportedly twists before it came together. There are reports that Trump and Putin pushed for the meeting, while Xi was cautious about taking on the role of host. Concern has also emerged that great powers may be deciding how the world is carved up over the heads of other countries, a “second Yalta.” What will be agreed at the meeting, and whether a joint document will be issued, is not clear at this point.

Nvidia's Huang Steps Down as CEO; Musk Also Backs Petition Seeking His Return

Jensen Huang of the semiconductor giant Nvidia has stepped down as chief executive officer (CEO). The departure is reported both as a “resignation” and as a “dismissal,” and the company has not given a reason. There are reports that Kress, the chief financial officer (CFO), has become interim CEO. There are also reports that Huang admitted an affair in a document to employees, and that the board of directors had been investigating over suspicion that the other party was connected with a Chinese intelligence agency, but neither has been officially confirmed. Because the company stands at the center of the supply chain for AI chips, some see the turmoil over management governance spreading to business partners.

A petition calling for Huang's return has spread, and the businessman Elon Musk wrote, “I'll sign it too.” The number of signatures differs by report. Musk said that important deals would be lost without Huang. While there are reports that employees and major shareholders want a return, the company is reported to have denied as fake a letter calling for the board's resignation and Huang's return. There is also a report that the company approached the CEO of Britain's Arm in choosing a successor, but this is unconfirmed.

Nationwide U.S. Departure Halt Caused by Switch From Old Copper Communications Lines to New Ones, Administration Explains

On the 13th (Fri.), aircraft departures were halted across almost the whole of the United States and hundreds of thousands of passengers were stranded. The Trump administration has explained that the cause of the failure was work to switch old copper communications lines to new ones. Trouble occurred in the switch between old and new communications lines, and the exchange of information needed for air traffic control was cut off, so a ground stop was issued across the country for safety. Operations are reported to have returned to normal on the 15th.

The Federal Aviation Administration has been replacing aging copper communications equipment with optical fiber, and this happened in the middle of that work. Since the summer, delays caused by communications equipment failures have followed one after another in the United States. The administration cites old equipment and Congress's response as causes, but the opposition Democratic Party is demanding an explanation. Whether there was a problem in the work procedure, and whether there are means to prevent similar failures, have not yet been shown.
