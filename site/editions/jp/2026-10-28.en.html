GMO Attack: Culprits May Have Published One Customer’s Personal Data; Five FX Brokers Also Hit

After unauthorized access to “Onamae.com Desktop Cloud,” the side believed to be the attacker appears to have published a customer’s personal information after a noon deadline.

On the 27th, GMO Internet Group, over unauthorized access to “Onamae.com Desktop Cloud,” a VPS for automated FX trading, urged users to back up their data by noon, then shut down their PCs and unplug them. Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that this was because a person believed to be the attacker had threatened to attack customers at noon the same day. NHK reported that a hacker group had demanded payment of just over 150 million yen from the company by noon, and Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported, citing CEO Masatoshi Kumagai’s explanation, that the attacker at one point demanded “about 7.9 billion yen.” According to ITmedia, GMO employees are collecting customers’ devices across the country, and the group’s white-hat hacker team is analyzing them.

ITmedia said that, as of 11:59 a.m. on the 27th, no malware, including ransomware, had been found on devices whose analysis was finished. After the noon deadline, it reported, the criminal group appears to have published one customer’s information, and it also reported GMO’s plan to provide new PCs to customers who were hit. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that the attacker’s statement contained wording to the effect of “publish every day at noon” and “stop activity if demands are accepted.” Yomiuri Shimbun carried in its headlines that GMO “will not yield to the threat” and that a man who lost 2.4 million yen in trades he did not recognize said he was “considering buying a new PC.” Mainichi Shimbun reported that Financial Services Minister Katayama announced customer damage at five FX brokers and that the Metropolitan Police Department is working to grasp the extent of the harm, and it also ran an article saying vigilance is needed against secondary damage from “theatrical” disclosures.

Manga Artist Tetsuya Chiba Dies at 87; Creator of “Ashita no Joe,” Passed Away on the 22nd

NHK reported on the 27th that manga artist Tetsuya Chiba, who made many hits including “Ashita no Joe” and also served as chairman of the Japan Cartoonists Association, died on the 22nd of this month. He was 87. In its obituary, Shogakukan’s Big Comic BROS.NET announced that Mr. Chiba, whose real name was Tetsuya Chiba (千葉徹弥), died at 6 p.m. on the 22nd at a hospital in Tokyo and that his career as an artist spanned 70 years. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that he was the first manga artist to receive the Order of Culture and that he devoted himself to nurturing younger artists and also served as a university president. Mainichi Shimbun reported that he had said that reading stories aloud while being repatriated was the origin of his creative work, carried the tribute of manga artist Machiko Satonaka, “He showed me a way to live,” and also ran an article looking back on the work that produced a social phenomenon with the line “Stand up, Joe!” Big Comic BROS.NET’s announcement expresses gratitude to those concerned and to readers.

Prime Minister Takaichi’s Absence From G7 Online Meeting: Government’s Explanation Shifts From Equipment to “Only One Remark”

Tokyo Shimbun reported on the 27th that the government’s Diet answers are wavering over why Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi skipped the online Group of Seven (G7) leaders’ meeting. At the Upper House Budget Committee on the 16th, the prime minister had explained that a large display for international conferences was not ready, but on the 27th Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi explained by citing, among other things, that “(the prime minister’s number of remarks) would be a few minutes, just once.” The paper’s headline says, “Not a problem of equipment but because it is ‘just one remark.’” Bunshun Online, in an article looking back on the prime minister’s words over the past year, took up the account that the prime minister skipped the G7 online leaders’ meeting on October 2 and, asked the reason in the Diet, answered that “the contractor was not in time.” The article’s headline also cites another remark by the prime minister, “three to four hours to get ready in the morning,” and offers the view that the year’s words share a common thread of “shifting the argument.”

Hamaoka Nuclear Plant Injunction Suit: Shizuoka District Court Rejects Residents’ Claims

The Shizuoka District Court on the 27th handed down a ruling rejecting the claims in a suit in which residents and others sought an injunction against operating Units 3 to 5 of Chubu Electric Power’s Hamaoka Nuclear Power Plant (Omaezaki, Shizuoka Prefecture). NHK reported that the residents argued that measures against earthquakes and tsunami were inadequate and, noting that data on earthquake assumptions had been falsified in January this year, reported that safety had been at issue. The ruling was delivered on the afternoon of the 27th, and NHK’s news flash came out shortly after 3 p.m.

Asahi Shimbun reported that the plaintiffs’ claim was “dismissed” (kyakka), and Mainichi Shimbun put the Shizuoka court’s finding of “doubts about technical capability” in its headline. Jiji Press carried a photo of the plaintiffs’ legal team holding up a sign reading “Unjust ruling.” NHK reported that on the evening of the same day the mayor of Omaezaki and others visited a senior official of the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy and asked the national government to continue guiding and monitoring Chubu Electric in light of the string of misconduct.

Former Osaka Chief Prosecutor Reverses Course at Trial Resumed After Two Years, Claims Innocence; Quasi-Forcible Sexual Intercourse Charge Over Subordinate

NHK reported on the 27th that the trial of the former chief prosecutor of the Osaka District Public Prosecutors Office, charged with sexually assaulting a subordinate who was drunk and unable to resist, was held at the Osaka District Court two years after the first hearing, and that the former chief prosecutor denied the charges and signaled he would contest them. Mainichi Shimbun reported that he said, “I will contest incapacity to resist.” Jiji Press and Asahi Shimbun gave the charge as quasi-forcible sexual intercourse and reported that the former chief prosecutor, who had admitted the charges at the first hearing, reversed course and claimed innocence. Asahi Shimbun treated the day’s hearing as effectively a “first hearing.”

Mainichi Shimbun reported that night that the two sides’ accounts differ over the former chief prosecutor’s influence and what happened behind “closed doors.” NHK reported that Justice Minister Yamashita said of the results of the harassment survey being conducted among all staff of the Public Prosecutors Office, “I will make them public on my own responsibility.” Asahi Shimbun had reported in a headline the day before the hearing that a verdict could come as early as March.

Word of the day
Quasi-forcible sexual intercourseLaw

A crime of having sexual intercourse or the like by taking advantage of a person’s loss of reason or incapacity to resist (a state of being unable to resist). In the 2023 revision of the Penal Code, it was merged, together with the crime of forcible sexual intercourse, into the crime of “non-consensual sexual intercourse.”
