COP31 Closes in Breakdown After Longest-Ever Extension of 49 and a Half Hours, Unable to Agree on Fossil Fuels

Countries failed to reach a comprehensive agreement, and the conference ended without even adopting an outcome document summing up its results.

COP31, the UN climate change conference held in Turkey, was extended again and again, by about 49 and a half hours, far beyond schedule and the longest ever, yet it closed by the 23rd without an agreement acceptable to all countries. It is reported that the divide over fossil fuels could not be resolved and that no comprehensive agreement pulling together the conclusions of the whole conference was reached. About 200 countries and territories take part in a COP, and decisions are in principle made by consensus, so if even one country strongly opposes, an outcome document (a document summing up the conference's conclusions) cannot be adopted. The reported decision by host Turkey to give up on adopting an outcome document led directly to the closing in breakdown.

Reactions from countries were reported one after another after the close, and some see it as the “worst ever” breakdown. Others are reported to hold that a breakdown in talks is not necessarily the same as failure. Some also see Saudi Arabia, an oil-producing country, as having shown its influence in the negotiations. Details such as what was left unresolved to the end and how far the proposal went in cutting fossil fuels are not known, within what has been confirmed. Also not yet reported are how countries will handle their own emissions-reduction targets and pledges of support for developing countries without a conference conclusion, and when a venue for renewed talks will be set.

Hanshin, Back-to-Back Champions for the First Time in Club History, Hold Victory Parade; Manager Fujikawa Vows Japan Champions Next Season

The Hanshin Tigers' championship parade, in pro baseball, was held on the 23rd, and about 300,000 fans are reported to have gathered to celebrate the club's first back-to-back league titles. Manager Kyuji Fujikawa spoke happily, saying “It's the best,” and voiced next season's goal, “Next year, Japan champions.” Fujikawa and others took part in the parade, while player Teruaki Sato did not attend. The figure of 300,000 was reported in the news, and whether it is an official announcement by the organizers has not been confirmed. The reason for Sato's absence and details of the scene along the route and the effect on traffic are also not known, within what has been confirmed. This was the first time the club has won back-to-back titles, and the parade became a milestone event heading into next season, with Japan champions as the aim.

Opposition to Confer as Early as the 24th on Censure Motion Against Education Minister Seki; Government and Ruling Parties Reportedly Weigh Response as Situation Develops

Regarding Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology Seki, over whom points about political fundraising parties keep coming up, the opposition is reported to plan talks as early as the 24th, with submission of a censure motion (a motion in which the Diet questions a minister's responsibility and urges resignation) in view. The government and ruling parties are weighing their response while gauging the situation. Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara has indicated the view that the minister's own explanation is important.

Points from within the government and ruling parties are reported to urge the minister to give a full explanation. There are also reports of a Liberal Democratic Party executive saying “Keeping the minister on will make it hard to pass bills,” but the speaker is not identified and this has not been confirmed. Whether a censure motion will actually be submitted, when it would go to a vote, and whether the minister will step down of the minister's own accord must await the opposition's talks from the 24th onward and the government's judgment.

Snow Keeps Power Out in Nagano, Gifu and Elsewhere, With About 4,200 Households Without Power in Gifu and Communications Trouble at 3 Major Mobile Carriers, Reportedly

Power outages are reported to be continuing in various places because of snow that has fallen on and off since the end of last week. In Gifu Prefecture, about 4,200 households were still without power as of 5 p.m. on the 23rd, communications at 3 major mobile carriers were also disrupted by the outages and other effects, and some rail services were suspended. Outages continue in Nagano city and elsewhere too, and Chubu Electric Power Grid aims to restore power within the 24th.

In Fukushima Prefecture, more than 500 households were temporarily isolated by trees felled by the snow, but that was resolved by the 22nd. Outages continue in places, and restoration work and snow clearing are under way. On the 24th, meanwhile, a low-pressure system and a front are expected to bring an unstable atmosphere across the country, with rain and wind strengthening on the Sea of Japan side. The Japan Meteorological Agency urges close attention to sediment disasters from strong winds and heavy rain, and also to lightning and violent gusts such as tornadoes. The restoration time is a target, not a certainty.

President Zelensky Admits Attacking Russian Ships Off Libya With Unmanned Boats; Sinking Is Ukraine's Claim

Ukrainian President Zelensky has revealed that unmanned boats were used off Libya in North Africa to attack and sink Russian military vessels and other targets. However, the scale of the damage and the details of the attack rest on Ukraine's announcement, and confirmation from the Russian side or corroboration by a third party has not been confirmed.
