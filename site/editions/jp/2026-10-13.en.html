North Korea Fires at Least 2 Ballistic Missiles Into the Sea of Japan; Defense Ministry Says They Fell Outside the EEZ, Some May Have Flown an Irregular Trajectory

Both are estimated to have fallen outside Japan's EEZ, and there is no report of damage. It is said to be the 11th launch of 2026, and the prime minister ordered full readiness. Tokyo's market reopens on the 13th.

On the morning of the 12th, the Defense Ministry announced that North Korea had fired at least two ballistic missiles toward the Sea of Japan (NHK, Mainichi Shimbun, Asahi Shimbun). In its first report, the ministry said it was gathering information on whether Japan was affected (NHK). According to South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff, the launch took place at around 9:20 a.m. from near Pyongyang toward the sea east of the Korean Peninsula (The Straits Times, CNA). The Nikkei reported that the first missile was launched at around 9:19 a.m., reaching a maximum altitude of about 20 km and flying about 220 km, while the second, at around 9:36 a.m., reached a maximum altitude of about 70 km and flew more than 910 km. The flight distances differ by a factor of about four, so the two flew very differently. The Defense Ministry estimates that both fell outside Japan's exclusive economic zone (EEZ). NHK reported that no entry into the EEZ has been confirmed, and TV Asahi said there was no report of damage. The Mainichi Shimbun said some may have flown an irregular trajectory, and reported that Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi ordered full readiness in gathering information and staying vigilant. Jiji Press, citing South Korea's military, reported that several ballistic missiles were fired. The gap with the number the Japanese Defense Ministry confirmed has not yet been resolved. The Nikkei also reported that the government protested to North Korea through the embassy channel in Beijing, but no other outlet has confirmed this.

This is said to be the 11th launch of 2026 (reported by the Nikkei as the Defense Ministry's announcement). GMA News reported that it was the fourth this month, and came a few days after South Korea tested a hypersonic missile. Mint reported that it also came a few days after North Korea warned South Korea that it would retaliate if its territory were violated. Meanwhile, on the 12th the EU added three individuals and four entities, including North Korean missile engineers, to its own sanctions list (Chosun Ilbo Japanese edition). Launches of ballistic missiles are prohibited for North Korea by UN Security Council resolutions. But the model of the second missile, the details of the irregular trajectory and the purpose of the launch have not yet been confirmed. Tokyo's market was closed on the 12th for Sports Day, so any reaction in stocks and the yen will come after it reopens on the 13th. The Japanese government's response will be tested on cooperation among Japan, the U.S. and South Korea, and on how effective a protest routed through China can be.

Dodgers Lose NLCS Opener 2-1; Nippon-Ham Clear the CS First Stage, Hiromi Ito Unshaken

The Major League Baseball National League Championship Series (NLCS) opened on the 12th, Japan time, and the Brewers beat the Dodgers 2-1 at home in Milwaukee (The Japan Times, Asahi Shimbun, CBC). The Dodgers scored first in the second inning but fell behind in the fifth (Liberty Times). The Brewers held on thanks to a homer by Bryce Turang and strong work from the bullpen (CBC). Dodgers starter Tarik Skubal struck out seven but got no reward (Liberty Times). Shohei Ohtani started in the cleanup spot (Asahi Shimbun), reaching base on a walk and scoring, and also had one hit (Liberty Times). Game 2 is on the 13th, Japan time, with Yoshinobu Yamamoto scheduled to start (Fox News, Number). At home, in Game 3 of the Pacific League Climax Series (CS) First Stage, Nippon-Ham beat Seibu 4-1 and advanced to the Final Stage with a 2-1 series record (NHK, Asahi Shimbun, Jiji Press). Ace Hiromi Ito stayed unshaken even when a batted ball struck his left foot (Asahi Shimbun, Jiji Press). Chusei Mannami hit the first postseason home run (Doshin Sports).

Nobel Economics Prize Goes to Americans Hall and Woodford for Building the Theory of Policy That Stabilizes the Economy

On the evening of the 12th, Japan time, the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics will go to Robert Hall of Stanford University and Michael Woodford of Columbia University, both of the United States (Reuters, Jiji Press, Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun, Yomiuri Shimbun). The citation is “the microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization,” and the prize is 12 million Swedish kronor, about $1.2 million (Mint).

The Asahi Shimbun credited them with building the theoretical basis of business-cycle stabilization policy, and the Mainichi Shimbun reported that they made it possible to use fiscal policy and monetary policy effectively in a crisis. The Academy explained that new macroeconomic models allow timely fiscal and monetary measures to soften downturns (The Hindu). The Hindu also described an aspect of the research holding that the effect of policy depends partly on how governments explain their reasons. This was the last of this year's prize announcements (The Japan Times).

Trump Claims an “Energy Ceasefire,” Russia Strikes a Kharkiv Power Plant Right After; Kremlin Rejects 60-Day Truce Proposal

U.S. President Trump claimed that an “energy ceasefire” had been reached between Russia and Ukraine. It was reported through the morning of the 12th, Japan time (NHK, Mainichi Shimbun, Asahi Shimbun). President Zelensky said Ukraine is ready to comply if Russia does the same (Reuters and others), and said Trump's announcement was “news to him” (Asahi Shimbun and others). But the Ukrainian side said Russian forces attacked a thermal power plant in Kharkiv right after the announcement, and condemned it as a violation of the ceasefire (Reuters, The Straits Times, The Hill). Reports differ on the casualties in Kharkiv: the Kyiv Post said 19 were injured (including 2 children), while Italy's TGcom24 reported 3 dead and 25 injured.

The Russian presidential administration (the Kremlin) said only that “contacts continue” on the energy ceasefire and did not confirm it (RFI, NU.nl). It rejected Ukraine's proposal for a 60-day truce and said it seeks an overall peace agreement (Reuters). On the 12th, the EU decided on sanctions against about 1,600 Russian companies and individuals. It is the largest ever and is focused on the defense industry (Jiji Press, Reuters). WTI crude briefly rose to the $94s during trading on the 12th (Fisco). The scope and duration of the ceasefire, and the contents of the “diesel deal” between Trump and President Putin, have not yet been confirmed.

Opposition Demands Agriculture Minister Yana Resign Over “Road Budget” Remark; Prime Minister Holds Off on Dismissal, LDP Policy Chief Wants Continued Explanation

Agriculture Minister Kazuo Yana is said to have said in May, at a Liberal Democratic Party meeting in Tochigi Prefecture, that he had “drastically cut” the road budgets of cities and towns. Mr. Yana retracted the remark and apologized on October 5, and at a press conference on the 7th he explained that he asked the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism about road budgets “because there were voices of local concern,” and denied he would resign (Nikkei, Jiji Press). The ministry's review found no confirmation of any approach from Mr. Yana. Still, the road budgets of two municipalities had fallen by as much as 54% from the previous year (Nikkei, Jiji Press).

On the 12th, the opposition stepped up its pressure. Furukawa, deputy leader of the Democratic Party for the People, said, “If he cannot explain himself, it is only natural that he step down cleanly” (NHK), and party secretary-general Takeshi Shina demanded that Mr. Yana resign on his own, or that the prime minister dismiss him if he does not (Mainichi Shimbun, Sankei Shimbun). Mr. Shina also pointed out that the remark could be taken as intimidating voters. Meanwhile, the LDP's Policy Research Council chair said Mr. Yana should keep explaining himself (47NEWS). Jiji Press reports that a suspected violation of the Public Offices Election Act has surfaced. Prime Minister Takaichi has held off on dismissal (Josei Jishin and others). His future and his explanation in the Diet are the focus.
