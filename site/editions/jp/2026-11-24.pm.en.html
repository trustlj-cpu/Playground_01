Nvidia Announces Huang's Return as CEO; Nikkei Average Jumps 2,207 Yen to 58,644 Yen in Tokyo

The company said its board decided on the return after hearing from senior U.S. government officials, and AI and chip stocks sold off last week drew buyers back.

U.S. chip giant Nvidia announced on the 23rd (U.S. time) that Jensen Huang, who resigned on November 12, would return immediately as president and chief executive officer (CEO) and as a director. The board of directors decided unanimously and explained that, having received input from high levels of the U.S. government, it is confident the return poses no national security risk. President Trump has described persuading the board on the 22nd. Personal matters and allegations involving China were rumored as the background to the resignation, but they have not been officially confirmed. The board had reportedly not accepted requests for a return until a few days earlier, so the change of course was sudden. In New York, Nvidia shares rose about 31% (31.5% and other figures are also reported, depending on the tally), the Nasdaq Composite closed up 6.51% at 24,089.56, and the S&P 500 closed up 4.73% at 7,237.17.

In Tokyo on the 24th, buyers returned to the AI and chip-related stocks that had been sold off the previous week. The Nikkei average closed at 58,644.63 yen, up 2,207.98 yen (3.91%) from the previous week's close, after gaining more than 2,800 yen at one point. Two high-priced stocks, Advantest and Tokyo Electron, are reported to have lifted the index by about 973 yen. The Nikkei average is an average of the prices of 225 stocks, so moves in chip stocks, whose shares cost a lot each, weigh heavily on the index. The Tokyo Stock Price Index (TOPIX) rose 90.66 points to 3,764.88. On Huang, an inquiry by independent directors is reported to be continuing, and there are calls for an explanation in Congress.

Six Opposition Parties in Upper House Agree to Submit Censure Motion Against Education Minister Seki; Seki Eager to Stay On

Six opposition parties in the House of Councillors agreed on the 24th to submit a censure motion against Education Minister Yoshihiro Seki to the upper house. The Constitutional Democratic Party, the Democratic Party for the People, Komeito, Sanseito, the Japanese Communist Party and the Inochi Party are said to be joining, and the timing of submission was left to Yoshitaka Saito, the Constitutional Democratic Party's Diet affairs committee chair. The trigger was a political fundraising party Seki held at the members' office building with a 90,000-yen fee per person, and there are also reports that political funds were used for the rent on a home. After the cabinet meeting, Seki denied resigning at a press conference and said, “I will devote everything to my duties.” Saito indicated that, absent a resignation or dismissal, the party will for now be cautious about deliberating bills such as the consumption tax cut. The ruling parties lack a majority in the upper house, and the motion is reported to have a chance of passing if submitted, but a censure motion has no legal binding force.

Constitutional Democratic Party Submits Counterproposal to Consumption Tax Cut Bill in Upper House, Up to 50,000 Yen in Payments for Incomes up to 6 Million Yen

On the 24th, the Constitutional Democratic Party submitted to the upper house a counterproposal to the bill related to cutting the consumption tax on food. For two years, fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028, people with salary income up to 6 million yen a year would receive up to 50,000 yen per person per year, depending on income, and the proposal is expected to be deliberated in a special committee of the upper house, it is reported. As a response to high prices during fiscal 2026, it also includes a plan to give 50,000 yen per household to households exempt from residential tax and add 20,000 yen per child. From fiscal 2029, the party plans to introduce a refundable tax credit, which combines payments with a tax deduction. The government and ruling parties are said to plan to lower the consumption tax rate on food to 1% for two years, and the party wants to stand apart in the breadth and speed of its coverage, but the total funding required has not been shown.

Komeito Secretary-General Nishida Announces Policy of Not Endorsing or Supporting LDP-Aligned Candidates in Next Spring's Unified Local Elections

Komeito Secretary-General Minoru Nishida told reporters in the Diet building on the 24th that the party will neither endorse nor support candidates aligned with the Liberal Democratic Party in the unified local elections of spring 2027. As the reason, Nishida cited allegations over political funds involving ministers in the Takaichi cabinet and said the problem of politics and money has not been resolved at all. The LDP's reluctance to tighten rules on corporate and organizational donations is also said to be on Nishida's mind. Nishida is also reported to have voiced criticism that cutting the number of proportional representation seats in the lower house is being substituted for the core of political reform. The policy covers LDP-aligned candidates, including independents. Nishida stressed, on the other hand, that cooperation with the Constitutional Democratic Party, taking local circumstances into account, will continue. Voting in the unified local elections is said to be in April 2027.

Amendment Requiring Prior Government Permission to Acquire Security-Critical Land Passes Lower House, Goes to Upper House

An amendment to the Important Land Survey Act, which tightens regulation of acquisitions of land important to security, was passed by majority vote at a plenary session of the House of Representatives on the 24th and sent to the House of Councillors. The Liberal Democratic Party, Nippon Ishin no Kai, the Democratic Party for the People, Komeito, Sanseito and others voted in favor, and the Japanese Communist Party is reported to have opposed. Areas around Self-Defense Forces facilities and the like are designated as regulated zones, and prior government permission is required when transferring ownership of land or buildings, a system that applies regardless of nationality. Acquisitions by people connected to foreign governments or foreign militaries are, in principle, not allowed.

The regulated zones include the area of about 1,000 meters around facilities whose functions could be seriously impaired by drone attacks, and uninhabited border islands. Anyone who trades without permission faces imprisonment of up to three years or a fine of up to 3 million yen. The lower house Cabinet Committee also adopted a supplementary resolution asking the government to make the criteria and scope of permission clear. Opposition lawmakers have voiced concern that the criteria are vague and will affect real estate transactions. Passage in the current Diet session is expected, but the upper house deliberation schedule has not yet been set.
