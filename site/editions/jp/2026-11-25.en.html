U.S. Reports Say Hanshin's Teruaki Sato Agrees to Six-Year Deal With Cubs Worth About ¥19.1 Billion; Official Announcement Unconfirmed

The Triple Crown winner, who had been seeking a move to the majors through the posting system; contract terms have so far been relayed only as information from people familiar

Teruaki Sato of the pro baseball Hanshin Tigers, who won the Triple Crown this season, has agreed to a contract with the Major League Cubs, major U.S. media reported one after another through the early hours of the 25th. The total is reported to be 120 million dollars over six years, roughly ¥19.1 billion in Japanese yen. Simply divided, that comes to about ¥3.2 billion a year. Sato had been seeking the move through the posting system, a system under which a player approved by the club can negotiate with all Major League clubs. However, the contract details were relayed as information from people familiar, and an official announcement from the Cubs or Sato's side had not been confirmed as of the time of the reports.

A Triple Crown means finishing first in the league in all three categories of batting average, home runs and runs batted in. Sato achieved that this season, and the performance had drawn attention overseas. Major League contracts are customarily announced officially by the club after procedures such as a physical are completed, so the terms and timing of an announcement are still to come. If the move is finalized, the system provides for a transfer fee to be paid to Hanshin, but the amount is not given in this report.

Information on Customers' Asset Balances, 1.68 Million Records, Reportedly Leaked at Rakuten Securities; No Fraudulent Trades Confirmed

Rakuten Securities announced on the 17th of this month that some customer information may have leaked outside through unauthorized access. According to the company, it detected and blocked possible unauthorized access on the 16th, and its investigation found that information managed inside a test system may have leaked on the 11th. Branch and customer codes and login IDs were put at up to about 11.25 million accounts, including former customers, and names, addresses, withdrawal destination accounts, and self-reported annual income and financial assets may be included for up to about 4.47 million accounts. In the early hours of the 25th, it was reported that, of these, 1.68 million records of information on customers' asset balances had leaked. No fraudulent trades have been confirmed. However, the figure of 1.68 million rests on a single report, and the details, such as how it relates to the up to about 4.47 million accounts announced earlier and which items are included, have not been confirmed. The company explains that leaks of passwords or trading PINs have not been confirmed. Further disclosures are to come.

COP31 Closes Without Adopting an Overall Outcome Document; Environment Minister Sasagawa: “Review Needed Toward the Next Agreement”

COP31, the UN climate change conference held in Turkey, closed without adopting an outcome document pulling together the whole conference. The session ran past its original schedule, and the closing is reported to have come on the 22nd. The biggest point of contention was whether to write into the document a direction of moving away from fossil fuels, and the divide between countries could not be closed. Several countries opposed the final draft, and the number differs from report to report.

On the 24th, Japan's Minister of the Environment Sasagawa expressed the view that countries need to review the result toward the next agreement. That said, not everything went undecided. Some point out that decisions were adopted in areas dealing with finance and a just transition, so only the overall outcome document, the “centerpiece,” was left unadopted. The question is how much debate will be carried over to the next Conference of the Parties.

U.S. Commerce Department Designates Singapore Firm Megaspeed as Subject to Export Controls, a New Development in the Nvidia Matter

The U.S. Commerce Department has disclosed that it designated the Singapore company “Megaspeed International” as subject to export controls on advanced semiconductors and other items. The designation is to be published in the official gazette scheduled for the 25th. Once designated, exporting U.S. products or technology to the company requires a license, and applications are in principle treated as not approved. The measure adds the company to a list of trade restrictions called the Entity List, and, apart from the share price moves up to yesterday, the problem over where Nvidia-made semiconductors end up has entered a new stage.

In this matter, the Commerce Department and the Justice Department issued a joint statement on the 16th of this month, explaining that the Commerce Department has been investigating suspected export control violations since 2024. Nvidia has said it put the company on its own do-not-trade list in June 2025. The background is a suspicion that semiconductors flowed from the United States to China and elsewhere through circumvention exports that evade the controls, but the actual flow and where responsibility lies have not been confirmed. Whether other related companies are included, and the extent of the effects after designation, will become clear when the official gazette notice is published.

Tax on Short-Term Resale of New Condominiums to Be Tightened, Land and Transport Minister Says; Sales Within One Year in Focus

The Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism has indicated a policy of stepping up taxes on short-term resales, buying a new condominium and selling it soon after. The target is said to be limited to new units. Behind it is a view that one factor pushing up prices in central Tokyo is speculative trading, done to profit from selling rather than to live in the home. The government and ruling parties began studying the matter on the 16th of this month, and a proposal to tax more heavily cases of selling within one year of buying has emerged.

Reported proposals include setting the tax rate on individuals' gains from sales within one year at 52%, adding 10% to corporations' transfer gains, and removing the real estate acquisition tax relief measure when a unit is resold within one year. At present, gains from sales within five years of ownership are taxed at about 40% in income tax and resident tax combined (short-term capital gains). Nothing has been decided, and the focus is whether it will be included in the FY2027 tax reform outline to be compiled at year-end.
