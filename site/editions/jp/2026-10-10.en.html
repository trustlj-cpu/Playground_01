FSA Orders Prudential Life to Suspend Part of Its Business for Over 3 Months; Reported Losses Reach About ¥6.2 Billion

Insurance salespeople took money from customers. The Financial Services Agency halted new sales. What is in question is the system on the selling side itself.

On the 9th, the Financial Services Agency (FSA) ordered Prudential Life Insurance to partly suspend business. The action responds to employees defrauding customers of money. It runs more than three months, from the 13th through the end of January next year, and halts the solicitation of new contracts and more (Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun, NHK, Jiji Press). The parent, Prudential Holdings of Japan, also received a business improvement order demanding stronger governance. According to Jiji Press, the deadline for submitting an improvement plan is the end of November. It is said to be the first business suspension order against a life insurer since Japan Post Insurance in 2019. The scale of the damage has swelled sharply over the past few months. The company's initial figure was about ¥3.1 billion, but a report by an outside investigation committee released on the 8th put the reported losses at about ¥6.2 billion (Mainichi Shimbun). On the 8th the company announced that it had dismissed 64 sales employees involved in the fraud and suspended 68, that its president and other executives will return 30% of their pay for three months, and the president apologized at a press conference on the 8th (Mainichi Shimbun, NHK).

The Mainichi Shimbun reported that the FSA pointed to a strong fixation on money, among other things. The investigation committee analyzed that a way of doing business that relied too heavily on sales results and personal connections invited the runaway behavior (Asahi Shimbun), and reported that calls for a review had been raised since 2004 yet were overlooked again and again (Mainichi Shimbun). In testimony from former employees relayed by the Mainichi Shimbun, some people worked while carrying debt, and only a few earned high pay. The company has refrained from new sales since February, and the action will delay a restart further. The suspension covers the solicitation of new contracts and more; for how policies already signed are handled, check each company's notices. Note, however, that the ¥6.2 billion is a figure based on reports, and the actual total has not been determined. There are three things to watch from here: the start of the suspension on the 13th (the first business day after the three-day weekend), the content of the improvement plan due at the end of November, and how far refunds to the people affected progress. Did the pay system on the selling side give people a motive to take customers' money? The question is whether that is fixed, not whether the matter simply ends with the penalty. Note that the details of the action are based on each outlet's reporting, and the FSA's own announcement could not be checked.

Nobel Peace Prize Goes to Former ICC Judge Pillay; U.S. Sanctions the ICC Itself Right After, Prime Minister Takaichi “Deeply Concerned”

The Norwegian Nobel Committee announced on the 9th that the 2026 Peace Prize goes to South African jurist Navi Pillay (85). She has served as UN High Commissioner for Human Rights and as a judge of the International Criminal Court (ICC), and the citation was her contribution to promoting peace and international law (Asahi Shimbun, Jiji Press, Reuters). According to the Mainichi Shimbun, her age at the time of the award is the second highest ever. Israel strongly rebuked Pillay's finding of genocide in Gaza, saying it “legitimizes hatred and prejudice” (Asahi Shimbun, Jiji Press). ICC President Tomoko Akane, who is Japanese, said, “The importance of international justice has been recognized” (Jiji Press). Several hours after the announcement, Reuters reported that the United States had made the ICC a sanctions target, and NHK, the Asahi Shimbun and the Mainichi Shimbun followed. The United States had earlier sanctioned individuals including President Akane; this time the organization itself is the target. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi wrote on X that the U.S. sanctions are incompatible with Japan's position and she is deeply concerned, and that Japan will continue to provide the necessary support (Asahi Shimbun, Jiji Press). Russia's Prosecutor General's Office also asked the Netherlands and others on the 8th to hand over President Akane (NHK, Asahi Shimbun). The award and the sanctions fell on the same day. The precise scope of the measures has not yet been confirmed in an official announcement.

Skyticket 14.64 Million, Baitoru 3.88 Million: Leaks Pile Up in a Single Day, Government Asks Companies for Countermeasures

Leaks of personal information spread to at least five companies on the 9th alone. Skyticket, a flight-booking service, announced that member information on about 14.64 million records may have leaked (Asahi Shimbun, NHK). The Bookoff Group disclosed up to 6.43 million records, Dip, which runs the job site Baitoru, up to 3.88 million-plus email addresses, East Japan Railway (JR East) up to 2 million records of members of services including Eki-net, and JR Kyushu possibly up to 1.3 million (NHK). Bookoff's count is of membership numbers and does not necessarily equal the number of people. The JR East and JR Kyushu cases are said to involve unauthorized access to a SoftBank subsidiary. It is not one company's accident; the spread through contractors and shared platforms stands out.

On the 9th the government issued a request to businesses hit by successive incidents to take countermeasures (NHK, Mainichi Shimbun). At a Liberal Democratic Party meeting too, views were voiced calling on the government to strengthen countermeasures. Skyticket members, whose passwords are also said to have leaked, should change them on any other services where they reuse the same password. On services that offer multi-factor authentication, setting it up brings peace of mind. Emails and calls that use the leak as a pretext to extract numbers or card details may continue. Cabinet Approves Bill to Cut Consumption Tax on Food to 1% for Two Years From Next April; Upper House Opposition Weighs Censure Motion Against Agriculture Minister Yana

On the 9th, the government gave Cabinet approval to a bill to cut the consumption tax rate on food from 8% to 1% for two years from next April (NHK, Jiji Press, Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun). The bill states that it will not rely on deficit-financing bonds for funding, but leaves the specifics to year-end budget compilation, and the benefits for low- and middle-income workers meant to cover the remaining 1% have no decided eligibility or amount (Asahi Shimbun). The aim is passage by the end of the session.

Meanwhile, remarks by Agriculture Minister Kazuo Yana (簗和生) are shaking the Diet. He reportedly said he had cut road budgets for the municipalities of mayors who did not support him in the lower house election, and Prime Minister Takaichi again rejected calls to dismiss him on the 9th (Jiji Press, Asahi Shimbun). The six opposition parties in the upper house agreed the same day to consider filing a censure motion. Whether it has been filed has not yet been confirmed (Jiji Press, Mainichi Shimbun, NHK). In the upper house, where the ruling side is a minority, a motion would also affect the deliberation schedule. The upper house Budget Committee will meet on the 16th and 19th with the prime minister attending (NHK).

Nikkei Falls 11 Yen for a Third Straight Loss, Closing at 69,030.92; U.S. Tech Stocks Dropped on the 8th

On the Tokyo market on the 9th, the Nikkei Stock Average edged lower for a third straight day. It closed down 11.19 yen (0.02%) from the previous day at 69,030.92 (Jiji Press, Nikkei). AI and semiconductor-related shares were sold after the previous day's drop in U.S. tech stocks (NHK), and after the morning selling ran its course, dip buying held the decline in check. Short covering ahead of the three-day weekend is also said to have narrowed the drop (Jiji Press). The yen was in the low ¥158 range to the dollar, and NHK described small moves. Why the yen has not strengthened even as expectations of a Bank of Japan rate hike grow was covered in an explainer.

The latest U.S. closes are those of the 8th. The S&P 500 closed at 7,765.36 (down 0.47%), the Nasdaq Composite at 27,193.34 (down 1.25%) and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 51,231.64 (up 0.10%), and the U.S. 10-year yield was 5.231%. WTI futures were up 3.6% at about $91.5, and gold was about $4,157. The U.S. market on the 9th was still trading at this page's deadline, and Reuters reported that oil was lower and U.S. stock futures were higher. The TOPIX close could not be confirmed in reports.
