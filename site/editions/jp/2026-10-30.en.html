North Korea Fires More Than 10 Short-Range Ballistic Missiles Into the Sea of Japan, First Launch in 17 Days; Likely Landed Outside Japan’s EEZ

The missiles were launched from around Wonsan on the east coast shortly after noon on the 29th. South Korea’s military puts the flight distance at about 250 kilometers, and the Japanese government estimates they fell outside its exclusive economic zone.

North Korea fired more than 10 short-range ballistic missiles toward the Sea of Japan from around Wonsan on its east coast shortly after noon on the 29th. According to South Korea’s military, the launch took place at about 12:30 p.m. Some announcements put the number at “a dozen or more,” so the counts do not match. South Korea’s military puts the flight distance at about 250 kilometers and, together with U.S. forces, is analyzing the missiles’ capabilities; it has stepped up monitoring and vigilance in case of further launches. A short-range ballistic missile is a missile of relatively short range that is launched by rocket and then falls in a parabolic arc. Its hallmark is that it leaves little time to prepare an interception. Japan’s Ministry of Defense announced at 12:39 p.m. the launch of “something that may be a ballistic missile,” and seven minutes later, at 12:46, announced that it appeared to have already landed. According to government officials, the point of impact is estimated to be outside Japan’s exclusive economic zone (EEZ). The EEZ is the sea extending up to 200 nautical miles (about 370 kilometers) from the coast, within which the coastal state holds rights over resources. No information on damage has come in.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi instructed the relevant ministries to maintain a full state of readiness, to gather and promptly provide information, and to confirm the safety of aircraft and ships. Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara said Japan had lodged a strong protest with North Korea through its embassy channel in Beijing, calling the launch a violation of UN Security Council resolutions and a grave matter affecting the safety of the public. The resolutions prohibit North Korea from launching using ballistic missile technology. Because ballistic missiles can also carry nuclear weapons, the United Nations has restricted the launches themselves. North Korea’s previous launch was on October 12, so this one comes 17 days later. The type and capabilities of the missiles are being analyzed by South Korean and U.S. forces, and North Korea’s own explanation has not yet been confirmed. The Japanese government is sharing information on the launch with South Korea and the United States.

Electricity and Gas Subsidies to Resume From December; Gasoline Subsidy to Shrink by 5 Yen Each Month

On the 29th, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi announced that subsidies for electricity and gas charges will be provided from December through February next year. The relief is said to be about 7,000 yen for a standard household over the three months combined. It starts earlier than last year. Meanwhile, the gasoline subsidy will be scaled back. Under the subsidy, the state pays petroleum wholesalers so that the retail price approaches a benchmark price (about 170 yen per liter for regular). The prime minister set out a plan to raise this benchmark by 5 yen each month from December. As the benchmark rises, the subsidy falls by that amount. More than 10 billion yen in tax money a day is being spent on the subsidy. The government has decided to spend more than 1.14 trillion yen from this fiscal year’s reserve fund to keep in place, for the time being, the shock-mitigation measures it has maintained in response to the situation in the Middle East. Shock-mitigation measures are temporary steps that soften a sudden rise in prices. The reserve fund is money set aside in advance for sudden expenses not included in the budget. The spending is finalized after a formal decision procedure.

Data of a Cumulative 260 Million People Leaked Since April Through Unauthorized Access; Damage Spreads at Email Delivery Companies

The Personal Information Protection Commission has compiled preliminary figures showing that leaks of personal data through unauthorized access and similar causes reached a cumulative total of about 260 million people from April through October 16. That is more than twice Japan’s total population. The cumulative figure includes double counting, in which the same person is counted in several incidents. In October, 830 reports were filed through the 16th, exceeding the 303 in September. The damage is concentrated at email delivery companies, which send mass emails to customers on behalf of businesses. Yumirlink confirmed unauthorized access on October 16 and announced the leak of a cumulative 51,628,280 items. Arara announced on the 29th that about 3.01 million email addresses had leaked.

Companies using Yumirlink’s service were also affected: a Bandai Namco group company disclosed that 346,247 items, including customers’ email addresses, and one major newspaper company disclosed that 70,921 items belonging to event registrants and others may have leaked. Both say credit card numbers and passwords are not included. Arara confirmed unauthorized access on the 28th and halted its delivery service, saying a vulnerability in its web server had been exploited. A vulnerability is a flaw in a system’s design or implementation that serves as an entry point for attacks. Which client companies’ customers are included is under investigation, and the figures may change.

Spain Train Crash: Names of Two Japanese Nationals on Passenger List; Death Toll 68, Foreign Ministry Checking Whether They Are Safe

In the rear-end collision of high-speed trains that occurred in central Spain on the 28th, it has emerged that the names of two Japanese nationals are on the passenger list. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs was informed by the Spanish authorities. It is not known whether the two were actually aboard, and as of the evening of the 29th they could not be reached by phone. A task force has been set up on the ground, and Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said Japan would do “everything possible to protect Japanese nationals.” The death toll stands at 68 in the authorities’ tally, while some tallies in Spain put it at 69.

According to the Spanish government’s explanation, a malfunction in the system that automatically manages train operations stopped the train ahead; after traffic control switched to manual, it authorized the following train to proceed even though a train was stopped ahead. Traffic control is the unit that watches train movements and instructs departures and stops. The transport minister called the control decision “inexplicable,” and the prime minister declared a three-day mourning period from the 29th. In Spain, a collision between high-speed trains in January also left 46 people dead. No conclusion on the cause of the accident has been reached.

ECB Holds Policy Rate; President Lagarde Announces Departure on February 7, 2027

On the 29th, the European Central Bank (ECB) left its deposit rate, one of its policy rates, unchanged at 2.5%. The bank had carried out an additional rate hike at its previous meeting, so this is the first hold in two meetings. The statement pointed out that energy prices keep rising, posing risks both of an upside surprise to the inflation outlook and a downside to the growth outlook. It also said financial conditions have tightened since the previous meeting. The deposit rate is the rate paid on money that banks deposit with the ECB, and it is the central one among the ECB’s policy rates.

On the same day, President Christine Lagarde announced a departure on February 7, 2027. The formal term runs until October 2027, so the exit comes partway through it. The European Council appoints the successor, and the selection began in earnest on the 29th. Some see the background to the departure as a wish to settle on a successor before far-right parties gain ground in next year’s French presidential election. With French government bond yields rising sharply, Lagarde said the ECB has tools to respond if market moves impede the effect of monetary policy. Whether to raise rates again in December has not been indicated.
