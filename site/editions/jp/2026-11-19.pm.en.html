On Day Two of APEC, Prime Minister Takaichi Shakes Hands With President Xi Again; Detention of Japanese Nationals Also Raised in Talks With Putin, Chief Cabinet Secretary Explains

On the second day of the leaders' meeting in Shenzhen, the Japanese and Chinese leaders exchanged words again on the morning of the 19th. The Chief Cabinet Secretary said China's treatment was the same as in past years.

On the morning of the 19th, before attending the leaders' meeting of APEC (Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation) in Shenzhen, China, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi was greeted by President Xi Jinping of China and shook hands again, following the handshake before the previous evening's banquet. China is the host country of the meeting. The handshake is reported to have lasted about 10 to 11 seconds, and Xi is also said to have spoken to the prime minister. No formal Japan-China summit is expected to be set, and this contact has stayed within the exchange at the greeting. On China's handling, the Chief Cabinet Secretary explained that it was “the usual reception.”

The prime minister had spoken with Russian President Putin for about 30 minutes the previous evening. On the 19th, the Chief Cabinet Secretary revealed that the issue of the detention of Japanese nationals was raised in the exchange and that the two countries agreed to keep communicating. It is said to be the first time in seven years that the leaders of Japan and Russia have met in person. The detention of Japanese nationals refers to the problem of Japanese people being held in custody in Russia. Details of the exchange have not been made public beyond what the Chief Cabinet Secretary said. A three-way meeting of the U.S., China and Russia is also scheduled for the 19th, and how the prime minister deals with the leaders of each country remains a focus.

First-Ever U.S.-China-Russia Three-Way Meeting to Be Held on the 19th; Xi Reportedly Stresses Stable China-Russia Ties

A three-way meeting of U.S. President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Putin will be held on the 19th in Shenzhen, China, where the APEC leaders' meeting is taking place. A meeting at which the leaders of three countries sit at the same table is said to be the first ever. The 19th is the second day of APEC, and Xi, as leader of the host country, is reported to have said in a session attended by the leaders that the building of an Asia-Pacific community must be pushed forward. The agenda and duration of the meeting have not been officially disclosed. Ahead of the meeting, Xi and Putin are reported to have shown China-Russia coordination. Xi is reported to have described China-Russia relations as “unshaken by wind and rain, as secure as a mountain.” Some see the meeting as a “New Yalta Conference,” likening it to the 1945 Yalta Conference. The Yalta Conference was a meeting at which the leaders of the U.S., Britain and the Soviet Union discussed the postwar framework near the end of the Second World War. Japan is not within the framework of this three-way meeting, and the outcome has not yet been reported.

Tochigi Robbery-Murder: Man, 18, Who Introduced the Perpetrator Found Guilty, First Ruling in the Same Case; Utsunomiya District Court

On the 19th, the Utsunomiya District Court found guilty an 18-year-old male defendant who was charged with the role of introducing the on-site perpetrator in a robbery-murder case that took place in Tochigi Prefecture. Several people are said to have been involved in the case, and this is the first ruling in the same case. The man is also reported to be a high school student. The on-site perpetrator is the one who actually commits the crime at the scene.

The crime of robbery-murder is charged when a person is killed for the purpose of taking property, and the statutory penalty is said to be death or life imprisonment. However, the specific charge the man faced, the severity of the penalty and the details of the reasoning have not been confirmed. How sentencing was set out will have to wait until the details of the ruling are made public.

Aiko Names New Orchid Variety in Singapore; Also Touches on Hardships of War at Luncheon on the 18th

On the 19th, Aiko, eldest daughter of Their Majesties the Emperor and Empress, attended a naming ceremony at a botanical garden during the visit to Singapore, where a newly bred orchid was given a name. The name is said to be “Dendrobium Aiko Naishinno Denka,” and it commemorates Aiko's visit. It is also reported to have ties to three generations of parent and child. Singapore's custom of naming orchids after visitors and giving them as gifts is called “orchid diplomacy.”

On the 18th, Aiko paid a courtesy call on Singapore's president and attended a luncheon hosted by the president, dressed in Japanese clothing. In the remarks, Aiko spoke of the friendship between the two countries and touched on the history of hardship in the last war, saying in effect that the various hardships must not be forgotten. There were also words to the effect of wanting to become a partner in building a peaceful world. The rest of the schedule for the stay cannot be confirmed at this point.

Bear Sighted Again in Maebashi Residential Area on the Morning of the 19th, Student on the Way to School Reports Being Chased; Parent Bear Still Uncaptured?

On the morning of the 19th, a high school student on the way to school reported to police in a residential area of Maebashi, Gunma Prefecture, that “a bear chased me.” Police and the city say it may be the parent bear, which has not yet been captured, of the two bears believed to be a parent and offspring that were sighted in a string of reports on the 17th. The sightings on the 17th were in the same residential area, so information about a sighting has come out again in this neighborhood. The report came during the morning commute to school, and whether anyone was injured has not been confirmed.

Of the two bears believed to be a parent and offspring, the one not captured is the parent, and its whereabouts are unknown. Whether the report on the morning of the 19th concerned the parent bear has not been established.
