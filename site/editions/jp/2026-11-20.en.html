First 3-Nation Summit of U.S., Chinese and Russian Leaders; Prime Minister Takaichi Shakes Hands With Xi, but No Formal Talks Are Reported

On the sidelines of the APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen, Trump, Xi and Putin sat together for the first time. Japan was left outside the talks.

On the 19th, on the sidelines of the APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen, China, U.S. President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Putin held their first 3-nation talks. Trump raised a discussion on nuclear disarmament, and Xi and Putin are reported to have stressed their standing as “victor nations.” China and Russia explain that the 3 countries agreed on UN reform, expanded fuel supplies and dialogue on nuclear issues. The Russian Foreign Ministry published the full text of a declaration on peace by the 3 leaders. There are also reports that the 3 sides agreed to increase supplies of refined oil, but no volume or timing has been given. Trump left Shenzhen after the talks.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi of Japan visited China for the first time and attended the APEC leaders' meeting. Takaichi shook hands with Xi twice, on the 18th and the 19th, and on the 19th is reported to have spoken to Xi for ten-odd seconds. No formal Japan-China summit was arranged, which is described as unusual among the major countries. Accounts of what passed between them differ: one report says there was “no diplomatic exchange,” another says there was a brief word. The Chief Cabinet Secretary described China's reception as “the same as in past years.” At the leaders' meeting, Takaichi stressed the importance of a “rules-based international economic order,” and at the press conference after the close, asked about a Taiwan contingency, is reported to have answered that “Japan's position has not changed.” Takaichi also spoke with Putin at the venue, and it is described as the first contact between Japanese and Russian leaders in about 7 years.

Anthropic Lists on Nasdaq at an Offer Price of $83, Opening at $111.5

U.S. AI company Anthropic listed on the Nasdaq on the 19th (U.S. Eastern time). The IPO (initial public offering) offer price was $83 a share, and the valuation is put at about $1.5 trillion (about ¥239 trillion). The opening price was $111.5, reported to be a bit over 30% above the offer price, though the shares reportedly slipped at points afterward. Reports put the amount raised at about ¥13 trillion, or $81.3 billion, while one tally puts it at about $107.3 billion, so the numbers do not agree. It is described as the largest ever, surpassing the space development company that listed in June this year. On the same day on the New York stock market, selling spread amid uncertainty over the outlook for AI development and other factors, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average is reported to have fallen at one point by more than $1,100 from the previous day's close, dropping below $50,000. There is also a report that the S&P 500 fell on the 18th to its lowest level since July. In the morning session in Japan on the 19th, the Nikkei average extended its rise, reportedly supported by expectations for the Anthropic listing.

International Court of Justice Finds Myanmar's Persecution of the Rohingya to Be Genocide

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the UN's principal judicial organ, handed down a judgment on the 19th finding Myanmar's persecution of the Rohingya to be genocide (the killing of a group). The Rohingya are a Muslim minority living in Myanmar, and the judgment was issued in a case between states under the Genocide Convention. It is reported to be the first ruling to find a state responsible for genocide.

The judgment is legally binding on the parties, but the means of enforcing it by force are limited. Some see an effect on the case over Gaza in which South Africa sued Israel. The amount of reparations and concrete measures to prevent recurrence have not been made clear at this point. Myanmar's reaction has not been confirmed either.

Financial Services Agency Orders Rakuten Securities to Submit Report Over Unauthorized Access, Called an Unusual Step

On the 19th, the Financial Services Agency issued an order to submit a report to Rakuten Securities. The measure concerns a leak of customer information through unauthorized access and is described as an unusual step. An order to submit a report is a procedure for making a company report to the authorities on how a problem arose and on measures to prevent recurrence.

Rakuten Securities is hurrying to respond to the leak. It has been reported in some quarters that customer information was kept in a test environment, and experts point out that this is not a common practice. However, the number of records leaked, the type of information and the method of the unauthorized access have not been confirmed at this point.

Upper House Deliberates Consumption Tax Cut Bill While Ruling and Opposition Camps Keep Talking Over Education Minister Seki's Party

In a special committee of the House of Councillors, deliberation continues on the related bill to cut the consumption tax on food. The ruling parties are calling for the bill to be enacted within the current Diet session, while the opposition parties are reported to be stepping up their pursuit over a political fundraising party held by Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology Seki. At the directors' meeting on the 19th, the government side presented related materials, but the opposition side said they were “insufficient,” and it was agreed to keep talking.

Prime Minister Takaichi indicated at a press conference in Shenzhen, China, that the government would approach deliberation of the tax-cut bill “in good faith.” The directors' meeting says it will continue talks, and a conclusion has been carried over to the 20th or later. The specific problems with the party and the vote schedule for the bill have not been given in the reports.
