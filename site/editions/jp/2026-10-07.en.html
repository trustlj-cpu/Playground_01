Nobel in chemistry for Soai.
The discovery that "a tiny bias grows on its own"

Kenso Soai (硤合憲三), 76, professor emeritus at Tokyo University of Science, shares the prize with France's Henri Kagan. The citation: "the discovery of nonlinear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis." He is the 31st Japanese laureate, and the second in chemistry in two years after Susumu Kitagawa last year

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced on Oct. 7 that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry goes to Kenso Soai, professor emeritus at Tokyo University of Science, and Henri Kagan, professor emeritus at Université Paris-Saclay in France. Many molecules exist as mirror-image pairs, like a right hand and a left hand (enantiomers). In 1986 Kagan found the "nonlinear effect," in which the product tilts toward one hand more strongly than the bias in the catalyst would predict. Soai discovered asymmetric autocatalysis (the Soai reaction), in which a molecule made by a reaction becomes the catalyst for making more of itself, amplifying a vanishingly small bias to nearly 100%, and he showed how to reproduce it in the lab.

Soai held a news conference at Tokyo University of Science on the evening of the 7th. He said the call reached him while he was grocery shopping at a supermarket near his home. "I am especially glad to share the prize with the eminent Professor Kagan," he said. "I could not have found it without the staff and the students." Born in Hiroshima Prefecture in 1950, he graduated from the University of Tokyo's Faculty of Science. Outlets reported that the work is expected to find uses in developing drugs, fragrances and sweeteners.

Four big brewers raided.
Did an informal huddle after the "First Thursday Club" set the size of price hikes?

The Japan Fair Trade Commission on Oct. 7 raided Asahi Breweries, Kirin Brewery, Suntory and Sapporo Breweries in a compulsory investigation (a criminal-procedure investigation) on suspicion of violating the Antimonopoly Act through unreasonable restraint of trade. Citing people familiar with the matter, outlets reported that sales managers of the four met informally around the "First Thursday Club," the monthly meeting of the industry group Brewers Association of Japan, and for several years allegedly set targets for the size of increases in shipment prices to wholesalers. The prices at issue are shipment prices for beer-type drinks including happoshu and "third-category" beer, not retail prices themselves. All four made an across-the-board price increase in April 2025. Asahi and Kirin acknowledged being investigated and said they would "cooperate fully." DailyDrop has not confirmed comments from Suntory or Sapporo.

Word of the day
cartelANTITRUST

An agreement among companies that should be competing to fix prices, volumes or the timing of price increases. It counts as "unreasonable restraint of trade" under the Antimonopoly Act and can draw criminal penalties as well as surcharges. Even without fixing prices outright, aligning "targets" for the size of an increase can be a violation. It is the heart of this case.

Okinawa governor protests to the prime minister over Marine's robbery-murder arrest: "Convene the council now"

Okinawa Gov. Genta Koja (古謝玄太) met Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi at the prime minister's office on Oct. 7 to protest the arrest of a U.S. Marine stationed in Okinawa on suspicion of robbery and murder, after a 39-year-old woman was killed and her wallet and other belongings taken at a hotel in Naha. The governor called for an early meeting of the Okinawa Policy Council, which discusses easing the base burden, and for effective measures to prevent a recurrence. The prime minister replied that she would "respond appropriately." Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara sat in. On the 5th, the prime minister had told the Diet the case was "extremely regrettable" and that Japan had "protested strongly to the U.S. side and demanded steps to prevent a recurrence."

The crime occurred before dawn on the 3rd; a 20-year-old lance corporal assigned to Marine Corps Air Station Futenma was arrested before dawn on the 4th. The cause of death was reported as asphyxiation by strangulation. On the 6th the Naha city assembly unanimously passed a protest resolution demanding a fundamental revision of the Japan-U.S. Status of Forces Agreement. A special committee of the prefectural assembly is aiming for a unanimous resolution at its plenary session on the 9th. Within the Liberal Democratic Party, some asked whether "a protest alone is enough."

More than 12 million records in a week. Yakiniku King, GMO, Daiwa Securities, Rakuten Drive disclose breaches one after another

Restaurant operator Monogatari Corp. said on the 5th that unauthorized access to the official app of its "Yakiniku King" chain leaked the names, email addresses and phone numbers of 10,788,963 members, nearly all of its 10,808,784 registered users. Passwords and payment data were not included. GMO Research & AI said on the 6th that up to roughly 940,000 personal records had leaked from a survey site. Daiwa Securities said on the 5th that a system at an outside contractor was breached and about 220,000 records, including account numbers of some 110,000 customers, may have leaked. No intrusion into Daiwa's own systems has been confirmed.

Rakuten Drive disclosed on the 6th that credentials for an administrative account were stolen and that photos and documents in 15,382 accounts were viewable from Jan. 29 to Sept. 17. On Sept. 28, Times Car disclosed a breach of about 6.6 million records. Nikkei Asia framed the string of incidents as a "wave" sweeping companies in Japan and South Korea. All of the companies are still investigating the cause and the route of entry.

10-year Treasury yield touches 5.35%, highest since 2002. FOMC minutes: a further rate hike this year "would likely be appropriate"

The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield rose as high as 5.35% (5.365% on some feeds) in trading on Oct. 7, its highest level since 2002. A $39 billion auction of 10-year notes went smoothly; the high yield of 5.3% was the highest since 2000, but once demand was confirmed the yield eased back to around 5.288%. The 30-year yield briefly hit 5.724%, a 24-year high. Behind it: more attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz, and Brent crude back above $100.

In the FOMC minutes of the Sept. 15–16 meeting, released the same day, many participants judged that a further rate increase by year-end would likely be appropriate. There was little hint of timing. The Fed had raised its policy rate by 0.25 point in September to 3.75–4.00%, its first hike since July 2023. The next meeting is Oct. 27–28. In Japan, the 10-year JGB yield sits around 3.1%, a 30-year high, and the dollar traded in the 158-yen range.
