Shin-Meishin Crash That Killed 6: Ex-Truck Driver Who Looked Away at His Phone Gets 7 Years in Prison, as Prosecutors Sought; Tsu District Court Hands Down the Statutory Maximum

After the ruling, the victims’ families said at a press conference, “Seven years for 6 lives is too light,” and called for a change in the law.

In March this year, on the Shin-Meishin Expressway in Kameyama, Mie Prefecture, a large truck rammed into a line of congested traffic and 6 people died. On the 20th the Tsu District Court sentenced the former truck driver, Mizuyo Mizutani (55), who was charged with negligent driving causing death, to 7 years’ imprisonment, matching the sentencing request, NHK and Jiji Press reported. According to Nikkei, Judge Hiroaki Deguchi said in giving his reasons, “The negligence is grave.” Seven years’ imprisonment is the maximum statutory penalty for negligent driving causing death under the Automobile Driving Punishment Act, and Asahi Shimbun and Mainichi Shimbun also reported the same sentence. At a hearing in September, prosecutors said the defendant had looked at his smartphone to take a screenshot of a TikTok video and had taken his eyes off the road ahead for about 13 seconds. TBS NEWS DIG reported that the defendant was driving at about 82 km/h while looking at his phone. The defendant had admitted the charges.

After the ruling, the victims’ families held a press conference. According to TV Asahi, they said they “cannot accept it” and called for a change in the law. Nikkei reported that the families voiced, in essence, “Six lives were taken and 7 years is too light. Is a maximum necessary?” The defendant’s lawyer said of an appeal that “the defendant is considering it” (Nikkei). In connection with the accident, the Chugoku Transport Bureau has imposed a 30-day business suspension on the transport company the defendant worked for. Distracted driving means looking at a mobile phone screen while driving, and it is also punishable under the Road Traffic Act. Nikkei reported that this ruling falls among the heaviest of past comparable cases.

Education Ministry Asks for a Review of How the University of Tokyo Chose Its Next President, a First; Yuko Fujigaki, Second in the Preference Vote, Was Selected

On the 20th the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology asked the University of Tokyo to review how it selected its next president. According to Asahi Shimbun, the request went to the university’s auditors and its Management Policy Council, and it is the first time the ministry has made such a request of a university. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that in September the university’s President Selection and Oversight Council chose Professor Yuko Fujigaki, who had placed second in the preference vote of faculty and staff, as the next president. Education Minister Yoshihiro Seki said at a press conference after the cabinet meeting, “There are many voices saying that a clear explanation has still not been given, and this is regrettable” (Nikkei, Yomiuri Shimbun). NHK reported that it is unusual for the ministry to ask for a review of the leadership selection at an individual national university. The request letter was issued in the name of the director-general of the Higher Education Bureau and said that fulfilling its accountability is important (Kyodo News). Mainichi Shimbun also reported that the request said, “Fulfill your accountability.”

Nidec Panel Concludes 9 Former Executives, Including Shigenobu Nagamori, Are Liable for Damages; Accounting Fraud Losses Put at 27.6 Billion Yen

Nidec’s Executive Liability Investigation Committee concluded, in a report released on the 20th, that 9 former executives, including founder Shigenobu Nagamori, bear liability for damages to the company. According to Asahi Shimbun, losses from the accounting fraud were put at 27.6 billion yen. Mainichi Shimbun reported that the responsibility of former executives including Mr. Nagamori was recognized, and Nikkei reported that five successive presidents, including former president Mitsuya Kishida, were covered. According to Yomiuri TV, the report pointed out that the company “put achieving earnings targets above all else and, over many years, put off dealing with its negative legacy.” NHK reported that the company will consider legal action. According to Nikkei, the report said the scope of damages to be paid should be determined according to each person’s degree of contribution.

Unauthorized Access at Disaster-Alert Email Firm Arcadia; Data on All About 3,900 Tochigi Prefectural Police Staff May Have Leaked

Mainichi Shimbun reported that in the unauthorized access to Arcadia (Minoh, Osaka Prefecture), a company that delivers disaster information, personal information on all about 3,900 Tochigi Prefectural Police staff who used the company’s service may have leaked. According to Kyodo News, the data at risk of leaking includes names, affiliations and email addresses registered in the safety confirmation system. According to ITmedia NEWS, the company announced that about 10 hours after the intrusion the attacker obtained a configuration file containing a secret key for logins, and it reported its 105 hours of response minute by minute. According to NHK, in response to the string of data leaks, the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP)’s National Cyber Security Strategy Headquarters on the 20th drew up a draft emergency proposal calling for budget and organizational arrangements.

Debate Opens in Lower House Plenary on Bill to Cut Consumption Tax on Food to 1%; Democratic Party for the People Submits Counter-Proposal for a Resident Tax Deduction

A government bill to lower the consumption tax rate on food from 8% to 1% entered deliberation at the lower house plenary session on the 20th. According to Jiji Press, the ruling parties aim to get it through the lower house in early November. Kyodo News reported that Prime Minister Takaichi did not reveal, in the plenary session, the specific funding sources, said to be about 10 trillion yen, or who would receive the cash benefits. Mainichi Shimbun also reported that the specifics of the funding would be an issue. The tax cut would run for only two years, from April 2027.

The Democratic Party for the People submitted a counter bill the same day. According to NHK, it combines a resident tax cut with cash benefits, aiming to ease the burden by up to about 50,000 yen a year per worker. Kyodo News reported that because the government plan’s income-linked benefits would leave out the middle class, the aim is to provide more generous support.
