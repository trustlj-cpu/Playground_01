Binance Puts Impact at Estimated $6.3 Billion, About Half Frozen; Analytics Firms Point to North Korea-Linked Group

The world’s largest crypto exchange has said the impact of the hack is an estimated $6.3 billion and has halted withdrawals. Two private analytics firms point to involvement by a North Korean cyber group.

Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange, said on Oct. 30 that assets affected by what appears to be a hack came to an estimated $6.3 billion (about 1 trillion yen), and halted withdrawals for users. The $6.3 billion is Binance’s own estimate; the amount actually lost has not been determined. About $3 billion of it, roughly half, has reportedly been frozen. If the actual damage is on this scale, it would be the largest theft of crypto assets ever. To cover the losses, Binance explained, it has set aside more than $6.34 billion. CEO Teng said, “Users’ assets are safe,” and set a goal of resuming withdrawals within about 40 hours.

The blockchain analytics firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis pointed to suspected involvement by the North Korean cyber group “TraderTraitor.” Both are analyses by private companies, and no identification by a government has been confirmed. Binance has explained that the culprits used a “self-destruct mechanism” to erase their traces. Bitcoin fell to its lowest since September, and co-founder Zhao pushed back against Elon Musk, who had mocked the incident, saying, “This is no time to joke around.” The actual amount lost, the route of the intrusion and when withdrawals will fully resume have not been confirmed.

U.S. Stocks Close Lower; Intel Plunges More Than 20% as Selling in AI-Related Shares Spreads

On the 30th on the New York stock market, the share price of Intel, the major U.S. semiconductor maker, plunged by more than 20%. In the earnings it released the previous day, operating profit for the July–September quarter had risen to about 3.7 times the level a year earlier, but its earnings outlook for the October–December quarter came in below market expectations. The view that rising memory prices will shrink the PC market is cited as the reason for the worse outlook. The selling did not stop at Intel alone; amid concern about AI investment it spread across semiconductor and tech shares as a whole. The S&P 500 stock index ended October trading down 1.82% at 7,580.07. The Nikkei average fell 2.12% to 63,290.11 yen on the Tokyo market the same day, and how the Tokyo market after the weekend prices in this drop in U.S. stocks is the next focus. Whether Intel’s fall is a temporary reaction or the start of a correction in AI-related shares cannot be confirmed at this point.

Unauthorized Access at Ministry of Foreign Affairs; Personal Data on About 3,600 Records May Have Leaked

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced on Oct. 30 that personal information on about 3,600 records may have leaked through unauthorized access. What may have been exposed includes email addresses and names, and it includes information on about 900 people who are not ministry staff. The unauthorized access took place in July this year, and more than 3 months have passed before the announcement.

Whether there are signs of misuse, and by what route the intrusion was made, have not been confirmed at this point. A combination of a name and an email address is information that can be used in spoofed emails aimed at a specific target. How it came to take more than 3 months from the July intrusion to the announcement has also not been explained in detail.

Foreign Minister Motegi Demands Immediate Release of Hokkaido Shimbun Reporter From Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov; First Formal Talks in 5 Years

Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi, in talks with Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov, demanded the immediate release of a reporter for the Hokkaido Shimbun. It is the first formal meeting between the Japanese and Russian foreign ministers in 5 years.

When, where and for what reason the reporter was detained cannot be confirmed at this point. How the Russian side responded has not been made clear either. Japan-Russia relations have remained chilled since the invasion of Ukraine, and opportunities for the foreign ministers to face each other directly have been limited.

Japan, U.S., South Korea Foreign Ministers Sign Memorandum to Strengthen Arctic Cooperation; Also to Work Together on North Korean Cyber Response

The foreign ministers of the three countries of Japan, the United States and South Korea met on the occasion of the G20 foreign ministers’ meeting held in Atlanta, U.S., and signed a memorandum to strengthen cooperation in the Arctic. The aim is for the three countries to line up on the economic security front in the Arctic, where shrinking ice is raising interest in sea routes and resources. At the meeting they confirmed they would also work together in addressing cyberattacks by North Korea.

North Korean cyber groups have drawn heightened wariness from countries as they earn foreign currency by stealing crypto assets. In the large-scale hack of Binance, revealed on the 30th, too, private analytics firms point to involvement by a North Korea-linked group. The specific items of cooperation in the memorandum cannot be confirmed at this point.
