Nikkei Stock Average Plunges Nearly 5% to the ¥64,000 Range; Hot U.S. Prices Revive Rate-Hike Bets, AI and Chip Stocks Sold

The drop reached ¥3,000 at one point during trading. The Nasdaq's 511-point fall spilled over into Tokyo, and a Bank of Japan Policy Board member said there is no need to be “overly cautious” about raising rates.

On the Tokyo stock market on the 15th, the Nikkei Stock Average closed at ¥64,425.95, down 4.9% (market data). The Japan Times reported a “5% drop,” and the Mainichi Shimbun reported that it fell to the ¥64,000 range for the first time in a month, the third-largest drop on record. The losses widened over the course of trading: the TV Asahi network reported a fall of more than ¥2,000 at one point, the Asahi Shimbun more than ¥2,700, and the Mainichi Shimbun ¥3,000. The trigger was U.S. prices. The U.S. consumer price index came in above forecasts (TV Asahi network), and rate-hike expectations strengthened again (Asahi Shimbun). New York stocks fell for a second day, the Nasdaq dropped 511 points, and tech stocks were sold (NHK and others). Reuters reported that U.S. producer prices for September posted their biggest gain in four and a half years.

The selling centered on AI-related stocks. Reuters reported that shares and bonds of CoreWeave, which runs data centers for AI, fell, and that concern about AI-related debt is growing. Taiwan's TSMC posted a record net profit for the July–September quarter, up 68.7% from a year earlier (Jiji Press), but English-language dispatches said it failed to ease worries about an AI bubble. Bank of Japan Policy Board member Koeda said in a speech on the 15th that there is no need to be “overly cautious” about raising rates (NHK, Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun). NHK reported that the yen's exchange rate rose after these remarks and other factors. Dollar-yen stood at around ¥158.09 in the early hours of the 16th (market data). The results of the U.S. market on the 15th will come out after this paper is published. Whether the main cause of the fall was prices or AI-related worries has not yet been sorted out.

Agriculture Minister Yana Submits Resignation, Effectively Dismissed; Successor Arata Takebe Has No Cabinet Experience; Cabinet Approval Falls to 42.4%

Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries Minister Kazuo Yana submitted his resignation on the 15th, and Prime Minister Takaichi named Arata Takebe as his successor (Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun, Jiji Press). The Mainichi Shimbun explained that it took the form of his “own decision” but was effectively a dismissal, and the Asahi Shimbun likewise reported it as effectively a dismissal. Mr. Takebe has no cabinet experience; his experience as deputy agriculture minister was valued (Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun). The prime minister called him an “immediate asset” (Jiji Press). The opposition is pursuing responsibility for the appointment (Mainichi Shimbun). In Jiji Press's October poll, the cabinet approval rating was 42.4%, down 1.7 points from the previous month, and about 80% saw a problem with the decision to keep Mr. Yana in office (Jiji Press). In his home prefecture of Tochigi, some are calling for him to resign his Diet seat (Mainichi Shimbun). Jiji Press reported that a university professor filed a criminal complaint against Mr. Yana, but other outlets have not confirmed it.

North Korea Test-Fires 2 Types of Submarine-Launched Cruise Missiles, State Media Says; New Bridge Linking China and North Korea Opens

North Korea's state media, the Korean Central News Agency, reported that it carried out launch tests of two types of cruise missiles fired from a submarine (NHK, Mainichi Shimbun, The Japan Times, Reuters). The Mainichi Shimbun reported that North Korea cited “improving naval capabilities.” The date and place of the launches, the distance flown and the assessment of Japan's Defense Ministry could not be confirmed within the range of this paper's collection. Unlike ballistic missiles, cruise missiles use wings to fly at low altitude.

The same day, the 15th, the New Yalu River Bridge linking China and North Korea opened (Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun, Jiji Press, The Japan Times). It is a bridge that stayed closed for more than 10 years after it was completed. Jiji Press reported that expanded trade and travel and improved relations are moving ahead. No report links the two events.

Prime Minister: “No Espionage in Russia.” Detained Hokkaido Shimbun Reporter Cannot Be Reached by Government; Russian Ambassador Rejects Japan's Demand

On the 15th, regarding the detention of a Hokkaido Shimbun reporter in Russia, Prime Minister Takaichi said, “There was no espionage in Russia,” and also denied any government instruction (Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun, Jiji Press, The Japan Times). Jiji Press reported that the government has not been able to make contact with the detained reporter. According to the same Jiji Press report, the Russian ambassador to Japan rejected Japan's demand and said an investigation is under way under Russian law.

The Asahi Shimbun reported that a U.S. journalist protection group called on Russia to release him immediately, and the Mainichi Shimbun reported that experts issued a statement of protest, appealing that “silence will be seen as weakness.” The date of the detention, the substance of the charge and the reporter's health could not be confirmed within the range of this paper's collection.

Customer Data on About 22.51 Million Records May Have Leaked From Sourcenext; Burger King Hit for About 11.18 Million as Unauthorized Access Continues

Sourcenext disclosed that customer data on about 22.51 million records may have leaked (NHK). The Nikkei reported 22.52 million, so the outlets' counts differ by one record. Some reports say the data includes passwords and other items. Burger King announced that member information on about 11.18 million records from its app leaked (NHK, Asahi Shimbun). The president of Lawson apologized for the leak of personal information through unauthorized access (NHK). Systems used by obstetrics and gynecology clinics and others were also accessed without authorization, and up to 800 institutions may be affected (Asahi Shimbun). NHK reported that patient information may have leaked.

Separately, NHK reported that evidence was found that “AI agents” were used in the string of unauthorized accesses, but the detailed method has not been confirmed. JR Kyushu announced that no leak of personal information from unauthorized access has been confirmed (NHK). Bank of Kyoto changed how it verifies account openings in its app after images of driver's licenses were leaked (Asahi Shimbun).
