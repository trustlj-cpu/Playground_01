Nikkei Average Falls 2.78%, Giving Back Part of Previous Day's Surge, Closing in the Upper 50,000-Yen Range

On the 25th, the Tokyo market's losses topped 1,600 yen during trading. The move is reported to follow a sell-off in AI-related stocks in the United States the day before.

On the Tokyo stock market on the 25th, the Nikkei Stock Average (Nikkei average) closed at 57,011.62 yen, down about 1,633 yen from the previous day. The fall of 2.78% gave back part of the 3.91% gain of the previous day, when the index had recovered to 58,644.63 yen. At one point during trading, the loss topped 1,600 yen. The Nikkei Stock Average is an index calculated from the price moves of 225 representative stocks listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange; for someone who had put 1 million yen into a product tracking the Nikkei average, the value would have dropped by about 28,000 yen on this day alone.

The previous day's trend in the U.S. market is cited as the background to the decline. On the 24th in the United States, reports that new tariffs were being prepared sent stocks lower, AI-related stocks were also sold, and the S&P 500, a leading U.S. stock index, closed down 0.5%. At Nvidia, the rise that followed Jensen Huang's return as CEO is also reported to have lost momentum. On the same 25th, South Korea's leading stock index, the KOSPI, also fell 6.16% and closed at 4,697.51. Finer details of the market as a whole, such as how widespread the declining stocks were, have not been confirmed. On the 26th, the U.S. market is closed for Thanksgiving.

Six Opposition Parties Plan to Submit Censure Motion Against Education Minister Seki to Upper House, Over Fundraising Party and Other Issues

Over Education Minister Yoshihiro Seki's political fundraising party, six opposition parties in the House of Councillors agreed on the 24th to submit a censure motion to the upper house, on the grounds that the minister gave explanations to the Diet that differed from the facts and is unfit for the post. A censure motion is a resolution in which the House of Councillors holds a minister responsible, and even if passed it has no legal power to remove the minister. However, passage could make it harder for the opposition to cooperate on deliberating bills, and the opposition side is reported to have hinted at an effect on deliberations. The timing of submission has not been reported. The trigger is reported to be the problem of Seki holding a party at the members' office building in violation of the House of Representatives' internal rules. On the 18th, it reportedly came to light that a party had also been held in March 2024 with a fee of 90,000 yen per person. Seki said responsibility would be fulfilled through the job and denied resigning, it is reported. On the 24th there was also a report that political funds had been used for the rent on a home, but the facts have not been confirmed. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said on the 19th that there was no legal problem and that Seki should explain first, it is reported.

U.S. Federal Jury Convicts Huawei, Guilty of Conspiracy to Steal Trade Secrets and Other Counts; Not Guilty of Organized Crime Conspiracy

A jury in the federal court in Brooklyn, New York, returned a verdict on the 24th finding China's telecom equipment giant Huawei guilty of conspiracy to steal a competitor's trade secrets, bank fraud and other counts. Depending on the report, wire fraud and violations of sanctions concerning Iran are also said to be among the guilty counts, but how far the guilty findings extend and how many there are is described differently from report to report. Meanwhile, the company was found not guilty of organized crime conspiracy. Organized crime conspiracy is one of the most serious charges, asking whether a company operated like a criminal organization.

Sentencing will be handed down at a later date. The timing and the amount of fines are at the reported stage and are not final figures. Huawei is reported to argue that the not-guilty finding on organized crime conspiracy shows its compliance stance was recognized, and to be preparing to contest the guilty portions. Whether it will appeal, and what penalties the sentencing imposes, are the next points of focus.

Nvidia CEO Huang Apologizes to Employees the Day After Return; U.S. Attorney General Avoids Explaining National Security Judgment

Jensen Huang of Nvidia apologized at an all-hands meeting on the 24th, U.S. time, the day after the return as chief executive officer (CEO). Huang is reported to have said that a grave error had been committed and that violating the company's code of conduct is unacceptable, and to have stressed that going forward the board of directors is in the position of holding Huang accountable for Huang's actions. Huang is also said to have spoken of receiving psychotherapy every day, but how this point is worded differs from report to report.

Meanwhile, on the matter of President Trump's explanation that Huang poses no national security risk, Attorney General Blanche, whose office has been conducting the investigation, avoided explaining the basis for the judgment. In Congress, Democratic lawmakers and others are calling on the board and Huang to explain. Huang is reported to have rejected the view that the return came under pressure from Trump, but the share price lost momentum after the previous day's sharp jump. The substance of the national security investigation has not been made public at this point.

U.S. Reported to Be Preparing Tariff Increases on China, EU, Japan, South Korea and Other Major Economies, Following Excess Production Capacity Probe

The U.S. government is set to announce soon a plan to raise tariffs on imports from major economies including China, the European Union (EU), Japan and South Korea, according to people familiar with the matter. The measure is said to rest on the results of an examination of excess production capacity, where other countries' factories can produce more than demand requires. No specific items or rates have been reported, and no formal government announcement has been confirmed.

In the U.S. market on the 24th, stocks fell when the plan came to light, and gold was reported to have been sold as expectations of U.S. rate hikes grew stronger. Japan is reported to be likely among the targets, but how large the impact on Japanese exporters will be cannot be known until the items and rates are announced. With the U.S. market closed on the 26th for Thanksgiving, the next day the market can move is the 27th.
