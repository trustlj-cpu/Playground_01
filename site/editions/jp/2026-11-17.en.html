Trump Says Meeting With North Korea's Kim Jong Un “Might” Happen, Speaking Before Leaving on Asia Trip

On the 16th, as leaving the White House for a trip to Asia, U.S. President Trump said a meeting with North Korea's General Secretary Kim Jong Un is possible. Nothing has been decided, and no reaction from the North Korean side has been reported.

On the 16th, as U.S. President Trump left the White House for a visit to Asia, Trump spoke to reporters and said there is a possibility of meeting Kim Jong Un, North Korea's general secretary. The words stopped at “might,” and no date has been set. Trump was asked at one point whether a meeting, if held, would be in China, and also said whether it happens depends on President Xi Jinping. General secretary is the name of the highest post in North Korea's Workers' Party of Korea. It is reported that, if realized, it could greatly change the itinerary of this trip through the Philippines and China. Meanwhile, neither the place nor the time has been set, and it has not been confirmed whether the North Korean side has responded to the remark.

Trump will attend summits being held in the Philippines and China. In Shenzhen, China, a three-way summit among Trump, Xi and Russian President Putin is scheduled for the 19th. It is said to be the first meeting with all three present, and nuclear disarmament (reducing the number of nuclear weapons or limiting how they are used) is expected to be discussed. A Russian senior official was reported to have said a China-Russia summit would take place on the 18th and that a meeting with Trump is being arranged, but this rests on a single report and could not be confirmed. On the agenda, U.S. accounts differ over whether Ukraine will be taken up, and it cannot be confirmed which is correct. It is reported that Trump said the economy and trade would also come up, but this too rests on a single report. Details such as the time of the meetings are also handled differently from report to report. Whether U.S.-North Korea dialogue will start moving again, or remain only a topic around the Shenzhen three-way summit, is not known at present.

Woman Apparently in Her 20s Stabbed to Death in Nakano, Tokyo; Man Arrested on Suspicion of Attempted Robbery-Murder

On the night of the 16th, a woman who appeared to be in her 20s was stabbed with a blade on a street in Nakano Ward, Tokyo, and was confirmed dead at the hospital she was taken to. The Metropolitan Police Department arrested a man who appears to be in his 30s or 40s on suspicion of attempted robbery-murder and is investigating how the incident came about. Attempted robbery-murder is a charge that applies to a suspected attempt to kill a person in the course of a robbery. The incident took place on the night of the 16th; the woman is said to be in her 20s or so, but neither her name nor her workplace has been released. The man was reported to have been a customer at the shop where the woman worked, and there is also a report that the man said he was “treated coldly.” But this cannot be confirmed as something the Metropolitan Police Department has officially explained, and the motive and the course of events have not been made clear. The woman's name and the type of weapon have also not been confirmed. The scene was reported to be a residential area near Nakano Station, and residents nearby are reported to be voicing unease.

Robert Pattinson Is the Next James Bond; Amazon MGM Announces

On the 16th, Amazon MGM Studios announced that British actor Robert Pattinson (40) will play James Bond, the hero of the popular spy film series “007.” Pattinson will be the seventh Bond, succeeding Daniel Craig, who stepped down after “No Time to Die,” released in 2021. It is the first Bond casting since Amazon took over production, and the new film will be directed by Denis Villeneuve, director of the “Dune” series. Pattinson commented, “Being entrusted with 007 is the honor of a lifetime.”

The new film is the 26th. It is reported that release will not come before 2028, but this rests on a single report and has not been officially confirmed. The start of filming, co-stars and the plot have also not been announced. The names of several actors had been floated over months, but only Pattinson was announced.

Rocket “Vulcan” Fails Just After Launch; 40 Amazon Satellites Lost

The large rocket “Vulcan” of United Launch Alliance (ULA), the joint venture of Boeing and Lockheed Martin, ran into trouble just after being launched from Florida in the United States on the 15th, local time, and failed to deliver the 40 Amazon internet satellites it was carrying to orbit. According to ULA, the anomaly occurred about 10 minutes after liftoff. The cargo it carried (the payload) was lost. It is reported to be the first time a mission as a whole has failed for ULA.

Amazon said, in effect, that it “fell short of expectations.” The cause of the failure has not been made clear. The lost satellites are part of “Amazon Leo,” a satellite network (constellation) in which Amazon plans to line up many satellites in low Earth orbit, in competition with U.S. SpaceX's “Starlink.” It is reported that this was Vulcan's first launch in about nine months, but this rests on a single report. No date has been given for the next launch.

UN Conference on Regulating Lethal Autonomous Weapons Opens in Geneva; U.S. and Russia Reportedly Have Resisted

The Seventh Review Conference of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) has been under way since the 16th in Geneva, Switzerland. The session is reported to run five days, through the 20th. This review conference is a meeting at which member states gather to review how a treaty is operating. One of the agenda items is how to treat “lethal autonomous weapons systems,” which can select and attack targets without a person judging each attack, and several European capitals, including France, are reported to want to adopt a document for regulation. The convention is an international treaty that bans or restricts the use of conventional weapons that cause excessive harm.

Russia and the United States, meanwhile, are reported to have resisted moves toward regulation, but this rests on a single report. The European moves also come from the same report, and which positions countries have formally taken has not been confirmed. As military use of artificial intelligence (AI) advances, delegates will discuss what limits to place on weapons under development. A conclusion will come only after the session ends on the 20th, and whether agreement will be reached is not known at present.
