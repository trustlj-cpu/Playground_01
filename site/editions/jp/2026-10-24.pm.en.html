Typhoon No. 30 Stalls Over the Sakishima Islands, Expected to Approach Kyushu Around the 26th; One Dead in Taiwan, Typhoon No. 31 Also Forms

The large, strong Typhoon No. 30 was almost stalled on the 24th with the Sakishima Islands of Okinawa Prefecture inside its storm zone, and outlets reported it is expected to approach Kyushu around the 26th.

NHK reported on the morning of the 24th that the large, strong Typhoon No. 30 was almost stalled with the Sakishima Islands of Okinawa Prefecture inside its storm zone, and urged strict vigilance against violent winds, high waves, storm surge, landslides and flooding of low-lying land. NHK reported that two people were injured in Okinawa on the night of the 23rd, and that on the morning of the 24th the Japan Meteorological Agency issued a “linear rainband imminent forecast” for the Ishigaki Island area. On Taiwan, NHK reported that one person died and 350 were injured when strong winds knocked down street trees and more, and that schools in Taipei and elsewhere were still closed on the 24th.

TBS NEWS DIG reported that, with the effects of Typhoon No. 30 and the autumn rain front, western and eastern Japan will also see long-lasting rain, with a risk of warning-level heavy rain over a wide area. FNN Prime Online reported that No. 30 could come very close to Kyushu and make landfall on the 26th, then turn into an extratropical cyclone and approach the Kanto region on the 27th. tenki.jp reported that Typhoon No. 31 (Infa) formed on the 24th and, together with No. 30, made a “double typhoon,” and NHK also reported that No. 31 formed on the sea far to the southeast of Japan and urged people to watch for further information. The track of No. 31 and its effect on Japan are not given in what has been reported.

North Korea’s Kim Jong Un Enters Dandong, China, South Korean Media Say; Possibly Crossed Newly Opened Bridge, First Visit Since Last September

NHK, citing reports by multiple outlets including South Korea’s Yonhap News Agency, reported that North Korean General Secretary Kim Jong Un arrived on the 24th in Dandong, Liaoning Province, in northeastern China. According to NHK, it is Kim’s first visit to China since he attended the military parade held in Beijing in September 2025. Mainichi Shimbun reported that he is believed to have used the newly opened bridge, and Yomiuri Shimbun, citing Yonhap, reported that he is believed to have entered China via the “New Yalu River Bridge” linking China and North Korea. Jiji Press reported “North Korea’s Kim Jong Un visits China.” In a separate article, NHK reported that Kim spoke at a military rally and said “Let us build the strongest army and accelerate the military revolution,” again showing his stance of strengthening military power, but did not mention South Korea, with which tensions are deepening. China’s official announcement, the purpose of the visit, and whether he met President Xi Jinping have not been confirmed in what has been reported.

KDDI Says “No Violation of Law” in Mongolian Grassland Fire; Authorities Weigh Damages Claim, 80% of Reserve Damaged, Sankei Reports

On the 24th, Jiji Press reported that Mongolian authorities are considering a damages claim over the large grassland fire that broke out in a nature reserve in eastern Mongolia. According to the agency, KDDI explains that there was “no violation of law.” Sankei News reported that a vehicle of a KDDI subsidiary was the source and that 80% of the reserve was damaged. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that the fire is believed to have started from a convoy of KDDI subsidiary vehicles and that the Mongolian side intends to seek compensation.

NHK reported that the fire broke out on October 8 and that KDDI has made public the circumstances leading to it. According to NHK, the published account says that employees of the subsidiary and others were traveling inside the reserve with Mongolian authorities when it happened. The exact cause of the ignition, an official figure for the area damaged, and the amount of damages have not been confirmed in what has been reported.

U.S. Exempts Some Items From 50% Tariff on Canada, Including Packaging Materials and Seaweed; Republican Candidates in Midterm Swing States Had Asked

NHK reported that the Trump administration announced it will remove packaging materials, seaweed and other items from the targets of the 50% additional tariff it imposes on certain imports from Canada. According to NHK, the measure responded to a request from Republican candidates reported to be in tight races in the November midterm elections, and Jiji Press reported “Partial repeal” and “Boosting candidates in the midterms.”

Mainichi Shimbun reported that some agriculture-related goods were exempted, and put “Aimed at the midterms?” in its headline. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that the Trump administration accepted requests from candidates in battleground states and reviewed the scope of the “50% tariff,” and that discontent over high prices is growing. The full list of exempted items and the date the change takes effect have not been confirmed in what has been reported.
