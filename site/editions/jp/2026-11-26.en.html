Mayor of Awa, Tokushima, Arrested on Fraud Suspicion Signals Intent to Resign; Letter of Resignation Reported Likely This Week

The mayor, arrested over suspected swindling in a cable television construction project ordered by the city, was reported to have signaled an intent to resign through a lawyer acting as representative

It was reported on the 25th, through an interview with the mayor's lawyer acting as representative, that Mayor Hisato Machida of Awa, Tokushima Prefecture, has indicated an intention to resign. A letter of resignation is expected to be submitted as early as this week. On the 10th of this month, Machida was arrested by Tokushima prefectural police on suspicion of swindling the city out of ¥3.5 million over equipment replacement work related to cable television that the city had ordered. The construction price was ¥74.25 million, and the suspicion is that the repair cost for the mayor's own traffic accident was added on top and billed through the contractor, who was made to transfer ¥3.5 million. In law, fraud is the crime of deceiving a person into handing over money or goods, and the question is whether the city, as an organization, was deceived. The suspicion that the city's money paid for repairs to an individual's accident strikes directly at trust in city government. As head of Awa, the mayor holds ultimate charge of executing the city budget, and the fact that this top official is said to have been personally involved in padding the construction price is what gives the case its gravity.

Two people arrested together with the mayor, a city employee and a company employee, had been released by the 13th. Two city employees being investigated without being held in custody are reported to be explaining, in essence, that they “could not refuse when the mayor asked.” On the 25th it was reported that the mayor gave a statement admitting the fraud suspicion and said that the repair cost could not be covered by insurance. However, the details of the statement have not been confirmed at this point. If the resignation takes effect, an election to choose a successor would follow, but the date has not been set. When the letter will be submitted and accepted, and when prosecutors will decide whether to bring an indictment, have not yet been indicated. City government may remain practically hard to move until the resignation takes effect, and the form of management by the deputy mayor and others to soften the impact on residents also still needs to be confirmed.

Solomon Islands Signs New Treaty With Australia Including Security, Reported Not to Renew Security Agreement With China

The Solomon Islands in the South Pacific signed a comprehensive new treaty with Australia on the 25th, including the area of security. As China strengthens its influence in the South Pacific, the Solomon side showed that it puts weight on security cooperation with Australia. The Solomons signed a security agreement with China in 2022, and its handling has been the focus. On the 25th it was reported that the agreement will not be renewed, but this rests on a single report, and no confirmation from either government or from the Chinese side has been shown. The new treaty had been reported in September to have been broadly agreed by the two countries. The Solomon prime minister indicated in June an intention to review the agreement with China. But the detailed provisions of the new treaty, the scale of support Australia will offer and China's reaction were not sufficiently clear at the time of signing. The procedures and timing until the treaty enters into force are also matters to confirm.

Man, 70, Stabbed to Death at Bicycle Parking Lot of JR Station in Nishi Ward, Fukuoka; Police Search for Man Who Fled

On the afternoon of the 25th, a 70-year-old man who worked at the bicycle parking lot of a JR station in Nishi Ward, Fukuoka, was stabbed and died. Something that looks like a bladed weapon was found near the lot. Security camera footage and other material show that the man who stabbed him fled through a commercial facility near the scene, and customers and others inside the facility in broad daylight evacuated, leaving the scene briefly in uproar. Police are searching for the man who fled. The movement of customers at the commercial facility near the scene was briefly disrupted by the incident.

It was also reported that the man who stabbed him may have stayed near the commercial facility for about 20 minutes after the incident, but this rests on a single report and has not been confirmed in a police announcement. The man's identity, his relationship with the victim and his motive are all unknown. The man was said not to have been found as of the reports on the 25th.

In Last Year's Killing of 84-Year-Old Woman in Shobara, Hiroshima, Company Employee, 55, Arrested on Suspicion of Robbery-Murder

In the case of an 84-year-old woman who lived alone, killed last year at a home in Shobara, Hiroshima Prefecture, police on the night of the 24th arrested a 55-year-old company employee on suspicion of robbery-murder and other offenses. Robbery-murder is a grave crime charged when a person is killed for the purpose of taking money or goods.

It was reported, from interviews with investigative sources, that the arrested man formerly traveled around the area including the woman's home in a delivery job. It is therefore said that he may have known the geography well. However, this rests on a single report, and police have not yet disclosed the content of questioning or whether the suspect admits the charge. About a year passed from the incident to the arrest, and the decisive evidence has not been shown. In what circumstances the woman was found, and what is said to have been taken, are also unclear as far as reported.

Niigata Summary Court Judge Removed From Office, Diet's Impeachment Court Rules; Ninth Since the War

The impeachment court set up in the Diet handed down a ruling on the 25th of removal from office for a judge of Niigata Summary Court. It applies to judges whose convictions have become final, and this is the ninth removal since the war. The court judged that the judge should be dismissed, saying the conduct “shocked society and damaged trust in the judiciary.” The impeachment trial is a system defined in the Constitution under which, when a judge commits grave misconduct, members of the Diet sit as judges and decide whether to remove the judge.

The offense of which the judge was convicted differs between reports: some reported embezzlement of shared service fees at public servant housing, while others reported that a conviction for a gambling offense had become final. The offenses are based on the respective final judgments, and there may be more than one offense, but they could not be sorted out as far as can be confirmed. For the removed judge, the handling of matters such as qualification as a legal professional will also be a future focus. A ruling of the impeachment court is a final judgment, and no procedure for appeal is provided as in ordinary trials.
