First-Ever Cancellation of an Earthquake Early Warning Alert: M6.9 Near Kamchatka Overestimated as “M9.9 in the Kuril Islands,” Japan Meteorological Agency Apologizes

At midday on the 16th, an alert forecasting shaking of about intensity 7 from Hokkaido to the Tokai region was issued, then cancelled 8 minutes later. The agency denied a cyberattack.

The Japan Meteorological Agency issued an Earthquake Early Warning alert at around 12:40 p.m. on the 16th, saying strong shaking was possible from Hokkaido to the Tokai region (Nihon Keizai Shimbun, Jiji Press). The warning had forecast an earthquake of magnitude (M) 9.9 in the Kuril Islands with a maximum seismic intensity of about 7. In fact, an earthquake of M6.9 (preliminary figure) had occurred near the Kamchatka Peninsula at around 12:38 p.m., and it registered only intensity 1 in Hakodate, Hokkaido (Jiji Press, Hokkaido Shimbun). The agency issued a cancellation notice at 12:48 p.m., 8 minutes later. According to Nikkei Cross Tech, the cancellation was done manually, and outlets reported it was the first cancellation of a warning since the system began operating. A senior agency official in charge apologized at a press conference, saying, “We deeply apologize” (Jiji Press). According to TBS, the agency explained at the press conference that “there is no possibility that we were hit by a cyberattack.”

On the cause, the agency explained that it had overestimated the magnitude (Nihon Keizai Shimbun, ITmedia NEWS). Officials cited the fact that a domestic magnitude formula had been applied to an earthquake far from Japan, and that a different point closer to Japan had been used as the epicenter in forecasting intensity. Hokkaido Shimbun reported that there are few observation stations near the Kamchatka Peninsula. Saying no unauthorized access from outside had been confirmed, the agency denied the possibility of a cyberattack and said, “It is not hardware. We think it is a problem of (information) processing” (ITmedia NEWS). According to Jiji Press, operations on the Tokaido Shinkansen between Shin-Yokohama and Toyohashi were temporarily suspended and a streetcar stopped in Sapporo, but JR Central said at night that the Earthquake Early Warning was not the direct cause. The agency says it is investigating the detailed reasons and will announce them once known. The entertainer Akiko Wada commented on the alert, “It must not be that it differs depending on the model of device” (Sponichi).

Data Leak Announcements Keep Coming: Sourcenext Up to 22.51 Million Records, Yamato Transport 5.31 Million, Nikkei 1.83 Million

Sourcenext announced on the 15th that the system managing its member information had been hit by unauthorized access and that the customer information of 22,519,960 records may have leaked (NHK, INTERNET Watch). It includes names, addresses, email addresses and passwords; credit card information is reportedly not covered. Yamato Transport announced on the 16th that about 5.31 million records in total had been taken through unauthorized access to its “Kuroneko deferred-payment service” (ITmedia Business Online, Jiji Press). About 5.26 million of them contain names, addresses and itemized purchases; card information and passwords are not included, and no secondary harm has been confirmed. Nikkei Inc. also announced on the 16th that authentication credentials for a cloud service used in development had been stolen and about 1.83 million records leaked (NHK, ITmedia NEWS). About 150,000 contain names and similar data, while the rest are identifiers that cannot identify individuals (INTERNET Watch).

At Upper House Budget Committee, PM Takaichi Apologizes for Agriculture Minister's Dismissal: “I Take My Responsibility for the Appointment Seriously”; CDP Demands Yanagi Be Summoned as a Reference Witness

The House of Councillors Budget Committee on the 16th began two days of general questioning with the prime minister and all cabinet ministers present. Prime Minister Takaichi apologized that former Agriculture Minister Kazuo Yanagi had left office while the Diet was in session, saying, “I take my responsibility for the appointment seriously” (Jiji Press, Minami-Nippon Shimbun). Over the free rides for supporters, which had been flagged as a possible violation of the Public Offices Election Act, Mr. Yanagi admitted, “I was unable to give an answer that people could accept.” Kiyomi Tsujimoto of the Constitutional Democratic Party called for Mr. Yanagi to be summoned to the Budget Committee as a reference witness and pressed the prime minister over her handling (Shimotsuke Shimbun, KSB Setonaikai Broadcasting). The prime minister countered, “It is not that I was shielding him. I thought he should fulfill his duty to explain, and I urged him to do so.” Yosuke Tsuruho of the Liberal Democratic Party also urged the prime minister to take seriously her responsibility for the appointment (Jiji Press). Arata Takebe has been named the successor as agriculture minister (Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun). On the 9th, in the House of Councillors plenary session, the prime minister had indicated that Mr. Yanagi would stay on (Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun).

Cabinet Approves Police Duties Execution Act Amendment Introducing “Remote Analysis” to Prevent Harm in Advance From Tokuryu Smartphone Communications

The government on the 16th adopted by Cabinet decision a bill to amend the Police Duties Execution Act that would create “remote analysis,” under which police remotely access the communication devices of criminal groups and analyze the information (Jiji Press, Chunichi Shimbun). It is a countermeasure to the difficulty of detecting, at the planning stage, anonymous and fluid criminal groups (Tokuryu) that give instructions through highly secretive messaging apps; an expert panel of the National Police Agency had recommended its introduction in September. According to Jiji Press, it would apply only to organized serious crimes in which a specific crime, such as special-fraud phone calls, has already occurred and harm may spread, and only when information is hard to gather by other means. A warrant from a judge must be obtained in advance; unrelated information is deleted, the target is notified afterward, and a report to the Public Safety Commission is also mandatory (Chunichi Shimbun). Nihon Keizai Shimbun put the damage from Tokuryu at around 500 billion yen. The government aims to pass the bill in the current Diet session, and if enacted it will take effect within 6 months of promulgation (Jiji Press).

“Gyoza no Ohsho” President Shooting: Kudo-kai-Linked Senior Member Acquitted; Kyoto District Court Cites “Reasonable Doubt That He Was the Culprit”

In the case of the December 2013 shooting death of Takayuki Ohigashi, then president of Ohsho Food Service, which operates “Gyoza no Ohsho,” the verdict hearing for Yukio Tanaka (59), a senior gang member linked to the Kudo-kai, a designated especially dangerous crime syndicate, who was charged with murder and other crimes, was held at the Kyoto District Court on the 16th, and presiding judge Atsushi Nishikawa handed down a not-guilty verdict (Nihon Keizai Shimbun, Kansai TV). The sentencing request was life imprisonment. There was no direct evidence, and prosecutors had piled up circumstantial evidence, including a DNA profile matching the defendant found on a cigarette butt near the scene (Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun). The judgment held that none of the facts it could find was one that could be reasonably explained only if the defendant was the culprit (Nihon Keizai Shimbun). Through his lawyer, the defendant issued a comment to the effect that his life changed the moment he was suspected of being the culprit. Ohsho Food Service declined to comment, saying the trial may continue (Nihon Keizai Shimbun).
