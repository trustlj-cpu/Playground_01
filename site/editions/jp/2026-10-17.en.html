Earthquake Early Warning Cancelled for the First Time; Magnitude Overestimated as 9.9, Meteorological Agency Apologizes

The Japan Meteorological Agency cancelled a warning issued across a wide area from Hokkaido to the Tokai region. A cyberattack was ruled out, and the cause is still being investigated.

By the night of the 16th, it was being reported one after another that the Japan Meteorological Agency had cancelled an Earthquake Early Warning alert it had issued. The Mainichi Shimbun called it the “first cancellation of a warning” and reported that the agency had overestimated the magnitude of the quake as 9.9. The area covered was said to be wide, stretching from Hokkaido to the Tokai region, and the agency has apologized.

The central question is the cause. Multiple reports say the agency denied a “cyberattack,” but why such an off-the-charts figure was produced is under investigation, and it has not been disclosed at which stage of the system the problem arose. We will have to wait for analysis by experts and the agency's final explanation.

By Jev's tally, 17 articles covered the matter, with headlines framed around angles such as “no cyberattack” and “wide area from Hokkaido to Tokai.” The volume of coverage is large, but no new facts have been added; the core comes down to three points: the cancellation, the apology and the ongoing investigation into the cause.

The Earthquake Early Warning is a system that prompts people to take protective action before strong shaking arrives. A magnitude of 9.9 is a scale without precedent in recorded observation, and a figure at which a giant tsunami would also be expected if it actually happened. For that reason, a wrong estimate not only causes public anxiety but could undermine trust in the next real warning. How users, transport operators and local governments acted between the warning and its cancellation could not be confirmed within the range of this paper's collection.

There are three points readers should keep in mind. First, the cancellation is a correction of an erroneous estimate, and the material collected for this paper includes no report that a huge earthquake actually occurred. Second, because the cause is unresolved, the substance of any measures to prevent a recurrence is not yet visible. Third, the cyberattack theory has been denied by the agency, and baseless assertions should be avoided. Going forward, the focus will be on when the results of the investigation are published and on a review of how those who received the warning responded. It is also worth noting that the longer the agency's explanation is delayed, the more readily speculation spreads. In situations like this, it is all the more important to treat official announcements and unconfirmed information separately.

Personal Information Leak Announcements Pile Up: Sourcenext Up to 22.52 Million Records, Yamato 5.3 Million

Announcements of large-scale leaks overlapped on the 16th. According to ITmedia NEWS and others, Sourcenext said member information on up to 22.52 million records leaked, including passwords, and the cause was reported to be unauthorized use of an API. Yamato Transport announced about 5.3 million records tied to users of its “Kuroneko Pay-Later Service” (Yomiuri Shimbun), and Nikkei Inc. announced about 1.83 million records from unauthorized access to a cloud service it uses for development (Nikkei). Times Car, after a leak of about 6.6 million accounts' worth of data, plans to delete all the driver's license images it had kept for seven years after members left (ITmedia NEWS). Meanwhile, NHK reported that Izumi had been told that no information leaked. The figures all rest on the companies' own announcements, with no third-party verification, and the same person may be counted in more than one tally. People who reuse passwords should change them as a precaution. In particular, in the Sourcenext case, where member passwords are said to be included, people who use the same password on other services risk secondary damage, so action on the user's side is essential.

First Budget Committee Session After Agriculture Minister Swap: Prime Minister Takaichi, Pressed on Responsibility for the Appointment, Apologizes for Dismissal

In the House of Councillors, where the ruling parties are in the minority, the first Budget Committee session since the change of agriculture ministers was held on the 16th, and the opposition pressed Prime Minister Takaichi on responsibility for the appointment (NHK). The Shimotsuke Shimbun reported in a news flash that the prime minister apologized for the dismissal. The Constitutional Democratic Party is demanding a witness summons for Mr. Yana, who was dismissed. Why it came to a dismissal could not be confirmed within the range of this paper's collection.

Agriculture Minister Takebe said of the talks on lifting the ban on imports of fresh U.S.-grown potatoes, “Unless an agreement is found that both Japan and the United States can accept, the ban will not be lifted” (Nikkei). The Mainichi Shimbun also reported that the prime minister is considering premium vouchers to support the restaurant industry alongside a consumption tax cut, but this is a single-outlet report, not a formal government announcement. The Budget Committee debate could also affect upcoming bill deliberations under a minority ruling bloc. Whether the circumstances of the dismissal and the prime minister's explanation are sufficient will depend on the opposition's pursuit and on public reaction, and is likely to be a focus of Diet management from next week.

Cabinet Approves Police Duties Act Revision Bill: “Remote Analysis” of Smartphone Communications Targets Tokuryu Crime

On the 16th, the government adopted, in a Cabinet decision, a bill to revise the Police Duties Execution Act that would introduce “remote analysis” — grasping the contents of smartphone communications. The aim is to prevent special fraud and robberies by anonymous, fluid criminal groups (Tokuryu), and the Nikkei reports that the goal is passage in the current Diet session. The Nikkei put the damage at the ¥500 billion scale.

Because the power reaches into the contents of communications, how it squares with the secrecy of communications and with privacy is likely to be an issue in Diet deliberations. Details such as at what stage, and with whose permission, analysis could be carried out cannot be read from this paper's collection. In addition, according to NHK, the government is also considering legal revisions to protect individuals' sensitive data as part of strengthening economic security. The government cites the seriousness of the damage, but calls for careful debate on expanding investigators' powers are also expected. If passed, it would affect businesses that handle communications and their users, so the details of the provisions matter.

Defendant Acquitted in Shooting Death of Gyoza no Ohsho President, Kyoto District Court Rules

In the case in which the then president of Ohsho Food Service, which operates “Gyoza no Ohsho,” was shot to death in 2013, presiding judge Atsushi Nishikawa of the Kyoto District Court handed down an acquittal at the verdict hearing on the 16th to Yukio Tanaka (59), a senior yakuza member accused of murder and other charges (Nikkei). The sentence sought was life imprisonment.

More than 10 years have passed since the incident, and coverage before the verdict had focused on whether it would be “life imprisonment or acquittal.” The detailed reasons for the verdict, and whether prosecutors will appeal, could not be confirmed within the range of this paper's collection. Meanwhile, in the 2013 case, a long time has passed for the victim's side and the company, and the verdict has a major effect on those involved. Attention will now turn to the prosecution's decision and to the path toward uncovering the truth.
