Arara Email Delivery Service Hit by Unauthorized Access, About 77 Million Email Addresses Leaked; Up to 7.13 Million Suspicious Emails Posing as Card Companies

Unauthorized access to the business email delivery service “Arara Message” leaked about 77 million email addresses. Suspicious emails posing as card companies number up to about 7.13 million.

Arara, the company that runs the service, revealed on Nov. 2 that about 77 million email addresses had leaked outside through the unauthorized access. It confirmed that names, phone numbers, addresses and other data also leaked, but said it does not hold payment information, so none was exposed. The 77 million figure is the sum of the counts for each client company and may include duplicates. From Oct. 28 to 30, up to about 7.13 million suspicious emails posing as credit card companies were sent from two environments of the service. Their subject lines included “Request to confirm your usage details.” Arara grasped the situation on the 31st, and on the same day stopped providing the service and reported it to the police.

Client companies have begun to be affected. On the 3rd, Osaka City announced that the email addresses and names of users of its online administrative system may have leaked. Arara believes that, besides a vulnerability (a weakness in a system) in a web server, problems in the system environment are also involved, and is investigating the cause. The number of people who actually received the suspicious emails, and whether the leaked information was misused, have not been confirmed.

North Korea Tests Cluster Warhead for Cruise Missile, Claims It Can Devastate 5–6 Hectares; Says Four Types to Be Put to Use This Year

North Korea’s Missile Administration announced on Nov. 2 that it had conducted a test to check the power of a cluster warhead to be mounted on a strategic cruise missile and a test to check the characteristics of a vertical-attack flight. A cluster warhead is a weapon that scatters many small bombs from a single munition. The unit that researches warheads claimed that it can devastate a target area of 5–6 hectares at extremely high density. 5–6 hectares is about the size of seven to eight soccer fields. The head of the Missile Administration divided cluster warheads into four types by use and set out a plan to let units choose and use all of the types this year according to the aim of an operation and the target. North Korea also said it tested a cluster warhead for a ballistic missile on Oct. 31, claiming it can scorch 12–13 hectares. All of the figures are North Korea’s own assessment, and no independent verification has emerged. The actual flight distance and launch site have not been disclosed.

Russian Forces Bomb School and Shelter in Southern Ukraine, Killing 26; Site Was an Aid Distribution Hub

Russian forces attacked a school in Komyshuvakha, in Ukraine’s southern Zaporizhzhia Oblast, and a nearby shelter with aerial bombs on Nov. 2, killing at least 26 people and wounding five, according to the emergency service. The school was being used as a distribution hub for humanitarian aid, and the dead include two paramedics and four police officers. President Zelensky explained that after the attack on the school, nine guided bombs were used against residents who had fled into the shelter.

Zelensky said the attack came while a local ceasefire for humanitarian aid was moving ahead in Oleshky, Kherson Oblast, and condemned it as proof that Russia “does not want true justice, either.” The method of repeatedly targeting people who have taken shelter is called a “double tap.” The casualty figures may change. Russia’s account could not be confirmed.

Asian Cup Soccer Postponed to December Next Year; AFC Announces Move Over Worsening Situation in Saudi Arabia, New Dates Start Dec. 23

The Asian Football Confederation (AFC) announced on Nov. 3 that the Asian Cup, which had been due to open in Saudi Arabia in January next year, will be postponed and held from Dec. 23 next year to Jan. 21, 2028. The reason is a worsening situation in the host country, and the AFC cited difficulties in travel and operations accompanying developments in the region. Twenty-four teams take part in the tournament. It had originally been scheduled to open in Riyadh on Jan. 7 next year and run through Feb. 5.

Doubts had been voiced about hosting after attacks by Yemen’s armed group, the Houthis, but the tournament organizing committee had published the match schedule on Oct. 27, saying preparations were proceeding as planned. The policy changed in about a week. The effect on the Japan national team’s schedule and the handling of the group draw have not been made clear.

Ukraine–Russia Working-Level Talks Set for Nov. 10–11 in Abu Dhabi; U.S. Envoy Announces It Will Broker Them

U.S. special envoy Witkoff said on Nov. 2 that the United States will broker working-level talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, on Nov. 10 and 11. The talks are to cover the security framework after the war ends, measures to ease tensions, humanitarian aid and the next steps toward peace. The U.S. negotiating team will also meet representatives of France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the EU and NATO in Brussels this week.

The United States, Russia and Ukraine also held senior-level talks in Abu Dhabi in January, but the gaps on the territorial issue were not closed. In the talks so far, Russia’s demand that Ukrainian forces withdraw from about one-fifth of Donetsk Oblast and whether to deploy an international force after a ceasefire remain as points of contention. The makeup of the participants in these talks has not been shown in detail so far.
