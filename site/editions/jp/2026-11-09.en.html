Families of Abduction Victims Hold Large Rally in Tokyo; Prime Minister Takaichi Voices Eagerness for Japan–North Korea Summit

Families and supporters of people abducted by North Korea held a rally in Tokyo on the 8th. Prime Minister Takaichi said, “As prime minister of Japan, I will meet Kim Jong Un and talk frankly,” showing eagerness to hold a summit.

Families and supporters of Japanese abducted by North Korea held a large rally in Tokyo on the 8th. The rally called for the earliest possible return of every victim, and Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi also attended. At the venue, a draft resolution calling for the all-at-once return of all the victims was adopted. All-at-once return means bringing everyone home at the same time, rather than sending them back one at a time in stages. Takuya Yokota, representative of the families' association, appealed that holding a summit is the only means of bringing everyone home immediately and all at once. Voices from the families call for a resolution while the parents' generation is still alive. The government recognizes 17 people as abduction victims; 5 returned in 2002, but the remaining 12 have not returned home.

In remarks at the rally, the prime minister said it caused great anguish that the victims have yet to return home, and declared, “I will open a breakthrough by all means in my time and resolve the abduction issue.” The prime minister then showed strong eagerness for a Japan–North Korea summit, saying, “As prime minister of Japan, I will meet Kim Jong Un and talk frankly,” and stressed that outreach to North Korea continues. No summit between the two countries has been held since 2004, when then–Prime Minister Junichiro Koizumi and General Secretary Kim Jong Il held one. At a rally on the same theme last November, the prime minister disclosed having sounded out North Korea on a meeting with General Secretary Kim Jong Un, and at the rally this May said that all options, including a summit, are not ruled out. This time, no date for a meeting or response from North Korea has been indicated, and the prospects for realization are still unclear.

Airstrike on Humanitarian Aid Hub in Southern Ukraine Raises Death Toll to 55

On the 2nd, a school that served as a hub for distributing humanitarian aid supplies in the village of Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia Oblast, in southern Ukraine was hit by a Russian airstrike. Local authorities announced on the 7th that the death toll had risen to 55. It had been given as 31 as of the 3rd, so the figure has nearly doubled in a few days. President Zelensky has said Russian forces first bombed the school where residents receiving aid were, then dropped nine bombs on a place where people who had taken shelter and rescue workers were. Local authorities say there were at least nine airstrikes and that large 3-ton bombs, among others, were used. The Main Intelligence Directorate of the Ukrainian military announced that it had identified the Russian colonel who planned the attack and six pilots of Su-34 fighter-bombers that dropped the bombs. Recovery of bodies from under the rubble continues, and the number of victims may change further. The Russian side's account could not be confirmed this time.

Fire at Bridgestone's Tosu Plant; Employees Evacuate, Fire Extinguished About 3.5 Hours Later

On the night of the 8th, a fire broke out at the Tosu plant, in Tosu, Saga Prefecture, of the tire maker Bridgestone. Employees and others evacuated, and the fire was almost put out about 3.5 hours later. So far, no information on injuries has come in. There is information that the fire started near the tire vulcanization process, but this has not been officially confirmed. Vulcanization is a process in which sulfur is added to raw rubber and heated to give it elasticity and strength.

The Tosu plant began operating in 1970 and specializes in making radial tires for passenger cars (according to the company's published materials). The cause of the fire, the extent of the burned area, and the effect on plant operations and tire shipments cannot be confirmed at this time.

Food Consumption Tax Cut Bill Heads to House of Councillors After Lower House Passage; LDP Policy Research Council Chair Kobayashi Urges “Deliberations That Give a Clear Picture of the System”

The related bill to lower the consumption tax on food passed the House of Representatives last week by majority vote, with the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) and Nippon Ishin no Kai, among others, in favor, and was sent to the House of Councillors. It is the biggest focus of the current Diet session. LDP Policy Research Council chair Takayuki Kobayashi said on the 8th that debate in the House of Councillors should be the kind that gives a clear picture of the system.

The government's plan would lower the tax rate on food and beverages from the current 8% to 1%, for two years starting in April 2027. The opposition points out that the explanation of the funding source is vague, and how to show the funding source will be a point at issue in the House of Councillors. The schedule of deliberations in the House of Councillors could not be confirmed this time.

Attacks on Energy Facilities Continue in Eastern Ukraine; More Than 600,000 People Without Power

In Ukraine, where Russian attacks targeting energy facilities continue, more than 600,000 people in eastern oblasts are without electricity. On the 8th, President Zelensky held a meeting to review responses in each region and took up Dnipropetrovsk Oblast first. In that oblast, following the day's attacks, restoration work continues in Dnipro, Pavlohrad, Kamianske and other places.

On the same day, the president also consulted with the heads of the administrations of all oblasts and indicated that the needs of regions and municipalities near the front line and the border would be given top priority. When the outages will end has not been confirmed. The number of people without power changes from moment to moment with attacks and repairs.
