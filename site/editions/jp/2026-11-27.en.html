Birthdates of Over 700,000 Members Leak at Shueisha, 250,000 More at Shikoku Electric Power, as Unauthorized Access to External Services Continues

Shueisha announced on the 26th that part of the member information from its sales site had leaked. Shikoku Electric Power disclosed a new leak of about 250,000 records, and unauthorized access was found at the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology as well

On Thursday the 26th, Shueisha announced that part of the member information of the Jump Characters Store, which sells character merchandise, had leaked to the outside. What leaked included 707,852 birthdates, and names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, passwords and credit card information are said not to be included. The cause was unauthorized access to the external service “KARTE” (run by Plaid), which had been used for email delivery and other purposes. Shueisha was contacted by Plaid on Friday the 13th and received a report on the scope of the leak on Tuesday the 24th. “KARTE” is a business service for sending customers emails and app notifications and analyzing behavior on a site, and many companies use the same mechanism. Also on the 26th, Shikoku Electric Power announced that about 250,000 records of withdrawn members and others had newly leaked from its membership service “Yonden Concierge.”

Shikoku Electric Power disclosed a leak of about 600,000 records on Monday the 16th, so adding this batch comes to about 850,000. What leaked includes the names, email addresses, addresses, sexes and birthdates of individual members, and the card and account information used to pay electricity bills and login passwords are said not to be included. The affected are individual and corporate members registered as of the end of August 2023; information that should have been erased once use ended remained in the system of a contractor. In Ehime Prefecture alone it comes to about 60,000. Even if a company protects its own servers, a leak occurs if the data entrusted elsewhere is taken. On the 26th, the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology also announced that, through unauthorized access to a system that publishes bidding information for commissioned projects, staff email addresses and the names of companies that took part in bidding may have leaked. Whether this case has the same cause has not been reported, and the scope of the leak is under investigation.

Government Reported to Be Adjusting Toward ¥56.3 Trillion in Defense Spending Over Five Years From Fiscal 2027 to 2031, Up From the Current ¥43 Trillion

The government is reported to be adjusting toward a total of ¥56.3 trillion in defense spending over the five years from fiscal 2027 to 2031. The current plan is ¥43 trillion over the five years from fiscal 2023 to 2027, so it would be about 1.3 times as large. Averaged per year, it rises from about ¥8.6 trillion at present to about ¥11 trillion. The amount is expected to be decided in step with the revision of the three security-related documents (the National Security Strategy and others) scheduled for the end of the year, but as of the 26th it is not a formal government decision. Reports on the amount are not uniform. On the 13th it was reported that adjustment had begun at around ¥60 trillion, and it has been reported that the Ministry of Finance is in the ¥50 trillion range and the Ministry of Defense has estimated a required ¥70–80 trillion. The ¥56.3 trillion is a figure one outlet reported on the 26th, and no other confirmation has been obtained. At a joint meeting of the Liberal Democratic Party's defense division and others on the 25th, opinions calling for “securing sufficient defense spending” followed one after another. On the 20th the Fiscal System Council recommended to the finance minister that funding be secured without relying on government bonds. The timing of the decision and the source of funds have not been indicated. Adjustment is expected to continue until the revision at the end of the year.

Bank of Japan's Tamura Speaks in Kofu, Stresses Need to Raise Policy Rate Early to a Neutral Level, Does Not Rule Out December Hike

Naoki Tamura, a Policy Board member of the Bank of Japan, said in a speech in Kofu, Yamanashi Prefecture, on Thursday the 26th that the policy interest rate needs to be raised early to a “neutral level.” The neutral interest rate is the rate that neither heats nor cools the economy, and Tamura explained that about 2% is in mind. The Bank of Japan raised the policy rate in September from around 1.0% to around 1.25% and held it unchanged in October. When rates rise, interest payments on home loans and corporate borrowing increase, while interest on deposits also tends to rise.

Tamura said that the assessment of the economy and financial conditions after a rate hike will be done “carefully” but does not mean “slowly,” and also touched on the risk of prices overshooting. Tamura also expressed the view that underlying inflation has reached the 2% target but is still not firmly entrenched. The next Monetary Policy Meeting is on December 17–18, and at the press conference Tamura said it is quite possible that a judgment could be made three months after the September hike, and did not rule out the possibility of a further hike in December. Whether to raise rates is decided by the nine Policy Board members at the meeting.

Censure Motion Against Education Minister Seki Goes to Vote in House of Councillors Plenary on the 27th, Support and Opposition Evenly Split, Tied Vote Seen Possible

Eight opposition groups submitted a censure motion against Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology Minister Seki to the House of Councillors on Thursday the 26th. They are demanding resignation over the issue surrounding political fundraising parties. Seki said, “I want to explain carefully,” and has not stated clearly whether to stay or go. The directors' meeting of the House of Councillors Steering Committee decided to hold the vote at the plenary session on Friday the 27th. Before the submission, the opposition had hinted that deliberations on bills could be affected.

The ruling side does not hold a majority in the House of Councillors, and support and opposition are evenly split. A tied vote is also being talked about, in which case the president decides under Article 56 of the Constitution. The result is said to depend on how lawmakers of the Japan Conservative Party, independents and others respond, and there are reports of talk of abstentions within Nippon Ishin no Kai as well. A censure motion has no legal binding force, and even if passed it creates no obligation for the minister to resign. Prime Minister Takaichi is reported to have defended Seki, saying “it is not illegal, and accountability has been fulfilled.”

Chubu Electric Power Disciplines 13 Over Data Falsification at Hamaoka Nuclear Plant, Asks Seven Former Executives Including Former Chairman Katsuno to Voluntarily Return Pay

On Thursday the 26th, Chubu Electric Power announced that it had disciplined 13 people involved in the falsification of data on earthquake assumptions at the Hamaoka Nuclear Power Plant. It will ask seven retired former executives, including former chairman Satoru Katsuno, who was held responsible for the presidency, to voluntarily return pay. Former chairman Katsuno is reported to be asked to return 50% of monthly pay for six months. Of the employees, six in all, two will receive advised resignation and four disciplinary suspension. Since Chubu Electric Power disclosed the misconduct, how the management takes responsibility had been the focus.

The misconduct began around 2012 and is said to have continued even after the Nuclear Regulation Authority began its investigation. The assumed strength of earthquake shaking (the design-basis ground motion) had been reworked so that it looked smaller. In September, Chubu Electric Power announced the resignations of Katsuno (then chairman) and President Kingo Hayashi and the withdrawal of its application for restart review of Hamaoka units 3 and 4. The Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry issued a business improvement order on November 6, and on the 26th Chubu Electric Power also submitted a business improvement plan. The number of people disciplined may grow with further investigation, it is said.
