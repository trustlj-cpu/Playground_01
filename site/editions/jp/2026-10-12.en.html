Attack on Riyadh Airport Kills 12, Injures Over 300; Houthi Role Suspected, Trump Says He Will “Consider” Joining Retaliation

Americans are reported among the dead, and the United States reacted strongly. The focus is the oil market at the start of the week and the spillover to fuel prices in Japan.

On the 10th, Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority announced that King Khalid International Airport in the capital, Riyadh, had been attacked, leaving 12 dead and 309 injured (AP, Reuters). The Mainichi Shimbun reported that Americans were among the dead. AP and the Asahi Shimbun said the attack is suspected to be the work of the Houthis, an Iran-backed armed group in Yemen, and the Houthis have again issued a warning over Saudi airports (Reuters). The same airport was targeted by ballistic missiles last week as well, killing three Saudi nationals (Al Jazeera). The Houthis also claimed a new attack on the 8th, and the Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted two ballistic missiles (AP). This time the damage was on a far larger scale, and the airport's closure was extended (AP). King Khalid International Airport is one of Saudi Arabia's main airports, handling domestic and international passengers and cargo. Houthi-affiliated television reported that Sanaa airport, in Houthi-held territory, was also attacked (Reuters). However, who carried out the attack and the details of the means used have not yet been confirmed.

The U.S. reaction was swift. Secretary of State Rubio condemned the attack and said the United States stands with Saudi Arabia (Reuters and others, 4 outlets). The Asahi Shimbun and the Mainichi Shimbun reported that President Trump said he would “consider” joining a counterstrike (NPR likewise); no decision has been made on whether to take part, and the Saudi-led coalition vowed retaliation (The Guardian). Behind this lies the confrontation between the United States and Iran. Reuters reported that the U.S. military disabled a cargo ship that tried to break Iran's maritime blockade. The war in the Middle East is also expected to be the biggest topic at the annual meetings of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank (Reuters). The market impact shows up first in oil. WTI crude closed on the 9th at $91.85 (+0.39%), the S&P 500 at 7,811.54 (+0.59%) and gold at $4,216.3 (+1.43%), but all of these are levels from before the attack. The Tokyo market is closed on the 12th for Sports Day and reopens on the 13th. Japan imports most of its crude oil from the Middle East, so the focus is how this will affect supply and shipping. The breakdown of the dead by nationality, whether any Japanese were harmed, and the Japanese government's response have not yet been confirmed.

ICC Sanctions: Assembly Bureau Speaking for 21 Countries Including Japan Demands “Immediate Withdrawal”; Foreign Minister Motegi Phones Rubio and President Akane

On the 10th, the Bureau of the Assembly of States Parties of the International Criminal Court (ICC) issued a statement, in the names of 21 countries including Japan, calling for the immediate withdrawal of the U.S. sanctions (Jiji Press, Mainichi Shimbun, NHK). The statement argued that the sanctions obstruct the work of the judiciary. The United States has designated the ICC itself as a sanctions target, and ICC President Tomoko Akane issued a statement saying she “firmly rejects” them (Mainichi Shimbun). Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi consulted with U.S. Secretary of State Rubio by phone and also spoke with President Akane. Mr. Rubio conveyed the “necessity” of the sanctions (NHK, The Japan Times). Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara said he is “deeply concerned” and stressed the rule of law (Jiji Press). Nobel Peace Prize laureate Pillay criticized the sanctions as “unprecedented and unacceptable” (Mainichi Shimbun). The ICC is a permanent international court that tries individuals for war crimes and the like, and it is located in The Hague, the Netherlands. The Mainichi Shimbun points out that whether to dismantle the ICC has become a security “loyalty test” tied to the U.S. military. The Japan Times reported that Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi is once again caught in the middle. Japan hosts U.S. forces at home, and the concrete scope and grace period of the sanctions have not been confirmed in U.S. government documents.

DeNA Sweeps Giants in Two Games to Reach CS Final Stage; Shohei Ohtani Faces League Championship Series From the 12th, Munetaka Murakami's White Sox Eliminated

In the Central League's Climax Series (CS) of Japanese pro baseball, Yokohama DeNA beat the Yomiuri Giants in two straight games and advanced to the Final Stage (NHK, Mainichi Shimbun, Asahi Shimbun). The play of veteran Yasutaka Tobashira stood out (Mainichi Shimbun, Asahi Shimbun). According to the NPB schedule, the Final is set to be played against Hanshin at Koshien on the 14th. Giants acting manager Hideki Hashigami held a “final meeting” after the game, and a turbulent season came to an end (Sports Hochi). In the Pacific League, Nippon-Ham beat Seibu to level the series at one win apiece, and the decision was carried over to Game 3 on the 12th (NHK, Mainichi Shimbun).

In Major League Baseball, the Dodgers' Shohei Ohtani worked out in batting practice ahead of Game 1 of the National League Championship Series (Jiji Press, NHK). The opponent is the Brewers, and the series starts on the 12th (Sports Hochi). Meanwhile, the White Sox lost to the Guardians in the Division Series and were eliminated, and Munetaka Murakami struck out in all four at-bats in the final game (NHK, Jiji Press).

Murder Case Involving U.S. Soldier in Okinawa: Foreign Minister Motegi Conveys “Regret” to U.S. Side; U.S. Military Begins Joint Patrols, Local Voices Doubt “Effectiveness”

In the case in which a woman died at a hotel in Naha, Okinawa Prefecture, and a 20-year-old U.S. Marine was arrested on suspicion of robbery-murder, Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi conveyed “regret” to the U.S. side by the 11th (Mainichi Shimbun). The Foreign Ministry summoned U.S. Ambassador to Japan George Glass to express concern, and Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi called the case “extremely heinous” and asked the U.S. military to tighten discipline and cooperate fully with the investigation (Al Jazeera). The soldier denies the charges, and prosecutors have yet to decide whether to indict.

The U.S. military has started joint Japan-U.S. patrols around its bases. However, in the Koza district of Okinawa City, doubts about their effectiveness have been voiced (Mainichi Shimbun). The Mainichi Shimbun wrote that, with efforts to prevent recurrence having been repeated, the government's seriousness is being tested. The Japan Times conveyed residents' anxiety and anger: “everyone is angry.” In Okinawa, about 6,000 U.S. military personnel are said to have been arrested since the return to the mainland in 1972 (Al Jazeera). In 2024 the figure was 80, the highest in 20 years.

Trump: “Ukraine Needs a New President.” Zelensky Proposes Halting Refinery Attacks; Trump Announces Agreement on an “Energy Ceasefire”

On the 10th, U.S. President Trump criticized Ukrainian President Zelensky and said Ukraine “needs a new leader” (Jiji Press, NHK, Mainichi Shimbun). The Mainichi Shimbun reported that the United States is dissatisfied with Ukraine's attacks on Russian refineries and is demanding that they stop. Zelensky proposed that he would stop attacks on refineries if Russia stopped attacking energy facilities (Reuters). He told U.S. network ABC that he would hold elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire (Reuters). The German government backed Zelensky (Reuters).

Afterward, Trump wrote on his own social media that an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine would take effect immediately and that both sides had agreed (Reuters). Zelensky spoke with leaders from Europe and elsewhere, including Canadian Prime Minister Carney, and said he supports the ceasefire (NHK, Zelensky's social media). However, confirmation from the Russian side and the details of the scope and duration have not yet been confirmed.
