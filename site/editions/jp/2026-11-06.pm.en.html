Nikkei Average Closes at 57,434 Yen, Down 5.96%; First Drop Below 60,000 Yen Since May as AI-Related Stocks Are Sold

The index fell 3,643 yen from the previous day, and at one point during trading the drop topped 4,000 yen. The sharp fall in New York stocks on the 5th spread to Tokyo.

In the Tokyo stock market on Nov. 6, the Nikkei Stock Average fell sharply, closing at 57,434.77 yen, down 3,643 yen from the previous day, a fall of 5.96%. During trading the drop at one point exceeded 4,000 yen, and the index broke below the key level of 60,000 yen. It is the first fall below 60,000 yen since May this year, in about five and a half months. The previous day’s close, on the 5th, was 61,077.90 yen, down 1,110 yen and a five-and-a-half-month low, and the index had already been falling for four trading days in a row. This day, trading opened more than 700 yen lower from the morning, and the drop widened to 1,600 yen, 2,000 yen and 3,000 yen during the morning session, at one point topping 4,000 yen. The trigger was the New York market on the 5th. The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended $1,833 lower, the Nasdaq 5.5% lower and the S&P 500 4.36% lower. A view has emerged in the market that, after the result of the U.S. midterm elections, concern arose that regulation of AI-related investment and data centers will tighten, but no official explanation has been confirmed.

The selling centered on AI-related stocks such as semiconductors. The Tokyo Stock Exchange began publishing, a month ago, the daily balances of margin trading in individual stocks. Margin trading is a mechanism for buying shares with money borrowed from a securities firm, and losses swell when share prices fall. The unrealized losses of individual investors who bought AI-related stocks on margin have worsened, and the unrealized loss ratio of buyers of Kioxia Holdings has reached 26%. The next Tokyo trading day is Monday the 9th, and before then U.S. market moves will come first.

Food Consumption Tax Cut Bill Passes House Financial Affairs Committee; Prime Minister Says Support Payments Would Cover “At Least 30% of Workers”

A bill on a consumption tax cut, which would lower the consumption tax rate on food to 1% for two years from next April, was put to a vote at the House of Representatives Financial Affairs Committee on the 6th and passed with a majority of votes from the Liberal Democratic Party and Nippon Ishin no Kai. The bill was sent to the House of Representatives plenary session in the afternoon of the same day, but the result of the plenary vote could not be confirmed at the deadline. The Democratic Party for the People has indicated it will oppose a vote at the plenary session, and opposition parties have pointed out that the picture of the tax cut is vague. In committee, Prime Minister Takaichi said the government is considering setting the target of the income-based support payments to be introduced alongside the tax cut at 30% or more of workers. The prime minister also said the annual income ceiling would be set more broadly than the 3.5 million yen cited as a reference until now. The ruling parties aim to enact the bill by the end of the session on Dec. 12, and once it passes the House of Representatives it will be sent to the House of Councillors. The overall picture of funding has not yet been presented. The bill began deliberation at the House of Representatives plenary session on Oct. 20 and has been debated in the Financial Affairs Committee since the 21st. Komeito is wavering over its response with an eye on the unified local elections, and there are voices in favor in the House of Councillors.

METI Issues Business Improvement Order to Chubu Electric Power After Seismic Data Misconduct at Hamaoka Nuclear Plant

The Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) on the 6th issued a business improvement order to Chubu Electric Power under the Electricity Business Act. The measure follows, among other things, improper handling of the data used to create the assumed earthquake shaking (the reference seismic ground motion) in the national review for the restart of Units 3 and 4 of the Hamaoka Nuclear Power Plant in Shizuoka Prefecture. Chubu Electric Power had already withdrawn its applications for the restart review of the two units on Sept. 25. The reference seismic ground motion is the standard that sets the size of the earthquake shaking a nuclear plant must withstand, and it is the starting point of the review.

On Oct. 30, as a procedure before issuing the order, METI had sent Chubu Electric Power a notice giving it an opportunity to explain. The subjects include, besides the improper conduct in the review, deficiencies in the company’s internal whistleblowing system and management control structure. The punishment comes amid a series of problems, including misconduct in seismic design data. The specific measures the order requires and the deadline for reporting have not yet been confirmed.

Real Wages Up 2.2% in September, a Gain for the Ninth Straight Month; Summer Bonuses Rise for the Fifth Straight Year

In the Monthly Labour Survey for September (preliminary figures) released by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare on the 5th, real wages, which exclude price changes, rose 2.2% from the same month a year earlier. It was the ninth straight month of gains, and growth in nominal wages, the face-value amount actually received, also continues. Summer bonuses rose above the previous year for the fifth year in a row. Preliminary figures may later be revised in the final figures.

Real wages are an indicator that subtracts the rise in consumer prices from nominal wages to show the change in the quantity of goods that can be bought. A 2.2% rise works out to the same salary buying about 2% more than a year earlier. With high prices from the Middle East situation continuing, the focus is whether pay rises that outpace prices will continue from October onward.

Docomo Network Outage Restored After About 14 Hours; Cause Was Congestion at Switching Equipment, No Sign of Unauthorized Access

NTT Docomo announced that the outage affecting calls and data communications for “5G SA” subscribers, which had continued since just after 11 a.m. on the 5th, was restored at around 1:20 a.m. on the 6th. The outage lasted about 14 hours, and calls and data communications became hard to use nationwide. Docomo explains the cause as congestion, in which traffic concentrated on switching equipment and could not be processed, together with an equipment fault, and says there is no sign of unauthorized access in this outage. The outage was limited to 5G SA subscribers, and in some cases service was said to work if users selected 4G and rebooted.

In the early hours of the 5th, separately, the online shop and My docomo were halted for emergency maintenance over suspected unauthorized access. Docomo has announced that up to about 300,000 records of customer information, such as mobile phone numbers, may have leaked. The scope of the information suspected of leaking and when My docomo and other services will resume have not been decided.
