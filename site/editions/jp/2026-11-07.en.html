Consumption Tax Cut Bill Clears Lower House: All Opposition Parties Against, Four LDP Members Do Not Take Part in Vote; Upper House Begins Deliberations on the 11th

A bill to cut the consumption tax on food to 1% for two years passed the House of Representatives plenary session on the 6th. The House of Councillors will begin questioning on the 11th, with the prime minister asked to attend.

A bill to cut the consumption tax rate on food to 1% for two years from next April was passed by a majority at the House of Representatives plenary session on the afternoon of the 6th and sent to the House of Councillors. All opposition parties voted against it. From the ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP), Ishiba, Obuchi and others did not take part in the vote, four members in all. Prime Minister Takaichi has said of this point, “I will refrain from commenting.” Shortly before noon the same day, the House of Representatives Committee on Financial Affairs had passed the bill by a majority with the votes of the LDP and the Japan Innovation Party (Nippon Ishin no Kai). The Democratic Party for the People had indicated it would vote against the bill in the plenary vote. From committee approval to plenary approval, the bill moved ahead within the same day.

Along with the tax cut, the bill includes support payments scaled to income. At the committee on the 6th, Prime Minister Takaichi said the government is considering making 30% or more of working people eligible for the payments. The opposition is criticizing the bill with comments such as “we don’t know what is in it” and “there is no explanation of funding.” In the House of Councillors, the Diet Affairs Committee chairs of the LDP and the Constitutional Democratic Party of Japan met and agreed to hold a plenary session on the 11th, ask the prime minister to attend, hold questioning and begin deliberations. The Diet session runs through December 12, and because the ruling parties are in the minority in the House of Councillors, the opposition’s response will decide whether the bill passes. The number of members voting for and against at the plenary session and the concrete funding measures have not been confirmed.

Sagawa Express Data on Up to 53.23 Million Records May Have Leaked; Government Decides on Emergency Package

Delivery giant Sagawa Express suffered unauthorized access, and up to 53.23 million records, including the names and addresses of the senders and recipients of parcels, may have leaked outside the company. Takashi Takagaki, a director of parent company SG Holdings, disclosed this on the 6th at a press conference on earnings, saying the extent of the damage is still being investigated. On the same day, online non-life insurer Mitsui Direct General Insurance announced that more than 230,000 records, including policyholders’ names, addresses and phone numbers, had leaked. NTT Smile Energy disclosed that customers’ points had been fraudulently exchanged in an unauthorized access in October. At NTT Docomo, too, up to just over 290,000 customer records leaked, and President Yoshiaki Maeda apologized at a press conference on the 6th. In response to the string of leaks, the government decided on an “emergency package of measures” on the 6th. It asks companies that handle personal information to delete unnecessary data and, to stop attacks before they happen, will carry out measures strategically, including active cyber defense. Active cyber defense is a mechanism that detects signs of an attack in advance and also deals with the source of the attack. The government will accelerate study of forcibly cutting off attacks and create a new team to prevent damage. Increases in staff and expanded equipment are also included, with 10 billion yen allocated to strengthen the measures. The same day, the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications issued administrative guidance asking Docomo to prevent a recurrence, on the grounds that Docomo had handed phone numbers and other data to Amazon Japan without subscribers’ consent.

Okinawa Policy Council Meets for First Time in 13 Years; Prime Minister: “Reduce Base Burden in a Visible Way”

Prime Minister Takaichi attended the Okinawa Policy Council on the 6th and said that reducing the burden of the US military bases in Okinawa will be achieved “in a visible way.” The prime minister also said, “We will do our utmost to reduce the base burden.” The Okinawa Policy Council is a forum where the government and the prefecture discuss Okinawa’s base issues and development measures, and it was held for the first time in 13 years. What the prime minister presented was a policy. The specifics — which burden at which base will be reduced, by when and how — had not been confirmed as of the 6th. What requests the prefecture’s side conveyed at the council is also not yet known.

One Year Since Prime Minister Takaichi’s “Taiwan Contingency” Answer: Chief Cabinet Secretary Calls China an “Important Neighbor,” China Criticizes Again

The 7th marks one year since Prime Minister Takaichi gave an answer in the Diet on a Taiwan contingency. The prime minister had expressed the view that a contingency involving Taiwan could constitute a survival-threatening situation for Japan. Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara said at a press conference on the 6th that China is an “important neighbor,” but did not touch on whether the answer would be retracted. On the same day, a deputy spokesperson of China’s Foreign Ministry again criticized the prime minister’s answer. The fine details of the Chinese side’s remarks have not been confirmed.

US Adds 165,000 Jobs in October, Far Above Market Forecasts

In the October employment report released in the United States on the 6th, the number of employed people outside agriculture rose by 165,000 from the previous month, far above market forecasts. The number of employed people shows how many more people are working and earning pay, and it is a leading gauge of the strength of the US economy. Markets watch the statistic closely every month because it affects the yen exchange rate and share prices.

When employment is strong, wages and prices tend to rise, which can give the FRB (Federal Reserve Board) a reason to keep interest rates high. In judging how the FRB will move, the price data due next week is the next focus. In New York on the 5th, the S&P 500 fell 4.36%, and the market’s moves on the 6th following this employment report do not yet have a final close. The unemployment rate and wage growth have not been confirmed.
