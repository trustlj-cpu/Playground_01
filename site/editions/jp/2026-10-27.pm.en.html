Ahead of Noon Attack Warning, GMO Asks Customers to Power Off PCs; Damage at 5 FX Brokers, Attacker May Have Published One Customer’s Information

GMO Internet Group said on the 27th that, after receiving a warning from a person believed to be the attacker that customers’ PCs would be attacked at noon, it urged users to back up their data and power off.

On the 27th, GMO Internet Group, over unauthorized access to “Onamae.com Desktop Cloud,” its VPS for automated FX trading, urged users to back up the data on their PCs and then power them off by noon. ITmedia NEWS reported that this was because it had received a warning from a person believed to be the attacker that customer information would be published and a ransomware attack carried out at that time. The Japan Times reported that a hacker group is warning that it will publish stolen information. Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that GMO asked customers to back up their data, disconnect from the network and power down. According to ITmedia NEWS, GMO employees are visiting sites around the country to take custody of customers’ devices, and the group’s white-hat hacker team is investigating and analyzing them. GMO Internet Group posted its eighth report, summarizing its response, on its official site that morning.

On subsequent developments, ITmedia NEWS reported that, as of its article on the 27th, no malware, including ransomware, had been found on customer devices whose analysis was complete. The paper also reported that the company plans to provide new PCs to affected customers and that the culprits may have published the information of one customer. Mainichi Shimbun reported that the financial services minister announced that customers at 5 FX brokers have suffered damage, and Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that Financial Services Minister Katayama requested stronger monitoring. Asahi Shimbun reported that the government plans to expand its support measures and that special consultation desks will be set up nationwide to support small and medium-sized businesses’ countermeasures.

Manga Artist Tetsuya Chiba Dies at 87; Creator of “Ashita no Joe,” Recipient of the Order of Culture

Manga artist Tetsuya Chiba died on the 22nd of this month. He was 87. NHK reported that he produced many hits including “Ashita no Joe” and also served as chairman of the Japan Cartoonists Association. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that he was a recipient of the Order of Culture who created classics of boys’ sports manga, and that his death became known on the 27th. Jiji Press also reported his death on the 27th. According to an obituary on Shogakukan’s Big Comic BROS.NET, his real name was Tetsuya Chiba (千葉徹弥), and he died at a hospital in Tokyo at 18:00 on the 22nd. The obituary says that his 87 years, with a 70-year career as an artist, were “a truly happy life as a manga artist.” A NEWSjp distributed article reported that he returned home from former Manchuria after the war and debuted in 1956. On the 27th, Mainichi Shimbun ran an article looking back at the work that produced a social phenomenon with “Stand up, Joe!” and an article on “People Die Easily,” in which he entrusted his repatriation experience to manga, and also carried remarks by Machiko Satonaka, “She showed me a way to live,” and tributes from manga artists and voice actors. The Yahoo! News Topics headline reported that he kept up his serialization until August and that his wife issued a comment.

Nikkei Average Closes Up 1,078.72 Yen at 63,268.37 as Anthropic’s IPO Filing Draws Buying in AI and Chip Stocks

On the Tokyo stock market on the 27th, the Nikkei Stock Average ended trading up 1,078.72 yen from the previous day at 63,268.37 yen. In an article on Yahoo! Finance, Fisco reported the market close figure. Of the morning trading, Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported “Up 1,191 Yen in the Morning; ‘Anthropic IPO’ Reignites AI Rally,” and in its morning-session close article reported that Anthropic’s IPO filing drew buying in AI and semiconductor stocks. TV Asahi News reported that the index rose by more than 1,400 yen at one point at 10 a.m., and that the progress toward Anthropic’s listing spread a sense of relief.

Okinawa Times reported that the morning close on the Tokyo Stock Exchange was 63,381 yen, with buying in AI-related stocks following gains in U.S. tech shares. Fisco’s lunchtime comment reported a sharp rebound, saying expectations for AI-related stocks rose and drew buying in semiconductor stocks. In its article on the afternoon open, Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that the hunt continued in semiconductors and that higher Korean stocks were also a support. Bloomberg TV reported that Asian shares rose and that Anthropic’s IPO lifted semiconductor stocks.

Brazil Presidential Election: Right-Wing Party Issues Statement Accepting Defeat; He Himself Keeps Seeking Annulment, Currency Sharply Lower

NHK reported on the 27th that the party of Flávio Bolsonaro, the right-wing senator who lost the Brazilian presidential runoff to incumbent left-wing President Lula, issued a statement on the 26th accepting the election result. NHK said attention will now turn to Mr. Bolsonaro himself, and reported that Brazil’s currency fell sharply. On the 26th, Bloomberg TV aired a program saying Brazilian assets were plunging in the market.

Times Car Data Leak: Lawyer to Sue Seeking 30,000 to 50,000 Yen Per Person; Park24 Explains How It Set the 3,000-Yen Consolation Payment

ITmedia NEWS reported that, over the personal data leak at the car-sharing service Times Car, attorney Yuki Makino, who has been calling for a class action, announced on the 26th that he will file suit in the Tokyo District Court against Park24, the operator, and others, seeking damages of 30,000 to 50,000 yen per victim. NHK reported that the system was accessed without authorization and image data of driver’s licenses and other information leaked, and Jiji Press reported that a class action could be filed within the year, with the claim at up to 50,000 yen. Asahi Shimbun reported that, calling the 3,000-yen consolation payment “low,” a lawsuit is being considered.

Yomiuri Shimbun reported that “suspicious emails” reached 25,000 people, that impersonation damage in which loans were taken out in people’s names without their knowledge has also occurred, and that a group suit is planned. NTV NEWS NNN reported that site registrants number more than 100,000. On the 27th, ITmedia NEWS reported that Park24 told it in response to inquiries that it had set the amount of the consolation payment, worth 3,000 yen, after comprehensively weighing the responses users could take and the burdens that come with them.
