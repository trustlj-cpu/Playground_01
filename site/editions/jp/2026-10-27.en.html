Former Prime Minister Yasuo Fukuda Dies at 90; One Year as Premier, Chaired the Lake Toya Summit

Yasuo Fukuda, who served as chief cabinet secretary in the Koizumi cabinet and as prime minister for a year from 2007, died on the 22nd of this month. The papers reported that he had worked to improve Japan-China relations, among other things.

Former Prime Minister Yasuo Fukuda died on the 22nd of this month. He was 90. NHK reported his death on the 26th, and Mainichi Shimbun reported in a story on the 25th that he served as prime minister for one year and chaired the 2008 Lake Toya Summit. Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that he worked to establish the Consumer Affairs Agency and to stabilize relations with China. Asahi Shimbun reported that he worked to improve Japan-China relations and sought a “grand coalition.” Nikkan Sports reported that he was the first father-and-son pair of prime ministers in history, following his father Takeo, and that he also contributed to enacting the Public Records Management Act. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that, as chief cabinet secretary, Fukuda told Prime Minister Koizumi bluntly that his approach was “not the royal road” but “the way of the hegemon.”

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi issued a statement; according to TBS, she said, “I cannot help but feel deep sorrow,” and mourned that he “took on difficult issues one by one with his experience and insight.” Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that the prime minister said Fukuda “worked to raise Japan’s presence,” and Mainichi Shimbun reported that she paid a condolence call on the family. Jiji Press reported that former Prime Minister Koizumi said, “It was because of Mr. Fukuda,” and that Ozawa, the counterpart in the grand coalition talks, said he “is still moved.” Nikkan Sports reported that former Prime Minister Koizumi said he was able to hold power for about five and a half years because Fukuda was chief cabinet secretary. TBS reported that Chinese media also flashed news of his death and that Fukuda had stressed deepening the “mutually beneficial strategic relationship” and the importance of exchanges between peoples. Mainichi Shimbun also ran a piece looking back on his calm judgment and pacifism.

Attacker Demands About 7.9 Billion Yen Over Unauthorized Access to GMO; Chief Asks the Government for “Active Cyber Defense”

Mainichi Shimbun and TBS reported that GMO Internet Group CEO Kumagai disclosed that the attacker had demanded about 7.9 billion yen in connection with the unauthorized access to the group’s services. NHK reported that a hacker group is demanding payment of 1 million dollars (just over 150 million yen) by noon on the 27th. According to ITmedia NEWS, CEO Kumagai explained that an in-house white-hat hacker had flagged the problem beforehand, said the delay in responding was “the result of proceeding carefully while weighing the impact on the availability of the service and the priority against other matters to be handled,” and asked the government for active cyber defense. Sankei News reported that, following GMO’s urgent request, the government plans to judge appropriately whether to take neutralization measures and to respond as one across ministries and agencies. Mainichi Shimbun reported that the government would hold a ministerial meeting on cybersecurity on the evening of the 26th, and Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that the prime minister is expected to order emergency measures to be drawn up. ITmedia NEWS reported that criticism also emerged on social media asking whether this was “passing the blame.”

Up to 6.23 Million Records at Joshin, 2.6 Million at Rakuten Travel; Announcements of Personal Data Leaks Keep Coming

The consumer electronics retailer Joshin announced on the 26th that member information and other data on up to about 6.23 million records may have leaked through unauthorized access. According to ITmedia NEWS, the data includes names and addresses as well as 2 images of driver’s licenses, 1 item of My Number card information, and login credentials, and the scope of the impact is under investigation. Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that customer information of up to about 2.6 million people who logged in to Rakuten Travel’s overseas hotel booking site was accessed without authorization, including about 30,000 items of credit card information.

NHK reported that the email delivery company Yumiru Link disclosed that more than 51.62 million items of personal data in total, centered on email addresses, had leaked. Asahi Shimbun reported the same figure and, in a separate story, reported that personal information on 170,000 people leaked from the bullet train booking services Express Reservation and Smart EX. NHK also reported that the system of a subsidiary of the e-gift company Giftee Group was accessed without authorization and about 550 million yen worth of e-gifts were issued fraudulently.

Anthropic Releases IPO Prospectus: First-Half 2026 Loss Tops 12 Trillion Yen; Plans Nasdaq Listing

The U.S. AI developer Anthropic released a prospectus for its initial public offering (IPO) on the 26th. Jiji Press reported on its plan to list on Nasdaq, and NHK reported that it could raise more than 15 trillion yen. Asahi Shimbun reported that its loss in the first half of 2026 exceeded 12 trillion yen. Fortune reported first-half revenue of 16.3 billion dollars and a net loss of 80.7 billion dollars.

Reuters reported that revenue grew rapidly despite the huge loss, and Nihon Keizai Shimbun reported that the prospectus laid out a massive investment plan for AI infrastructure. XTB reported that the ticker symbol is “ANT” and that the listing date and price have not been disclosed.

Consular Visit With Hokkaido Shimbun Reporter Detained in Russia; Foreign Ministry Says “No Problem With Health”

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced on the 26th that an official of the Japanese embassy in Russia held a consular visit with the Hokkaido Shimbun reporter detained by Russian security authorities. NHK reported that the ministry says the reporter’s health shows no problems, and Asahi Shimbun, Mainichi Shimbun and Jiji Press reported the same.

According to a story distributed by Kyodo News, the treaty between Japan and Russia gives the right to a consular visit within 4 days of detention, and Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara said on the 20th that it was “extremely regrettable” that no visit had taken place. Kyodo News reported that Foreign Ministry bureau directors and others had asked the Russian side for an early visit and that the prime minister denied any espionage. Al Jazeera reported that the Russian Federal Security Service (FSB) announced it had detained the reporter on the 13th on suspicion of spying.
