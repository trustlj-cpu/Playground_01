Eight Opposition Groups Submit Censure Motion Against Education Minister Seki to Upper House; Seki Does Not Say Whether to Resign

Over continuing questions about political fundraising parties, eight opposition groups submitted a censure motion against Education Minister Seki to the House of Councillors on the 26th

Eight opposition groups on the 26th submitted to the House of Councillors a censure motion against Education Minister Seki, who faces a string of questions over a political fundraising party. The opposition cites Seki's giving explanations to the Diet that differed from the facts, which it says makes Seki unfit for a cabinet post, and damage to the fairness of government administration. A censure motion is an expression of the House of Councillors' will to hold a minister responsible; even if passed it has no legal power to force a resignation, and whether to step down is left to the minister and the prime minister. The party at issue invited government officials as speakers, and its profit margin is reported to have reached 92%. A profit margin of 92% means that, of every 100 yen collected in fees, 92 yen is left in hand after expenses.

After the submission, Seki said, "I want to explain carefully," but did not say whether to resign. The Takaichi government is reported to have held firm, as of the 25th, on keeping Seki in the post. Whether the motion will pass is uncertain, and the response of independent members is reported to hold the key to the vote. An effect on bills under deliberation in the House of Councillors has also been pointed out. When the vote will be held, and where Seki will offer further explanation, had not been confirmed as of the evening of the 26th.

U.S. Proposes Country-by-Country Tariffs on 14 Countries and Regions, With Japan at 15%, Reported as the Level Before the Unconstitutionality Ruling

The United States on the 25th (Wed.) is reported to have presented a new plan setting tariffs country by country for 14 major trading partners and regions. The rate for Japan is 15%, a return to the same level as before the U.S. Supreme Court's unconstitutionality ruling on the tariffs, it is said. Tariffs are taxes on imported goods; at a rate of 15%, $15 is added to a $100 import. Under the 2025 U.S.-Japan agreement, Japan had settled on reciprocal tariffs of 15%. In the U.S. market on the 25th, when the plan was presented, tariff turmoil and a fall in tech stocks combined, and investors tired of volatility (the size of price swings) are reported to have been jolted. The Nikkei average closed on the 26th at 57,499.16 yen, up 0.86% from the previous day. For other countries and regions, an additional 20% on China and up to 20% on Singapore, among others, are reported, and rates differ by country. The timing of taking effect and the legal basis could not be confirmed from what was reported. How this relates to the 15% in the U.S.-Japan agreement, and what the Japanese government will do, had not been shown as of the evening of the 26th.

North Korea's Kim Jong Un Inspects Nuclear-Related Facilities, Stresses Never Seeking Anyone's Approval of Nuclear Status

North Korea's General Secretary Kim Jong Un inspected a nuclear material production plant, a nuclear weapons research institute and other sites, and said that the country would "never seek anyone's approval" of its nuclear status, it was reported on the 26th. The thrust was that North Korea will not ask the United States to recognize its nuclear status, and it was seen as a renewed show of unwillingness to accept denuclearization. Nuclear material refers to uranium, plutonium and the like, which serve as materials for nuclear weapons.

The date and place of the inspection, who accompanied the visit, and whether newly disclosed facilities were included could not be confirmed from what was reported. The full text of the remarks has not been confirmed either. Japan, the United States and South Korea take the position of calling on North Korea to denuclearize, and the governments' official reactions to the remarks could not be confirmed as of the evening of the 26th.

Osaka Metropolis Plan Draft Settled, Heading to a Third Referendum; Five Bureaus of the Special Wards Say They Are Short of Staff, Reported

The draft system for the Osaka metropolis plan has been settled, reflecting the wishes of the Japan Innovation Party, and the city is heading toward a third referendum, it was reported on the 26th. The Osaka metropolis plan would abolish Osaka City, reorganize it into several special wards, and redivide roles with Osaka Prefecture; it was rejected in both of the two previous referendums. Hirofumi Yoshimura, leader of the Innovation Party, is reported to have called on the 25th for a referendum to be held next spring.

Meanwhile, five bureaus at the core of the special wards say they lack both people and time and that "maintaining services will be difficult," citing a shortage of staff, internal documents show, it is reported. The referendum date, the number of special wards and their boundaries, and the concrete contents of the draft could not be confirmed from what was reported. How procedures in the assembly proceed, including the link to the idea of a secondary capital, will be the focus ahead.

U.S. FCC Approves Up to 1 Million SpaceX Satellites for Orbital Data Center, Scientists Reported Concerned

The U.S. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has approved a plan for SpaceX to launch up to 1 million satellites for its orbital data center project, it was reported on the 26th. The satellites are called "Starmind," and the idea is said to be to place computing facilities for AI in space. A data center is a facility that gathers computers performing large amounts of computation, and placing one in orbit rather than on the ground is what marks this plan.

Scientists and others are reported to have raised concerns about the plan. The specifics of those concerns could not be confirmed from what was reported. When launches will begin, whether approval was granted in stages, and the conditions attached to the approval had also not been confirmed as of the evening of the 26th.
