Trump Says “FBI Is Investigating” Nvidia CEO’s Relationships With Women; CEO Admits Affair and Apologizes

President Trump said on the 9th that the FBI is investigating CEO Huang’s relationships with women. Trump said Huang had told the president of knowing nothing about the allegations, and that the president believes that explanation.

U.S. President Trump said on the 9th that the FBI (the Federal Bureau of Investigation) is investigating the relationship between Jensen Huang, CEO of chip giant Nvidia, and a certain woman. Trump also said that Huang had told the president of knowing nothing at all about allegations involving the woman, and that the president believes that explanation. Huang earlier admitted an extramarital affair and apologized in a message to employees, and the company’s board of directors has begun an independent investigation. With this remark, the allegations were described as going beyond an internal company inquiry to also being the subject of a U.S. government investigation.

Anonymous U.S. officials claim that the woman is suspected of being a collaborator of a Chinese intelligence agency. But neither the officials’ names nor their grounds have been made public, and the claim is unconfirmed. A spokesperson for China’s Foreign Ministry dismissed the allegation on October 30 as “groundless speculation.” Nvidia explains that Huang’s meetings with executives of customer companies are within the normal scope of a CEO’s work. The share price fell, but the size of the drop differs by tally, so it is not given here. Trump earlier also indicated a wish to keep Huang on as an adviser unless facts to the contrary are found. The FBI’s own explanation of whom it is investigating and how far could not be confirmed. The deadline and result of the board’s investigation have not been officially disclosed either.

Nikkei Average Closes at 60,459.58 Yen for a Second Straight Gain, Back in the 60,000 Range at the Close

On the Tokyo stock market on the 10th, the closing price of the Nikkei Stock Average was 60,459.58 yen, up 1,684.08 yen (2.87%) from the previous day. The index posted a second straight gain and returned to the 60,000-yen range at the close. The Nikkei average fell 5.96% on November 6 to end at 57,434.77 yen, and after the 58,775.50 yen on the 9th, it has recovered 3,024.81 yen (5.27%) in two trading days from the close on the 6th. That works out to a close of 100 yen on the 6th becoming about 105 yen in two trading days. No explanation of the reason for the rise by a specific speaker has been confirmed this time. For that reason, we refrain from tying the move to any individual factor. The U.S. S&P 500 on the 9th ended at 7,549.94, up 2.35% from the previous day. The close on the 6th was 57,434.77 yen, which has not yet reached the level before the 5.96% fall.

September Household Survey: Real Spending by Households of Two or More People Down 6.8% on the Year, a Tenth Straight Monthly Decline

According to the September Household Survey released by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications on the 10th, the consumption spending of households of two or more people was, in real terms, net of price changes, 6.8% lower than in the same month a year earlier. The decline has lasted ten months in a row; a household that spent 100 yen in September last year spent only about 93 yen this year. The Household Survey is a statistic that asks households across the country how much they actually spent, and it is a basis for gauging the momentum of personal consumption.

The ministry believes that moves to refrain from going out and shopping because of unsettled weather such as long rains, and weaker demand for summer goods, had an effect. With year-on-year declines lasting ten months, the ministry’s explanation does not show whether a temporary factor like the weather alone can account for them. Whether spending will recover from October on is not yet known.

LDP-Ishin Working Team Holds First Meeting on Revising Three Security Documents; Government Makes “Capability to Deny the Other Side’s Objectives” the Basis

A working team of practitioners from the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) and Nippon Ishin no Kai held its first meeting on the 10th toward revising the Three Security Documents on national security. The government side set out the direction for strengthening defense capability and put the possession of a capability to deny the other side the achievement of its objectives at the center of its basic thinking. It also explained its response to new ways of fighting using drones and AI. The talks will focus on defense spending and nuclear policy.

The two parties agreed on a policy of strengthening the Japan-U.S. alliance. Ishin, however, is calling for a review of the “not allowing introduction” wording among the Three Non-Nuclear Principles and for early introduction of nuclear-powered submarines, and the LDP’s proposal does not touch on these points. The government intends to proceed with the revision within the year, and neither how far defense spending will be raised nor the conclusion on nuclear policy has been shown.

Honda to Build New Plant in Ohio, U.S., for About $3 Billion, With Production to Start in 2029

Honda announced on the 9th that it will build a new automobile plant in Ohio in the United States. The investment is about $3 billion, and the plan is to start production in 2029. Annual production capacity will rise by 240,000 vehicles, and according to Honda, employment will exceed 2,000 people. The plant will be built between the existing East Liberty plant and Marysville plant.

It is designed to be able to make hybrid vehicles, gasoline vehicles and electric vehicles alike, and the models to be produced have not yet been announced. The U.S. is a market accounting for about 40% of Honda’s sales. The effect on production in Japan is not shown in this announcement.
