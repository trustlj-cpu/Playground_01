Nikkei Falls 2.42% to 59,662 Yen, Down More Than 1,200 Yen at One Point; First Drop Below 60,000 Since the 10th

The Nikkei average briefly fell below 60,000 yen. No announcement has been confirmed that names a reason for the drop. At Nvidia, the U.S. Treasury Secretary called for the successor to be chosen from outside the company.

On Monday the 16th in the Tokyo stock market, the Nikkei average at one point fell more than 1,200 yen below the previous Friday and dropped under 60,000 yen. It is the first time below 60,000 yen since the 10th of this month. The closing price (the last trading price of the day) was 59,662.02 yen, a fall of 2.42%. That works out to a share price of 100 yen becoming 97.58 yen. No announcement from the exchange, the government, a company or anyone else has been confirmed that names the reason for the day's drop. Moves involving Nvidia were reported the same day, but no institution or person has said that they were the cause of the drop either.

On Nvidia, U.S. Treasury Secretary Bessent said on the 16th that the successor to Jensen Huang, who earlier stepped down as CEO, should be brought in from outside the company. It is reported that Huang is discussing the possibility of a return with close aides, but Nvidia has not confirmed this, and there has been no announcement that the board of directors has approved a return. A petition drive calling for a return continues. The number of signatures differs by report, so no figure is given here. How the choice of a successor will proceed has not been made clear. Together with how the Nikkei average moves on the next trading day, what the board of directors announces is the next focus.

Former Jungle Pocket Member Shinji Saito Given Four-Year Unsuspended Prison Sentence; Tokyo District Court, Defense Appeals Same Day

On the 16th, the Tokyo District Court sentenced Defendant Shinji Saito, a former member of the comedy trio “Jungle Pocket,” to an unsuspended prison term of four years. Saito had been charged with sexually assaulting a woman in her 20s. An unsuspended prison term is a sentence whose execution is not suspended, so the person actually goes to prison. The court said, “There is nothing to take into account in the motive of a crime committed to satisfy one's own sexual desire.” The defense had pleaded not guilty, saying “I thought there was consent,” but the ruling did not accept this. The defense filed an appeal on the day of the ruling. An appeal is a procedure for seeking a retrial from a higher court, a high court, when a party is dissatisfied with a first-instance ruling. The filing of an appeal keeps the first-instance ruling from becoming final, and a decision from the appeal court is awaited. Some reports have focused on the “difference in standing” between the parties to the case. The date of the appeal trial and what will happen to the defendant's custody have not been confirmed at this point.

China Confirms U.S.-China-Russia Leaders' Meeting; Shenzhen on the 19th, No Details of Agenda Given

A leaders' meeting of U.S. President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Putin will be held on the 19th in Shenzhen, China. What is new is that China confirmed on the 16th that it will take place. The United States and Russia had already announced it, but China has not given details. The meeting will be held to coincide with the leaders' meeting of Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC), and it is said to be the first time the leaders of the three countries have come together.

A senior U.S. official has explained that the agenda will be nuclear arms reduction and the Iran situation and that Ukraine will not be taken up. China, however, has not stated an agenda, so how it sees the meeting is not known. Whether any agreement is possible at the meeting, or whether a joint document will be issued, has not been shown at this point. Details such as the time of the meeting are treated differently by different reports and cannot be confirmed.

Fukuoka Mayoral Election Heads for Fresh Election With No Candidate Reaching Statutory Vote Threshold; 13 Run, Top Vote-Getter Inatomi Reportedly Also Short

In the Fukuoka mayoral election held on the 15th, 13 newcomers ran, but no winner emerged, and a fresh election is expected. Shuji Inatomi, a former member of the House of Representatives who is said to have won the most votes, is reported to have also fallen short of the statutory vote threshold, one quarter of the total valid votes needed to win. The shortfall is reported at about 10,500 votes, but the final vote counts must wait for confirmation by the election board. The Public Offices Election Act provides that when no candidate in a head-of-government election meets this requirement, the election is held again. The confirmed turnout was 45.58%, 11.27 points higher than last time.

It is also reported to be the first fresh election for mayor of an ordinance-designated city in 23 years and the 8th case among head-of-government elections overall, but this has not been confirmed. The fresh election is not a runoff but a new election, so the lineup of candidates may change. Two of the top four are reported to have signaled they will run again, and the candidate with the third-most votes said, “I will see it through until I win.” The date of the fresh election and the final vote counts to be confirmed by the election board have not been confirmed at this point.

Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara Calls Successive Arrests of U.S. Military Personnel in Okinawa “Regrettable”; Urges U.S. Side to Ensure Tightening of Discipline and Prevention of Recurrence

At a press conference on the morning of the 16th, Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara said it was “regrettable” that U.S. military personnel had been arrested one after another in Okinawa. Those arrested are U.S. military personnel suspected of driving a car under the influence of alcohol or of public indecency. The government conveyed its regret to the U.S. side and called for a thorough tightening of discipline and prevention of recurrence.

The offense of driving under the influence refers to driving with at least 0.15 milligrams of alcohol per liter of breath detected. Public indecency is the crime of committing an indecent act in a place where an unspecified number of people can see it. The number of people arrested, their affiliations, and the date and time the incidents occurred cannot be confirmed at this point. How the U.S. side will present measures to prevent recurrence has not been made clear either.
