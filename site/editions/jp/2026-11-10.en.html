Nitori Discloses Unauthorized Access; Personal Information on Up to 37.62 Million Records May Have Leaked

Names, addresses, phone numbers and similar data are affected; the company says card information and passwords are not included. Corporate members are also covered.

Nitori Holdings, the major furniture and household goods retailer, announced on the 9th that its systems had been hit by unauthorized access and that personal information on members and on people who bought its products may have leaked, in up to 37.62 million records. The company confirmed the unauthorized access on the 6th of this month, and it took three days to announce it. The affected members include corporate members. The data that may have leaked is names, addresses, phone numbers and the like; the company says credit card information and passwords are not included. Unauthorized access means an unauthorized third party getting into someone else's system without permission. Counting 37.62 million records at one per second would take about 435 days.

The figure of 37.62 million is the upper limit of records that “may” have leaked. How many records actually left the company, and how many people were affected, cannot be confirmed from this announcement. The breakdown between members and purchasers, the route and cause of the intrusion, and whether there is any sign the personal information has been misused are also unconfirmed. How the company will contact members and set up a contact desk for inquiries has not been shown so far. The announcement used the word “possibility” and did not state that a leak had definitely occurred. That card information and passwords are not included is the company's explanation; the result of any verification by a third party cannot be confirmed. What measures the company took in the three days between confirmation and announcement is also unconfirmed.

LDP Reprimands Four Including Former Prime Minister Ishiba for Leaving Plenary Session Before Vote on Consumption Tax Cut Bill

Secretary-General Suzuki of the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) said on the 9th that four members, including former Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba, who left the House of Representatives plenary session ahead of the floor vote on the related bill for the consumption tax cut on food, have been given a reprimand by the secretary-general. Explaining the reason, Suzuki said, “It is not something that can be overlooked from the standpoint of party unity.” This is the first time a former prime minister has been disciplined. A reprimand is a disciplinary measure in which a lawmaker who has violated party discipline is sternly cautioned in writing or orally. Obuchi is among the four. The bill passed the House of Representatives last week by a majority including the LDP and Nippon Ishin no Kai, and has been sent to the House of Councillors. Ishiba had previously voiced criticism of the tax cut plan on the grounds that no funding source had been shown. On the walkout, Ishiba said, “Precisely because I once served as party president, I feel strongly that the party's traditions and sense of responsibility must not be lost.” The names of the other two who received the reprimand, and how the disciplinary action will affect deliberations in the House of Councillors, could not be confirmed this time. How Ishiba personally will respond to the action is also unconfirmed.

Mitsui Chemicals Announces Acquisition of Semiconductor Materials Leader JSR; Shares Held by Public-Private Fund Are the Target

Mitsui Chemicals announced on the 9th that it will acquire JSR, a major semiconductor materials company. The JSR shares are held by the Japan Investment Corporation (JIC), a public-private fund with government funding, and Mitsui Chemicals will take over that stake jointly with the Development Bank of Japan. A public-private fund is a fund that combines government and private money to invest in companies expected to grow. The scale of the acquisition is reported as about 1.8 trillion yen (1.84 trillion yen) in most accounts, while one flash report puts it at about 1.4 trillion yen, so the figures differ.

The timing of the acquisition, the stake held afterward and how the funds will be raised could not be confirmed this time. The amount stated in the company's official materials is also unconfirmed. Before the announcement, reports had said moves were under way toward a sale on the scale of 1.3 trillion yen. The effect on the employees and business partners of the target company, as explained by the parties, could not be confirmed.

Takaichi Cabinet Approval at 53%, Disapproval at 32% in November Opinion Poll

In the November opinion poll released on the 9th, 53% of respondents said they approve of the Takaichi Cabinet, up 2 points from the October poll. Disapproval was 32%, unchanged from October. The 53% works out to about 53 people out of every 100 saying they approve. A Cabinet approval rating is the result of asking whether people support the current Cabinet; because question wording and samples differ by polling organization, the figure cannot be compared directly with other polls.

As for party support rates, the LDP stood at 35.9%, Nippon Ishin no Kai at 2.4%, the Democratic Party for the People at 3.4%, Komeito at 1.3%, the Constitutional Democratic Party at 3.5% and Sanseito at 3.0%. “No party preference” was 37.1%, slightly above the LDP. Of the parties listed, the figure for the “Democratic Reform Association” could not be confirmed this time. The survey period and number of respondents are also unconfirmed.

Sony Life Discloses Leak of About 2.9 Million Policyholder Records, Including Policy Numbers, Names, Addresses and Phone Numbers

Sony Life announced on the 9th that the system of its dedicated site for policyholders had been hit by unauthorized access and that about 2.9 million records of personal information, including policyholders' policy numbers, names, addresses and phone numbers, had leaked. Of the damage the company has suffered from unauthorized access, this is the portion in which leaks were newly confirmed. A policy number is a number used to tell insurance contracts apart one by one. So far, no case of the leaked information being misused has been confirmed.

The route and cause of the intrusion and the period over which the leak occurred could not be confirmed this time. How policyholders will be contacted and how a contact desk for inquiries will be set up are also unconfirmed. The roughly 2.9 million figure the company gave is not the number of policyholders themselves but the number of leaked records. The number of policyholders affected, and whether corporate contracts are included, are also unconfirmed.
