Russia Detains Hokkaido Shimbun Correspondent on Spy Charges, Moves Him to Moscow; Japanese Government Says It Is “Checking”

State-run TASS reported it on the 13th. The Russian side says he had been gathering information on mobilized soldiers. The Japanese government says it is still verifying the facts.

Russia's state-run TASS news agency reported on the 13th that a Japanese man who serves as a Hokkaido Shimbun correspondent had been detained on suspicion of espionage in Sakhalin Oblast, in Russia's Far East (NHK, Jiji Press, 47NEWS). The Sankei Shimbun reported that the Federal Security Service arrested the man and moved him to Moscow, and the Yomiuri Shimbun also reported that he may have been transferred and arrested. TV Asahi reported that he had been indicted for espionage, but other outlets have not confirmed the details of the indictment procedure. Headlines distributed by two regional papers said a Russian court had approved two months of pretrial custody. Russian authorities explained that the man had been gathering information on mobilized soldiers (headline distributed by regional papers). The New York Times reported that the security service said it had detained a reporter who was covering the recruitment of military personnel in the Far East. The Russian Foreign Ministry claimed that Japan was mobilizing journalists to collect information (47NEWS).

The Japanese government said it is checking the information on the detention (47NEWS). The man's name and age, the date he was detained and the specific grounds for the espionage charge have not been disclosed, as far as we could confirm. We also found no statement from the Hokkaido Shimbun in the articles we checked. The Financial Times placed this detention as following a series of detentions that have been criticized as politically motivated. Does the charge Russia cites target the reporting itself? No material that a third party can verify has emerged yet. Detention, arrest and indictment are legally separate stages, and the reports do not use the words consistently.

Agriculture Minister Yana's Free-Ride Row: Prime Minister Says “Will Not Hesitate” if Illegal; to Meet Him on the 14th

The House of Representatives Budget Committee began on the 13th, and the problem involving Agriculture Minister Kazuo Yana became the center of the debate. Mr. Yana acknowledged the reports that he had given supporters free rides but denied any illegality (Mainichi Shimbun). The Tochigi prefectural election commission said that the exchange with the prefectural commission that Mr. Yana described in the Diet was “impossible” (Asahi Shimbun) and disclosed that it has no record of an inquiry from Mr. Yana's camp (Kyodo News, 47NEWS). It said that, had it been asked, it would have answered that there could be a violation of the Public Offices Election Act. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi criticized his “cut” remark on road budgets as “inappropriate and truly uncool,” and said the question, including whether he can be entrusted with administration, would get “careful thought” (Jiji Press, Yomiuri Shimbun). On the free rides, the prime minister answered that there would be no hesitation if anything illegal were found (Jiji Press, ABEMA). The prime minister is due to receive a phone report from Mr. Yana within the 13th and intends to meet him after the 14th's budget committee session (Jiji Press, Mainichi Shimbun). The opposition is discussing its response, including a censure motion (NHK).

Saudi-Led Coalition Bombs Oil Facility Near Yemen's Capital; Houthis Vow Retaliation, Japanese Government Approves Three “Beyond Hormuz” Bills

A Saudi Arabia-led coalition on the 13th bombed an oil-related facility on the outskirts of Yemen's capital, Sanaa. The coalition had urged civilians in the area to evacuate beforehand (The Guardian, BBC, Le Monde). The Houthis warned that they would retaliate by targeting Saudi Arabia's Riyadh and Dammam (BBC, Australia's ABC). FinancialJuice reported that the Houthis called on civilians and employees to leave the area around Aramco facilities. Reuters, citing people familiar, reported that loud explosions were heard near Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran, in the east. No damage has been confirmed. U.S. President Trump said the Houthis are trying to push up oil prices before the November 3 midterm elections (Reuters, Al Jazeera, Financial Times).

Riyadh's airport closure has been extended to Saturday the 17th (Mainichi Shimbun, Asahi Shimbun). Crude oil reflected the tension. FinancialJuice reported that November WTI crude closed up 40 cents at $94.14 on the 13th, while Brent crude futures closed down 96 cents at $105.69. That is a dispatch from one outlet only. At its cabinet meeting the same day, the Japanese government approved bills to broaden the sources from which it procures crude oil and other supplies (NHK). According to the Asahi Shimbun, there are three bills, which provide grants for detour routes that avoid the Strait of Hormuz. It is a “beyond Hormuz” move to reduce dependence on the Middle East. In Hokkaido, kerosene prices have risen, and a federation of cooperatives in the prefecture handed the national government an urgent written request (NHK).

Personal Data Leaks Keep Coming: Up to 6.5 Million Records at “Gin no Sara,” 15,000 at JR Central, Even Municipal Disaster-Alert Emails; NICT Warns of “A2B Attacks”

New personal-data leaks came to light on the 13th as well. The company that operates the delivery sushi chain Gin no Sara and others announced that information on people registered on its official website and app, up to about 6.5 million records, may have leaked (NHK). JR Central said customer information of up to 15,000 records may have leaked from its online shopping site (NHK, Asahi Shimbun). According to NHK, it was an effect of a cyberattack on the system of IDC Frontier, a SoftBank subsidiary. Arcadia, which provides disaster-information emails to local governments, announced that it had confirmed an incident in which unauthorized access by a third party is suspected (NHK). Delivery has stopped in various places, and in Ayase, Kanagawa Prefecture, personal information on about 11,000 people may have leaked (Mainichi Shimbun). Minato Ward in Tokyo has also stopped sending emergency alert emails (NHK).

On the cause, the National Institute of Information and Communications Technology (NICT) said on the 13th that “A2B attacks,” which analyze publicly released apps and target the servers behind them, are one cause of the string of unauthorized access incidents, and urged caution (Nikkei, ITmedia). NHK reported that when a security company analyzed the attack traffic, a variety of methods were used in a short time, and AI agents may have been abused. No other outlet has confirmed this; it remains at the stage of possibility. The Mainichi Shimbun reported that cases where an outsourced service served as the entry point stand out. Jiji Press reports that the government relies on warnings and has few moves to make. On the 13th, PayPay Securities halted trading in Japanese stocks because of a failure at an external connection point. No link to the leaks has been reported (NHK, Nikkei).

Chip Stocks Crumble: Kospi Plunges Over 5%, Nikkei Average Falls for a Fourth Day on China's Memory Catch-Up and Rising Rates

On the 13th, after the holiday weekend, chip stocks were heavily sold in Asian equity markets. In Tokyo, the Nikkei Stock Average fell for a fourth straight session (Reuters, Nikkei), and according to NHK the intraday drop at one point exceeded ¥1,900. AI and chip-related names pushed the index down, with Tokyo Electron alone accounting for about ¥450 of it (Fisco's tally). Kioxia plunged on worries that competition from Chinese companies would intensify (Nikkei). d menu News reported that the close was ¥67,777.52, down ¥1,253, but only one outlet has confirmed it. South Korea's Kospi fell more than 5% (AP, Chosun Ilbo). Reports that China's memory maker CXMT had achieved a technical breakthrough set off fears about competition (Bloomberg, Reuters).

The selling continued in the United States too. Reuters reported that U.S. chip stocks extended their losses during trading on the 13th. The reasons were reports on China's memory and anxiety about AI investment. Shares of Micron and SK Hynix also fell (CNBC and others). Big financial firms reported earnings: Goldman Sachs beat market expectations, and JPMorgan's profit fell short of expectations (Reuters). Interest rates are also a weight. Federal Reserve official Williams said one more rate hike this year would be appropriate (Bloomberg, FinancialJuice). The IMF urged caution about an adjustment in AI-related equity markets, war and debt (Reuters, The Japan Times). On Monday the 12th, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose for a third straight day, up $195 (Jiji Press). The U.S. close for Tuesday the 13th will come out after this paper's deadline.
