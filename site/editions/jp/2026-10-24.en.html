Sagawa Express: Up to 53.23 Million Records of Personal Data May Have Leaked in Unauthorized Access to Its Package Inquiry Service

On the 23rd, Sagawa Express disclosed that the personal information that may have leaked externally in the unauthorized access runs to as many as about 53.23 million records.

NHK reported that the personal information that Sagawa Express, the major courier, had said might have leaked externally after unauthorized access could cover as many as 53.23 million records, including names, addresses and telephone numbers. According to ITmedia NEWS, Sagawa Express issued its fifth report on the 23rd on the unauthorized access to its “Package Inquiry Service,” announced on September 30, and the number of affected records was established in that report. The package tracking service, which had been suspended, was also resumed the same day, it said. TBS NEWS DIG reported that the information that may have leaked is the names and addresses of senders and recipients written on shipping labels.

Asahi Shimbun described the personal information that may have leaked as “a cumulative count of up to 53.23 million records.” ITmedia NEWS notes that Sagawa Express announced the possible leak of personal information on October 1. According to ScanNetSecurity, as of the fourth report on October 9, the number and scope of the affected records were described as under investigation. With this fifth report, a number was given for the first time. Whether the information has actually been misused and caused damage has not been confirmed in the reporting.

Nintendo’s Shigeru Miyamoto Awarded Order of Culture, First From the Game Field; Eight Recipients

NHK reported that the eight recipients of this year’s Order of Culture, announced by the government, include Shigeru Miyamoto, the game producer behind “Super Mario Bros.,” and Kenzo Kyōai (硤合憲三), chosen for this year’s Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Yomiuri Shimbun reported that Miyamoto was honored in the field of media arts (games) and that it is the first Order of Culture for the game field, and said Motoko Ishii (88), in design (lighting), and Shunichi Tokura (78), in popular music and music copyright, are also among the recipients. Jiji Press reported that the creator of “Mario” is the first from the game world to receive it, and that the saxophonist Sadao Watanabe was among those named Persons of Cultural Merit. Famitsu.com ran an article on Miyamoto’s path and philosophy as described at the press conference under a headline carrying the words “What does it mean to ‘make’ something? Everyone is thinking about it as AI advances.”

Government to Review Gasoline Subsidies: Prime Minister Takaichi to Instruct Economy Minister, 170-Yen Level Also Covered

According to Jiji Press, Prime Minister Takaichi told reporters on the 23rd that she intends to instruct the economy, trade and industry minister to consider subsidies for fuel oil such as gasoline. Its headline reads “Flexible, including an exit strategy.” NHK reported that, in view of the situation in the Middle East, she plans to consider the future shape of support, including a review of the level at which the price-shock mitigation measures hold gasoline prices to about 170 yen per liter.

Mainichi Shimbun reported “Considering gradual reduction of gasoline subsidies,” and Asahi Shimbun reported that the prime minister announced her intention to instruct the minister. NHK said that if the subsidies holding down prices are reduced, the level and timing would be the focus from here.

Revised Imperial House Law Takes Effect on the 24th, Allowing Adoption of Male-Line Males From Former Imperial Branch Families

NHK reported that the revised Imperial House Law, which provides for female members of the imperial family to remain in the family after marriage and for the adoption of male-line males of the former imperial family, with the aim of securing enough members of the imperial family, takes effect on the 24th. Jiji Press reported “Male-line adoption from former imperial branch families made possible; female members keep status after marriage,” and Asahi Shimbun reported that the question of female and female-line emperors has been shelved.

According to Sankei News, Kihara, the chief cabinet secretary, said that adoption by the former imperial branch families is meaningful for securing male members of the imperial family. Asahi Shimbun reported that an expert warned it is “anachronistic, putting the family first,” and that while some imperial family members are open to adoption, the Imperial Household Agency is struggling with an unprecedented situation.

New York Stocks Drop by as Much as 800 Dollars Intraday as AI Outlook Worries Hit Tech Stocks

Jiji Press reported that on the 23rd stock prices on the New York market fell by as much as 800 dollars at one point. It cited worries about the outlook for AI as the reason. In a separate article, the same news agency reported that tech stocks plunged against a backdrop of AI concerns.

On a program on the 22nd, Bloomberg Television reported that Meta’s shares fell 7% as AI-related spending fuels credit concerns. On the 23rd, it reported that Asian investors are looking for a safe haven as selling of AI-related stocks deepens, and also that hedge funds suffered their largest one-day loss since the COVID-19 period. In Tokyo, according to market data, the Nikkei average closed at 62,693.16 yen on the 23rd, down 0.34% from the previous day.
