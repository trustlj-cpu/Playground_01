Consumption Tax Cut Bill Enters Substantive Deliberation in House of Councillors; Prime Minister Considers Extending Support Payments to 50% or More of Workers

Deliberation on the bill to cut the consumption tax on food began on the 13th in a House of Councillors special committee. The prime minister indicated a plan to widen the group eligible for the support payments set up alongside the cut, from the current 30%.

On the 13th, deliberation began in a House of Councillors special committee on the related bill to cut the consumption tax on food, the biggest focus of the current Diet session. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said that, for the income-based support payments to be introduced alongside the cut, the government is considering widening the eligible group, set until now at 30% of workers, to 50% or more. An explanation was also given that households with two children would receive up to 60,000 yen. Support payments are a mechanism that backs, in cash, people who gain little even when the tax rate is cut; if the income ceiling that decides eligibility rises, more people can receive them. The calculation is that, out of 10 workers, those who can receive them rise from 3 to 5 or more. However, the payment period and the income ceiling could not be confirmed in the day's answers.

In the deliberations, the prime minister repeatedly answered that Komeito's argument would be “taken seriously.” Meanwhile, Komeito Policy Research Council Chair Nakano said at a press conference the same day that the prime minister should answer the questions, and that the public would not accept a mechanism that leads to a tax increase in two years. Questions over the bill's content remain. What happens to the tax rate once the cut period ends, and how to secure the funds needed for the cut and the support payments, remain points of contention for ruling and opposition parties. The Diet session runs until December 12, and whether the bill is enacted within the session depends on how deliberations in the House of Councillors proceed and on each party's response. From the ruling-party discussions up to the previous day, the stage has moved to the House of Councillors committee.

Two Major Non-Life Insurers to Halt New Sales of Fire Insurance Through Tamahome; Suspected Fraudulent Insurance Claims

At the housing builder Tamahome, suspicions have emerged that insurance payouts on fire insurance were fraudulently claimed, and two major non-life insurers have decided on a policy of ending new sales of fire insurance through Tamahome. Another insurer is also leaning toward withdrawing, so the number could reach three. However, the third insurer's final decision could not be confirmed. Only new sales are being stopped; no effect on contracts already concluded has been reported. Tamahome, besides building houses, also runs an insurance agency business and has sold fire insurance on commission from non-life insurers. The suspicions pointed to are methods such as using insurance payouts obtained improperly in collusion with customers to cover premiums at policy renewal, or inflating repair costs in claims so that customers bear no real burden. All are at the suspicion stage; fraudulent claims have not been established. By halting sales, the insurers aim to prevent damage from spreading while the investigation continues. Tamahome's explanation and the scope of the fraudulent claims could not be confirmed.

Nitori Holdings Says Up to More Than 37 Million Personal Records May Have Leaked; Chair Apologizes at Earnings Briefing

Nitori Holdings, the major furniture and household goods retailer, has announced that, through unauthorized access by a third party, personal information of members and others may have leaked in up to more than 37 million records (37.62 million). At an earnings briefing on the 13th, Chair Akio Nitori apologized, saying that great worry and inconvenience had been caused. According to Nitori, the unauthorized access was confirmed on the 6th, the connection was cut immediately, and it is investigating with outside specialist organizations. The 37.62 million is an upper-limit figure, and the number actually taken out has not been confirmed.

Those who could be covered are members' information, information on people who bought products, corporate members' information, and information on orders placed through online shopping malls. Nitori explains that login passwords and credit card information are not among the information that may have leaked. As the investigation proceeds, it has emerged that there may also have been access from mid to late October. Those who may be covered are to be contacted in turn, and people are urged to beware of suspicious contacts claiming to be from Nitori or delivery companies. The cause and the scope of the unauthorized access await the results of the investigation.

Two Women Walking in Ebina, Kanagawa, Hit and Killed by Truck; Vehicle That Fled Found in Sagamihara

On the afternoon of the 13th, at an intersection in Ebina, Kanagawa Prefecture, two elderly women on foot were hit by a truck and died. The truck fled the scene. Police are investigating it as a hit-and-run case, have found a truck believed to be the one that fled in Sagamihara, and are questioning people connected to it. A hit-and-run is an act in which a person who has caused an injury or death accident leaves the scene without fulfilling the duty to help the injured or notify the police. A report of the accident had come in, and at the time of the report it was relayed as a traffic accident between a truck and pedestrians. The ages and names of the two who died, the details of the accident and the identity of the driver could not be confirmed.

Suspected of Beating and Killing 3-Year-Old Eldest Daughter in Hirakata, Osaka; Father Arrested on Suspicion of Murder, Mother Also Arrested

In Hirakata, Osaka Prefecture, a father has been arrested on suspicion of murder, suspected of beating and killing the 3-year-old eldest daughter. The mother was also arrested, suspected of knowing of the assault, leaving the child unattended and causing the death. A report came in from a hospital that the child's whole body was bruised, and the body reportedly had dozens of bruises. Police are investigating the circumstances.

Hirakata city made clear at a press conference that it had received an abuse report about the household and had confirmed bruising. A city official explained that the injuries were not serious, that the child was not taken into protection, and that it was judged safe, and said reports had come about five times from three organizations. Whether the city's response was appropriate has not yet been examined. Where the mechanism to protect children failed to work must await future examination.
