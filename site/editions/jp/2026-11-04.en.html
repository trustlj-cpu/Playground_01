Asoview Member Data on More Than 15 Million Records Leaked Via “Arara”; Company Says Passwords and Card Information Not Included

Asoview, which runs a reservation site for leisure facilities, announced on November 3 that member information had leaked through unauthorized access to its email-delivery contractor.

Asoview announced on November 3 that more than 15 million member records had leaked. The cause was unauthorized access to “Arara Message,” an external system to which it had outsourced email delivery. The leak covers a total of about 15.82 million records, and the company is examining the contents. The information that may have leaked is email addresses, member information, contact details such as phone numbers, postal codes and areas of residence, and ticket purchase and usage information. The company says passwords and credit card information are not included. People who bought tickets for the Fukui Prefectural Dinosaur Museum through Asoview may also be affected, and Fukui Prefecture urged caution about secondary harm.

Arara Message is an email delivery service for businesses whose leak was announced on November 2 to have spread to about 77 million records. The 77 million is the sum of the counts for each client company and may include duplicates. From October 28 to 30, up to about 7.13 million suspicious emails posing as a credit card company were sent. The impact continues to reach client companies, and Kintetsu Miyako Hotels also announced the leak of about 130,000 email addresses. Asoview urges people to be wary of phishing emails posing as the company, and of messages asking for personal information or payment on the grounds of refunds, cancellations or identity verification. This is a separate case from the other cyberattack it announced in May (about 27,000 records), and it has not been confirmed whether the leaked information has been misused.

Attack on School in Southern Ukraine: Death Toll Rises to 31, More Than 50 Missing

The death toll has risen to 31 in the November 2 Russian attack on a school and a nearby shelter in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia Oblast, in southern Ukraine. Ukraine’s emergency authorities had initially announced at least 26 dead and 5 injured. The school was being used as a distribution point for humanitarian aid. More than 50 people are missing, the figures differ depending on the tally, and the search continues. After the local ceasefire that had held in a southern city ended, Russian attacks hit various areas from the 2nd to the 3rd, and more than 70 people were also injured in the east. President Zelensky visited Berlin on the 3rd, met Germany’s leader, and called for European involvement ahead of three-party talks among the United States, Russia and Ukraine. Working-level talks will be held in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, on November 10 and 11. Russia’s account could not be confirmed.

Former Unification Church Head Han Hak-ja’s Bail Revoked After Leaving Hospital Where Admitted for a Church Facility

The Seoul High Court on November 3 revoked the bail of Han Hak-ja, head of the former Unification Church (Family Federation for World Peace and Unification). Han was granted bail on October 19 on grounds of age and health, on the condition of residing at a hospital in Seoul. It was confirmed that Han left the designated hospital and visited a church facility even though bail had been granted for treatment, and this was deemed a violation of the conditions. Bail is a system under which a defendant’s detention is lifted midway through a trial on conditions such as a deposit.

Han is accused of crimes including violating the political funds law by giving money and valuables to the previous Yoon Suk Yeol administration, and received an unsuspended prison term of two years in the first trial at the Seoul Central District Court on August 31. Prosecutors had sought 13 years; Han denies the charges and has appealed, and the appeal trial continues. Details of how Han will be held after the revocation, including where, have not been made clear.

Tenerife’s De Miguel, of Spain’s Second Division, Dies at 30 After Collision During a Match

Spain’s second-division club Tenerife announced on November 3 that forward De Miguel (30) had died. Late in the match on the 2nd, De Miguel ran onto a pass and collided violently with the opposing goalkeeper who rushed out, fell, and was taken away by ambulance.

The Spanish Football Federation decided to cancel an official match scheduled for the 3rd. The club issued a statement saying, “Nothing can ease the sadness.” The details of the collision and the cause of death have not been made public officially.

Defense Minister Koizumi Considers Revising Defense Ministry’s Basic Policy on AI Use; Principle of Human Judgment Stays

Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi indicated on November 3 that the Defense Ministry would consider revising its basic policy on the use of AI. Advanced technologies such as AI are advancing rapidly in the security field, and the view is that the current policy cannot keep up with that change. The Defense Ministry and Self-Defense Forces set the basic policy on AI use in 2024.

The plan is to proceed with the review in light of discussions on the revision of the three security documents scheduled within the year. Koizumi also said that it is important not to let AI decide on its own but to involve human judgment. Taku Yamazoe of the Japanese Communist Party criticized Koizumi’s remarks on AI, saying “How appalling.” No specifics or timing for the review have been given.
