False M9.9 Earthquake Early Warning: Japan Meteorological Agency Cancels It and Apologizes at Three Press Conferences

In the Earthquake Early Warning delivered on the 16th, the magnitude was overestimated from 6.9 to 9.9, and the warning was cancelled. The agency explained the cause as two errors and apologized.

On the Earthquake Early Warning it announced on the 16th, the Japan Meteorological Agency saw its estimated magnitude jump sharply from the initial 6.9 to 9.9, and then cancelled the warning itself in an unusual step. TBS's “news23” reported that the agency admitted it had overestimated and held three press conferences the same day to apologize. A magnitude of 9.9 exceeds that of any earthquake in recorded history, so a value of a scale that cannot occur in reality was sent out as a warning. The program's headline picked up on the point that the magnitude was shown as 9.9.

As for the cause, the program reported that there were “two errors.” Keitai Watch reported that the cause of the false alarm in the 16th's warning had come to light and that the agency had made an urgent correction. The specifics of the correction, and at which stage of processing each of the two errors occurred, are not detailed in the coverage we could confirm this time; we are waiting for a detailed explanation from the agency. Keitai Watch's article presents it as the cause of the false alarm having come to light, and reports the agency's urgent correction.

TBS characterized the cancellation as “unusual.” The sequence was that only the magnitude figure was sharply rewritten after the warning went out, and in the end the entire warning was withdrawn. Coverage also takes up, as a sign of how seriously the apology was meant, the fact that the agency did not stop at one press conference but held three within the same day. However, the areas where shaking was forecast in this warning, and the number of people who actually received the notification, do not appear in the coverage we could confirm this time.

Separately, the Asahi Shimbun reported on the night of the 17th that the response to the NTT Docomo malfunction, in which Earthquake Early Warnings were not delivered, had been completed. Two problems pointing in opposite directions, a false alarm and a missed delivery, thus surfaced in the same week. No technical connection between the two has been reported at this point, and no basis for linking them has been shown. The timeline of at what stage the agency noticed the anomaly, and how many minutes it took to cancel, is likewise not shown in current coverage. The results of the verification are best confirmed in the agency's official announcements.

The Earthquake Early Warning is a system for prompting people to take protective action before strong shaking arrives, and the reliability of the information itself is the premise of its function. With cancellations and apologies piling up, the focus is on how recipients will take it. The next points to check are the content of the recurrence-prevention measures the agency announces and the results of inspections of delivery routes, including the mobile carriers.

Prime Minister Takaichi Offers Masakaki to Yasukuni Shrine, Expected to Skip Visit at Autumn Festival

The Yomiuri Shimbun and others reported that Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi offered a masakaki to coincide with Yasukuni Shrine's autumn grand festival. A masakaki is a decoration of tree branches placed before the deity, a form used when the prime minister does not visit during the festival period and limits the gesture to an offering. The Yomiuri reports that, out of consideration for relations with China and South Korea, the prime minister is expected to skip a personal visit. In the coverage we could confirm this time, three outlets agree on the fact of the offering. The skipping of the visit, on the other hand, is described by the Yomiuri as an “outlook,” and it must be watched until the festival schedule ends before it can be treated as final. Whether the Chinese and South Korean governments issued official reactions could not be confirmed within the scope of our reporting this time. The prime minister's handling of Yasukuni Shrine tends to be received in connection with diplomatic schedules and remarks involving neighboring countries. The combination of an offering and a skipped visit is a pattern seen with past prime ministers, and this time too it is placed within that frame. Going forward, the focus is on whether cabinet ministers and Diet members visit during the festival period, and on the reactions of neighboring countries. The Yomiuri Shimbun writes that consideration for relations with China and South Korea lies behind the prime minister's skipping the visit. However, how the prime minister herself or the Prime Minister's Office explained this consideration is not included in the articles we could confirm this time, and it must be read as the Yomiuri's own reading. Because the festival runs for several days in autumn, whether cabinet ministers visit or Diet members visit as a group during the period will be the subject of follow-up reports.

DeNA Sweeps in Four Straight to Reach Japan Series for First Time in Two Years; Hanshin Loses All Four

The Mainichi Shimbun reported on the 17th that DeNA beat Hanshin in four straight games and clinched a berth in the Japan Series for the first time in two years and the fifth time overall. According to Sports Hochi, the decider came late in the game, when rookie Shuto Naruse hit a go-ahead two-run homer off Hanshin's Kotaro Otake. With this loss Hanshin was eliminated from the Climax Series Final Stage, finishing winless in all four games. In the J1, Hiroshima moved into provisional first place.

The Mainichi Shimbun described DeNA as having come through this stage “unscathed” with all-out team baseball, and said Hanshin could not get out of being “dependent on Teru and Morishita.” According to NHK, Hanshin's Teruaki Sato was asked right after the elimination about a challenge to the major leagues and answered, “It has just ended, so I can't say anything.” The Mainichi summed up the four straight losses by saying Hanshin never managed to rebuild a lineup that relied on this season's main hitters through to the end.

On the same 17th, it was also reported that Tsuyoshi Nishioka is expected to become Nippon-Ham's infield defense and baserunning coach (Sponichi). The aim is to rebuild a defense that committed 88 errors this season, and farm team manager Inaba will move to first-team batting coach. The DeNA and Hanshin results are reported consistently by multiple outlets including Sports Hochi, the Mainichi Shimbun and NHK; the Japan Series opening date and the opposing team are not included in the coverage we could confirm this time.

BASE Subsidiary's Information Leak Widens to About 23.35 Million People, Including Card Data

According to ITmedia NEWS and others, a BASE subsidiary announced an information leak covering the data of about 23,356,000 people. Of these, about 2,096,900 people's records included the cardholder name, part of the card number and the expiration date. Only the first six and last four digits of the card number are included, and it has not been reported that all digits were exposed. Ehime's Saijo Festival: Deputy Mayor Who Was Carrying a Taikodai Dies Pinned Beneath It

According to NHK, on the afternoon of the 17th, the deputy mayor of Saijo, Ehime Prefecture, who was taking part as a bearer of a “taikodai” at the Saijo Festival, which has continued since the Edo period, was pinned under the taikodai as it toppled and died. The Asahi Shimbun likewise flashed that the deputy mayor, who was taking part in the festival, died pinned under a taikodai. Police are investigating the detailed circumstances at the time.

The Saijo Festival has continued since the Edo period and is known as an event in which bearers carry taikodai through the streets. NHK reported the accident as occurring on the afternoon of the 17th, and the Asahi Shimbun's flash came out in the early hours of the 18th. Three outlets, including a Yahoo! News article, reported the matter, but the details of where the accident occurred, whether anyone else was hurt, and why the taikodai fell are not included in the articles we could confirm this time, because the police investigation continues.

Separately, NHK reported that smoke from forest fires in Indonesia has spread to neighboring countries such as Malaysia, with effects including school closures.

Among the main developments in the same time frame: in the J1's 10th round Hiroshima took provisional first place with its fourth straight win, the Fukuoka Prefectural Assembly passed the ordinance protecting employees and the political ethics ordinance, and the U.S. president held off on implementing the Russia sanctions law, with Zelensky saying it was “regrettable.” These are also covered in the briefs below.
