Earthquake Early Warning Overestimates Quake as “M9.9”; Meteorological Agency Cancels It for the First Time. Calculated From a Wrong Epicenter

At 12:40 p.m. on the 16th, a warning was issued calling an M6.9 quake near the Kamchatka Peninsula an “M9.9 in the Kuril Islands”; it was cancelled eight minutes later. The Japan Meteorological Agency apologized, saying it “deeply apologizes.”

The agency issued an Earthquake Early Warning (warning) at 12:40 p.m. on the 16th, saying a magnitude (M) 9.9 earthquake had occurred with its epicenter in the Kuril Islands, and cancelled it at 12:48 p.m. (Jiji Press, FNN). What actually occurred was an M6.9 earthquake near the Kamchatka Peninsula, and the shaking in Japan was limited to seismic intensity 1 in Hakodate, Hokkaido (Jiji Press). According to Jiji Press, the alert had forecast a maximum seismic intensity of 7. The first news conference was held at 2 p.m., where the head of the Earthquake and Tsunami Observation Division explained that the cause was fixing the epicenter in the Kuril Islands and overestimating the magnitude (TV Asahi). FNN reported that this was the first time a warning had been cancelled. Shinya Tsukada, director-general of the agency's Earthquake and Volcano Department, said at the conference, “We deeply apologize for the great inconvenience and burden placed on the public,” and explained that there had been no external cyberattack (Jiji Press).

On the cause, the agency explained at a second news conference that it had wrongly fixed the epicenter in the Kuril Islands and overestimated the magnitude (FNN). According to Jiji Press, it first assumed an epicenter at a point east of the Kamchatka Peninsula and estimated M9.9 using a formula for domestic use, then moved the epicenter to the northern Kuril Islands and forecast the seismic intensity for each region. TV Asahi reported that the epicenters used for the magnitude calculation and for the intensity forecast were different. The agency held a third news conference late on the 16th, revealing that a function that should have been working had not operated normally this time and announcing that it had taken emergency measures, and it also touched on the possibility of human error (ABEMA TIMES). It says details are not yet known. In the same earthquake, a malfunction also occurred in which NTT Docomo's emergency alert mail was not delivered. According to Keitai Watch, Docomo explained that the cause was that its system failed to recognize the data correctly when it received data in a partly different format from the agency.

Prime Minister Takaichi Offers Masakaki at Yasukuni Shrine's Autumn Grand Festival; Visit Expected to Be Skipped, but Growth Strategy Minister Kiuchi Visits

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi on the 17th, to coincide with the autumn grand festival that began that day at Yasukuni Shrine in Kudankita, Tokyo, offered a masakaki, a ritual offering, in the name “Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi” (Jiji Press, NHK). Citing sources close to the prime minister, Jiji Press reported that she is leaning toward not visiting during the festival period through the 19th, and that she is expected to skip it as she did at the spring festival in April and on August 15, the anniversary of the end of the war. The prime minister has not visited since the autumn festival last October, just before she took office, and the agency reports she is putting priority on consideration for China and for South Korea, with which relations continue to improve. According to NHK, Minoru Kiuchi, minister in charge of the growth strategy, and Takayuki Kobayashi, chairman of the Liberal Democratic Party's Policy Research Council, visited. Jiji Press reported that Eisuke Mori, Speaker of the House of Representatives, Masakazu Sekiguchi, President of the House of Councillors, and Kenichiro Ueno, Minister of Health, Labor and Welfare, also offered masakaki. South Korea's Foreign Ministry expressed “deep disappointment and regret.”

DeNA Beats Hanshin Four Straight and Reaches Japan Series for the Fifth Time, First in Two Years; Mimori Delivers Go-Ahead Hit With Two Out in the Ninth

Game 4 of the Climax Series (CS) Final Stage in Japan's Central League was played on the 17th at Koshien, and DeNA, third in the regular season, beat Hanshin 8–5 for its fourth straight win; with a 4–1 record including Hanshin's one-win advantage, it clinched a berth in the Japan Series for the fifth time and the first time in two years (Mainichi Shimbun, NHK, Kanagawa Shimbun). According to Nikkei, with two out and runners on second and third in the ninth, trailing by one run, Daiki Mimori, who had entered the game as a substitute, hit a go-ahead two-run single, and Sports Nippon reported that Inoue and pinch-hitter Hayashi then added run-scoring hits. DeNA won the first three games of the Final Stage by shutout in a row and had gone 6–0 without a loss since the First Stage against the Giants (Sponichi, Chunichi Sports). The MVP was Shugo Maki. Reaching the Japan Series from third place is the third time, following 2017 and 2024, and DeNA will face Pacific League representative SoftBank in the Japan Series, which opens on the 24th at Yokohama Stadium. According to NHK, asked about a challenge to Major League Baseball, Hanshin's Teruaki Sato answered, “It just ended, so I can't say anything.”

Data Leak at BASE Subsidiary Estore Reaches About 23.35 Million People; Partial Card Numbers Included for 2.09 Million

Estore, a wholly owned subsidiary of BASE, announced on the 16th that information on about 23,356,000 people had leaked in unauthorized access to ShopServe, its online-store building and operation support service (Nikkei, ITmedia NEWS). Purchasers' names, addresses, telephone numbers, email addresses and more were included, and for about 2,096,900 of them, the cardholder name, the first six and last four digits of the card number, and the expiry date were also affected (ITmedia NEWS). According to Nikkei, the company does not hold full card numbers or security codes and says card payments cannot be made with the information involved alone. The affected stores total 7,858, current and past, and the login IDs and passwords for the admin screens used by the stores also leaked. The unauthorized access took place from May 21 to August 1, and in August the company had announced a possible leak of up to about 8,850,000 records (ITmedia NEWS, @IT).

Deputy Mayor Akihiro Nanbae, a Bearer, Dies After Being Pinned Under a Taikodai at Ehime's Saijo Festival

At about 5:50 p.m. on the 17th, at the Saijo Festival in Saijo, Ehime Prefecture, a man was pinned under a taikodai (Nikkei, Hokkaido Shimbun). According to prefectural police, the man was Akihiro Nanbae (62), deputy mayor of the city, who had been taking part in the festival; he was taken to a hospital, where he was confirmed dead about an hour later (Tokyo Shimbun, Nishinippon Shimbun). The deputy mayor was serving as a “kakifu,” a carrier of the taikodai, and prefectural police are investigating the cause of the accident. Ai TV reported that the deputy mayor had been in office about three months. Mayor Mitsuyoshi Ochi issued a comment: “Losing a dear comrade is so sudden that I cannot believe it” (Nikkei). According to NHK and others, the Saijo Festival is an autumn festival that has continued since the Edo period, and on the 17th 11 taikodai were being dedicated at a shrine.
