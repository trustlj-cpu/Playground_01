Remains of 731 martyrs of Tết Mậu Thân 1968 given memorial service and reburied at Ho Chi Minh City Martyrs’ Cemetery; thousands line the route holding white flowers

The ceremony took place on the morning of Oct. 14, starting from Lê Thị Riêng Park; Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng attended, and a candlelight vigil was held the night before.

On the morning of Oct. 14, Ho Chi Minh City held a memorial service and burial for the remains of 731 martyrs found at Lê Thị Riêng Cultural Park, Hòa Hưng Ward. Thanh Niên wrote that these are martyrs who fell in Tết Mậu Thân 1968 and lay in the heart of Saigon for nearly sixty years; Dân trí said the collection of remains took more than four months. In the early morning, Deputy Prime Minister Phạm Thị Thanh Trà, together with leaders of the Ministry of National Defense and the city People’s Committee, attended a prayer service at the park (Dân trí); Tuổi Trẻ noted that the Ho Chi Minh City Buddhist Sangha also held a prayer ritual. On the evening of Oct. 13, the arts program “Khúc tri ân” (Song of Gratitude) lit candles in remembrance at the same place (Dân trí). Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng attended the memorial service and burial (Dân trí, VnExpress’s English edition). After the service, a motorcade carried the remains from the park to Ho Chi Minh City Martyrs’ Cemetery; earlier, Thanh Niên had published an expected route of 26 km, with the first group of vehicles setting off at 8:45 a.m. VnExpress wrote that thousands of people had stood along the road since early morning, holding white chrysanthemums; Dân trí wrote that many sang as the motorcade left the park. By noon, Thanh Niên reported, hundreds of chrysanthemums were still lying on the pavement of Võ Nguyên Giáp Street, where the motorcade had passed.

Around the ceremony were very concrete acts by ordinary people. A couple in Đà Lạt drove more than 300 km carrying 4,000 stems of white chrysanthemums they had grown themselves, to give away for free (Thanh Niên, Tuổi Trẻ); a florist donated 1,000 blooms, and two sisters donated 500 stems (Thanh Niên). Dân trí wrote that students of Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai High School lined Điện Biên Phủ Street and medical staff in white coats laid flowers, while Lao Động wrote that many artists, including People’s Artist Trịnh Kim Chi, Huỳnh Lập and Emoura Phạm, came to pay their respects. Behind the figure of 731 are individual cases. Dân trí told how martyr Huỳnh Văn Quên is a rare case because the belongings found contained enough information to establish his identity, and his wife visited her husband’s grave for the first time. We do not have an overall figure for how many remains have been identified by name, so we do not generalize from this case. The same day, Thanh Niên reported on awards for scientists of Vietnam National University, Ho Chi Minh City in the “500 Days and Nights Campaign” to search for, collect and identify martyrs, while VnExpress ran a piece on a group of lecturers using radar to detect graves; both pieces are about the search work in general, and we do not claim they are directly tied to Lê Thị Riêng.

VN-Index falls six sessions in a row to just under 1,700 points; foreign investors net sell for a 16th straight session, more than VND 770 billion

The VN-Index fell for a sixth straight session and dropped to just under the 1,700-point mark in Wednesday’s session on Oct. 14 (Dân trí). VnEconomy wrote that the index slid to 1,704.87 points as VHM was sold heavily; during the morning, decliners overwhelmingly outnumbered advancers and 90 stocks fell by more than 1% even though the index had fallen only about 0.5%. We do not have a close that can be cross-checked from two sources, so the index box at the top of the page is left blank. Foreign investors’ net selling came to VND 772.24 billion, with VIC and VHM sold hardest, at VND 152.99 billion and VND 151.94 billion respectively (VnEconomy); VnExpress said this was the 16th straight session of outflows, the longest streak since 2024. Dân trí tied the foreign net selling to high international interest rates, and another Dân trí piece said rising savings rates have made many investors less keen on stocks. VnEconomy wrote that liquidity on the HoSE floor in the morning had not reached VND 4,364 billion. US inflation data was released on the evening of Oct. 14 Vietnam time, after the domestic session had closed, so today’s session is the first time the domestic market reacts to it.

US September inflation rises to 3.7%, above forecast; Wall Street has its sharpest drop since July, gold slides below $4,100, markets price in a possible Fed rate hike this month

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reported that the September consumer price index (CPI) rose 0.6% from the previous month, in line with forecasts, and 3.7% from a year earlier, higher than the 3.6% analysts had expected (Tự do Thời báo, citing Dow Jones). CNA, Dân trí, Yonhap and CNBC all gave the 3.7% figure, and CNA and Dân trí put most of the blame on fuel prices; Reuters wrote that core CPI, which excludes food and energy, was also stronger. Markets immediately repriced the chance of a Fed rate hike: the probability of a hike as early as this month was put anywhere from 44% (per CME, via Tự do Thời báo) to about 50% (Yonhap Infomax), versus about 22% before the data (Chosun Ilbo). Citi shifted its forecast to a 0.25 percentage point hike in December (Valor, Jiji).

In the New York session on Oct. 14, the S&P 500 fell 1.1% and the Nasdaq fell 1.9% (SCMP, Clarín), with the Nasdaq at 26,692.87 points (Straits Times); the Dow fell 48 points, or 0.1% (SCMP). The New York Times called it the S&P 500’s biggest one-day drop in more than two months, and the Straits Times wrote that the Nasdaq had its worst session since July 23. The dollar and bond yields rose together (Bangkok Post, Clarín); gold fell more than 1% and dropped below $4,100 an ounce (Dân trí; Reuters, FXStreet). Fed officials spoke cautiously: Daly said a rate rise is likely needed but not urgent (Reuters), while Bowman, speaking before the CPI data, said there is still time to “wait and see” (Reuters). No decision has been made; the policy meeting at the end of the month is where it will be decided.

World · Middle East · EnergyPress ✓ 6 sources · oil price per Bloomberg

Houthis fire missiles at Aramco headquarters in Dhahran, container ship sinks near Hormuz; IEA warns of diesel shortage until mid-2027

Tensions between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis in Yemen continue to escalate. Thanh Niên, citing Reuters, wrote that the headquarters of the national oil and gas company Saudi Aramco in Dhahran was hit by a ballistic missile, with no casualties; Reuters, citing sources, reported major damage and said the Houthis pledged further attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities. Haaretz wrote that a fire at an Aramco complex in Dhahran spread across several floors, and that Riyadh airport is closed until Oct. 17. The Houthis said they again fired at King Khalid airport in Riyadh (Le Figaro), a capital that has been attacked daily for more than a week.

In the Strait of Hormuz, a container ship sank after being hit by a projectile (Reuters, citing a source). Singapore’s MPA said two Singapore-flagged container ships were attacked, one of which sank, with all crew safe (CNA, Straits Times). Security data show at least 10 ships attacked in or near Hormuz since Oct. 11, the highest since the war between the US, Israel and Iran broke out (Straits Times, GMA). Bloomberg wrote that Brent traded above $106; the IEA warned that diesel supply will remain tight until mid-2027 (VnExpress). Mr. Trump said the Houthis are deliberately pushing up oil prices ahead of the US midterm elections (Thanh Niên). At home, fuel prices are being adjusted this afternoon, Oct. 15; Thanh Niên forecast that E10 gasoline could fall the most.

Ministry of Industry and Trade proposes letting petroleum companies set their own prices, but they must announce new prices at least 30 minutes ahead and may not raise early or delay cuts

Under the Ministry of Industry and Trade’s latest draft decree on petroleum trading, all principal traders and distribution traders would be free to set their own selling prices (VietNamNet). In return, companies must announce new prices at least 30 minutes ahead (VnExpress), may not raise prices before they take effect or delay cutting them, must explain price movements and keep data for regulators to monitor (Dân trí). VietNamNet said the Ministry will use price-declaration data to monitor pricing behavior and prevent tacit collusion and monopolistic alliances. Thanh Niên wrote that this is a new addition in the draft.

This is only a proposal. We have no information on the issuance date, effective date or penalties for violations; these details must await the official document. The backdrop is that world energy prices are swinging sharply because of the conflict in the Middle East, while the IEA warns that diesel will stay scarce until mid-2027 (VnExpress), and the domestic price-adjustment period falls this afternoon (VietNamNet, Thanh Niên). A rule on early price announcements sounds simple, but its effectiveness will depend on whether regulators have enough declaration data to detect price coordination.
