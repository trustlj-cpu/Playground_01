Vietnam signs free trade agreement with four EFTA countries after 14 years of negotiations

The signing took place on the morning of Oct. 18 in Hanoi, during the official visit of the Swiss President and the Prime Minister of Liechtenstein.

On the morning of Sunday, Oct. 18, in Hanoi, Minister of Industry and Trade Lê Mạnh Hùng and representatives of the member states of the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) signed the Vietnam-EFTA Free Trade Agreement, known as VEFTA, in the presence of General Secretary and State President Tô Lâm, per Dân trí, VietnamPlus and Znews. VnEconomy and VietnamPlus said the EFTA side included Swiss President and Minister of Economic Affairs Guy Parmelin and Liechtenstein Prime Minister and Minister of Finance Brigitte Haas. EFTA has four members: Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein. Tuổi Trẻ and VnBusiness said the agreement closes 14 years of negotiations. The newspapers cited Ministry of Industry and Trade figures showing that two-way trade before the signing held steady at nearly $2 billion a year. VnExpress and Thanh Niên said the agreement includes commitments on tariffs, technical barriers, sanitary and phytosanitary measures, customs and trade remedies, along with provisions on services, investment and intellectual property. The newspapers have not published the line-by-line tariff elimination schedule.

The signing was part of Mr. Parmelin’s official visit to Vietnam on Oct. 17 and 18, per VietnamPlus and Tuổi Trẻ. The same day, National Assembly Chairman Trần Thanh Mẫn met Mr. Parmelin at the National Assembly House as the two countries marked 55 years of diplomatic relations. Nhân Dân, VietnamPlus and Dân trí reported that Mr. Mẫn asked Switzerland to work with the other EFTA countries to speed up ratification of the agreement so that it can enter into force soon. Mr. Parmelin stressed the role of protecting intellectual property and geographical indications, expressed a wish for cooperation on clean energy, transport and vocational training, and invited Vietnam’s leaders to the AI Summit in Geneva in June 2027. Also on Oct. 18, the Prime Minister of Liechtenstein, who is on an official visit to Vietnam from Oct. 17 to 19, was received by General Secretary Tô Lâm and held talks with Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng. Dân trí and VietnamPlus said Vietnam and Liechtenstein also signed an agreement on the avoidance of double taxation after the talks.

Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng: land and property prices will surely fall once the amended Land Law is passed

At a National Assembly group discussion on the afternoon of Oct. 18, Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng said that once the National Assembly passes the Land Law (amended), the State will tightly regulate land prices and decide them, and he concluded that land prices and property prices will surely fall, per Dân trí and Znews. Dân trí also quoted him as saying that the apartment market has gone down since the Government stepped up social housing and rental housing. Dân trí and Thanh Niên reported earlier that the draft law drops the principle of pricing land at market rates; per Dân trí, the land price table and adjustment coefficients would be drawn up by provincial people’s committees and decided by provincial people’s councils. Thanh Niên, VnExpress and Tuổi Trẻ reported the Prime Minister as saying the Government is resolute in cutting intermediary links in the distribution of petrol, electricity, medicine and medical equipment so that people do not have to pay high prices. The newspapers did not give a specific size of the reduction or a timeline.

Politics · Local governmentPress ✓ 3 outlets · official guidance on core communes and wards not yet issued

Home Affairs Minister: provinces and cities may propose merging communes and wards that are too small or splitting areas that are too large

Answering delegates at a National Assembly discussion group on the afternoon of Oct. 18, Minister of Home Affairs Nguyễn Tiến Hải said provinces and cities may propose merging communes and wards that are too small or splitting areas that are too large, per Tuổi Trẻ and VnExpress. VnExpress noted that a proposal must go through the local collective leadership and gather residents’ views before it is submitted to the central level. Tuổi Trẻ said the Ministry of Home Affairs is studying a model of core communes and wards, with each locality choosing about 3 to 4 units, and will report to the Government and the Politburo before issuing guidance. That morning, per Dân trí and VnExpress, Deputy Prime Minister Phạm Thị Thanh Trà said the Government cut 53% of the cost and 53% of the time of administrative procedures in one peak month. She also pointed to a tendency to avoid and fear responsibility among some officials and to difficulties in running two-tier local government, especially at the commune level.

Economy · Ho Chi Minh CityPress ✓ 4 outlets · the city’s full-year target figures differ among the outlets

Party Secretary Trần Lưu Quang: Ho Chi Minh City will not chase double-digit growth at any cost

At a National Assembly discussion group on Oct. 18, Ho Chi Minh City Party Committee Secretary Trần Lưu Quang said the city must grow sustainably and not seek quick fixes, per Dân trí, VnExpress and Thanh Niên. Thanh Niên wrote that he admitted the risk of missing the double-digit growth target. Nhân Dân and Thanh Niên said the city’s gross regional domestic product (GRDP) grew 9.06% after nine months, so the fourth quarter needs growth of 13.23% to reach the full-year target. VnExpress noted that the city has asked for a progress report on growth every two weeks.

The same day, Dân trí, VnExpress and Nhân Dân reported that the main span of the pedestrian bridge shaped like a nipa palm leaf across the Saigon River, weighing more than 3,400 tonnes, made up of a 1,400-tonne steel arch and a 2,000-tonne supporting frame, left the port on the morning of Oct. 18 and anchored overnight. The newspapers said the towing convoy is expected to reach the Bạch Đằng Wharf area at about 14:00 to 15:00 on Oct. 19, with the closure ceremony expected on Oct. 21.

World · Notable newsPress ✓ 2 to 6 outlets per item · cause and death toll in Canada not confirmed

Explosion and fire at Canadian military base, US rejects Starlink accusation, Super Typhoon Champi heads toward Taiwan

VnExpress, VietnamPlus, Dân trí and Tuổi Trẻ reported an explosion and fire on the evening of Oct. 17 at the Wainwright military base in Alberta, Canada. Dân trí and VietnamPlus, citing Global News, said at least 7 people died, a figure not confirmed by the authorities; Defence Minister David McGuinty confirmed there were casualties and Prime Minister Mark Carney called it a serious incident. Tuổi Trẻ said the fire was brought under control at about 23:00. Dân trí and VnExpress reported that US Secretary of State Marco Rubio rejected the Ukrainian President’s accusation that Washington deliberately limited Starlink speeds, while President Trump said he did not know who gave the order and that it certainly was not him.

VietnamPlus, Nhân Dân and VnExpress said Super Typhoon Champi has reached level 16, gusting above level 17, with a probability of about 10 to 20% of entering the northeastern East Sea and an 80 to 90% chance of affecting Taiwan and the coast of China.
