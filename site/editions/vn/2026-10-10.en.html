Foreign investors net sell more than VND 9,000 billion in one session, almost all of it a negotiated trade in MCH shares; VN-Index falls 0.22%

More than 64 million Masan Consumer shares, about 4.9% of its capital, changed hands at VND 150,000; the buyer has not been confirmed, while Masan Group had registered earlier to buy more.

The VN-Index closed Friday, Oct. 9 at 1,735.09 points, down 3.88 points (0.22%), having dipped to 1,724.17 at one point before recovering, according to Nhân Dân, VietnamPlus, Hà Nội Mới and Tạp chí Điện tử & Ứng dụng. The papers put foreign investors’ net selling at between about VND 9,270 billion and VND 9,740 billion, but most of it came from a single negotiated trade in MCH, not from broad selling through matched orders.

According to the papers, more than 64.2 million MCH shares, equal to about 4.9% of Masan Consumer’s charter capital, were traded by negotiation at VND 150,000 a share, for a total of more than VND 9,641 billion (one paper gives VND 9,592 billion). One paper said the seller was a foreign investor and the buyer a domestic one. On Oct. 6, Masan Group announced that a subsidiary (Lepus) had registered to buy up to 73 million MCH shares to raise its holding from about 69.4% to about 74.9%; DailyDrop has not seen any paper confirm that Lepus was the buyer in the Oct. 9 trade.

Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng ends his Laos visit with 11 cooperation documents, heads to Cambodia

Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng held talks with Lao Prime Minister Saleumxay Kommasith on Oct. 8 and ended his official visit to Laos on the morning of Oct. 9, according to VietnamPlus, Dân Việt, Thanh Niên, Dân trí and VietNamNet. The two sides signed 11 cooperation documents, co-chaired the Vietnam-Laos Investment Promotion Conference, and witnessed the handover of a record on the population management and citizen identification system in Laos between the Ministry of Public Security of each country.

Vietnam pledged to build and donate two schools in Houaphanh and Champasak provinces, worth $2 million in total. Mr. Hưng also met General Secretary and President Thongloun Sisoulith and National Assembly Chairman Sonexay Siphandone. The visit to Cambodia began on Oct. 9; the press has not given details of the documents, so DailyDrop does not list what each one covers.

US eases sanctions on Russian diesel after Trump-Putin deal; Zelensky calls it a weak decision

On Oct. 9 Mr. Trump said on Truth Social that Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US and world markets, followed by 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that, according to CNBC and the Kyiv Independent. The US Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) issued a general license permitting the sale and import of Russian-origin diesel until April 7, 2027; the import bans of the European Union and the UK remain in place, according to Ship & Bunker.

Mr. Trump said the aim was to lower fuel prices for Americans before the midterm elections. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called it a weak decision and said letting Russia sell fuel was an investment in the war; Kaja Kallas, the EU’s representative for foreign affairs, said this was not the time to ease pressure on Russia. Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera said the deal would have little effect on the global energy crisis. The 300,000-tonne figure is Mr. Trump’s own claim; the 4.8 million tonne total is how some outlets add up the deliveries.

US stocks rise as oil cools, S&P 500 up to 7,811.54; gold gains 1.43% to $4,216.3

US stocks rose on Friday, Oct. 9 as oil prices cooled and tech shares recovered: the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, according to market data; Yahoo Finance reported the Dow up about 0.8% and the Nasdaq up about 0.6%, with all three indexes higher for the week after a volatile week.

WTI crude closed at $91.85 a barrel (+0.39%), while Brent was around $104 after the announcement on Russian diesel. Gold rose 1.43% to $4,216.3 an ounce. DailyDrop has not verified the Dow and Nasdaq closing figures against a second source.

Health · MedicinePress ✓ 5 outlets · figures per the National Children’s Hospital

Cases of influenza A climb fast among children; Ministry of Health orders treatment drugs secured and advises against taking Tamiflu on one’s own

According to the National Children’s Hospital, positive influenza A cases rose from 106 in week 35 to 779 in week 40, and the number of children admitted rose from 18 to 124, according to Sức khỏe & Đời sống, Tiền Phong and health outlets. In Da Nang, the Obstetrics and Pediatrics Hospital has received about 100 influenza A cases a day over the past two weeks.

The Ministry of Health warned that seasonal flu is rising and has not recorded any abnormal increase in severe cases or deaths. The Drug Administration of Vietnam asked that the supply of flu treatment drugs, especially for influenza A, be secured after reports of a Tamiflu shortage. Doctors advise that Tamiflu is a prescription drug, to be used only when prescribed for cases with complications or high-risk groups; people should not buy it on their own.
