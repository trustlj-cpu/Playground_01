Prime Minister: land prices and the real estate market must be regulated, intermediaries cut to lower fuel prices

Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng calls for control of social housing and rental housing prices and for removing intermediary costs; the press recorded his remarks on Oct. 18 and 19.

Thanh Niên and VnExpress reported on Oct. 19 that Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng said land prices and the real estate market must be regulated. According to VnExpress and Đại biểu Nhân dân, he demanded that prices of social housing and rental housing not be allowed to surge unreasonably; Sài Gòn Giải Phóng and PetroTimes relayed that he asked for the prices of this type of housing to be regulated and kept under control. CafeF and Đời sống và Pháp luật quoted him as saying land and real estate prices will certainly fall, while Dân trí took the line “manage tightly, housing prices cannot be allowed to rise unreasonably” as its headline. The Voice of Vietnam portal recorded him saying that the amendment of the auction law must be tied to social housing and rental housing prices.

A day earlier, Thanh Niên and Vietnam.vn relayed his call to firmly cut back intermediaries and lower fuel prices; VnEconomy summarized the demand to remove intermediary costs in order to ease price pressure on housing, fuel and health care. Earlier, on Aug. 19, VTC News and CafeF reported that he explained high housing prices as the result of land prices, borrowing costs and long delays in land allocation piling up in the cost of a unit; CafeF also recorded him asking for the draft Land Law and the housing and real estate business laws to be completed for submission to the National Assembly at the end of 2026. The outlets did not state a specific cut or a timeline for land and home prices.

Economy · BankingPress ✓ 5 outlets · the actual cut on existing loans at each bank has not been fully disclosed

Banks cut lending rates one after another following the Government’s directive; Governor asks that existing loans be reviewed too

Vietnam.vn and Laodong.vn recorded the Governor of the State Bank of Vietnam, Phạm Đức Ấn, asking for lending rates to be cut by 1.5–2% a year and for a review of cuts on both existing outstanding loans and new loans; CafeF reported similar content. After the directive, Thanh Niên said Sacombank cut lending interest rates by 2%, VietNamNet recorded Agribank cutting lending rates by up to 2% a year and BIDV launching a loan package from 6%, and VnEconomy reported that Vietbank also cut interest rates. Dân trí wrote on Oct. 19 that the first banks have cut deposit rates, and cited a warning that a bank that does not cut rates could have its credit room cut next year. The same day, Dân trí said the average lending rate remains high, with only 6 banks cutting in September.

Health · Ho Chi Minh CityPress ✓ 3 outlets · not yet reconciled with Tiền Phong’s Oct. 16 report (3 cases, 1 child dead)

Ho Chi Minh City: school with 4 meningococcal cases stops day boarding, more than 2,000 people given preventive antibiotics

Thanh Niên reported on Oct. 19 that Ho Chi Minh City recorded 4 cases of meningococcal disease at a school and that 2,031 people were given preventive antibiotics; VnExpress said more than 2,000 pupils and teachers at the school took preventive antibiotics. Dân trí and VnExpress reported that the school stopped day boarding after the cluster, while Dân trí cited information that the city has the cluster well under control and no new cases have emerged.

Earlier, Tiền Phong on Oct. 16 cited the city’s Department of Health on 3 pupils at Hai Bà Trưng Lower Secondary School in Hiệp Phước commune who contracted group B meningococcal disease on Oct. 14 and 15, one of whom, a 7th grader, died. Thanh Niên ran a piece in which a doctor points out what to watch for at a school with cases, and a piece on whether people who have had the vaccine still need preventive antibiotics.

Foreign affairs · EuropePress ✓ 5 outlets · content of the talks not yet fully disclosed

General Secretary and State President Tô Lâm departs to visit Denmark, the EU and Belgium, to speak at the University of Copenhagen

Thanh Niên and VnEconomy reported that General Secretary and State President Tô Lâm and his wife left on Oct. 19 for a state visit to Denmark and an official visit to the European Union (EU) and Belgium. According to Mekong ASEAN and VietnamPlus, citing the Vietnam News Agency (VNA), the trip runs from Oct. 19 to 24, coinciding with the 55th anniversary of Vietnam–Denmark diplomatic relations; at the EU he is expected to hold talks with the President of the European Council and the President of the European Commission, and in Belgium to hold talks with the Prime Minister and meet the King.

Nhân Dân điện tử and Kinh tế - Tài chính Online reported that he will give a policy speech at the University of Copenhagen, while the Central Committee of the Vietnam Fatherland Front reported that he and his wife met the King and Queen of Denmark. VnEconomy wrote that the visit widens room for cooperation in trade, investment and technology; VietnamPlus and Mekong ASEAN recalled that Vietnam–EU relations were raised to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership in January 2026.

Zelensky says US has resumed intelligence sharing, and sends a message to Trump as Russia strikes Kyiv

Dân trí and Tuổi Trẻ reported that President Volodymyr Zelensky thanked the US for resuming intelligence sharing with Ukraine; The Jerusalem Post and Il Sole 24 Ore recorded him saying Washington had continued to share intelligence after a pause. Dân trí reported the same day that a Russian air strike shook Kyiv, and VTC News on Oct. 14 recorded Mr. Trump saying the US still shares intelligence with Ukraine.

Bloomberg and Bloomberg Television quoted Mr. Zelensky as saying Mr. Trump is pressing Ukraine to make concessions to Russia; Thanh Niên and Dân trí ran reports of Mr. Zelensky sending a message to Mr. Trump amid the risk of a rift. WION recorded him saying the US and Ukraine are in constant contact after talks with envoys Witkoff and Kushner, while Times Now described a 40-minute call with Mr. Trump’s envoys after weeks of tension over a Russian diesel deal.
