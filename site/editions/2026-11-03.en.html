Binance net outflow nears 5 trillion won after withdrawals reopen… FSC tells Korean exchanges to add reserves

Once withdrawals reopened, users pulled out their assets, and the Financial Services Commission summoned domestic crypto exchanges on the 2nd.

From Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange, $6.3 billion, about 8.4 trillion won, drained out at the end of last month, and when withdrawals reopened, users began pulling out their money. The theft took place from wallets on 10 blockchains, and most of it moved within about 20 minutes. Binance has frozen about $3 billion worth and said the company, not users, would bear the loss. It says it found no evidence that a private key had leaked. Withdrawals resumed in stages from 3 p.m. Korea time on the 1st, and by 12:30 a.m. on the 2nd, $4.649 billion in withdrawals and $1.01 billion in deposits had been processed. The net outflow, money out minus money in, was about $3.64 billion, roughly 4.85 trillion won, which is 2.1% of the $171 billion in assets under management as of Oct. 28. A later tally put it as high as the 6 trillion won range, so the figure differs depending on when it is counted.

The North Korean hacking group ‘TraderTraitor’ has been named as the culprit. Blockchain analytics firm TRM said it assesses this “with high confidence,” Chainalysis also said the group is likely responsible, and the US Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) named the group in a statement on the 31st of last month. Binance has said it is working with investigators to identify the perpetrators. An early inquiry raised the possibility that a breach of an engineer’s laptop was the starting point, but how it happened has not been confirmed. In Korea, the Financial Services Commission (FSC) on the 2nd urgently summoned won-market exchanges and coin-market exchanges, including Upbit, Bithumb, Coinone, DigitalX and Gopax, and urged them to raise the share held in cold wallets, check deposits and withdrawals at all times and secure response staff, report hacking attempts promptly, and set aside additional reserves. A cold wallet is an offline wallet kept disconnected from the internet, and reserves are money set aside so that users can be compensated in the event of an incident. The FSC is also considering ways to block withdrawals quickly when a hack is suspected.

Passport numbers of presidential couple among data of 168 ‘Air Force One passengers’ left exposed on malware-scanning site for five months… Cheong Wa Dae: “Aware in advance, we responded”

It became known on the 2nd that the personal data of 168 Air Force One passengers, including the presidential couple, had been exposed for about five months on a paid malware-scanning website. On June 2, an Excel file titled ‘Air Force One passenger list’ was posted, containing the birth dates and passport numbers of President Lee Jae-myung and the president’s spouse, as well as data on senior officials such as Prime Minister Han Seong-sook (then Minister of SMEs and Startups) and National Security Office chief Wi Sung-lac, security guards and reporters who cover the president. It is the list from the June tour of Europe. Cheong Wa Dae said, “We took response measures after becoming aware of the content of the report in advance, and the Foreign Ministry and others plan further measures.” The Foreign Ministry takes the matter seriously and is investigating how it happened. It has not been settled whether hacking was the cause or whether internal staff uploaded the file directly in the course of a security check of the tour documents. Whether the file was downloaded during the exposure period, and when the government first learned of it, have also not yet been disclosed.

Jeon Han-gil, accused of spreading ‘North Korea involvement in 5·18’ claims, referred to Prosecution Service… police apply special-law violation, defamation and other charges

Police have sent to the Prosecution Service Jeon Han-gil, a YouTuber and former Korean-history instructor suspected of saying that North Korea was involved in the May 18 Democratic Uprising. The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency’s Metropolitan Crime Investigation Unit sent the case to the Seoul Central District Prosecution Service on the 30th of last month, and the referral became known on the 2nd. The charges applied center on violation of the Special Act on the May 18 Democratic Uprising and others, and also include defamation under the Information and Communications Network Act and violation of the Framework Act on Telecommunications. Jeon is suspected of saying on a YouTube channel in April that North Korea was involved in 5·18. The special act provides that spreading false facts about 5·18 through an information and communications network is punishable by up to five years in prison or a fine of up to 50 million won.

The Ministry of National Defense has said the claim of North Korean troop involvement is groundless, and the Supreme Court has also found it false. The same referral includes charges of spreading false information about President Lee Jae-myung and former Reform Party leader Lee Jun-seok. A referral is the stage where police finish an investigation and pass the case on, so the Prosecution Service decides whether to bring an indictment. Jeon’s side’s rebuttal has not been confirmed.

Democratic–Rebuilding Korea merger talk reignites… Jung Chung-rae sets “January next year at the latest” deadline; Lee Hae-sik: “Would setting it out of the blue help?”; Rebuilding Korea: “Not a ploy to change the subject”

Talk of a merger between the Democratic Party and the Rebuilding Korea Party has flared up again. Jung Chung-rae, former Democratic Party leader, set a deadline, saying, “The sooner the better, and it should be wrapped up by January next year at the latest,” and gave as the reason that those from the Rebuilding Korea Party would need at least six months to recruit voting members in the primary for the 2028 general election. Lawmaker Lee Hae-sik publicly criticized Jung, who is not free of responsibility for the failed merger, asking, “Would setting a merger deadline out of the blue, like a prophet, help?” The merger idea was first proposed in January, when Jung was party leader, but fell through amid backlash within the party.

Lawmaker Park Beom-kye, head of the Grand Unification Task Force, said it is time to consider pushing ahead quickly through a political decision by the two parties’ leaderships, but that there is leeway on the deadline until next summer. Rebuilding Korea Party floor leader Kim Joon-hyung said the party is not a means for the ruling party to change the subject and that a merger without principle would struggle to win support. The task force, which the Kim Min-seok leadership launched in August, has no visible results yet, and there is no scheduled agenda for merger talks.

Economy · Business✓ 4 outlets · Hoban’s current stake and whether the largest shareholder changes unconfirmed

Hoban files business combination notice with Fair Trade Commission over possible largest-shareholder status in Hanjin KAL… Hanjin KAL discloses ‘undetermined’; Chair Cho Won-tae buys 37 billion won more

Hoban Group has bought more shares of Hanjin Group’s holding company Hanjin KAL on the open market and, saying it may become the largest shareholder, has filed a business combination notice with the Fair Trade Commission. The Fair Trade Act requires a notice when a company above a certain size that holds 15% or more of a listed company acquires more shares and becomes its largest investor. Hoban’s stake was 20.15% as of July 10, lower than the roughly 20.6% of Hanjin Group Chair Cho Won-tae’s side, and the exact stake after the additional purchase has not been disclosed. Hanjin KAL filed an ‘undetermined’ disclosure saying that whether the largest shareholder changes has not been settled, and said it will disclose again by Dec. 1. Hoban said its purpose of holding has not changed from ‘simple investment.’

On the same day, Chair Cho disclosed a contract to buy 325,000 common shares of Hanjin KAL for 37.05 billion won. The price is 114,000 won per share, the trade will be made as an after-hours block trade between Dec. 3 and Jan. 1 next year, and the purpose is ‘strengthening responsible management.’ Chair Cho’s personal stake rises from 5.75% to 6.23%, and with related parties it comes to 21.06%. Hanjin Group explained that it had been preparing this beforehand. Delta Air Lines (14.90%) and the Korea Development Bank (10.58%) are classed as friendly shareholders of Chair Cho’s side, and together with that side they total 46.05%.
