Korea–Turkey summit adopts four MOUs on nuclear power and critical minerals… FTA Joint Committee raised to ministerial level

President Lee Jae-myung held talks at Cheong Wa Dae on the 3rd with Turkish President Erdogan, on a state visit to Korea, and said cooperation on nuclear power, defense and infrastructure would be expanded.

President Lee Jae-myung held a summit at Cheong Wa Dae on the 3rd with Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, who is on a state visit to Korea, and said, “We will make practical cooperation in strategic fields such as nuclear power, defense and infrastructure more substantive.” The two countries adopted four memoranda of understanding (MOUs) to strengthen cooperation in nuclear power, critical minerals and other areas. A memorandum of understanding is not a legally binding contract but an agreement that puts the direction of cooperation and promises on paper. On the occasion of the summit, the Korea–Turkey free trade agreement (FTA) joint committee will be raised from director-general level to ministerial level. The joint committee is a body that reviews how the agreement is being implemented and coordinates issues such as tariffs and customs clearance, so at ministerial level decisions are made one step higher.

The two sides also discussed cooperation on the Sinop nuclear power plant project that Turkey is pursuing, and a state banquet followed at the Cheong Wa Dae state guest house after the talks. At the banquet, President Lee said President Erdogan’s visit symbolizes the past, present and future of Korea and Turkey. The two countries also agreed to strengthen cooperation in AI and resources in addition to nuclear power. However, it has not yet been decided whether the documents adopted this time will lead to actual orders or investment contracts, or in what form Korean companies will take part in the Sinop nuclear project.

Kim Yo-jong calls North Korea being named in Binance hack a “grave political provocation”… “absurd rumor”

Kim Yo-jong, director of the General Affairs Department of the Workers’ Party of Korea, pushed back against the naming of North Korea in the large-scale hack of Binance, the world’s largest crypto asset exchange, calling it a “grave political provocation.” In a statement on the 2nd, Kim said, “For the US to mislead international opinion as if all the cyber fraud occurring around the world were our doing is clearly a grave political provocation against us,” and called tying the incident to North Korea a “habitual provocation” and an “absurd rumor.” The statement was released through North Korean state media on the 3rd. Binance estimated the damage from the incident at about $6.3 billion. In won that is in the 8 trillion range, or about 8.4 trillion won at an exchange rate of around 1,340 won. Kim did not mention specific response measures. However, Kim said, “We are keeping a close eye on the US continuously making various provocations against our state,” and “there will be matters to reconsider in various respects.” The incident also had a big impact in Korea, as net outflows continued even after Binance resumed withdrawals and the Financial Services Commission urged domestic exchanges to set aside more reserves. Beyond Kim’s statement, no position from North Korea has come out, and no independent confirmation of who actually carried out the hack has been released.

Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok apologizes to lawmakers over ‘three-way secret alliance with Shincheonji’ claim… report on Cho Hee-dae probe for the 5th shelved

Democratic Party of Korea leader Kim Min-seok apologized at a closed-door caucus at the National Assembly on the 3rd over the claim of a ‘three-way secret alliance of anti-Lee, divisionism and Shincheonji’ that was raised at the 8·17 party convention, aimed at former leader Jung Chung-rae’s side, saying, “There was no evidence of a secret alliance.” Kim said, “I do not think anyone in our party led such a thing in the course of Shincheonji’s activities,” and “I am deeply sorry to those who were harmed unintentionally.” The party had meant to report at the caucus that day the results of its own inquiry into allegations of mass joining of the party by Shincheonji members, but canceled the report and decided not to release the results. Jung demanded accountability, saying Kim should not apologize in hiding but “apologize publicly.”

At the same caucus, the Democratic Party put on hold its plan to report a request for a parliamentary investigation of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae to the plenary session on the 5th, and decided to gather more opinions. This came a week after it made pursuit of the investigation official. By contrast, the ‘fabricated-prosecution special counsel bill’ was unanimously adopted as the official party line, and the party aims to pass it at the plenary session on the 5th. People Power Party lawmaker Jung Jeom-sik said that if the bill is put to the plenary session on the 5th, the party will counter every bill with a filibuster. The National Conference of Judges’ Representatives will hold its regular meeting on Dec. 7.

Court revokes bail of Unification Church leader Han Hak-ja… 200 million won deposit forfeited after leaving hospital for ‘Cheonjeongung’

Criminal Division 13 of the Seoul High Court (presiding judge Kim Mu-sin) revoked on the 3rd the bail of Han Hak-ja, leader of the Unification Church, who is facing an appellate trial on charges including violation of the Political Funds Act, and also ordered the forfeiture of the 200 million won bail deposit paid by Han, which will revert to the state treasury. It came 15 days after bail was granted on the 19th of last month on condition of hospital treatment. Bail is a system that releases a detained defendant on conditions such as a deposit. It became known on the 2nd that Han left Seoul Asan Hospital, where treatment was being received, and went back and forth to the church facility ‘Cheonjeongung’ in Gapyeong, Gyeonggi Province, and the court judged that Han had left the residence designated by the court. The court cited Article 102, paragraph 2, items 2 and 5 of the Criminal Procedure Act as grounds for revocation.

With bail revoked, Han will stand trial in custody again. The appellate trial continues as before. The first trial earlier sentenced Han to two years in prison. How Han’s side will respond to this decision is not yet known.

Trump acknowledges strike on Iranian air defense sites, calls it a “small attack”… targets and damage not disclosed

US President Donald Trump said on the 3rd (local time) that US forces struck Iranian air defense facilities the night before. Trump described the strike as a “small attack,” saying it was carried out as soon as the facilities were completed. Neither the targets nor the scale of damage were disclosed. Earlier, Iranian state media had reported several explosions heard around Qeshm Island, a strategic point on the Strait of Hormuz. Iran’s military on the 3rd hinted at retaliation against US forces, saying it would “respond more strongly and broadly.”

The strike came one day before the US midterm elections and while the US and Iran were seeking to resume peace talks through mediators. US Vice President J.D. Vance had earlier said the outlook for a deal was optimistic. Iran’s security chief said on the 3rd that the US response to Iran’s demands for reopening the Strait of Hormuz and resuming peace talks was not convincing. The specific scale of damage from the strike and the timing and manner of any Iranian retaliation have not been confirmed.
