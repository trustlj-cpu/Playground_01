Musk joins petition for Jensen Huang’s return… report says mass-resignation letter in executives’ names is fake

Musk signed the petition for Huang’s return that followed Huang’s exit as CEO, and a report said that the mass-resignation letter circulating in executives’ names is fake.

Signatures are growing fast on an online petition to put Nvidia founder Jensen Huang back in the post of chief executive officer (CEO). The number of signatories is reported differently from outlet to outlet. Elon Musk also put his name to the petition, which began under the name ‘Stand with Jensen’, and on the 14th (US time) Musk said that Nvidia without Huang would lose key contracts in its supply chain. Nvidia has not said why Huang left the CEO post. There are reports that ties with a woman named Alice Huang, suspected of links to Chinese intelligence, came to light and that the board of directors had been investigating. Reports also conflict on the form of the exit, with some saying resignation and others dismissal. There are reports that chief financial officer Colette Kress took over as interim CEO, a post that runs the company until a permanent successor is named.

Documents reported to be fake have also been mixed in as the petition has grown. A report said that a letter supposedly issued by Nvidia’s executive team, saying they would leave the company en masse unless Huang was reinstated, is fake, and there is a point that it was stitched together from the letter employees sent during the OpenAI turmoil three years ago. Claims also emerged that employees joined the petition in large numbers, but the numbers conflict from report to report and have not been verified. There are reports that Huang explained to executives who asked about the relationship with Alice Huang that it was ‘ordinary customer communication’, and that this explanation does not match the facts. Some investors sent the board a letter asking it to consider a return, and Nvidia will report third-quarter results on the 17th, its first results without Huang. The government said GPU supply to Korea will be maintained. There has been no official announcement on whether the board is considering a return or who the successor will be.

North Korean hacking group Lazarus targeted 30 servers in Korea… government and financial institutions the targets

The hacking group Lazarus, reportedly linked to North Korea’s Reconnaissance General Bureau, has been found to have targeted 30 servers in Korea. The targets were reported to be servers in key sectors such as government agencies, financial institutions and telecoms. Lazarus was reported to have seen Korea’s IP address range, meaning the range of internet addresses assigned within the country, as a ‘promising’ target. The Reconnaissance General Bureau is the North Korean military’s foreign intelligence and covert operations agency. The 30 servers are the number picked out as attack targets, and how many were actually breached is not known. If one server is breached, it can become a foothold for entering other internal networks, so which servers they were matters more than how many. Lazarus is a group known for attacks aimed at financial institutions and the crypto-asset industry. Which institutions were targeted and whether information leaked have not been confirmed.

People Power Party demands special counsel over Democratic Party’s ‘Unification Church allegations after Shincheonji’

On the 14th the People Power Party demanded a special counsel over the ‘Unification Church allegations following Shincheonji’ surrounding the Democratic Party. The People Power Party claimed that if the Democratic Party rejects a special counsel, it amounts to admitting collusion with Shincheonji, and it said the Democratic Party’s survey results on the scale of Shincheonji enrollment cannot even be viewed, demanding that the source of the data be disclosed. A special counsel is a system in which a separate investigation team, not the prosecution, takes the case, and introducing one requires the National Assembly to pass a special counsel law. What exactly the Unification Church allegations are, and whether the Democratic Party will accept the demand for a special counsel, have not been confirmed.

Last December the positions were the reverse. When the Democratic Party introduced a Unification Church special counsel bill and included Shincheonji among the investigation targets, the People Power Party objected, calling it ‘diluting the issue’. The two parties also clashed over who holds the power to recommend special counsel candidates. This time, the dispute over the investigation targets has turned to aim at allegations on the Democratic Party’s side.

Economy · Prices✓ 3 outlets · regional differences in housing rent unconfirmed

October housing rent up 1.4%, biggest rise in 3 years 10 months… monthly rent biggest in 12 years 10 months

In the October consumer price trends released by the National Data Office on the 3rd, housing rent, which combines jeonse and monthly rent, rose 1.4% from a year earlier. It is the biggest rise in 3 years and 10 months. Jeonse rose 1.4% and monthly rent 1.5%, and the rise in monthly rent is the highest in 12 years and 10 months. It means housing rent that was 1 million won a month became 1,014,000 won. Housing rent is an item that reflects not only the monthly rent paid every month but also the rise in jeonse prices.

Overall consumer prices in October rose 2.8%, 0.1 percentage point lower than September (2.9%). Oil products jumped 14.9%, and the government estimated that without the oil price cap system, inflation would have reached 3.6%. Inflation excluding farm products and oil products, known as core inflation, rose 2.8%, above the Bank of Korea’s price target of 2%. Housing rent is an item that does not easily come down once it rises, so the path from next month onward is the key.

Finance · Individual Investors✓ 4 outlets · distribution of loans by individual unconfirmed

30% of ‘debt-fueled investing’ money in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix… overdraft balances top 44 trillion won

Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix account for about 30% of the margin loans balance on the domestic stock market. Margin loans are borrowing money from a brokerage to buy shares, and the amount tied up in the two stocks is about 7 trillion won. With both stocks’ prices having fallen sharply, there are warnings that because the borrowed money is concentrated in one place, forced selling could follow when prices wobble and amplify volatility.

Debt-fueled investing has also grown outside brokerages. Money individuals have borrowed through overdraft accounts topped 44 trillion won, the most in 4 years and 3 months. The explanation is that individuals who want to buy as prices fall are drawing on overdraft accounts too. An overdraft account charges interest only on what is used within the limit, but because the rate is variable the burden grows right away. With margin loans, if the price of the shares held as collateral falls below a certain line, the brokerage sells them on the borrower’s behalf to recover the debt.
