Nvidia board opens independent investigation into reports of foreign intelligence targeting CEO Jensen Huang… Huang admits to an affair; espionage and chip-smuggling claims unconfirmed

The board has launched an investigation with an outside legal team, and CEO Huang has admitted to an affair. Espionage ties and AI chip smuggling have not been officially confirmed.

Nvidia’s board said in a statement on the 8th (local time) that its independent directors will investigate, with the help of an outside independent legal team, the reports that foreign intelligence agencies targeted founder and chief executive Jensen Huang. The board said it saw no reason to believe national security or company secrets had been compromised, and also expressed confidence in CEO Huang and management. In an internal message, CEO Huang admitted to an affair, apologized to family and employees, and promised to cooperate with the investigation. Alice Huang (Chinese name Huang Le), named as the other party, is a 42-year-old Chinese businessperson with Hong Kong permanent residency and a former executive of the Singapore data firm Megaspeed. Foreign media reports have continued that U.S. intelligence authorities suspect a link between Alice Huang and Chinese intelligence, but this has not been officially confirmed, and China’s Foreign Ministry rebutted the espionage claim as groundless slander.

The issue is less private life than whether AI semiconductors subject to export controls flowed to China. A foreign press report on the 10th, citing a U.S. IT outlet, said Alice Huang was involved in supplying China with at least $7.5 billion (about 10 trillion won) worth of Nvidia chips, around 240,000 units including the H100, H200 and A100. The claim comes from a single report, and neither Nvidia nor U.S. authorities have confirmed it. In a statement on the 10th, Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said President Donald Trump had been briefed on the relationship a year earlier yet took no security measures, calling it “dereliction of duty on national security” and demanding a thorough investigation. The scope, deadline and outcome of the board’s investigation have not been disclosed.

Security · Troop deployment✓ 4 outlets · sending the unit to Hormuz operations requires a separate motion

Cabinet approves consent motion to extend Cheonghae Unit deployment, widening its mission area to the open sea south of Oman… Defense Ministry: “This is not involvement in war”; Iran warns

At a Cabinet meeting chaired by President Lee Jae-myung on the 10th, the government approved a consent motion to extend the Cheonghae Unit’s deployment. The motion widens the unit’s mission area from the existing Gulf of Aden and Somali waters to the open sea south of Oman, and adds one maritime patrol aircraft and the personnel to operate mine clearance equipment. According to the plan the Defense Ministry announced on the 5th, troop strength rises by 75, from within 320 to a maximum of 395, and the deployment period runs from National Assembly consent through Dec. 31, 2027. From the edge of the widened area to the Strait of Hormuz is about 650 km in a straight line, and the Gulf of Oman, which borders the strait, is not included in the area. The Defense Ministry cited worsening safety conditions such as rising piracy and said it does not take part in or intervene in war. The government’s judgment is that a separate deployment motion would be needed to send the unit on a freedom of navigation operation in the Strait of Hormuz. The Rebuilding Korea Party and the Progressive Party called it a de facto indirect deployment and demanded withdrawal, and Iran warned that it hopes to see no complicity in military aggression. The motion takes effect only with National Assembly consent.

Politics · Elections✓ 4 outlets · how the forgery happened and whether orders were given are under investigation

NEC special counsel: signs of concealment and forgery of tally sheets in Gyeonggi education superintendent election… search and seizure at Gwangju election commission in Gyeonggi

The election commission’s special counsel team (Special Counsel Lee Tae-han), which is investigating the ballot-paper shortage in the June 3 local elections, said on the 10th that it had confirmed signs that an election commission employee hid and forged tally sheets during the count for the Gyeonggi education superintendent election. According to the special counsel, while examining a case in which count results had been entered wrongly at the Gwangju election commission in Gyeonggi, signs emerged that an employee had concealed and forged tally sheets so that they differed from the actual count. The special counsel began a search and seizure at the Gwangju election commission from the morning of the same day.

The special counsel began its first compulsory investigation on Sept. 17 by searching 9 places including the central election commission, and has been investigating the commission broadly since. This case concerns the count result itself, separate from the ballot-paper shortage. How many employees were involved in the forgery, whose orders were behind it, and how the actual result differed have not been confirmed.

Politics · People Power Party✓ 3 outlets · basis of the rigging claim and the leadership’s explanation unconfirmed

People Power Party ethics committee member Hwang Gyeong-seong apologizes, claiming Han Dong-hoon’s discipline was “rigged”… Han: “The reality of a political purge is revealed”

People Power Party Central Ethics Committee member Hwang Gyeong-seong issued a statement on the 11th claiming that the party’s disciplinary actions against independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon and lawmaker Jin Jong-oh were “rigged discipline,” and apologized. Hwang said that on Oct. 27 the party audit office demanded a seal on a resignation letter, saying it was the leadership’s wish, and also claimed that the leadership of party leader Jang Dong-hyeok had demanded the resignation of all ethics committee members. Hwang also raised the suspicion of a secret agreement to rig the discipline between Jang and ethics committee chair Yoon Min-woo. The party audit office is reported to be considering reconstituting the Central Ethics Committee.

Han, aiming at Jang, said that “the reality of a political purge has been revealed,” and asked whether Jang should face punishment beyond resignation. Jin demanded Jang’s resignation. However, the claim that rigging took place rests on Hwang’s own statement. No official rebuttal or explanation from Jang or chair Yoon has been confirmed, and the content of the evidence Hwang says was secured has not been disclosed.

Politics · Confirmation hearing✓ 4 outlets · the defense of Kim Hyun-ji’s case by the law firm where the nominee had worked rests on a single report and is unconfirmed

Justice minister nominee Lee Yong-chul: “On withdrawal of the president’s case, now is the time to focus on establishing the truth”… past pay-cut penalty ending in a deferred sentence on a fine comes to light

Lee Yong-chul, head of the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, whom President Lee Jae-myung named as justice minister nominee on the 6th, answered reporters’ questions on the 11th. Asked about the possibility of withdrawal of the indictment in criminal cases involving President Lee Jae-myung, the nominee said now is the time to focus on establishing the truth, that the special counsel would be watched without preconceptions, and that it is not appropriate to comment on the many predictions and conjectures. The nominee also stated a position of realizing judicial justice through fair exercise of the power to prosecute.

Earlier, on the 10th, a court ruling revealed that in April 2015, while head of the Seongnam Industry Promotion Foundation, the nominee imposed on an employee a pay cut exceeding the limit in the work rules, and received a deferred sentence on a 700,000 won fine for violating the Labor Standards Act. The opposition points to the justice minister’s authority to direct and supervise the head of the Public Prosecution Office and is raising the possibility of withdrawing the indictment as a problem. The date of the confirmation hearing has not yet been confirmed. One report said the law firm where the nominee had worked defended the criminal case of Kim Hyun-ji, head of the president’s First Attached Office, but this comes from a single report and is unconfirmed, and whether the nominee was personally involved is not known.
