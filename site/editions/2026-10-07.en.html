Nuri lifts 15 satellites, four successes in a row.
This time the test was launching a flock

Liftoff at 12:25 p.m.; all five micro constellation satellites reached their target orbit. Nine of 10 CubeSats separated, one did not deploy. Contact confirmed with three by evening

Korea's Nuri launch vehicle (누리호) lifted off for the fifth time at 12:25 p.m. on Oct. 7 from the Naro Space Center and carried 15 satellites into orbit. The primary payload was five micro satellites, Nos. 2 through 6 of a satellite constellation built for national security and disaster monitoring; the secondary payload was 10 CubeSats from universities and companies. All five constellation satellites separated as planned, and nine of the CubeSats separated while one was not deployed, the Korea AeroSpace Administration (우주항공청) said. At about 1:08 p.m. satellite No. 6 made first contact with the King Sejong Station in Antarctica; No. 3 followed (3:27 p.m., the Svalbard ground station in the Arctic) and then No. 4. Contact with the rest is being confirmed in sequence, one orbit at a time.

From its second flight (2022) through its fifth, Nuri has now succeeded four times in a row, for a cumulative success rate of 80%. The space agency chief said reliability had "moved up a level" and set the sixth launch for the second half of next year, with a target of at least one launch a year through 2032. Since the fourth flight, Hanwha Aerospace has taken part in manufacturing and launch operations as the system integrator.

Four years of war. Did two companies set the price of gas?
SK Energy, HD Hyundai Oilbank face a 44 trillion won collusion case

An examiner at the Fair Trade Commission (공정거래위원회) has concluded that SK Energy and HD Hyundai Oilbank exchanged information on supply price, final price and sales policy for gasoline, diesel and kerosene for about four years, from February 2022 to March of this year, and that there are signs they agreed on prices themselves twice this March. The examiner recommended a corrective order and a surcharge. HD Hyundai Oilbank says "there was no collusion whatsoever"; SK Energy says it will "explain itself fully." The verdict comes after the commission's deliberation.

PoliticsPress ✓ 4 outlets · remarks attributed to speakers

Audit, day two — 'a shouting match every seven minutes' in Judiciary; pensions and abortion pills in Health

On the second day of the parliamentary audit, with 10 standing committees in session, one analysis reported that in the Judiciary Committee's audit of the Supreme Court a call to order came roughly every seven minutes and that the chairman's remarks took up a third of the proceedings. After People Power Party lawmaker Kim Tae-kyu was stripped of the floor, the ruling party demanded his 'expulsion' over what he said to the chairman outside the chamber ('Did you scrape yourself on Owl Rock?' among other things). The Defense Committee split over the DMZ landmine accident: 'first DMZ mine clearance' on one side, 'delay and cover-up' on the other.

Behind the noise there was substance. The Health and Welfare Committee's first day took up a pause in the National Pension Service's asset rebalancing and the introduction of abortion medication. It is a question of whether my pension holds stocks longer, and whether medical practice changes.

CourtsPress ✓ 6 outlets · ruling summary attributed

Yoon Suk Yeol acquitted on appeal in 'free polling' case — the two-year sentence is overturned

The Seoul High Court's 7th Criminal Division (presiding judge Koo Hoe-geun) acquitted former President Yoon Suk Yeol and Myung Tae-kyun of violating the Political Funds Act. The trial court had given them two years and 18 months in prison. The appeals court said there was "no evidence whatsoever of an agreement, request or consultation to conduct and provide opinion polls free of charge," and that intent to donate political funds was hard to establish. What was received, it found, was not money but 'political benefit': a read on public opinion, political advice, introductions to influential politicians.

Former first lady Kim Keon Hee was acquitted earlier on the same charge. Because the two courts read the same facts in opposite ways, the Supreme Court's judgment will set the standard. This case is separate from Yoon's other trials, including the insurrection case.

SecurityPress ✓ 5 outlets · Defense Ministry testimony at audit attributed

'They crossed the line' will go on paper. Defense Ministry seeks a joint UNC investigation of North Korea's MDL incursions

At the Defense Committee's parliamentary audit on Oct. 7, the Defense Ministry said it would conduct a joint investigation with the United Nations Command (유엔군사령부), which administers the armistice, into North Korea's crossings of the Military Demarcation Line (MDL) during its 'border fortification' work, formalize any violation, and send the North a 'notice of Armistice Agreement violation' demanding a halt and restoration. The step follows the Sept. 21 accident in which soldiers of the Army's 25th Division were wounded by North Korean landmines while searching the DMZ. After the North's Foreign Ministry warned of a "strong response to even a 1 mm incursion," the government answered with "corresponding measures."

In the audit room, the ruling party stressed the achievement of the 'first DMZ mine clearance' and the opposition a 'slow investigation and attempted cover-up.' The 'empty rifles' guard-duty controversy from yesterday's front page came up again in the same session.
