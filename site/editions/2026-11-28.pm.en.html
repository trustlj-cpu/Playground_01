Police search Justice Ministry, Prosecution Service over alleged leak of investigation files to lawmaker Han Dong-hoon

On the 27th police moved with a compulsory investigation to establish how the indictment plan of former Justice Minister nominee Kim Seung-won came to be in the hands of independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon.

On the 27th police carried out a search and seizure at the Justice Ministry, the Prosecution Service and other bodies. It is a compulsory investigation to check the allegation that independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon received prosecution investigation materials. The document police are examining is the indictment plan of former Justice Minister nominee Kim Seung-won. An indictment plan is an internal document in which a prosecutor sets out the charges and applicable laws before sending a case to trial, and it is material that must not leave the investigating authority. The Justice Ministry has explained that if this document went outside, it could amount to disclosure of official secrets or a violation of the Personal Information Protection Act. The search and seizure is a procedure meant to trace backward where and how the document left, and through whom it reached Han.

The allegation surfaced at the National Assembly in September. At the interpellation of the government on the 9th, acting Justice Minister Lee Jin-su answered that leaking investigation materials could constitute a crime, and the Justice Ministry’s Prosecution Bureau began checking how it happened. Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Dong-a filed a complaint against Han and an unidentified provider of the materials with the National Office of Investigation of the National Police Agency, and police opened an investigation. Han has said that no materials were ever received from prosecutors, and criticized this search by asking why police look for the tipster first. It is not known what was seized or whether a date has been set for questioning Han. How the document left is the central issue of the investigation.

Former Chief of Staff Kim Dae-ki gets 2 years, suspended for 3, at first trial over diversion of residence relocation budget

Criminal Division 36 of the Seoul Central District Court (presiding judge Lee Jung-yeop) on the 27th sentenced former Presidential Chief of Staff Kim Dae-ki to two years in prison, suspended for three years, on charges of ordering the illegal diversion of budget during the relocation of the presidential residence under the Yoon Suk Yeol government. Yoon Jae-sun, former general affairs secretary, and Kim O-jin, former facilities management secretary, each received one year in prison, suspended for two years, and former Interior and Safety Minister Lee Sang-min was acquitted. The charge is abuse of authority and obstruction of rights: that to cover the 2022 residence construction costs, 2.09 billion won of the Ministry of the Interior and Safety budget was made to be used for other purposes. A suspended sentence sets a punishment but defers its execution for a set period, and if the person stays out of trouble during that period, the sentence loses effect. The comprehensive special counsel had earlier sought four years in prison for Kim, and the sentence handed down is half of that. The full reasoning of the sentence and whether there will be an appeal are not yet known.

KOSDAQ surges 8.13% to close… buy-side sidecar triggered intraday, 41st on the KOSDAQ this year

The KOSDAQ closed on the 27th at 708.99, up 8.13%. At 10:22 a.m., KOSDAQ 150 futures were up 6.06% from the previous day’s close and the KOSDAQ 150 index up 6.65%, and a buy-side sidecar was triggered. A sidecar is a device that halts the effect of buy orders from program trading for five minutes when futures prices move sharply. A KOSDAQ buy-side sidecar is triggered when KOSDAQ 150 futures are up 6% or more and the index up 3% or more for one minute. This was the 41st KOSDAQ sidecar this year, 23 on the buy side and 18 on the sell side. Even after the trigger, at 11:02 the index traded at 694.37, up 5.90%, and the gain grew to 8.13% by the close.

The KOSPI also rose 4.34% the same day and closed at 4,915.68. Foreign investors turned net buyers on the KOSPI and lifted the market, and expectations that a market stabilization fund would be deployed were mentioned in and around the market. It has not been confirmed that the government said it would actually run the fund. The 27th was a Friday, so the next trading day is Monday the 30th, when it will be clear whether the gain continues.

Construction starts on GS AI data center in Donghae… President Lee attends; first gigawatt-class project, 30 trillion won in total

GS Group held a groundbreaking ceremony for an AI data center on the 28th at the Bukpyeong Second General Industrial Complex in Donghae, Gangwon. President Lee Jae-myung and GS Group Chairman Huh Tae-soo attended. It is the first of the three mega projects the government announced in June to move into construction of a gigawatt (GW)-class AI data center. GS has said it will directly invest a total of 30 trillion won in the project, and that including computing equipment such as GPUs the project size reaches up to 120 trillion won. One gigawatt is a power scale similar to the output of one large nuclear reactor. The first phase is 1.2 GW and will cost 15 trillion won, with completion targeted for 2029, and the full plan is up to 2.4 GW.

President Lee called it the largest infrastructure in Asia and said all capabilities would be mobilized so that the Republic of Korea becomes a leading nation that sets global standards and shows the direction. Chairman Huh Tae-soo said this place will become a future landmark of the AI industry. When the project is completed, 71,000 direct and indirect jobs are projected. How power will be supplied and the equipment procurement schedule were not confirmed in published materials.

Internet Post Office hacked, personal data of registered-mail customers leaked… 113,000-plus people in a single day’s records alone

The Internet Post Office, run by the Seoul Central Post Office, was hit by a hacking attack, and personal data of customers who use registered mail leaked. The confirmed scale is more than 113,000 people from a single day’s records alone. Tallies differ slightly, all in the 110,000 range. The leaked data was reported to be that of people sending or receiving registered mail.

Because the figure was confirmed from a single day’s records, whether data from other dates also got out remains the open issue. How far the leaked items go, whether name and address, and whether post office financial services usage information is included are not confirmed in published materials. The time and route of the intrusion and the way individual notice will be given to victims are also not yet known.
