British Prime Minister Burnham says Russia interfered in the 2016 Brexit referendum and points to Iran over an incident at RAF Fairford

His statement reopens an old debate. At the same time he calls next month’s summit with the EU in Brussels a first step towards closer ties.

Andy Burnham, prime minister of the United Kingdom, says he was told after taking office that Russia interfered in the 2016 referendum on membership of the European Union (Reuters, The Guardian, HLN). Reuters reported his statement on Sunday evening; HLN carried it on Monday morning and wrote that Burnham “confirms” the Russian interference. The Guardian calls it an unexpected step that reopens the debate over whether the Kremlin tried to influence the vote. What information Burnham was given exactly and whether it changed the outcome is not confirmed; DailyDrop has seen no underlying documents. The Brexit referendum of June 23, 2016 produced a narrow majority for leaving the EU.

Burnham also spoke in the House of Commons on Monday. He said the current relationship with the EU is “not good enough” and called next month’s summit in Brussels a first step towards closer ties, although The Guardian warns that tensions over trade could complicate the talks. On an incident at RAF Fairford, a British air force base, he said, according to Reuters, that Iran’s Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) was probably behind it. DailyDrop has not been able to establish which incident it was or what evidence there is; a second outlet does not confirm the statement. For Belgian readers the main point is that the summit takes place in Brussels, the city of the European institutions and the NATO headquarters.

Train strike over: rail service back to normal, nine minors arrested after riots at Brussels-South

The 24-hour rail strike ended on Monday evening with the last trains of the day; traffic is back to normal afterwards (La Libre). Between Brussels-South and Denderleeuw traffic was also at a standstill after a body was found next to the tracks; since Monday 10 p.m. all tracks are back in service and on Tuesday morning traffic can run normally, Infrabel says via HLN (HLN, RTBF).

HLN also reports that the riots at Brussels-South are over: nine minors were arrested and a trail of destruction remains. A youth court judge is sounding the alarm because rioters were allowed to go home for lack of space in a youth institution (HLN). On which evening the nine youths were arrested is not in the headlines DailyDrop has seen.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · attacks and interception according to the parties themselves, not independently confirmed

After the deadly attack on Riyadh airport, the Houthis claim a new missile attack on Monday evening; Washington and London advise against travel to Saudi Arabia

Early on Monday, HLN reported that the United States and the United Kingdom are advising their citizens against flying to Saudi Arabia after a new deadly attack on Riyadh airport. Reuters wrote that the sky above the Saudi capital was largely empty, that airlines cancelled flights and that top oil-industry figures were taken off the programme of a congress in Riyadh. Turkey, Pakistan, Egypt and Saudi Arabia condemned the attacks (Reuters). The Saudi authorities are investigating why the air defence could not stop the attack (AP).

On Monday evening the Houthis, the Iran-backed movement in Yemen, claimed a new attack with ballistic missiles on the same airport (HLN, De Morgen). According to a statement from the Saudi-led coalition, relayed by FinancialJuice, one missile was shot down; La Libre reports heavy explosions and a warning message on phones in Riyadh. HLN writes that Iran has also attacked a supertanker again in the Strait of Hormuz; the Iranian Revolutionary Guard says it is the third in two days (HLN, Fars via FinancialJuice). Casualty figures for the new attack are not confirmed. In the attack of Saturday, October 10, 12 people were killed, according to the Saudi authorities (AP, Bloomberg).

World · Ukraine · EnergyPress ✓ 6 outlets · Trump’s statements according to his own post on Truth Social

Trump defends the diesel deal with Russia despite criticism from both parties; Zelensky says Russia is ignoring the energy ceasefire

Donald Trump on Monday defended his diesel deal with Vladimir Putin: “I made the right choice” (CNBC, Reuters, Quartz). Lawmakers from both parties are complaining about the deal (Reuters). According to Reuters, Washington hopes the deal will make Moscow more flexible in the peace talks. On Truth Social, Trump wrote that the European Union paid Russia 250 billion dollars for energy and that the media do not report on it. Russia says it has supplied Europe with 320 billion dollars’ worth of hydrocarbons since 2022 (La Libre, Ouest-France, 7sur7); whether the two figures measure the same thing is not confirmed. According to Reuters, Russia says it can export up to 3.5 million tonnes of diesel a month by the end of the year, but Bloomberg writes that Russia cannot supply as much as Trump needs, because Ukrainian attacks hit the refineries.

The energy ceasefire proclaimed by Trump has already been violated, Zelensky says: Russia hit energy installations, he says (Reuters, The Hill, PBS, AP). Ukraine will hit back if Moscow violates the ceasefire, he said (Bloomberg). Moscow is saying little, the BBC reports. NBC reports that energy prices rose because the ceasefire moved neither Russia nor Ukraine. Reuters writes that the EU approved almost 1,600 new sanctions listings against Russia.

World · SecurityPress ✓ 5 outlets · accusation against Russia by the Polish government, not independently confirmed

Two Turks arrested in Germany after fire at a Starlink station in Poland; NATO’s top commander says a Russian attack would be “completely pointless”

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk announced that two Turks have been arrested in Germany who may be responsible for the September fire at a Polish ground station for Starlink satellite communications. Poland calls it hybrid warfare and blames Russia (HLN, Reuters); evidence of a Russian role has not been publicly confirmed. The German interior minister warned that Germany is experiencing a “shadow war” that goes beyond the threats of the Cold War, and the Czech intelligence chief said Russia can carry out riskier operations in NATO countries (Reuters).

The American general Grynkewich, NATO’s top commander, gave a rare speech and said a Russian attack on NATO would be “completely pointless” and that NATO can match every step on the escalation ladder (Reuters, GVA, HLN, De Standaard); De Standaard writes that in doing so he contradicts Trump. NATO’s headquarters is in Brussels. On Monday France boarded a ship south of the island of Réunion that is believed to belong to the Russian shadow fleet, President Macron said (HLN, De Morgen).
