National demonstration Friday: about 140 flights cancelled at Brussels Airport, 4 in 10 De Lijn buses and trams not running

The ABVV and ACV are protesting against the policy of the De Wever government, with the drawing up of the budget as the direct trigger. On Monday, October 12, actions in schools follow.

Brussels Airport expects about 140 cancelled flights on Friday, October 9, report Paris Match Belgique, Econostrum and Déplacements Pros. The cause is that the ground handler Alyzia has fewer staff because of the national demonstration by the unions ABVV and ACV against the measures of the federal government. Alyzia serves 21 airlines; at Brussels Airlines it concerns about 40% of flights. Aviapartner, the other ground handler, would not be affected for now.

Public transport is disrupted too: the Brussels MIVB expects a heavily disrupted network and the NMBS advises travelers to consult the journey planner, which is updated about 24 hours in advance. According to VRT NWS, 4 in 10 De Lijn buses and trams are not running. The socialist education union ACOD announces actions in schools on Friday and on Monday, October 12; the direct trigger is the drawing up of the budget. DailyDrop has not yet seen official turnout figures.

Pupil protests in Liège: already 107 arrests since October 1, 53 on Tuesday alone

Liège police counted 53 arrests on Tuesday, October 6, 7 of them judicial and 46 administrative. Since Thursday, October 1, the total is 107 arrests: 27 judicial arrests and 80 administrative arrests, report La Libre and L’Avenir. Al Jazeera writes that more than a hundred people were rounded up or detained in total; the sources differ on what exactly counts as an arrest. On Monday 18 officers were lightly injured; the police used tear gas and water cannon.

The pupils are protesting against the rising cost of education, overcrowded classes, a lack of materials and a measure that requires teachers in the last three years of secondary school to teach 22 hours in front of the class instead of 20. Paris Match Belgique explains that the movement spilled over to Belgium after the French example. In Brussels, witnesses complain of police violence, according to BX1; the police say rioters had mixed in among the demonstrators. DailyDrop could not verify that independently.

Fed minutes: all 19 members backed the rate hike, most expect one more this year; US long-term rate at its highest in more than twenty years

The minutes of the Federal Reserve, published on Wednesday, October 7, show that all 19 members of the policy committee backed a rate hike of 0.25 percentage points on September 16, to a range of 3.75% to 4.00% (Babypips, Finance Magnates, Investing.com). Most members expect another hike this year, but Fed officials have said since then that they can wait. The market puts the chance of a hike in October at 20% to 25%.

The US ten-year yield rose above 5.36% intraday, the highest level in more than twenty years, linked to higher oil prices and concern about government finances (Anadolu, Herald). All three major US stock indexes closed lower. Fed staff estimate August PCE inflation at 3.8% year on year, core inflation at 3.4%. The sources differ on the reaction of the dollar, which barely moved.

Nobel Prize in Chemistry goes to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for research into the “handedness” of molecules

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry goes on Wednesday, October 7, to the Frenchman Henri Kagan (Université Paris-Sud in Orsay) and the Japanese Kenso Soai (Tokyo University of Science), report Khaleej Times, Ynetnews and LatestLY. The Nobel Committee awards the prize for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis. Each receives half of 12 million Swedish kronor, about $1.2 million.

Committee chair Heiner Linke tied the prize to chirality: molecules can have forms that mirror each other, like a left and a right hand. That matters for medicines, because one form may work and the other not, or may be harmful. Secondary sources describe the work as proof that a small excess of one form can be amplified during a reaction. We found no primary source for that wording.

Society · PolicePress · one outlet · the League’s position according to VRT

Antwerp police want to detain known nuisance-makers for up to 12 hours, even though they have done nothing wrong

The Antwerp police want to place known nuisance-makers who are on a list under immediate administrative arrest anywhere in the city for up to 12 hours, even if at that moment they are not committing an offense or disturbing the peace, VRT NWS reports. The measure applies across the whole city, but is applied mainly in the Northern district. The aims are liveability, reporting people without residence rights to the Immigration Office (Dienst Vreemdelingenzaken) and referring people with addictions to help.

The Human Rights League (Liga voor Mensenrechten) calls the approach unlawful. The law on the police function allows an administrative arrest, but only if someone actually disturbs public order, VRT writes. The League denies that anyone may be held for 12 hours because he is on a list. DailyDrop saw no second independent outlet and no ruling by a judge.
