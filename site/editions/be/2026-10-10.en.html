Society · Trade unionsPress ✓ 3 outlets · turnout per police and unions

30,000 to 50,000 demonstrators in Brussels against government policy; riots with masked youths, shared bikes set on fire

The national ABVV and ACV demonstration itself passed off peacefully. Alongside it, hundreds of masked youths sought confrontation with the police. An initial tally speaks of about twenty arrests.

The national demonstration of Friday, Oct. 9 in Brussels drew between 30,000 and 50,000 people according to police and unions, report Paris Match Belgique and BX1. The march set off around 11:00 at Central Station and ended at South Station. Many participants came from education. The ACV took part and also staged actions at the offices of the five government parties. The VRT wrote that shared bikes and scooters ended up on the bonfire en masse: one operator puts the cost at about 40,000 euros, for the hardware alone.

Hundreds of masked youths in black sought confrontation with the police, according to reports, and the police used water cannon and tear gas. Street furniture was damaged and an MIVB bus was wrecked at South Station. An initial tally counts 18 judicial arrests (13 minors, 5 adults) and 3 administrative arrests. The police seized Molotov cocktails, petrol, fireworks, a knife and a hammer. The general secretary of the ABVV says he has received testimonies about sometimes unjustified violence and complains that the attention paid to the incidents overshadows the demands. DailyDrop has not yet confirmed the final arrest figure.

World · Nobel PrizePress ✓ 5 outlets · UN and EU reactions as reported

Navi Pillay wins the Nobel Peace Prize; hours later Washington sanctions the International Criminal Court

On Friday, Oct. 9, the Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to the South African jurist Navi Pillay (85), a former judge of the International Criminal Court (ICC), president of the Rwanda tribunal and UN High Commissioner for Human Rights from 2008 to 2014. The committee presented the prize as a message to those who want to undermine international legal institutions (Khaleej Times, Arab News, VRT NWS).

Shortly afterwards, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced sanctions against the ICC as an institution. Any assets in the US are blocked, and American companies and individuals have six months to wind down their dealings with the Court (ADN/AP, MercoPress). The UN spokesperson spoke of a serious blow to international criminal law and the EU called the step highly regrettable. The reports link the timing to the prize but say the American campaign against the Court has been under way for some time; no source draws a direct connection.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 5 outlets · volumes per Trump

Trump says Russia is supplying diesel in bulk to the US and the world market, America eases sanctions; Zelensky: “an investment in the war”

After a conversation with President Putin, Donald Trump said on Friday, Oct. 9 that Russia will immediately supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the American and world market, followed by 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that; a further 3 million tonnes depends on the Russian refineries. In total that comes to about 4.8 million tonnes (The National, MercoPress, Ynetnews). The US Treasury Department issued a temporary licence allowing Russian diesel, loaded from Friday, to be traded without sanctions until April 2027. An Italian source speaks of six months. Who pays for the shipments and when the fuel will become available, the White House has not yet clarified.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called it a weak decision by strong partners and an investment in the war, because it allows the war to be prolonged (MercoPress, Avvenire). The announcement comes a few weeks after Trump signed a sanctions law against the Russian energy sector. Democratic Congressman Don Beyer accused him of letting Russian fossil fuels off the hook after all. Trump himself wrote about the diesel supply on Truth Social. For Belgian drivers, the diesel price is sensitive to the international market; no direct effect at the pump is visible yet.

Politics · Federal budgetPress · one outlet · positions per VRT NWS

Few still believe in a budget deal by Tuesday: MR blocks VAT reform, Vooruit and Les Engagés want contribution from the wealthy

According to VRT NWS, almost nobody still believes that Prime Minister De Wever will have a budget deal by Tuesday, Oct. 13. Several government parties are struggling with his proposals to find at least 10 billion euros. The MR does not want a VAT reform, while Vooruit and Les Engagés think the wealthy should contribute more. Since the joint meeting, the talks have been running through bilateral rounds between the prime minister and each government party separately.

The Monitoring Committee states that the government must find at least 7.7 billion euros more; De Wever is aiming for 10 billion. The next deadline falls at the end of October: if it fails then, the government will have to work with provisional twelfths for the second time in a row. DailyDrop saw no official confirmation of a new meeting date for the weekend.

Markets · Stock marketsPress ✓ 2 sources · closing prices per Belga and market data

Brussels bourse rebounds 1.02% after heavy Thursday, Wall Street and gold rise; US long-term yield remains the worry

The BEL 20 rose 1.02% on Friday, Oct. 9 to 5,454.74 points, after the 2.31% fall on Thursday (La Libre, Belga). Azelis and Aperam each gained more than 4%, and argenx recovered about 3% after the blow the day before. Melexis and Lotus Bakeries were the only stocks in the index that closed lower. European bourses closed higher, helped by calmer government bond markets after a week full of unrest over the oil price and the budgetary and political situation in France.

In New York the S&P 500 closed 0.59% higher at 7,811.54 points, WTI closed 0.39% higher at $91.85 and gold rose 1.43% to $4,216.3. The American 30-year bond was sold at 5.618% in an auction on Thursday, the highest level since August 2000, according to RoboForex; the firm points to the consequences for the euro and the dollar. The euro recovered slightly to $1.1206 according to the ECB reference rate.
