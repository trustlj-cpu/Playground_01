Simón strengthens to a Category 3 hurricane off Colima and Jalisco; Sheinbaum urges people to take shelter and the Army activates Plan DN-III-E in Michoacán

The rains have already caused flooding in Acapulco and one death in Guerrero. What remains undefined is where and with what force it will make landfall: the media speak of Category 3 or 4 between the weekend and Monday.

Simón intensified into a Category 3 hurricane on Friday night, about 245 kilometers from Manzanillo, Colima, according to El Comentario; El País, La Jornada and El Universal agreed on the category. President Claudia Sheinbaum asked the public to take shelter and evacuate vulnerable areas, and Infobae counted torrential rain forecast in five states. The Army activated Plan DN-III-E in Michoacán, the protocol for assisting the population in disasters, with 1,815 troops, El Financiero reported, and temporary shelters were opened in Colima. In Puerto Vallarta, the authorities set up three command posts and shopkeepers along the seafront boardwalk protected their premises with planks and sandbags, according to El Universal.

The landfall forecast remains open. El País and El Financiero published that Simón will make landfall as a Category 3 or 4 over the weekend, and El Universal reported that it could do so in Puerto Vallarta as a Category 4. On the Saffir-Simpson scale, that level implies sustained winds of between 210 and 250 km/h. Expansión recalled that the SMN had forecast winds of up to 215 km/h for Saturday, and CNN en Español cited the authorities: Simón could hit Mexico twice as a hurricane. Waves of more than eight meters and extraordinary rainfall are expected in Jalisco and Colima, with a risk of flooding and landslides (El Universal). The CFE warned of possible blackouts in Jalisco, Colima and Guerrero (Ámbito), a figure and a scope that only that outlet reported. Because the projections change from one bulletin to the next, neither the time nor the landfall point is confirmed.

The damage has already begun. El Economista reported flooding and swell in Acapulco, and El Universal an elderly man dead in Chilpancingo, Guerrero, because of the rains. The United States issued an alert for its citizens in Mexico (El Economista) and Aeroméxico allowed passengers to change flights to the Pacific (El Universal). Separately, Hurricane Isaías made landfall as a Category 2 in Florida, with 5,000 troops deployed, according to El Universal and El Economista. For anyone living on the coast of Colima, Jalisco, Michoacán or Guerrero, what is useful today is simple: follow only the notices from the SMN and Civil Protection, locate the nearest temporary shelter, keep documents, water and batteries at hand, and postpone road trips to the coast. Meteored added that Tropical Storm Rachel could reach Baja California on Sunday with winds of up to 120 km/h; that is a forecast from a single outlet.

IMSS workers block Mexico City avenues for hours; police push them back and Sheinbaum sets out on a tour of hospitals

IMSS workers cut key roads in the capital on Friday, including Paseo de la Reforma, Eje 3 Oriente, the Viaducto and the La Raza area, where Infobae counted six hours of blockade. They demand a pay raise and the provision of supplies, medicines and medical equipment, according to El Universal and El País. In Monterrey, other workers rejected a pay increase of 2.9%. The Citizen Security Secretariat (SSC) reported that its police “channeled” the protesters in order to clear the streets, and Reforma and Infobae reported that by early morning the protests had pulled back; Infobae showed the workers singing the national anthem. UnoTV reported that the protesters made way for a woman taking her mother to the emergency room. The IMSS said its units are operating normally, according to El Universal. Meanwhile, Sheinbaum began in Hidalgo a tour of IMSS-Bienestar hospitals, the program that serves people without social security, to “listen” to grievances; Radio Sonora placed it in 23 states. IMSS-Bienestar and the IMSS are different institutions, and it is not confirmed that the government has responded to the demands.

The IPC rises 1.63% and ends the week with gains, but the peso weakens to 18.39 per dollar; Wall Street advances

The S&P/BMV IPC gained 1.63% on Friday and closed at 66,048.57 points, driven by Grupo México, according to El Economista, which described it as a positive close to the week. The peso, by contrast, lost ground: the dollar ended at 18.39 pesos, 1.29% more than on Thursday. During the session it reached 18.44 (El Financiero) and nearly 18.50 (Expansión), and El Economista noted that the peso has fallen 9% over five consecutive weeks of losses. In New York, the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq gained 0.64% to 27,366.17, and the Dow Jones 0.83% to 51,654.95. The yield on the 10-year Treasury bond closed at 5.244%.

WTI rose 0.39% to 91.85 dollars a barrel, and gold gained 1.43% to 4,216.3 dollars an ounce. Reuters reported that US consumer confidence worsened further in October. On monetary policy, El Financiero published that Banxico defies criticism of further rate cuts; this is a single outlet and we did not verify the detail. The Finance Ministry, for its part, cut the IEPS on gasoline (see story).

Security · SinaloaPress ✓ 5 outlets · death tolls vary by outlet

Two brawls at El Castillo prison in Mazatlán leave 12 dead according to reports; Sinaloa will build a new prison

A new brawl at El Castillo prison in Mazatlán left two inmates dead on Friday, El Financiero reported, barely hours after Sheinbaum promised more security. El Financiero and Milenio reported that the governor of Sinaloa confirmed an urgent meeting with Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch. Sinaloa will build a new prison after two clashes that left 12 dead in total, according to El Financiero. The first reports of the earlier clash spoke of six dead and 15 injured (UnoTV); El Economista reported at least 10.

Because the figures change from one outlet to another, the full official toll is not confirmed. It was also not made clear when construction would begin or what it would cost.

Navi Pillay wins the Nobel Peace Prize and hours later the US sanctions the International Criminal Court; Trump speaks of an “indelible stain”

South African jurist Navi Pillay, the UN high commissioner for human rights and a former judge of the International Criminal Court (ICC), won the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize, BBC, Bloomberg and El Universal reported. A few hours later, Reuters reported ahead of others that Washington had imposed sanctions on the ICC, and AP and BBC confirmed the measures. The ICC prosecutor’s office condemned them and said its work will continue (Reuters); a group of countries signed a joint statement of support for the court (Reuters). The ICC rejected the sanction and warned of an attack on the legal order, according to El Financiero.

Donald Trump said Norway bears an “indelible stain” for not giving him the prize (Reuters, BBC, The Guardian). Pillay joked that she might share the prize with him, according to El Financiero. Separately, Reuters reported that Trump launched a committee to investigate Fed governor Lisa Cook.
