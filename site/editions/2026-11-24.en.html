President Lee names Kang Geum-sil intelligence chief… mid-size reshuffle with Im Ung-sun at Foreign Affairs, Hong Ik-pyo at Unification, Han Jun-ho at Interior, Kang Yu-jung at Culture

Former Justice Minister Kang Geum-sil tapped to lead the National Intelligence Service; nominees for the foreign, unification, interior and culture ministries also named at once

On the 23rd, President Lee Jae-myung announced a mid-size reshuffle, nominating former Minister of Justice Kang Geum-sil as director of the National Intelligence Service, Im Ung-sun, second deputy director of the National Security Office, as Minister of Foreign Affairs, and Hong Ik-pyo, former senior presidential secretary for political affairs, as Minister of Unification. For Minister of the Interior and Safety the pick is Han Jun-ho, a second-term lawmaker of the pro-Lee faction, and for Minister of Culture, Sports and Tourism, Blue House spokesperson Kang Yu-jung, a former film critic. With the foreign and unification ministers and the intelligence director all changing at once, the foreign and security team is in effect being rebuilt. Cabinet and intelligence-director nominees must go through a National Assembly confirmation hearing, but the president can appoint them even without Assembly consent, so no vote decides the outcome.

A foreign outlet reported that nominees Kang Geum-sil and Im Ung-sun will be tasked with restoring sensitive intelligence sharing with the United States, which is known to have been partly suspended recently. The presidential office’s position is that the basic course of policy toward the US and North Korea will not change. It is also notable that figures who worked alongside President Lee at the Blue House and in the Democratic Party of Korea were moved to the front. People Power Party floor leader Jeong Jeom-sik criticized the picks as “recycled musical chairs,” while the game industry welcomed nominee Kang Yu-jung, saying the nominee understands the industry well. No date for the confirmation hearings has come out, and it has not been confirmed whether a successor for the Education Ministry, where Minister Choi Kyo-jin has announced a resignation, is on this list.

Report says TPG filed 1.5 trillion won-range international arbitration claim against Kakao… dispute stems from collapse of Kakao Mobility’s US listing

A report came out on the 23rd that TPG, the US private equity fund and second-largest shareholder of Kakao Mobility, has filed for international arbitration worth at least 1.5 trillion won (about 1.1 billion dollars) against Kakao and Kakao Mobility. International arbitration is a procedure in which a dispute is left to a panel at a private arbitration body instead of a court. An earlier report said the filing itself was lodged with the Hong Kong International Arbitration Centre at the end of October, and this time the size of the claim became known in specific terms for the first time. However, the 1.5 trillion won figure is confirmed only in these two reports, and neither Kakao nor TPG has given an official figure. The root of the dispute is the US listing. TPG is the second-largest shareholder with about 29% of Kakao Mobility and is known to have invested about 640 billion won in total, including 500 billion won in 2017 and 140 billion won in 2021. TPG, seeking to recover its investment, pushed for a listing by American depositary receipt (ADR), but the listing fell through after Kakao voiced opposition, and TPG is reported to claim a breach of the shareholder agreement. Kakao’s official rebuttal was not confirmed in this report. An arbitral award is generally known to take from one year to several years, so the outcome will come long afterward.

Korean Air pilots’ union rejects tentative agreement in vote… report says post-merger seniority issue is the stumbling block

A tentative agreement on wages and the collective agreement, drawn up by Korean Air and the Korean Air Pilots’ Union, was voted down by union members on the 23rd. Labor and management reached the tentative agreement on the 13th, handling three years of wages and the collective agreement together, and the union had been holding a general-assembly-style vote for five days from the 19th. A tentative agreement is a draft deal that the union leadership and the company put forward after negotiation, and it is confirmed only if a majority of members vote in favor. With the rejection, the deal does not take effect and negotiations must reopen.

No one has officially stated why it was rejected. However, there is a report that the issue at stake is the seniority ranking, which decides in what order pilots will be lined up after the merger with Asiana Airlines. Korean Air is said to have maintained that the seniority question is not a subject for labor-management agreement, and the pilots’ union is known to have won about 80% approval in an earlier vote on industrial action. The vote count for the rejection, the schedule for renegotiation and whether industrial action will resume have not been confirmed.

Society · Education✓ 3 outlets · payout procedure and funding not confirmed

Anti-Corruption and Civil Rights Commission announces rewards of 100 million to 500 million won per case for tips on CSAT cheating

On the 23rd, the Anti-Corruption and Civil Rights Commission said it will pay informants a reward of at least 100 million won and up to 500 million won per case to stamp out cheating on the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT). On the 18th, the government had announced it was pursuing a plan to pay up to 100 million won if a tip leads to actual detection of cheating, and within five days the ceiling has been raised fivefold. The step follows growing public concern about cheating using artificial intelligence (AI).

The commission explained that rewards go to tips in which cheating is confirmed and will be handled under the Public Interest Whistleblower Protection Act. Protection of informants, the reward application procedure and how the money will be raised were not confirmed in this announcement. In the National Assembly, Rep. Jeong Seong-guk pointed out that despite last year’s guideline to keep CSAT exam papers for one year, more than 100,000 sheets nationwide were discarded, so the problem of preserving evidence also came under scrutiny.

Financial Services Commission fines Fidelix 370 million won, Lotte Card 1 billion won over inadequate disclosure of leak risk

At its 20th meeting on the 23rd, the Financial Services Commission imposed an administrative fine of 370 million won on Fidelix, a memory semiconductor company that prepared and disclosed financial statements in violation of accounting standards, and on related persons. Earlier, the Securities and Futures Commission judged that the company colluded with business partners over five years from 2018 to book about 15 billion won in false sales, and that amount is confirmed by only one report. The exchange has already halted trading in the company’s shares and is reviewing whether to delist it.

The same day, the commission also fined Lotte Card 1 billion won. The reason is that when it issued corporate bonds last year, it did not adequately tell investors about the risk of personal data leaks from hacking. Disclosure is the act of informing investors, in a set manner, of information they need to judge, so leaving it out is grounds for sanction. Lotte Card had already received a business suspension and a 5 billion won sanction after a hack last year leaked the data of 2.97 million people, so this action is a separate matter.
