Gunman kills at least four at an Oregon elementary school; teenage suspect is dead

Bend police say the shooter died inside Silver Rail Elementary on Thursday, and investigators have not said who fired the fatal shot.

A gunman opened fire at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon, on Thursday afternoon, Oct. 15, and at least four people were killed, CNN and CBS News reported, citing sources who described the dead as three children and one adult. The Associated Press later reported that two law enforcement officials put the preliminary toll at five, adding that it was unclear whether that count included the shooter. Bend police spokeswoman Sheila Miller told reporters that dispatchers first received a call at about 12:59 p.m. reporting a shot fired into a car near the school, followed by calls from inside the building, Oregon Public Broadcasting and the AP reported. Police said at least two people were taken to a hospital and that "children are among the victims." The school serves kindergarten through fifth grade, with about 450 students and 40 staff members, according to the AP.

Police said officers arriving within seconds found the suspect already dead, and that the gunman may have been shot by a bystander; Miller said investigators had not confirmed who fired, OPB and the AP reported. CNN reported Thursday evening that the shooter was a 16-year-old boy. The school was locked down while officers cleared it room by room, and families were sent to a reunification site at Journey Church, local and wire reports said. The Oregon State Police and FBI agents joined Bend police at the scene, the AP reported. Bend-La Pine Schools Superintendent Steven Cook said the district was "stunned and heartbroken by this senseless tragedy," and Sen. Ron Wyden issued a statement, according to the AP. Police had not released the names of the victims or a motive by early Friday.

Letters with white powder reach congressional and election offices as FBI investigates

The FBI, the U.S. Postal Inspection Service and the U.S. Capitol Police are investigating letters containing an unidentified powdery substance mailed to congressional and election offices, PBS NewsHour and the Associated Press reported Thursday. The probe was first disclosed in a notice from the Senate sergeant at arms, which urged staff to treat the mailings as "potentially hazardous" and warned that more letters could still arrive, the AP said. The FBI said it was working with partners to identify whoever sent them. Election offices in at least two states, Louisiana and Oklahoma, were evacuated this week after packages with powder arrived, and Ohio's secretary of state said the Postal Service intercepted a package with white powder addressed to his office, according to the AP and Gray Television stations. Authorities had not said what the substance was or announced any arrest.

Wholesale prices jump 1.3% in September, the most since 2022, as energy costs surge

The producer price index for final demand rose 1.3% in September, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Thursday, the biggest monthly gain since a 1.7% rise in March 2022, according to Marketplace, the ABA Banking Journal and First Trust. The Wall Street Journal's consensus of economists had expected 0.6%. Energy prices rose 10.8% in the month and gasoline 20.3%, accounting for more than 40% of the increase, the reports said. Excluding food and energy, producer prices rose 0.7% and were 5.0% higher than a year earlier; goods prices climbed 2.6%, the largest increase since the series began in 2009. The ABA Banking Journal put the 12-month rise at 6.4%.

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh was scheduled for a fireside chat with IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva at the IMF-World Bank meetings in Bangkok at 10:30 a.m. local time Friday, 11:30 p.m. Thursday Eastern, previews in financial outlets reported. They described it as the last scheduled public remarks by a Fed official before the Oct. 27–28 policy meeting. The Fed raised its benchmark rate by a quarter point in September to a range of 3.75% to 4%, and minutes released Oct. 7 showed most officials saw another increase as appropriate before year-end. On Wall Street Thursday, the S&P 500 closed at 7,744.06, up 0.33%, and the Dow at 52,177.69, up 1.08%.

Ukraine hits Russia's Ryazan refinery, ending a three-day pause on energy strikes

Ukraine attacked an oil refinery in Russia's Ryazan region, southeast of Moscow, early Thursday, the Associated Press and Reuters reported. Russia's Defense Ministry said air defenses downed four Ukrainian Flamingo missiles and 69 drones aimed at the refinery, but that falling drone debris started a fire there; a senior Ukrainian official, speaking anonymously, confirmed the strike to the AP. Ryazan Gov. Pavel Malkov reported the fire and damage to a warehouse and vehicles at another industrial site, with no casualties, and Meduza reported that classes were canceled at 14 schools and 25 kindergartens.

The strike came after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Kyiv had paused attacks on Russian refineries for three days at Washington's request but was lifting the restriction because Moscow kept hitting Ukraine's energy grid, the AP reported. Neither side confirmed a truce, the AP said. Russian authorities did not say which units were damaged or whether refining slowed, Meduza and Kurs reported.

Voters frustrated with Trump want big change in Washington, AP-NORC poll finds

A strong majority of voters say President Trump is falling short at home and abroad, and about half want "substantial change" in how the country is run, an AP-NORC poll released Thursday found, as reported by the AP and PBS NewsHour. Roughly 3 in 10 want "complete and total upheaval." Among Republicans, 45% want substantial change and 13% want upheaval, and only 44% approve of Trump's handling of the cost of living. About half of registered voters prefer Democratic control of the House or Senate, roughly 4 in 10 prefer Republican control and 15% are undecided. About 8 in 10 voters name the economy or cost of living among their top issues. The margin of error for registered voters is plus or minus 4.2 percentage points.

In Maine, Republican Sen. Susan Collins said at her Oct. 13 debate with Democrat Troy Jackson that she had won exemptions for concrete, pulp and road salt from the tariff list, while Jackson said she should use her appropriations power against the tariffs, the Maine Morning Star reported.
