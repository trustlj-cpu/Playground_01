Oregon elementary school shooting leaves three children and one adult dead

Police in Bend say a 16-year-old suspect fired a long gun at the school's locked doors on Thursday; officials have not yet laid out a full account.

A shooting at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon, on Thursday, Oct. 15, left three children and one adult dead, according to sources cited by Fox News and CBS News. Reuters, as carried in wire coverage, reported the same toll, and the New York Times placed the school on what it called a grim list of American school shootings stretching back three decades.

Early reports were inconsistent. Politico initially relayed police saying at least two people were hospitalized and that the shooter was dead, while CNN and CBS reported four dead. Later coverage cited police saying a 16-year-old suspect used a long gun to shoot at the school's locked doors. That detail comes from police statements reported by wire services; the readers should treat the exact sequence of events, and the suspect's status, as still developing until Bend authorities publish a formal timeline.

The locked-door detail matters because many districts have spent years and large sums on controlled-entry systems meant to slow an attacker. Whether the doors held, and how quickly officers reached the building, are the questions investigators and parents will press in the coming days. None of the outlets we reviewed has yet reported a motive.

Because the event occurred Thursday afternoon Pacific time and the toll was reported through the evening, some figures may change. The names of the victims had not been released in the coverage reviewed. We will carry the confirmed numbers once police or the school district release them.

Federal agents investigate suspicious letters sent to congressional and election offices

Federal authorities are investigating letters described as containing suspicious substances that were mailed to congressional offices and election offices, ABC News and PBS NewsHour reported Thursday. NPR added that the FBI and the U.S. Postal Inspection Service are both looking into the mailings, some of which included an unidentified substance. The coverage did not say how many offices received letters, which states are involved, or whether anyone was made ill. No outlet we reviewed has named a suspect or a motive, and officials have not said what the substances are. With early voting under way in many states ahead of the November midterms, election administrators are on watch, but there is no confirmation that voting operations were disrupted.

Wholesale inflation jumps by the most in four years as fuel costs climb

Wholesale prices rose faster than economists expected last month, ABC News reported Thursday, led by a sharp jump in fuel costs. It was the biggest monthly gain in four years, a sign that stubborn inflation has not eased. The New York Times separately reported that the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate is hovering around 7.4%, the highest since 2023, compounding affordability problems for buyers.

The data lands as Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh prepares to speak in Bangkok on Friday morning, local time. NBC News said investors will look to the speech for hints on whether the central bank might raise interest rates at its meeting this month. NBC also reported that cracks are showing in the stock market after record highs, as doubts grow about returns on artificial intelligence spending.

Wall Street still finished Thursday higher in our price feed: the S&P 500 closed at 7,744.06 (+0.33%), the Dow at 52,177.69 (+1.08%) and the Nasdaq at 26,713.03 (+0.08%). The 10-year Treasury yield and oil prices are not shown because their readings did not reflect a completed close.

Ukraine resumes strikes on Russian oil refineries after a brief truce

Ukrainian drones hit a refinery in Russia's Ryazan region early Thursday, hours after Ukraine's president spoke, NPR, PBS NewsHour and Google News coverage reported, citing officials. The attack resumed Ukraine's campaign against Russian oil infrastructure following a short pause. Details of the truce and who observed it were not independently verified in the coverage reviewed.

In Washington, the New York Times reported that Republicans joined Democrats in asking the Pentagon's inspector general to examine whether the Trump administration was responsible for a disruption to satellite service used in Ukraine. That claim rests on one outlet in our review and on the senators' own statements. For American readers the link is energy prices: oil near 96 dollars a barrel on the latest quote is not a completed close, but fuel costs are already feeding the inflation numbers above.

Voters want change, AP-NORC finds, as midterm legal fights multiply

A strong majority of voters say President Trump is falling short at home and abroad and want change, an AP-NORC poll found, according to PBS NewsHour and Google News. NBC News reported that the last Maine Senate debate ended with tension over how closely candidates tie themselves to Trump, and that Republicans in some races are distancing themselves from his tariffs.

Legal fights over the vote continue. The New York Times reported the Trump administration sued California over voter rolls, seeking to temporarily block counting of mail ballots from voters it claims are ineligible. The Guardian reported that Texas was sued after a glitch delayed almost 600,000 voter registrations. CBS and NBC reported Trump invoked executive privilege in a legal fight over Kennedy Center records sought by a Democratic congressman.
