White House · Press accessPress ✓ 15 outlets · judge’s name not in our sources

A judge extends the order that put CNN, MS NOW and Politico back in the White House press corps. The administration says it will appeal

The ruling keeps their access in place while the lawsuit over the ban runs its course. The headlines we reviewed did not say why the White House barred them, and none named the judge.

A federal judge extended an order that restores White House access for reporters from CNN, MS NOW and Politico, which the administration had barred. The Guardian, NBC News, USA Today, Politico, The Hill, the New York Times, Reuters and Axios reported it overnight, and CBS News and ABC News carried it too. The first order was temporary. NBC called the new ruling a longer-term decision, ABC called it a preliminary injunction, and Axios said the outlets keep access until the litigation concludes. The Hill described the order as stopping the White House from revoking their credentials. NPR summed it up as a win for the three outlets “for now,” and the Washington Post called it another blow to the administration’s media bans, which tells us this fight has more than one round behind it. Fox News described the judge as a Trump appointee. We did not see the judge’s name, the date of the first order, the White House’s stated reason for the ban or the legal grounds for the ruling, so we are not reporting any of them.

The administration did not wait. CNBC reported that Trump will appeal, and Fox News said the White House confirmed an appeal. We saw no filing, no hearing date and no request to pause the order, so for now the reporters keep their credentials. PBS NewsHour spoke with a lawyer for the banned outlets about the fight. The ruling arrives in a week when the press office was itself in the news. Katie Zacharia, the new press secretary, held her first briefing Tuesday, CBS News reported criticism of her debut, and Reuters, the New York Times and Snopes looked at her legal credentials. CBS also reported that Trump told an Arizona rally crowd that leakers “should be shot.” Neither item is part of the court case, and we do not link them. They do show a White House and a press corps in open conflict less than three weeks before the Nov. 3 midterm elections. Open questions for Thursday: whether an appeals court is asked to pause the ruling, and whether all three outlets’ reporters are in the building today.

Economy · InflationPress ✓ 10 outlets · market closes not confirmed

Inflation rose to 3.7% in September on energy costs, and stocks sold off as bets on a Fed rate hike grew

Consumer prices were 3.7% higher in September than a year earlier, CBS News and the Guardian reported. The Washington Post called it the last inflation report before the midterms. Reuters credited costlier gasoline and said core CPI was firmer, and the New York Times reported that gasoline costs a third more than a year ago. Trump said inflation is low, CBS reported, and Democrats blamed him for prices, the Times said. AP reported that Social Security benefits will rise 3.6% in 2027, about $75 a month on average; NBC, the Times and Reuters carried it. Stocks fell. CBS said the Dow closed lower, the Times reported a tumble on inflation and AI-investment worries, and Bloomberg’s Closing Bell said chip stocks dropped. CNBC reported that CoreWeave’s bond yield neared 15%. Fed officials sounded patient: Reuters quoted Daly saying hikes are likely needed but not urgently, and Bowman saying there is time to wait and see. By Thursday morning in Asia, Reuters reported Nasdaq futures down 1%, and CNBC’s headline put South Korea’s Kospi down 7%. We could not confirm U.S. closing levels from two sources, so both ears show a dash.

Middle East · EnergyPress ✓ 7 outlets · cause of the sinking disputed

A container ship sinks near Hormuz, with Iran saying mine and other accounts saying projectile, and the Houthis hit Aramco’s headquarters

A container ship sank near the Strait of Hormuz, CNBC, Reuters, PBS NewsHour and AP reported. Iran said it hit a naval mine, AP and PBS reported, and FinancialJuice relayed the same account from the Revolutionary Guards. Reuters, citing sources, said a projectile hit the vessel, and a UKMTO notice relayed by FinancialJuice said a container ship was struck by a projectile on Oct. 13. We saw no ship name, crew count or casualties. Separately, PBS reported, citing officials, that the Houthis struck the headquarters of Saudi Aramco, and Reuters, citing sources, reported extensive damage. FinancialJuice, citing two sources, said Aramco told staff to work from home while operations continued normally. Reuters said the Houthis vowed more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities.

Fuel prices carry the effect. The Times reported that oil rose as Iran stepped up attacks and Houthi–Saudi fighting intensified, and CNBC and Reuters said the IEA expects the diesel squeeze to last well into 2027. CBS cited an analyst who expects the average diesel price to hit an all-time record this month, and ABC said gasoline has set a record for days above $4 a gallon. Brent crude settled at $106.06 a barrel Wednesday, FinancialJuice reported; we saw no second source. Reuters reported, citing an official, that Washington is discussing easing sanctions on some shadow-fleet vessels to bring freight rates down. Watch for a Saudi response, the sunken ship’s identity and whether the Houthis follow through.

Justice · CongressPress ✓ 8 outlets · no charge seen · citizenship angle single-source

Federal agents seize Rep. Ilhan Omar’s phone and subpoena her relatives in an immigration probe. Democrats object

Federal agents seized Rep. Ilhan Omar’s phone in a search of her home, Fox News reported, and the Guardian, citing a source, said her relatives were subpoenaed in a criminal inquiry. NBC News, CBS News and USA Today reported that the Justice Department issued subpoenas in a probe of her immigration status, including to family members. Fox tied the search to an immigration-fraud investigation. The Wall Street Journal reported that the investigation aims to build a case for stripping her of citizenship; we saw no second outlet confirm it, and we saw no charge against her. Vice President Vance said the department is investigating her, The Hill reported, and she answered that he is “delusional.” Her husband said he has “no concerns,” Fox reported.

The Washington Post reported that a pre-election search of a sitting lawmaker’s home breaks with Justice Department norms, and Axios said Democrats fear more October surprises. Snopes published a list of 18 rumors about her that it has examined, a sign of how much unverified material is moving. A search or a subpoena is a step in an inquiry, not a finding. Watch for a Justice Department statement on what it is investigating, any charge, and a court challenge to the subpoenas.

Ukraine war · TechnologyPress ✓ 10 outlets · Starlink restoration single-source

Ukraine says Starlink limits crippled its drone missions as Zelensky vows to resume refinery strikes. Officials say the U.S. still shares intelligence

Ukraine accused SpaceX of crippling drone missions by limiting its military-grade Starlink service, Reuters, PBS NewsHour, NBC News and the Guardian reported. A presidential aide made the charge, Reuters said, and the Guardian reported that soldiers in combat lost access. The Financial Times described Kyiv as blindsided by the limits and by gaps in intelligence during a Russian attack, and ABC News tied the loss of satellite support to the tension between Trump and Zelensky. Elon Musk pointed at Washington: The Independent and MS NOW reported that he blamed Trump, and smaller outlets relayed his statement that the U.S. government may be behind the slowdown. The headlines we reviewed offered no evidence, and we saw no SpaceX statement. A report that service was partly restored reached us only through a Reddit post, so we are not counting it.

Intelligence is the second strain. CBS News reported that Ukrainian officials accuse the U.S. of withholding intelligence before a Russian missile strike. The New York Times reported that officials say the U.S. continues to provide it, and FinancialJuice, citing Semafor, said the White House is actively discussing the arrangement. Russian strikes left 600,000 people without power, Zelensky said, as ABC News, CNBC and Reuters reported, and the Guardian carried his charge of “ostentatious disrespect” toward the U.S. Politico and Reuters, citing the Financial Times, reported that Zelensky said Ukraine will resume strikes on Russian refineries, defying Trump. The diesel deal behind that quarrel drew pushback from Republican senators, The Hill and Politico reported, and Italy’s Meloni called it “very risky,” Reuters said. Watch for Starlink service, any U.S. statement on intelligence and the next refinery strike.
