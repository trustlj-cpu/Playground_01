Justice Department asks the Supreme Court to order California to set aside 20,000 mail ballots the night before Election Day

The emergency application, filed Monday, targets ballots the Justice Department says belong to likely noncitizens. Lower courts have already turned the department down.

The Trump administration on Monday asked the Supreme Court to step into a fight over 20,000 California mail-in ballots, one day before Election Day. In an emergency application, an urgent filing that skips the normal appeals timetable, Justice Department lawyers asked the justices to order California to separate the ballots of voters the department says are likely noncitizens. Lower courts have already turned the department down, and judges who reviewed its list have questioned the evidence behind it. California keeps counting mail ballots for days after Election Day, so the timing matters: an order from the Court would reach ballots that are still being processed.

The filing arrives alongside a second appeal. The department is also challenging the dismissal of its lawsuit over Alaska's Senate election, which a federal judge threw out on Sunday. Together the two cases put the courts at the center of the final hours of the midterm election. The 20,000 figure is the department's own count of voters it considers suspect; it is the department's claim, not a finding by any court. An emergency application goes first to the justice who handles urgent matters for the circuit involved, who can rule alone or refer it to the full Court. What is not known is whether the justices will act before polls begin closing Tuesday evening, or whether they would order any change in how California counts. No ruling from the Court had been announced by Monday evening.

The NTSB says the Learjet that killed nine in Palm Beach County may have been breaking up before it hit the ground

The National Transportation Safety Board said Monday that evidence suggests the Learjet 60 that crashed Sunday afternoon in Palm Beach County, Florida, may have been breaking up before it hit the ground. All nine people aboard died. The jet had left Austin, Texas, bound for the Bahamas, the Federal Aviation Administration said. Investigators call a breakup before impact an in-flight breakup. It narrows the questions they must answer to what failed first, and why. The cause has not been determined. NTSB investigators are working the crash, a preliminary report usually follows within weeks, and a final finding of probable cause can take a year or more. The board has not laid out what evidence points to a breakup or which parts came off. Whether the crew sent any warning is also unknown.

Palantir's U.S. commercial revenue jumps 148% and passes its U.S. government revenue for the first time, and it raises its full-year outlook

Palantir, the data-analysis software company, said Monday after the market closed that its U.S. commercial business brought in more money than its U.S. government business for the first time. In the third quarter, U.S. commercial revenue jumped 148% to $984 million, while U.S. government revenue was $953 million. Together the two segments brought in $1.937 billion, so businesses supplied about 51 cents of every dollar and agencies about 49. The company also raised its forecast for revenue for the full year.

Commercial revenue means sales to private companies, as opposed to federal, state and local agencies. The gap is small, $31 million in a quarter, but it reverses the order the two segments held before. The report came on a day when technology shares were falling, with the Nasdaq Composite down 1.07%. What is not yet known is how much higher the company set its full-year forecast and how its shares trade when markets open Tuesday.

Judge dismisses the case against two former FBI officials accused of lying to Congress about the Hunter Biden laptop investigation

A federal magistrate judge in Washington, Zia Faruqui, on Monday dismissed the criminal complaint against two former FBI counterintelligence officials, Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides, who were charged with lying to Congress about their involvement in the investigation into Hunter Biden's laptop. The complaint accused them of making false statements to lawmakers in 2023 by downplaying their firsthand knowledge of the laptop or their involvement in investigations. The two have denied wrongdoing. Faruqui found no probable cause, the legal threshold for a criminal charge to go forward, and described the prosecution as rushed and political, suggesting it was pushed out ahead of the midterm election.

A complaint is the document that starts a case before a grand jury hears evidence, so the dismissal does not necessarily end the matter. The Justice Department can still ask a grand jury for an indictment, a formal charge returned by a panel of citizens, which would restart the case on different footing. The department had not said by Monday evening whether it will try. The ruling removes, for now, one of the most visible criminal cases against former FBI personnel brought by this administration.

Markets · Stocks and oil✓ 4 outlets · whether the oil drop holds; the deal's price on a single basis

Wall Street ends mixed as oil plunges on Iran talk hopes, and Marvell agrees to buy Lumentum in a deal valued at about $115 billion

U.S. stocks ended mixed Monday as a plunge in oil prices helped part of the market while a continued slide in technology shares weighed on the rest. The S&P 500 fell 0.51% to 7,541.40, the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.07% to 25,164.32, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.41% to 51,983.98. The major indexes had started the day higher after Friday’s sharp tech selloff before backing off. West Texas Intermediate, the U.S. crude benchmark, ended near $96.42 a barrel, down 7.4%, as hopes grew that U.S.-Iran talks could lead to a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, the shipping lane that carries much of the Gulf’s oil.

The day’s biggest corporate news was in chips. Marvell Technology agreed to buy Lumentum, a maker of lasers and optical parts for data centers, in a deal valued at about $115 billion; one count puts it nearer $80 billion, and the gap has not been explained. The companies said they are combining in a bet on optical networking, the technology that moves data inside AI data centers. Stocks and oil will now react Tuesday to the vote and to Washington’s reply to Tehran’s conditions.
