Federal grand jury declines to indict Cassidy Hutchinson, the former White House aide who testified about Jan. 6

NBC News, ABC News and PBS NewsHour, citing sources, report a Washington grand jury refused the Justice Department's request to charge the former Jan. 6 committee witness.

A federal grand jury in Washington, D.C., on Tuesday, Oct. 27, rejected the Justice Department's attempt to indict Cassidy Hutchinson over her testimony to lawmakers about President Trump's actions during the Jan. 6, 2021, attack on the Capitol, The Hill reported. ABC News reported that sources confirmed a grand jury refused to indict her on charges of lying to Congress, and Fox News reported that the panel declined to charge her over statements made to the Jan. 6 House select committee. NBC News headlined the outcome citing a U.S. official, and PBS NewsHour carried an Associated Press report that cited the agency's sources. CBS News reported that federal prosecutors in Washington failed to secure an indictment against Hutchinson, whom it described as a Trump critic.

The New York Times, in a breaking-news post, described her as the former White House aide Trump targeted after she gave damning testimony about Jan. 6, and called the rejection the latest extraordinary setback in his wide-ranging campaign to go after his political opponents. PBS NewsHour called it the latest setback in the Justice Department's efforts to charge Trump's perceived adversaries. NBC News reported that the failed indictment is one of many for federal prosecutors during the second Trump administration, and Axios, noting that multiple outlets reported the outcome on Tuesday, described the grand jury's refusal as another blow. The reports cited do not include a Justice Department response, a vote count or word on whether prosecutors will ask another grand jury to act.

World · Israel✓ 6 outlets · final seat counts and the next coalition are not confirmed

Updated Israeli exit polls put the Netanyahu and Eisenkot blocs level, both short of the 61 seats needed to govern

Israel's election ended in apparent deadlock on Tuesday, Oct. 27, with exit polls from the major television networks showing neither side close to a majority, CBS News reported. Axios reported that the first exit polls had Gadi Eisenkot's bloc at 52 to 53 seats and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's at 50, short of the 61 needed in the 120-seat Knesset, and FinancialJuice reported that Channel 12 put Likud at 24 seats and the Arab parties at 18. Later in the night FinancialJuice relayed updated polls: Kan 11 raised Likud to 27 seats and put the two blocs level, Channel 13 had Netanyahu's bloc matching Eisenkot's, and Channel 12 had Netanyahu's bloc ahead. The Guardian's live coverage on Oct. 28 headlined that the exit polls point to a tie between the blocs, reporting that both would fall short of 61 seats as support for Palestinian parties surges. The Hill reported that turnout shattered a record, and CBS News reported that both sides will likely struggle to assemble a coalition. PBS NewsHour headlined that it could be weeks or months before the winner is known. Final seat counts had not been published in the reports cited.

World · Iran war✓ 7 outlets · the draft deal's final terms and any decision on new strikes are not confirmed

Pentagon tells lawmakers the Iran war has cost $47.1 billion through Oct. 16 as Trump says it will end 'when I want it to'

The US military has spent $47.1 billion on the war with Iran through Oct. 16, the New York Times reported, citing an estimate the Pentagon gave lawmakers. CBS News reported that a U.S. Central Command estimate puts the cost at at least $47.1 billion as of mid-October. The Straits Times, carrying a Reuters report, said the total is more than 25% above the Defense Department's estimate in July, and The Hill reported an increase of more than $3 billion since last month, with $29 billion of the total for replacing munitions. The New York Times reported that bombs, missiles and other munitions made up more than 60% of expenses. CNBC headlined that Trump said the war will end 'when I want it to.'

The Hill reported that Trump is weighing strikes on Iran after the midterm elections. The Guardian reported that Gulf states are working on a multipart peace deal that would reopen the strait of Hormuz within four days of sanctions being lifted and have the Houthis withdraw substantial forces inside Yemen. FinancialJuice, citing an official briefed on the negotiations, reported that Qatari mediators gave Iran a new draft deal in Tehran on Sunday, that a response is expected by the end of the week, and that the draft sets a seven-day timeline of daily confidence-building steps. The reports cited do not say whether either side has accepted terms.

Politics · Elections✓ 5 outlets · whether the court will dismiss the suit and the Senate result are not confirmed

Alaska asks a federal court to halt the Justice Department's lawsuit over its Senate race, a week before Election Day

Alaska asked a federal court on Tuesday, Oct. 27, to halt the Justice Department's lawsuit over the state's Senate race, the New York Times reported, saying the administration filed the suit to prevent certification of the election if the Republican incumbent, Sen. Dan Sullivan, narrowly loses. The Washington Post headlined that Alaska's Republican-led government moved to kill the Trump-ordered suit. The Hill reported that Democratic nominee Mary Peltola separately asked a judge to dismiss the case, prompting the judge to expedite it. The Hill also reported that Vice President Vance, asked about the suit, called an independent candidate with a name similar to Sullivan's guilty of 'electoral fraud,' and Politico headlined that the lawsuit supercharges the fight over Alaska's ranked choice voting.

In the governor's race, NBC News reported that Sen. Lisa Murkowski, a Republican, endorsed Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins over Trump-backed Bernadette Wilson a week before Election Day. The Hill reported she backed the former state lawmaker over three Republican candidates and praised his commitment in a statement released by his campaign. The Hill separately reported that senators are bracing for a possible floor fight over accepting the Alaska Senate result if Trump urges allies to dispute outcomes he views as unfair.

Climate · Energy✓ 6 outlets · final rule text, the timeline and the savings figure are not confirmed

EPA moves to loosen Biden-era methane rules for oil and gas producers, Trump says

President Trump said on Tuesday, Oct. 27, that the Environmental Protection Agency will ease Biden-era rules aimed at curbing methane leaks from oil and gas operations, NBC News reported, describing methane as a potent greenhouse gas. The New York Times headlined that the EPA will loosen methane rules for oil firms, and The Hill reported the plan would loosen requirements for low-producing, high-emitting oil wells. The Verge headlined that Trump is making it easier for fossil fuel companies to leak methane.

The Hindu reported that Trump is pushing ways of bringing down energy costs ahead of the midterm elections, amid voter anger over soaring oil costs caused by the war on Iran. Mint headlined that Trump loosened the rules to 'save $45 billion.' Final rule text and a timeline for the change were not given in the reports cited.
