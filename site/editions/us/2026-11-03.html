Election Day: voters decide all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships, with the first polls closing at 6 p.m. Eastern

Polls are open across the country today. Results begin arriving this evening, but close races, especially in states that count mail ballots for days, may not be decided tonight.

Americans vote today, Tuesday, November 3, in the midterm elections, the national vote held halfway through a president's four-year term. On the ballot are all 435 seats in the House of Representatives, 35 seats in the Senate and 36 governorships, along with state legislatures and local measures. Control of both chambers is at stake: a party needs 218 House seats for a majority and 51 of 100 Senate seats for an outright one. Two of the 35 Senate contests are special elections, held to fill seats that came open before their terms ended. Democrats need a net gain of four Senate seats to take control, and Michigan, Texas, Alaska and Iowa are among the races watched most closely. Voters will also decide ballot measures, among them ten constitutional amendments in Louisiana, Missouri's Proposition A and several New York City proposals. Alaskans will vote on whether to scrap ranked-choice voting, the system that lets voters rank candidates in order of preference. Each state sets its own rules for identification, early voting and counting, so what voters encounter at the polls differs from place to place, and a share of ballots has already been cast early or by mail.

States set their own hours, and some precincts stay open later, but the first polls close at 6 p.m. Eastern in parts of Indiana and Kentucky. At 7 p.m. polls close in Georgia, Virginia, South Carolina, Vermont and most of Florida. Pennsylvania, most of Michigan and most of Texas close at 8 p.m., and Arizona, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Colorado, Iowa and New York at 9 p.m. Alaska and Hawaii close around midnight Eastern. Anyone still in line when a polling place closes is allowed to vote. Counting speed varies widely. California counts mail ballots that arrive up to seven days after Election Day, and Pennsylvania, Nevada, Wisconsin and Arizona can take several days to finish, so a close race may not be called tonight. No results exist yet, and the order in which states report is not a guide to who is ahead, because early returns can include different kinds of votes than late ones.

World · Iran✓ 4 outlets · what was hit; whether Iran responds

Unnamed U.S. officials describe limited, defensive strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island on the eve of the election; neither Washington nor Tehran has given an official account

Two U.S. officials, speaking without being named, said the U.S. military carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday night. The Pentagon and the White House have not announced the strikes. They described the strikes as defensive and said they did not amount to a resumption of major combat operations. Qeshm sits in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow channel through which much of the Gulf's oil ships. Explosions were heard on the island, Iranian state media said, but Tehran had not given an official account of what was hit or whether anyone was hurt, and the U.S. has not confirmed the targets. The strikes came while Washington and Tehran trade proposals over reopening the strait. Vice President JD Vance said Monday that he and the president "feel pretty good about the prospects" of reaching a negotiated settlement and that Washington has set no deadline. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said earlier that the strait will reopen only if the United States accepts Iran's conditions. Oil, which fell sharply Monday on hopes of a deal, rose after the strikes became known. Not yet known: what was struck, whether Iran will respond, and whether the strikes change the talks.

Politics · Courts and the vote✓ 4 outlets · whether the department pursues its appeal and criminal cases

Supreme Court rejects the Justice Department's emergency request to make California set aside 20,000 mail ballots

The Supreme Court on Monday rejected the Trump administration's emergency application to order California to set aside about 20,000 mail ballots cast by voters the Justice Department calls likely noncitizens. An emergency application is an urgent filing that skips the normal appeals timetable. The Court acted within hours of the filing and rejected it in a brief order. The ruling leaves in place the lower-court decisions against the department: a federal judge in Los Angeles, a 2-1 Ninth Circuit panel on Friday, and a separate federal judge who has blocked the administration from pressuring states to segregate such votes.

The department says it has identified 20,000 voters on California's rolls who are likely not citizens. No court has accepted that figure, and California officials dispute it. California counts mail ballots that arrive up to seven days after Election Day, so every ballot now goes into the count under the state's normal rules. The department still has its appeal in the lower courts and criminal cases against individual voters. Monday evening's edition noted the filing when no ruling had yet been announced. Not yet known: what the department does next, and whether any new filing arrives before California's polls close at 11 p.m. Eastern.

Health · Measles outbreak✓ 3 outlets · the child's age and vaccination status

New York reports its first measles death in more than 30 years, a child in Lewis County

New York state health officials announced Monday that a child in Lewis County died after contracting measles, the state's first measles-associated death in more than 30 years. The State Department of Health and Lewis County Public Health issued a joint statement and released no other details, including the child's age or whether the child had been vaccinated. Gov. Kathy Hochul said vaccination remains the best protection against measles deaths and that the death reflects eroding vaccination levels. Measles is a highly contagious virus that spreads through the air, and roughly one to three of every 1,000 infected children die from it even with medical care.

The death comes during an outbreak that has produced more cases in a year than the state has seen since 2019. Hochul declared a state disaster emergency over measles about a month ago as cases rose in rural counties. As of October 24 the state had counted 178 cases this year, 170 of them outside New York City. In Lewis County, 15 cases had been counted by October 14, all in people who had not received the MMR vaccine, which protects against measles, mumps and rubella. Not yet known: how the child caught the virus, and whether the child was too young to be vaccinated.

Crime · California✓ 3 outlets · formal charges; the full motive

A 17-year-old is arrested in the Spirit Halloween store shooting in Fairfield, California, that killed a pregnant woman and wounded six others

Police in Fairfield, California, arrested a 17-year-old boy Monday in connection with Friday night's shooting at a Spirit Halloween store in the Solano Town Center that killed a pregnant 31-year-old woman and wounded six other people. Officers took the suspect into custody at a home in nearby Suisun City after a school resource officer recognized him in surveillance footage, police said. The boy faces murder and attempted murder charges, and the police chief said an additional murder count is possible because the woman was pregnant.

Investigators believe the woman who died was the shooter's intended target. They say he used a handgun and acted alone, and the chief said there may have been an earlier altercation between him and one of the victims. Four of the six wounded people remained in the hospital Monday, including a 19-year-old man in critical condition. The chief said the suspect served several months in juvenile hall after shooting into a car at a Fairfield school last year. Not yet known: the full motive, the formal charges prosecutors file, and whether he will be tried as an adult.
