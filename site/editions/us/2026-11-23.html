Democrats Are Projected to Hold at Least 258 House Seats, the Most Since 1992, After Chuck Hubbard Is Projected to Unseat Virginia Foxx in North Carolina

Hubbard, a former journalist, is projected to have won North Carolina's 5th District after a recount and Foxx has reportedly conceded, but none of the results is certified yet.

Democrats are projected to have won at least 258 of the House's 435 seats in the November midterm elections, the party's biggest House majority since 1992 and the largest for either party in a national election in three decades. The milestone came on Sunday, Nov. 22, when Chuck Hubbard, a Democrat and former journalist, was projected the winner in North Carolina's 5th District, ousting Republican Rep. Virginia Foxx, who had served 11 terms. Hubbard's win came after a recount, a second tally of ballots in a race close enough that officials want to be sure the first count was right, and Foxx has reportedly conceded. Forecasters had rated the district as likely Republican before Election Day. A majority needs 218 seats, so 258 would put Democrats 40 seats above the line, or about 59 of every 100 seats in the chamber. Every one of these results is a projection. None has been certified, which is the formal step in which state officials sign off on the final count, so the total is a floor and could still move as the last races are certified.

Foxx chaired the House Rules Committee, the panel that sets the terms of debate for bills on the House floor, so her projected defeat would end a long run at the head of one of the chamber's most powerful committees. The size of the margin matters beyond bragging rights. The majority party sets the agenda, runs the committees, controls the use of subpoenas, the legal orders that compel testimony or documents, and can lose more of its own members on a close vote without losing the vote. Reports say Democrats are preparing for clashes with President Trump over investigations and health care, and they have said future probes will focus on the president's business dealings, including his crypto ventures. Which Democrats will chair which committees has not been set, and the new Congress is not sworn in until early January, so Republicans still run the House until then.

Trump Headlines a Third Dinner for Top Holders of His Meme Coin, Reports Say, as Democrats Accuse Him of Profiting From the Presidency

President Trump was scheduled to headline a third gala on Sunday night for the biggest holders of his own meme coin, at his country club in Virginia, according to reports. A meme coin is a cryptocurrency built around a joke or a brand rather than a practical use, and its price depends mostly on how many people want to buy it. Reports describe the coin as declining in value. At the first such dinner, in May 2025, about 220 of the largest holders dined with the president, and a crypto-intelligence firm, Inca Digital, estimated that roughly $148 million of the coin was bought to qualify.

Democrats accuse the president of improperly profiting from his cryptocurrency ventures and call the dinners a conflict of interest, a situation in which an official's private financial stake could shape public decisions. At the 2025 dinner the White House said the president was attending in his personal time. Democrats, who will hold the House majority from January, have said they will focus coming investigations on his business dealings, crypto included. Who attended this time, what was said and how much of the coin changed hands had not been reported by early Monday.

Reports Say COP31 Climate Talks in Turkey Broke Down Sunday After the European Union and Other Nations Rejected a Draft That Dropped Fossil Fuels

The United Nations climate summit in Turkey, known as COP31, broke down on Sunday, Nov. 22, without a major agreement, according to multiple reports. The European Union and other countries rejected a draft text that had cut every reference to fossil fuels, the coal, oil and gas whose burning drives most of the warming. One account says Saudi Arabia and other oil producers had blocked any mention of a phase-out, the gradual end of fossil-fuel use, before European and climate-vulnerable nations turned the text down; the producers' own position was not detailed. Earlier, the summit's president had said the talks were close to a final deal, and a new draft was circulated to try to avert a collapse. The negotiations ran two days past their scheduled end and are described as the longest in the summit's history.

The split echoes last year's summit in Brazil, which ended with a formal agreement that did not address phasing out fossil fuels. Climate summits decide by consensus, which means a single bloc can stop a text, so a fight over one phrase can sink the whole package. No restart date or fallback meeting has been reported.

A Federal Appeals Court Rules 9-8 That the 2022 $1.7 Trillion Spending Law Was Unconstitutionally Passed, Putting the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act in Doubt

The New Orleans-based 5th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled 9-8 on Friday, Nov. 20, that the $1.7 trillion government funding bill Congress passed in December 2022 was unconstitutional, because it was approved under a pandemic-era rule that let House members vote by proxy. The court said a majority of House members were not physically present when the package passed. The challenge reached the court through the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, a part of that package that requires employers to make reasonable accommodations for pregnant workers, and the ruling is reported to cover it. One vote separated the two sides.

A quorum is the minimum number of members who must be present for a chamber to do business, and the Constitution sets it at a majority. The core question is whether proxy voting, in which a colleague casts the vote of an absent member, can count toward that majority. In earlier rounds of the long-running Texas case over the law, a three-judge panel upheld it in August 2025, and the full court agreed in January 2026 to rehear the case. Whether the decision will be appealed, how far it reaches beyond this case and what it means for other parts of the 2022 package has not been reported.
