Jeffries Says He Is a “Hard Yes” on Impeaching Hegseth, and House Democrats Will Take Up the Resolution on Tuesday

The House Democratic leader says he backs the impeachment resolution Rep. Thomas Massie filed; the party's lawmakers are due to discuss it Tuesday.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said in a Sunday television interview that he is a “hard yes” on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, adding that Hegseth “has got to go.” It is a firmer position than in late October, when Jeffries said Democrats would “deal with” Hegseth if the president did not remove him but did not say how he would vote. Jeffries accused Hegseth of abusing his power, undermining national security and running a costly war of choice in Iran. The resolution was filed by Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican. Early reports of its contents, which we have not seen in full, say it has eight articles of impeachment, the formal charges the House votes on, including a charge that Hegseth violated the War Powers Resolution of 1973, the law that limits a president’s ability to wage war without Congress. Jeffries said his caucus will discuss the measure when it meets Tuesday, its first gathering since the midterms, and predicted “incredibly strong support.” Hegseth has called Massie a “disgruntled, defeated congressman,” in a video last week.

Impeachment in the House takes a simple majority; removing an official then takes a trial and a two-thirds vote in the Senate, a high bar. Republicans still run the current House until the new Congress is sworn in, so a vote during this lame-duck period would need some of them to cross over. So far, Rep. French Hill, a Republican, has said on a Sunday interview program only that he will weigh whether Hegseth’s actions merit impeachment. A single report says Republican leaders are bracing for defections; no Republican leader has said so on the record, so that is unconfirmed. Whether and when the sitting House holds a vote has not been set. Next: Tuesday’s Democratic meeting, and whether other Republicans say where they stand.

Attorney General Blanche Will Not Rule Out Investigating Democrats Who Threaten Oversight of Trump

Attorney General Todd Blanche declined on Sunday to rule out an investigation of Democratic lawmakers who have vowed to probe the Trump White House. Appearing on a Sunday interview program, he was asked about Trump’s order, posted Saturday, for the Justice Department to look into Democrats he accuses of threatening impeachment of the attorney general and others. Blanche said there could be an investigation and that it depends on what is said, without naming any lawmaker or any offense. No probe or charge has been announced. Democrats have pledged sweeping oversight, meaning hearings, subpoenas for documents and testimony, of the administration and of businesses that have worked with it. The White House issued a statement saying Blanche dismantled Democrats’ “oversight charade” on the Sunday shows.

The legal line matters here. The Constitution’s Speech or Debate Clause shields members of Congress from being questioned elsewhere over their legislative acts, which generally includes speeches, hearings and votes, so a prosecution over oversight itself would face a steep challenge. Threatening to investigate lawmakers is also different from opening a case, which would require facts pointing to a crime. Democrats are expected to take over House committee gavels in January and have said investigations will be a priority. Next: whether the Justice Department opens any inquiry, and how House Democrats respond when they meet Tuesday.

Poland Opens a Terrorism Probe Into the Krakow Shooting After the Bodies of the Suspect’s Parents and Sister Are Found

Polish prosecutors opened a terrorism investigation on Sunday into the Saturday shooting in Krakow’s Main Square, where a gunman killed six people and wounded 15. The suspect, a 24-year-old Polish citizen with no previous criminal record, has not been formally questioned, and his custody status has not been confirmed. Officials say he appeared drawn to far-right ideology, an early description rather than a finding; the interior minister described his views as “very radical” and “ultranationalistic.” Investigators found the bodies of the suspect’s parents and sister at the family home in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, and weapons were seized there. Whether he is responsible for those three deaths has not been established. Poland declared two days of national mourning. Two police officers who responded have been hailed as heroes, and a British man is among the dead, according to the regional governor. Prosecutors are preparing murder charges, and autopsies on the victims begin Monday. Next: whether the suspect is questioned, and what investigators say about any wider network or online activity.

A Philadelphia “Boycott Ride” Aimed at Jewish-Owned Restaurants Draws Counterprotesters and Condemnation From State and City Leaders

A bike ride organized to promote a boycott of several Jewish-owned restaurants in Philadelphia drew large crowds of counterprotesters on Sunday, many waving Israeli flags and holding signs against antisemitism, and tensions rose around Rittenhouse Square. The ride was promoted by a group called Philly Bike On Your Left and other local pro-Palestinian organizations, which described the owners as Zionists; its flyer named six restaurants. Organizers did not lay out specific complaints about the businesses and said the ride would go ahead despite criticism that its timing falls on the eve of the anniversary of Kristallnacht, the 1938 Nazi attacks on Jewish businesses and synagogues.

Before the event, Gov. Josh Shapiro’s office called the ride antisemitism meant to push Jewish businesses out of their communities. Sens. John Fetterman and David McCormick, Pennsylvania’s two senators, condemned it, as did Mayor Cherelle Parker, who said singling out businesses because of the faith of their owners has no place in the city. Attorney General David Sunday called the ride deeply unsettling. A boycott is a peaceful refusal to buy from a business, but critics say this one targeted owners for who they are rather than for anything they did. Reports describe verbal confrontations between the groups; no serious injuries have been reported.
