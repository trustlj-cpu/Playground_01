Trump defends waiving new Russia sanctions, saying it keeps Moscow at the negotiating table

In a Sunday post, the president said enforcing the sanctions could derail Ukraine talks and hinted at progress on hostages, as Republicans in Congress pushed back.

President Trump on Sunday defended his decision to waive a new round of sanctions on Russia, The Hill, Newsweek and Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty reported. Writing on Truth Social on Oct. 18, he said he was negotiating peace between Russia and Ukraine and did not want to do anything that would "foolishly" drive Moscow away from the bargaining table, according to The Hill. He added that "great progress" was being made, including in "current hostage negotiations," but RFE/RL noted that he gave no details and did not say who the hostages were. The New York Times described the move as a reversal of almost five years of sanctions policy toward Russia. According to RFE/RL, the waiver came through an executive order signed Oct. 16 that sets aside new penalties required by legislation named for the late Sen. Lindsey Graham. The outlet also reported that Trump said he could still sign them later, but did not want to antagonize Moscow because he believes Russia might otherwise step up its support for Iran.

The weekend defense came after a separate step on Russian fuel. On Oct. 9, the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License 135, which allows the sale and import of Russian-origin diesel through April 2027, Deccan Herald and Spectrum News reported. Trump said Vladimir Putin had agreed to supply more than 300,000 metric tons right away, then 500,000 tons in November and 1 million tons after that. Those volumes came only from Trump's announcement, and the Kremlin published no figures of its own. Axios reported that Energy Secretary Chris Wright presented the diesel deal as an incentive for peace, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Russia would sell the fuel anyway. Newsweek said the waiver drew criticism from Republicans who had backed the sanctions bill. Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the package "could have helped enormously," RFE/RL reported, and NBC News quoted him as warning that the U.S. formally taking Putin's side would be a "historic mistake." A group of 22 House members had asked the White House to cancel the diesel deal and answer them in writing by Oct. 18, according to Ukrainian outlet LIGA.net.

Pickup kills a 6-year-old and a man at a Wichita youth soccer game; driver released

A pickup truck drove onto a youth soccer field at the Stryker Sports Complex in Wichita, Kan., around 3:25 p.m. on Saturday, Oct. 17, killing two people and sending 10 others to hospitals, CNN and The Washington Post reported. Police identified the dead as Cole Adrian, 6, and Nicholas Schafer, 35, both of McPherson, Kan., and said they were not related, according to Fox News. ABC News reported that the driver, a 34-year-old woman from Garden City, had two of her children in the truck while a third was playing on another field. Police released her without charges pending toxicology results and a mechanical inspection of the truck, The New York Times and CNN reported. TMZ said police interviewed more than 30 witnesses before her release. NBC News reported that witnesses said they remain haunted by what they saw. Investigators have not said what caused the crash.

Three national polls put Trump's approval at or near record lows as Democrats lead by 8 to 10 points

Three national polls released over the weekend showed President Trump's approval at or near the lowest of his second term, Axios reported. The Washington Post-ABC News-Ipsos poll put his approval at 33% among U.S. adults, with 66% disapproving, a drop of four points since July, the Post reported. A Fox News poll found 36% of registered voters approving and 64% disapproving, with Democrats ahead 54% to 45% on the generic ballot for the House. NBC News put Trump's approval at 34% and found 71% of adults saying the country is on the "wrong track." Among registered voters, Democrats led by eight points in the Post poll, nine in Fox's and 10 in NBC's, according to Axios. Axios also reported that 95% of Republicans who identify with MAGA approve of Trump, compared with 47% of other Republicans. Election Day is Nov. 3.

Trump endorses Netanyahu nine days before Israel's Oct. 27 election

President Trump endorsed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Sunday, nine days before Israel's Oct. 27 election, CNN, PBS NewsHour and The Jerusalem Post reported. In a Truth Social post addressed to the people of Israel, Trump called Netanyahu a "Great Warrior Prime Minister" and an even greater partner for peace, PBS reported. He wrote that if Israel chose anyone other than Netanyahu, "America will have to do this work ourselves, and I am not inclined to do so," according to CNN. CNN noted that Trump had declined to endorse any candidate in a Newsmax interview just days earlier. Netanyahu thanked Trump in a statement posted on X. Axios reported that most polls show Netanyahu's coalition falling short of the 61 seats needed to form a government in the 120-seat Knesset.

China's economy grew 4.5% in the third quarter as exports carried weak home demand

China's gross domestic product grew 4.5% year-on-year in the July-September quarter, up from 4.3% in the second quarter, the National Bureau of Statistics said Monday, Xinhua and the Seoul Economic Daily reported. The Seoul Economic Daily said the figure beat a 4.4% consensus forecast, and that output for the first three quarters reached 107.1 trillion yuan, up 4.6% from a year earlier. Growth was 1.2% quarter-on-quarter. Industrial output rose 5.5% in September, but retail sales grew just 1.1%, and property and fixed-asset investment fell, the reports said. The New York Times wrote that a prolonged housing crash, sluggish consumer spending and falling investment have left China dependent on exports. China's customs agency said on Oct. 14 that third-quarter exports rose 22% and passed 8 trillion yuan in a single quarter for the first time, metal trade outlet SMM reported.
