World · Venezuela✓ 8 outlets · what she said, where she appeared and how long she was away are not consistent or stated

Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado returns home after months in exile, her first appearance since the U.S. military deposed Maduro, PBS NewsHour reports

NPR, CBS News, the BBC and The New York Times report the return; Fox News calls it a surprise after nearly a year abroad, and the AP says doubts over democracy have deepened.

Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado has returned to the country after months in exile, PBS NewsHour, NPR, CBS News and the BBC reported on Sunday, Oct. 25, and The New York Times carried its own report headlined “María Corina Machado Returns to Venezuela.” PBS NewsHour said it was her first appearance since the U.S. military deposed the country’s then-President Nicolás Maduro. Fox News described a surprise return home after nearly a year abroad and said it could reshape opposition dynamics.

The outlets differ on how long she was away: Fox News says nearly a year, while the PBS NewsHour, NPR and BBC headlines say months. Bloomberg’s headline says she returned to urge a democratic transition, and the AP headline says she came back as doubts over democracy deepen.

Politics · Alaska Senate✓ 6 outlets · what crime Blanche meant and any hearing date are not reported

Blanche defends Justice Department’s Alaska Senate suit on Sunday shows, and Politico quotes him saying “There could be a crime”

Attorney General Todd Blanche defended the Justice Department’s lawsuit over Alaska’s Senate race in Sunday interviews, NPR and the Guardian reported on Oct. 25. NPR described the suit as one that would block the state’s Senate results if the Republican loses, and the Guardian called it a pre-emptive suit to block certification of the results. CBS News posted a transcript of Blanche’s appearance on “Face the Nation with Margaret Brennan” and a clip headlined that the department is “doing our own research under the law” on the Alaska ballot challenge. The Hill reported that he accused the “Face the Nation” host of a “misleading” description of the lawsuit. Politico’s headline quotes Blanche on the Alaska suit saying “There could be a crime,” and The Hill’s separate headline says he called the Alaska Senate race “not a fair fight” to Trump. On Saturday, The Hill reported, Blanche had said the department was not trying to “stop the certification of Alaska’s election.” A motion to dismiss was filed Friday by attorneys for Mary Peltola and Senate Democrats, NBC News reported.

World · Israel✓ 8 outlets · result and turnout not yet known; scale of the West Bank operation not stated beyond “massive”

Israel votes Tuesday in its first parliamentary election since Oct. 7, 2023, days after a massive military operation in the West Bank, CBS News, AP and Politico report

Israel will hold its first parliamentary election since the Oct. 7, 2023 attacks on Tuesday, Oct. 27, CBS News reported on Sunday, Oct. 25, describing the vote as a test of Benjamin Netanyahu’s ability to hold power. NPR reported that Israel is holding a general election this week after years of war and that many Israelis were shaken by the Oct. 7 attacks, in a piece asking whether Israelis who have changed will change their leadership. AP and Politico reported that the Israeli military conducted a massive operation in the West Bank days before the vote.

Reuters headlined a report that disenchanted Netanyahu supporters are the prize of the campaign, and the Guardian’s headline quotes the line “Gaza’s suffering has become campaign fodder” in a report on Palestinians who fear the worst from the election. The Times of Israel reported that Bill Ackman, a billionaire Trump backer, said Ben-Gvir and Smotrich should not serve in the next Israeli government, and the Jerusalem Post, as relayed by UA.NEWS, reported that Netanyahu asked Trump for new statements of support ahead of the vote.

World · Ukraine war✓ 5 outlets · U.S. response to the Oleshky proposal and who imposed the Starlink limits are not reported; Zelensky’s posts are quoted in translation

Zelensky says Oslo talks with the U.S. faced air defense, Starlink and intelligence limits, and Schiff wants Hegseth to testify

Zelensky said in a series of Telegram posts on Sunday, Oct. 25 (quoted here in translation), that his conversation with the American side in Oslo was accompanied by three challenges: a shortage of air defense, restrictions on Starlink and Starshield, and restrictions on intelligence sharing by the United States. In another post he wrote that the American side proposed a regime of quiet in the Oleshky area from Oct. 27, saying it cannot be one day and will be several days. He also wrote that Ukraine is ready for trilateral talks among technical groups that must produce a concrete result, and that he would not want to reach a meeting of the leaders of Ukraine, the United States and Russia after all sanctions on Russia had been lifted.

On security guarantees, Zelensky wrote that Ukraine’s guarantees cannot be dictated by the Russian side. NBC News and The Hill reported that Sen. Adam Schiff said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth should testify before the Senate about the Starlink slowdown in Ukraine. PBS NewsHour reported on Oct. 24 that Starlink satellite speeds in Ukraine were restored after a week of front-line disruption, following a new U.S. diplomatic push to end the war, and Newsweek’s headline said Zelensky revealed a massive Russian barrage amid Musk’s Starshield restriction.
