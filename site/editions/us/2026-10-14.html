Justice · PoliticsPress ✓ 7 outlets · key details rest on anonymous sources

The Ilhan Omar probe turns to her birth date and citizenship papers, the Washington Post reports, as subpoenas go out and Democrats question the timing

The New York Times says the Justice Department has issued subpoenas, and Fox News says relatives were served. We found no charges and no on-the-record description of the alleged offense.

Federal agents searched Rep. Ilhan Omar’s home in Washington on Monday, Oct. 12, and the Justice Department called it part of an ongoing criminal investigation. On Tuesday the case grew more specific, mostly through anonymous sources. The New York Times reported that the department has issued subpoenas in the investigation. Fox News reported that investigators subpoenaed Omar’s relatives and are examining her immigration history. The Washington Post, citing people familiar with the matter, reported late Tuesday that the probe focuses on her birth date and her citizenship papers. CBS News, also citing sources, had said on Monday that a yearslong probe centers on immigration paperwork completed by her late father when she was a child, that it is tied to potential false statements and immigration fraud, and that Attorney General Blanche was personally aware of the search. We saw no second outlet confirm those details. Axios reported that the investigation is being run by the office of Jeanine Pirro, the federal prosecutor in Washington. Omar is a U.S. citizen who was born in Somalia. She answered with a defiant video, USA Today reported, and the Guardian said she vowed not to be silenced. So far the established facts are few: a search took place, the department says an investigation is under way, and subpoenas have gone out. No outlet we reviewed reported charges.

Democrats treated the timing as the story. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said nothing justified an “extraordinary raid,” The Hill reported, and the Guardian said party leaders condemned the search. Democratic strategist David Axelrod questioned why it came weeks before an election, The Hill said, and Axios reported that Democrats fear more “October surprises.” The Hill also reported that a legal group filed a complaint over the search under the Hatch Act, the law that limits political activity by federal employees; we could not tell which group. The Times and PBS noted on Monday that the Justice Department has long avoided overt steps against political figures close to an election. The midterms are Nov. 3, 20 days from today, and CBS News reported that millions of Americans have already voted early. Trump called Omar “totally crooked” on Monday, and The Hill reported that she is fundraising off the investigation. We saw no explanation from the department of why it moved now. The CBS and Post accounts fit together, but each rests on unnamed sources, and prosecutors have confirmed neither. A search warrant requires a judge’s approval, yet the public has not seen what was shown to the judge. Until charges or an unsealed affidavit appear, treat the birth-date detail as a lead, not a finding. Watch for more subpoenas, any court filing and any explanation of the timing.

Vatican · HealthPress ✓ 13 outlets · stage and prognosis not released

Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer, the Vatican says, after surgery removed part of his right lung

The Vatican announced Tuesday that Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer. He had said on Sunday that he would have a lung nodule removed, and Reuters reported that he entered the hospital Tuesday for the operation. The news came after the surgery, AP, NBC News, CBS News, NPR, Politico, USA Today and The New York Times reported. The Hill and WBAL-TV said the lower lobe of his right lung was removed. USA Today said the Vatican called the operation successful, and the BBC said it concluded successfully. Reuters reported early Wednesday that he had a peaceful night in the hospital, and the Times said leaders around the world sent messages of support. What is missing is the useful part. In the coverage we reviewed, the Vatican gave no stage for the cancer, no plan for further treatment and no date for his return to public duties, and CBS called the details scant. The Times wrote that the diagnosis tests a papacy still taking shape, and CBS looked at how it may change his responsibilities and travel. A nodule is often not cancer. This one was. Expect plain-language bulletins and few details.

Law · Press freedomPress ✓ 11 outlets · judge’s name not in our sources

A federal judge extends the order keeping CNN, MS NOW and Politico in the White House, blocking Trump’s ban for now

A federal judge ruled late Tuesday that CNN, MS NOW and Politico can keep their White House access while their lawsuit continues, Reuters, the Guardian, Politico, USA Today, NBC News and The New York Times reported. The ruling extends an earlier order and blocks Trump’s ban again. NBC News and USA Today called it a longer-term decision, and the Times headline said the judge “again” blocked the ban. ABC News called it a preliminary injunction, a ruling that holds the status quo until the case is decided, and The Hill said the White House may not revoke the three outlets’ credentials in the meantime. NPR’s headline said the outlets prevailed “for now.” The judge’s name and reasoning were not in the headlines we reviewed.

The fight is part of a wider dispute. Last Friday, the Times reported, five networks were told the Justice Department is investigating them for possible antitrust violations after they stopped supplying shared pool coverage of the president. The Guardian linked that stoppage to the ban on the three outlets. Press secretary Katie Zacharia held her first briefing Tuesday. USA Today said it lasted 14 minutes, and the Washington Post said she called on Trump-friendly outlets. We saw no appeal and no White House statement on the ruling. Watch for both.

Ukraine war · EnergyPress ✓ 14 outlets · diesel futures figure single-source

Senators in both parties urge Trump to scrap the Russian diesel deal as Russia hits Kyiv’s power again and Zelensky says allies gave no warning

Senators of both parties pressed Trump on Tuesday to reverse the deal that eased sanctions on Russian diesel, Politico, Axios, The Hill, PBS NewsHour and The New York Times reported. Reuters and USA Today carried the harshest words, “gift to our adversary” and “a betrayal.” Trump answered that it is “not a USA deal,” Yahoo Finance reported, and said the fuel would go to the world, not to Americans, in a line relayed by FinancialJuice. CNBC calculated that the volumes equal less than two weeks of U.S. demand, and NBC News said diesel futures rose. FinancialJuice relayed that November futures settled at $4.9344 a gallon. Italy’s prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, called the deal a “very risky choice,” Reuters reported, and the International Energy Agency said diesel shortages could last well into 2027, Reuters and CNBC reported.

The fighting did not wait. Russia struck Kyiv’s energy sites again on Tuesday and overnight, the BBC, Reuters and Politico reported, and Reuters said an attack on the city killed two people. Zelensky said Ukraine got no warning from partners, a complaint he also made earlier Tuesday, Reuters, the Guardian and Politico Europe reported. Overnight, ABC News and CNBC said 600,000 people were without power across Ukraine. Zelensky told NPR that the administration warned it could cut off intelligence. Trump said sharing continues, Bloomberg reported, and the Times said both governments say so. Reuters, citing a presidential aide, said SpaceX has limited Starlink operations in Ukraine, and the Guardian, the Financial Times and Politico reported the same restrictions. Watch for whether Washington says what it shares, and for any legislative move.

Middle East · EnergyPress ✓ 11 outlets · damage claims are Saudi and Houthi statements

Houthi attacks hit Saudi Aramco’s headquarters and keep coming at Riyadh. Trump says they are trying to lift oil prices before the midterms

Houthi attacks struck Saudi Aramco in Dhahran on Tuesday. Bloomberg said the company’s headquarters was hit, The New York Times said the Saudi energy giant was struck, and Reuters cited sources who heard loud booms near the headquarters. Saudi state media, relayed by FinancialJuice, said the attack caused a limited fire and no casualties. Early Wednesday, AP said residents of Riyadh heard new explosions, and the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile fired toward the city. The Houthis said they targeted Aramco in Dhahran, King Khalid International Airport and an Aramco refinery in Riyadh, and that attacks on airports and oil sites will continue, FinancialJuice relayed. The coalition struck oil depots near Sanaa, the Yemeni capital, the BBC and the Guardian reported.

Trump said the Houthis are “behaving very badly,” CBS News and FinancialJuice reported, and Reuters said he accused them of trying to drive up oil prices before the midterms. CBS said he suggested he will not act against them until after the election. We saw no U.S. decision. UKMTO, the British maritime-security center, reported through FinancialJuice that a tanker and a container ship were struck near the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday. Reuters reported that oil rose, and, citing officials, that Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks on shipping before the U.S. vote. Watch for Saudi retaliation, flights in and out of Riyadh and tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.
