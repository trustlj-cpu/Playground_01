World · Iran✓ 4 outlets · whether or when strikes would resume is not known

U.S. and Israel have drawn up plans to renew strikes on Iran, The New York Times reports, and The Hill says the options are for after the midterms

The Times says Iranians are waiting anxiously and calls the war’s new phase dangerous ahead of votes in the U.S. and Israel; The Hill says Tehran has threatened aggressive retaliation.

The New York Times reported on Saturday, Oct. 24, that the United States and Israel have drawn plans to renew strikes against Iran, but that the uncertainty about whether or when such attacks might happen has created more anxiety among Iranians. In a separate piece, the Times said that, with new intelligence and the possibility that all sides are looking for pretexts for action, the war in Iran is entering a dangerous phase ahead of elections in the United States and Israel. The Hill reported that the U.S. military has drawn up options to potentially attack Iran after the U.S. midterm elections, possibly renewing a conflict that has lasted more than eight months, and that U.S. Central Command has plans ready for follow-up strikes. The Hill also reported that Tehran has threatened aggressive retaliation.

Other outlets described the backdrop. PBS NewsHour reported that the United States is reducing staffing at 4 Middle East embassies after Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told Secretary of State Marco Rubio in a Friday memo that Iran has substantially increased efforts to target U.S. personnel. Bloomberg quoted Ziad Daoud, its chief emerging markets economist for Bloomberg Economics, as saying the risk of renewed fighting between the United States, Israel and Iran is rising, which he said is helping keep oil prices elevated even as energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz recover.

Crime · Tallahassee✓ 4 outlets · counts differ (12 injured vs. 11 shot); no arrest or motive reported

Shooting near Florida A&M’s stadium after homecoming injures 12, including 6 minors

Twelve people, including several minors, were injured in a shooting near Florida A&M University’s Bragg Memorial Stadium, where homecoming celebrations included a football game on Saturday, NBC News reported. The New York Times reported at least 12 injured, including 6 minors, said the shooting occurred hours after the university’s homecoming game, and quoted police as saying the youngest victim was 11. Fox News likewise reported 12 injured, including six juveniles, the youngest just 11 years old. USA Today’s headline said 11 people were shot after the homecoming game, a count one lower than the other three outlets.

Politics · Midterms✓ 5 outlets · Trump’s reply is his own post; rally crowd sizes not independently stated

Obama campaigns in Ohio and Michigan, hitting Trump and Ramaswamy, and Trump answers on Truth Social that Obama was “grossly incompetent”

Former President Barack Obama held a rally in Cleveland on Saturday, Oct. 24, for Amy Acton, the Democratic candidate for Ohio governor, and Sherrod Brown, the Democratic Senate candidate, The Hill reported. CBS News reported that he blasted Republican gubernatorial candidate Vivek Ramaswamy, who has doubled down on an ad featuring Acton’s estranged mother, and The Hill reported that Ramaswamy’s campaign is spending $4 million to broadcast the ad statewide. NBC News reported that Obama described President Donald Trump as an agent of “chaos and cruelty” and a man of broken promises, and The Hill reported that he compared Trump’s White House renovations to “Saddam Hussein opening a Cheesecake Factory.” The Hill also reported that he mocked Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s “focus on jawlines and testosterone.”

In Detroit, NBC News reported, Obama rallied for Michigan’s Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed in a contest that could come down to the city’s substantial Black vote, and the Guardian quoted him telling the crowd El-Sayed is “fighting for change, real change, that will get money out of politics and put money in your pocket.” Early on Sunday, Oct. 25, Trump wrote on Truth Social that “Obama was a grossly incompetent President,” saying he “paid off Iran with $Billions” and “gave them a road to a Nuclear Weapon,” according to the post as archived on trumpstruth.org.

Religion · Abuse settlement✓ 2 outlets · NPR says agreed, Times says close; final court approval not reported

New York Archdiocese reaches $800 million abuse settlement with all 1,300 survivors, NPR reports; the Times says it is close to a deal for more than $800 million

The Archdiocese of New York has agreed to an $800 million settlement of sexual abuse claims, NPR reported on Saturday, Oct. 24, citing an attorney for some of the plaintiffs who said the 1,300 survivors of abuse by clergy members and lay workers have all agreed to the deal. The New York Times reported that the Roman Catholic archdiocese is close to settling the claims for more than $800 million and that, by reaching a settlement with the 1,300 people who said they had been abused by clergy members and lay leaders, it might now avoid bankruptcy. The two outlets differ on the stage and the figure: NPR describes an agreement at $800 million, the Times a deal that is close at more than $800 million.

Sports · World Series✓ 2 outlets · series continues

Brewers rally from 4-0 down to beat Rays 5-4 in World Series Game 2 and lead the series 2-0

The Milwaukee Brewers erased a four-run deficit to beat the Tampa Bay Rays 5-4 in Game 2 of the World Series, the Guardian reported on Sunday, Oct. 25. Sal Frelick tied the score with his first home run in three months and Cooper Pratt delivered the go-ahead single, the Guardian said, adding that the Rays were undone by defensive mistakes in the seventh inning. Fox News described Frelick’s clutch solo homer as the blow that erased the deficit, and reported that the comeback was completed after a controversial call.

The win gives Milwaukee a 2-0 lead in the series. The Guardian reported that the Brewers took Game 1 by 3-2 behind a first-inning triple by Christian Yelich, and Fox News reported that the Game 1 victory was Milwaukee’s first World Series game win in 44 years.
