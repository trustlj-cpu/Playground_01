White House and Kremlin Say Trump, Xi and Putin Will Hold an Unprecedented Joint Meeting in China, and the Kremlin Puts It in Shenzhen on Nov. 19

Both governments say the three leaders will meet together on the sidelines of a regional summit; Trump has said he wants nuclear arms control on the agenda, and Beijing has been the hesitant party.

The White House and the Kremlin both say President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin plan to meet together in China, the first joint session of the three leaders. The Kremlin says the meeting will take place in Shenzhen on Nov. 19, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, an annual gathering of leaders from economies around the Pacific. A trilateral meeting means all three sit down at once instead of holding a series of two-person talks, and none has been held before. Trump has said he wants nuclear arms control on the agenda, meaning negotiated limits on the size of nuclear arsenals. The United States and Russia hold most of the world’s nuclear warheads, and China has been building up its own stockpile, which is why Washington wants it at the table.

Beijing has been the hesitant party. It has rejected three-way arms talks ever since Trump first proposed them, and it has offered a different idea: a no-first-use pledge among nuclear-armed states, a promise never to be the first to use nuclear weapons. In early November the Chinese government confirmed only that discussions with the other sides about a possible meeting were under way. Moscow has linked the meeting to ending the war in Ukraine, and Ukraine’s foreign minister has argued that a three-way summit would encourage Putin to keep fighting. Australia’s prime minister has welcomed the plan, while some European leaders have voiced reservations. Still open are the format of the session, whether any joint statement or agreement is planned, and whether China will agree to anything beyond a conversation.

SpaceX Catches the Starship Upper Stage at Its Texas Launch Site After a Return From Orbit, a First for the Spacecraft

SpaceX caught the upper stage of its Starship rocket at the launch site in Texas on Saturday after it returned from orbit, the first time the spacecraft has been recovered that way. The ship came back to the same spot it had lifted off from, where the launch tower’s arms took it out of the air instead of letting it land on legs or splash into the ocean. The booster, the huge first stage that lifts the ship off the pad, has been caught this way since October 2024. Catching the ship is the harder step, because it re-enters the atmosphere at orbital speed and has to survive the heat first. The flight was Starship’s fifteenth, and SpaceX had said it intended to try catching both the booster and the ship on it. A ship that can be caught and flown again quickly is central to the company’s plan to launch satellites in large numbers and to serve as the lunar lander in NASA’s Artemis moon program. Not yet public are whether the booster catch also succeeded, what condition the ship is in, and when the next flight will be.

Zelensky Says U.S. Negotiators Asked Whether Ukraine Would Cede Donbas in Exchange for NATO Membership

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Saturday that U.S. negotiators asked whether Ukraine would be prepared to cede the Donbas, the eastern industrial region made up of the Donetsk and Luhansk provinces, in return for NATO accession. He described it as an idea the Americans had floated. Membership in the alliance is the security guarantee Kyiv has sought most, because Article 5 of the NATO treaty treats an attack on one member as an attack on all. Trading land for that guarantee would be a sharp break: Zelensky has rejected giving up territory and has said before that any such change would need a referendum.

Zelensky also said U.S. envoys told him Putin had agreed to include Europe in the next round of negotiations. Both claims come from Zelensky’s own account, and Russia’s response is not known. The remarks come days before the planned three-way meeting of Trump, Xi and Putin in China, which makes the question of what Washington is willing to ask of Kyiv more pressing.

U.S. Loses Its Measles-Elimination Status as Cases Surge Nationwide

The United States has lost its measles-elimination status, the designation that a country is no longer spreading the virus continuously within its borders. The country earned the status in 2000, and it is generally lost when one chain of transmission runs for 12 months or more. Cases are surging nationwide. Doctors quoted in one report say the surge is presenting conditions they have never treated, and that the youngest patients carry the greatest unknowns. One report says Pennsylvania and Wisconsin have each reported additional deaths linked to measles.

Measles is among the most contagious viruses known, and two doses of the vaccine are the main protection. Counts of cases and deaths differ from one tally to another. What the loss of the status changes in practice, who made the formal determination and what steps follow have not been laid out.

Copa Airlines Jet and UPS Cargo Plane Collide on a Taxiway at Miami International Airport; Two People Treated for Minor Injuries

A Copa Airlines Boeing 737 and a UPS Boeing 767 collided on a taxiway at Miami International Airport on Saturday, a day after the nationwide ground stop, in an incident reported early Sunday. The wingtip of the Copa jet struck the nose of the UPS cargo plane. Airport officials said two people were treated at the scene for minor injuries.

A taxiway is the paved path aircraft use to move between runways and gates, and contact there usually happens at low speed. What caused the collision, how badly either aircraft was damaged, how many people were aboard and whether flights at the airport were affected have not been announced. No link between the collision and Friday’s ground stop has been reported.
