Rep. Thomas Massie Formally Introduces Eight Articles of Impeachment Against Hegseth and Forces a House Vote

The filing confirms weekend reports of eight articles; because the resolution is privileged, the House must vote within two legislative days.

Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, introduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Monday and presented them on the House floor, setting in motion a vote that must be taken within two legislative days. The filing confirms weekend reports that the measure has eight articles, and it reintroduces charges Massie brought in September. It accuses Hegseth of “high crimes and misdemeanors,” chiefly for waging war against Iran without congressional authorization in violation of the War Powers Resolution, the law that limits a president’s ability to commit U.S. forces to sustained combat without Congress. The articles also allege that Hegseth “abused” his powers, “knowingly executed and implemented unlawful orders” and suppressed free speech. Because the resolution is privileged, any member can force the House to take it up instead of leaving the timing to party leaders, and Massie used that route.

Hegseth has said he is “not worried” about the effort, and reaction in Congress is split. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Sunday that he is a “hard yes” on impeaching Hegseth and that “Pete Hegseth has got to go.” Rep. Don Bacon of Nebraska, a Republican who has criticized Trump on foreign policy, said Monday that he is conflicted about a fellow Republican leading the push. The filing came as Congress returned to Washington for a lame-duck session after the midterm elections, with a crowded agenda. A House vote to impeach only brings charges; removing Hegseth would take a trial in the Senate and a two-thirds vote there. The date and hour of the House vote have not been confirmed, and no count of how many Republicans will join Democrats has been established.

Supreme Court Lifts the Order Halting Construction of Trump’s Border Wall in Texas’s Big Bend Region

The Supreme Court on Monday lifted a lower-court order that had paused construction of President Trump’s border barrier in the Big Bend region of southwest Texas, a remote area that includes land in and around Big Bend National Park. The administration’s plans call for physical walls, access roads, vehicle barriers, cameras and sensors along a stretch of more than 500 miles. The Supreme Court’s action does not decide the case. A coalition of environmentalists, cattle ranchers, farmers, river guides, outdoor activists and church leaders had gone to court to stop the construction, and the lawsuit continues in the lower courts. How the justices voted, and whether they gave reasons, has not been confirmed.

Trump Suggests the Midterm Results Were Not “Honest,” Days After Saying He Was “Absolutely Willing” to Accept Them

President Trump suggested on social media on Monday that the Democratic victories in last week’s midterm elections were not “honest.” Days earlier he had said he was “absolutely willing” to accept the results.

A president’s statement does not change a count. Results become official when election authorities certify them after counting ends, and a statement of doubt is not a finding by an election authority.

Wall Street Closes Sharply Higher, With the S&P 500 Up 2.35% and the Nasdaq Up 2.74%

U.S. stocks closed sharply higher Monday. The S&P 500 rose 2.35% to 7,549.94, the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.74% to 25,253.14 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 2.15% to 52,389.75. Utah Judge Sets an Early July 2028 Murder Trial for Tyler Robinson, Accused of Fatally Shooting Charlie Kirk

A Utah district judge on Monday set the murder trial of Tyler Robinson for early July 2028. Robinson, 23, is accused of fatally shooting the activist Charlie Kirk in Utah in September 2025, and prosecutors say he assassinated Kirk. If he is convicted, he faces a potential death penalty.

The trial date is about 20 months away and nearly three years after the shooting. A trial date decides nothing about guilt: Robinson is accused, not convicted, and the case will turn on evidence presented in court.
