Fed holds rates steady in its last decision before the midterms as two officials dissent in favor of a hike

NBC News, CBS News and CNBC report the central bank left its benchmark rate unchanged, and CNBC reports both dissenters wanted a quarter-point increase.

The Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged on Wednesday, Oct. 28, in its final major decision before the midterm elections, NBC News reported. The Guardian reported that the central bank raised rates last month for the first time since 2023, a move it said infuriated President Trump. CNBC reported that two of the 12 Federal Open Market Committee voters dissented and that both wanted to raise rates by a quarter percentage point. Bloomberg reported that the committee voted 10-2 to hold its benchmark rate in a range of 3.75% to 4%, and that Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack were the dissenting votes. The Hill reported that annual inflation was 3.7 percent in September, up from 3.4 percent.

PBS NewsHour reported that the Oct. 27-28 meeting was the fourth of Chairman Kevin Warsh's tenure. NBC News reported that Warsh said the Fed plans to eliminate some rate-setting meetings, and Bloomberg Television reported that he said the meeting schedule will change as of next fall and that politics had no role in the rate decision. Yahoo Finance reported that the S&P 500 and Dow climbed and the Nasdaq wavered after the vote, and headlined that prediction markets on Polymarket saw 82% odds of a December hike. Politico headlined that Cook, whom it called Trump-targeted, dissented.

World · Spain✓ 6 outlets · the final death toll and the investigation's findings are not confirmed; the cause rests on the minister's account

At least 68 killed when a high-speed train hits a stopped train in Spain, and the transport minister says one train was told to continue despite a red signal

A high-speed train slammed into the rear of another train that had stopped on the tracks in central Spain on Wednesday, Oct. 28, derailing two cars, NPR reported. Fox News reported that a Renfe train hit a stopped Iryo train in Toledo province, and ABC News reported the crash was on the Madrid-Andalusia high-speed line. The reported toll climbed through the day: NBC News, ABC News and NPR first reported at least 10 dead, CBS News reported at least 30, AP and Bloomberg reported at least 64, and the New York Times, PBS NewsHour and the Guardian reported at least 68. The New York Times reported it was one of the deadliest train accidents in Spain's history, and the BBC described it as the country's worst disaster since 2013. DW reported that Spain's transport minister said one of the trains was told to continue its journey despite a red signal being in effect, and the New York Times reported the minister said an 'inexplicable' decision by traffic control operators sent a train toward another stopped miles ahead. PBS NewsHour described it as the country's second deadly rail collision this year, and the Guardian headlined that a second fatal collision casts doubt over Spain's high-speed rail network. The New York Times reported that survivor Fernando Seco, 62, helped move at least a dozen wounded passengers using wreckage as makeshift stretchers. The reports cited do not give a final toll or the findings of an investigation.

Dolores Huerta, who co-founded the United Farm Workers with Cesar Chavez, dies at 96

Dolores Huerta, the labor organizer who co-founded the United Farm Workers and coined the rallying cry 'Sí, se puede,' died Wednesday, Oct. 28, at 96, Axios reported, citing her family's announcement on her Instagram account. NPR and PBS NewsHour reported that she co-founded the union to improve working conditions in the nation's fruit and vegetable fields. The Hill quoted the family's statement that 'our beloved mother, Dolores Huerta, has passed away today.' The Los Angeles Times described her as the godmother of the Chicano power movement.

The New York Times reported that she founded the union with Cesar Chavez and that in early 2026 she told the paper she had been raped by him decades earlier, and Fox News headlined that she accused Chavez of sex abuse. The Guardian reported she died in California. Reports differ in the details they give of her death, and funeral and memorial arrangements were not announced in the reports cited.

James O'Keefe and Cam Higby are charged with felony unlawful voting and identity theft over a Minnesota voting stunt

A Minnesota district attorney on Wednesday, Oct. 28, charged the conservative influencers James O'Keefe and Cam Higby with felony counts of unlawful voting and identity theft, NBC News reported. The New York Times reported the two content creators are accused of breaking the law when they posed as Islamic voters in August. The Guardian reported the pair were trying to show vulnerabilities in a rule that allows one voter to vouch for another, and that they disguised themselves in what they called 'Muslim garb.'

The reports cited do not include a court date, the individual counts against each man or a response from either defendant. The New York Times described O'Keefe as a right-wing provocateur, while NBC News and the Guardian called both men conservative influencers.

Health · Policy✓ 4 outlets · the six peptides' full list, the rule's timeline and the permission's length are not confirmed

HHS lets compounding pharmacies make six unapproved peptides while it works on a formal rule, over FDA objections

The Department of Health and Human Services is giving compounding pharmacies a temporary green light to make six peptides while the agency continues to work on a formal rule change, NBC News reported. MarketWatch reported the government will not take action against pharmacies that make six of the popular wellness products, including BPC-157 and TB-500. The New York Times reported on Wednesday, Oct. 28, that the move is a unilateral declaration by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. that makes injections promoted by online influencers widely available, and that the FDA had advised against it.

AP reported that FDA scientists opposed the Trump administration's effort to ease access to peptides. The peptides are not approved by the Food and Drug Administration, and the reports cited describe the step as a temporary enforcement decision rather than an approval. The reports cited do not say when the formal rule change will be finished or how long the temporary permission lasts.
