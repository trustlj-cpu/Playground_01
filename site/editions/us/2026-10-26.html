World · Iran✓ 4 outlets · no strike or deal has been announced

Trump says there will be no strike on Iran “today” but that a decision is near, as Qatari mediators visit Tehran

FinancialJuice relayed Trump’s Sunday remark that the U.S. will not attack Iran that day, and Bloomberg quoted him saying it is “getting to be decision time” on a deal or renewed attacks.

President Donald Trump said on Sunday, Oct. 25, that the United States would not attack Iran that day, FinancialJuice reported, relaying his remark “We will not attack Iran today.” Bloomberg reported on Monday that Trump said it is “getting to be decision time” on whether he will make a deal with Tehran or resume attacks, as Iranian and Houthi threats against Americans continue to grow. PBS NewsHour, citing the Associated Press, reported that Trump vows to “finish the job” in Iran eight months after the war began, that a third aircraft carrier and thousands of troops are heading to the region, and that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who faces a close vote on Tuesday, says Iran’s leadership must be eliminated. FinancialJuice also reported that Netanyahu, speaking at an Arrow missile defense battery, said Israel has intelligence indications that Iran and its proxies may try to disrupt the Israeli elections with an attack.

On the diplomatic track, FinancialJuice reported, citing an official briefed on the situation, that Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Sunday to try to ease regional tensions. PBS NewsHour, citing the AP, reported that Iran said messages are still being passed to the United States, that an April ceasefire paused the war but several exchanges of fire have followed, and that Iranians face medicine shortages and soaring inflation. CNBC reported that Saudi Arabia said it intercepted missiles and drones launched by Iran-backed Houthis, and FinancialJuice relayed a Saudi-led coalition statement that it destroyed a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh. CNBC also reported on Monday that oil fell more than 2% as traders weighed the risk of further attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure against demand destruction. FinancialJuice relayed a claim by Iran’s Tasnim news agency that Iran’s air defense shot down a drone near Bandar Abbas; the claim has not been confirmed.

On CNN and CBS, Blanche defends the suit tied to a same-name candidate in Alaska, saying Trump sees the contest as “not a fair fight”

Attorney General Todd Blanche defended the Justice Department’s lawsuit over Alaska’s Senate race in Sunday interviews, PBS NewsHour, CBS News and The Hill reported. CBS News reported that the department filed the suit last week to challenge pre-emptively the certification of the race if Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan loses, and that Blanche told “Face the Nation” the department is “doing our own research under the law.” PBS NewsHour reported that Blanche told CNN’s “State of the Union” that Trump, who said Friday he was “the one that said do it,” is not trying to overturn state results “in any way, shape or form” before the Nov. 3 election. The Hill reported that Blanche said Trump does not consider the contest a fair fight because a candidate with the same name as the incumbent is on the ballot, which Blanche argued could confuse voters. NBC News reported that attorneys for Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola and Senate Democrats filed a motion Friday asking a federal judge to drop the suit, which seeks to block the results if Sullivan loses. The Hill reported that Sen. Thom Tillis, R-N.C., said it is “appropriate to take a look at” the ballot, and that Trump attacked Sen. Lisa Murkowski and Alaska’s ranked choice voting system. NBC News and PBS NewsHour reported that Peltola and her family suffered “minor injuries” when another vehicle struck theirs, and that her campaign is rescheduling events.

Politics · Midterms✓ 4 outlets · the Michigan race outcome is open

Trump calls Michigan Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed a “jihadist” at a Port Huron rally, and NBC News says the race is a dead heat

President Donald Trump attacked Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed over his Muslim faith at a rally in Port Huron, Michigan, on Sunday, Oct. 25, and called him a “jihadist,” CBS News reported, ahead of the Nov. 3 midterm elections. The New York Times reported that Trump said El-Sayed would “invite hordes of jihadis to our shores,” and headlined its story “In Islamophobic Rant, Trump Seeks to Stir Republican Base in Michigan.” NBC News reported that Trump leaned into Islamophobic rhetoric and that El-Sayed is locked in a dead heat with Republican Mike Rogers. The Hill reported that Trump said El-Sayed should have to pass through a metal detector to enter the Senate building.

In an interview on “PoliticsNation” with Al Sharpton, El-Sayed said Trump has as much regard for the Constitution as “a piece of toilet paper,” The Hill reported. The New York Times reported that at rallies this weekend, former President Barack Obama and Trump delivered two starkly contrasting pitches, differing in style and substance.

World · Brazil✓ 5 outlets · the final margin is not confirmed

Lula wins a fourth term in Brazil’s runoff, and Flávio Bolsonaro says he will seek annulment of the vote

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term in Sunday’s runoff, defeating far-right candidate Flávio Bolsonaro, The Guardian reported, calling it a comeback weeks after Lula narrowly lost the first round. Axios reported that Lula was elected to a fourth, nonconsecutive term according to early results released Sunday evening. The New York Times said Lula narrowly triumphed over the eldest son of the right-wing leader he defeated four years ago, and reported that Lula accused officials in some areas of slowing transit while Bolsonaro said flooding was hampering his voters. The Guardian reported that polls closed after Brazil’s supreme court ordered a two-hour extension.

Reuters reported that Flávio Bolsonaro refuses to concede and says he will seek annulment of the vote. Bloomberg reported that traders were bracing for steep losses across Brazilian markets on Monday, and that Chicago soybean futures fell to a two-month low on expectations that Lula’s win will bring closer ties with China, which could erode the U.S. share of the world’s biggest soybean import market.

World · Venezuela✓ 5 outlets · whether elections will be called is not known

Nobel laureate María Corina Machado returns to Venezuela after 10 months in exile, a challenge to the interim government

Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado returned to Venezuela on Sunday, Oct. 25, after 10 months in exile, The Hill reported. She announced her arrival on the social platform X with the words “Estoy aquí. Con ustedes,” which translates to “I am here. With you,” according to The Hill and NPR. The Hill identified her as the 2025 Nobel Peace Prize recipient, and The Guardian reported that she left the country almost a year ago to receive the prize. PBS NewsHour reported that it was her first appearance since the U.S. military deposed then-President Nicolás Maduro, and The Guardian wrote that her return is unlikely to please interim president Delcy Rodríguez.

The Associated Press reported, in a story carried by ABC News, that Rodríguez took over after U.S. forces captured Maduro in January, was initially expected to serve 90 days as acting president, and has stayed in office without calling an election. The AP reported that Machado has called for elections soon while Washington says Venezuela must first stabilize.
