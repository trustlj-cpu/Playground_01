U.S. intelligence chief warns Iran has stepped up plots against Americans as the State Department cuts embassy staffing across the Middle East

A memo from Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to Secretary of State Marco Rubio says Iran has “substantially increased efforts to target U.S. personnel,” and the State Department has ordered departures from Saudi Arabia and cut staff at four more embassies.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton warned Secretary of State Marco Rubio in a memo that Iran has “substantially increased efforts to target U.S. personnel,” CBS News and Fox News reported on Friday, Oct. 23. The Hill reported that the memo describes the efforts as “advanced,” and the New York Times reported, citing officials, that Iran is in “advanced stages” of planning attacks against Americans. The Financial Times reported that the memo warns Iran is plotting to kill Americans in the Middle East. Axios reported that the memo does not identify specific targets, countries outside the Middle East or the nature of the plots. A memo is an internal written message between officials, and the reports describe this one as addressed to the head of the State Department.

The New York Times reported that the U.S. government has ordered diplomats and other federal employees to leave Saudi Arabia, and Bloomberg’s headline said Iranian plots and Houthi attacks spurred departure orders in the Middle East. The Associated Press reported that the United States is reducing staffing at four Middle East embassies over increased threats from Iran. The Spokesman-Review’s account of the orders said the State Department ordered reductions at embassies in the United Arab Emirates, Lebanon, Qatar and Jordan on Friday, after ordering family members and nonessential staff out of the Riyadh embassy and the consulates in Jeddah and Dhahran earlier in the week. It quoted an American official saying, “we need to reduce our footprint and keep only essential staff,” and said the State Department had earlier called reports of broader evacuations inaccurate. The same account put the orders nearly eight months into the U.S. and Israeli war against Iran and cited threats from Houthi fighters in Yemen as a second driver.

Dow falls 1.23% and S&P 500 slips 0.52% as oil climbs and the 10-year Treasury yield edges up to 4.99%

U.S. stocks ended Friday, Oct. 23, lower, with ABC News and The Washington Times carrying a wire report that said rising oil prices were the main pressure. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.23%, or 652.82 points, to 52,215.16, the S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,623.68 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.21% to 25,955.34, the reports said. The 10-year Treasury yield edged up to 4.99% from 4.96% on Thursday. Nearly three of every four S&P 500 stocks fell, with energy the only clear sector winner. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq each fell for a second straight week, while the Dow rose about 0.4% for the week. The reports put Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, up 3.1% to settle at $116.72 a barrel, while the New York Times headlined that global oil hit $116 a barrel, the highest since April. Bloomberg’s closing coverage was headlined that the AI trade stumbled again as investors rediscovered Treasuries as a haven, and Bloomberg reported that hedge funds logged their widest daily drawdown since Covid in the stock rout. The wire report said investors will turn to the Federal Reserve’s policy meeting next week, where the central bank is widely expected to leave interest rates unchanged.

Elections · California ballots✓ 8 outlets · county responses and any court action are unconfirmed

Justice Department gives California counties 48 hours to identify and set aside ballots it says may belong to noncitizens

The Justice Department sent letters to California county election officials on Friday, Oct. 23, demanding that they help identify potential noncitizen voters and set aside their ballots, the New York Times, NBC News, the Los Angeles Times and the Guardian reported. CBS News and ABC7 Los Angeles reported that counties have 48 hours to provide identifying information and to set aside and verify ballots the department believes belong to people illegally registered. According to those reports, the letters warn of risks for officials who do not comply. According to those reports, the Los Angeles County registrar’s office said at least 18 counties received similar letters, and First Assistant U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli wrote that people who knowingly tabulate ballots cast by individuals identified by federal agencies as noncitizens risk becoming aiders and abettors to a federal crime.

The reports described an earlier demand from Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter, who gave Los Angeles County and Secretary of State Shirley Weber 48 hours to review a federal list of suspected noncitizens sent mail-in ballots, and a lawsuit seeking to block the county and the state from counting the votes of more than 20,000 people the government alleges are likely noncitizens. One letter said the government’s list includes 1,133 people in San Bernardino County. The Associated Press’s coverage cited data indicating that illegal voting by people who are not citizens is rare, hard to prove and has not been shown to change the outcome of races. The San Francisco Chronicle’s headline said the department gave local registrars 48 hours to quarantine suspected noncitizen ballots, and the Los Angeles Times headline carried the quote “This is not normal.”

Brewers beat Rays 3-2 in World Series Game 1, their first Series game win in 44 years

The Milwaukee Brewers beat the Tampa Bay Rays 3-2 in Game 1 of the World Series on Friday, Oct. 23, at American Family Field, the Associated Press and MLB.com reported. The Guardian reported that Christian Yelich led the Brewers in the opener after a 44-year wait, and Fox News reported that Milwaukee won its first World Series game in 44 years. Recaps said Yelich’s first-inning RBI triple and a William Contreras RBI single gave Milwaukee a 2-0 lead, and Jackson Chourio’s RBI single in the seventh made it 3-1. The New York Times and ESPN reported that Jacob Misiorowski set a World Series velocity record with the first pitch of the game, and recaps said he allowed one run and four hits over five innings to earn the win.

The Rays scored their second run in the ninth, when a video review overturned what had first been ruled a game-ending double play and left runners at second and third; Trevor Megill then struck out Jonny DeLuca swinging to end the game, recaps said. ESPN’s headline said Megill escaped a ninth-inning jam to close out Game 1, and CBS Sports headlined that the Brewers survived a late scare while the Rays’ offense let them down. Fox News reported that the Brewers rallied past a chaotic late-game replay and premature celebrations. The Brewers lead the series 1-0.

Foreign policy · Cuba✓ 7 outlets · no operation, timetable or deployment has been announced

Trump says U.S. warships could make a “stopover” near Cuba after Iran, and Rubio urges Cubans to work with the CIA

President Donald Trump told a Florida rally on Friday, Oct. 23, that the United States would deal with Cuba after Iran and that U.S. warships could “do a little stopover” near the island, AP, Reuters, PBS NewsHour and ANI reported. AP said Trump revived the idea of military intervention in Cuba, and Reuters headlined that he vowed to deal with Cuba once Iran is settled. The reports did not describe an operational timetable or deployment details.

After the rally, Secretary of State Marco Rubio posted on X that “now is the time” for political change in Cuba and urged Cubans seeking change to work discreetly with the CIA, Fox News, The Hill and the Guardian reported. The Hill reported that Rubio floated a CIA “partnership” to Cubans tired of “failed promises, corruption.” According to the reports, the post did not identify an operation or say what cooperation would involve. Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez rejected “all threats regarding U.S. ambitions toward Cuba,” the reports said.
