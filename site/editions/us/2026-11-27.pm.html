Andrea Stewart-Cousins, New York's First Female State Senate Majority Leader, Dies at 76, Reports Say

The Democrat who has led the state Senate since 2019 and served in it for two decades died Friday; the cause has not been disclosed.

Andrea Stewart-Cousins, the Democratic majority leader of the New York state Senate and the first woman to hold that post, died Friday at age 76. The Senate majority's communications director, Mike Murphy, announced her death. One report says she was found dead at her home in Yonkers; no cause of death has been disclosed. She led the Senate Democrats since 2019 and had served in the chamber for 20 years.

The job she held carries real power. A majority leader decides which bills reach the floor for a vote and in what order, negotiates the state budget with the governor and the Assembly speaker, and keeps the party's members together on contested votes. With her death, the Senate Democrats lose the person who held all of those levers, and the conference will have to choose a successor. Who that will be, and when the choice will be made, has not been announced. Arrangements for a funeral or public tribute have not been announced either.

OpenAI Countersues Apple, Alleging Apple Sets Up Departing Employees to Be Accused of Stealing Trade Secrets, Reports Say

OpenAI filed a countersuit against Apple on Friday, alleging that Apple sets up employees who leave the company so that they can be accused of stealing trade secrets. The filing also accuses Apple of discouraging hardware suppliers from working with OpenAI on future devices. A countersuit is a claim a defendant files against the party that sued it. Apple opened this fight in July, when it sued OpenAI, OpenAI's hardware unit io Products and two former Apple employees in federal court in Northern California, accusing them of misappropriating hardware trade secrets. OpenAI answered in August with a motion to dismiss, arguing that Apple has not said which trade secrets were taken, and with a public post saying Apple was getting the case wrong. Apple has also asked the judge for a preliminary injunction, an order that would bar OpenAI from using the disputed information while the case continues. The new filing moves OpenAI from defense to offense as both companies race to build AI products. No ruling on the motion to dismiss or the injunction request has been reported.

'GTA VI' Release Sweeps PlayStation 5 Consoles Off Shelves Worldwide Amid a Global Memory Chip Shortage, Reports Say

Grand Theft Auto VI, released last week, has pulled PlayStation 5 consoles off store shelves around the world. The launch landed in a market that was already tight: console makers have been hit hard by a global memory chip shortage, a scarcity of the memory components every console needs, which has strained the supply chain that carries finished machines to stores. Retailers had reported sellouts of the higher-end PS5 Pro since late summer as buyers prepared for the game.

The first week has not been smooth for every player. Some PS5 owners streaming the game posted freezes and crashes, and a Turkish report says a few consoles began overheating during live streams. Other players have listed disappointments with the game itself. How soon retailers can restock before the holiday shopping weekend has not been announced.

Mexico Files a Formal Complaint Over a U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration Tip Line Seeking Reports on Corrupt Officials in Sinaloa, Reports Say

Mexico said Friday it is filing a formal complaint with the United States over a Drug Enforcement Administration tip line that invites anonymous reports about criminals and corrupt Mexican officials, including officials in Sinaloa. President Claudia Sheinbaum described the line as a form of U.S. interference. Sinaloa is the Pacific-coast state that is home to the cartel of the same name, and U.S. drug agents have long worked there with Mexican counterparts.

Sheinbaum's charge of interference goes to sovereignty, a country's exclusive authority over its own territory and officials. A tip line run by a foreign agency that asks the public to report Mexican officials by name sits on the line between cooperation and intrusion. The U.S. has not publicly answered the complaint.

Republican Governors in Alabama, Louisiana and Oklahoma Invite Texans to Relocate After Democrats' Apparent Sweep of Every Statewide Office in Texas, Reports Say

Democrats appear to have won every statewide office in Texas in this month's midterm elections, according to unofficial results that have not been reported as certified. Governors in three Republican-led states are using the result to recruit. Leaders in Alabama, Louisiana and Oklahoma have issued relocation pitches inviting Texans to move to their states, presenting the apparent Democratic sweep as a reason to leave.

In Texas itself the mood is different, at least for now. Democrats and business leaders are in what one account calls a honeymoon phase, with the newly elected officials and the state's business community working to show they can get along. How the incoming officials will govern, and whether any of the relocation pitches draw takers, has not yet been tested.
