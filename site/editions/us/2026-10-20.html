Justice Department charges 28 California green card holders with illegal voting two weeks before Election Day

Federal prosecutors filed cases in all four of California's federal districts on Monday, Oct. 19, as the administration presses a wider fight with the state over its voter rolls.

Federal prosecutors charged 28 people across California on Monday, Oct. 19, with illegally registering to vote or casting ballots while not U.S. citizens, The New York Times and the Los Angeles Times reported. The Guardian and NPR said the defendants are lawful permanent residents, or green card holders. ABC7 Los Angeles reported that the sweep is part of a Justice Department effort called Operation Protect the Vote, that arrests began early Monday and that at least 11 people were in custody by the afternoon, citing an assistant U.S. attorney. According to KQED, the cases were filed in all four federal districts in the state, with 13 in the Central District, which covers Los Angeles. PBS NewsHour described the defendants as foreign nationals from several countries. CalMatters reported that some of the alleged votes are so old that they fall outside the statute of limitations, so prosecutors charged those defendants with lying about their voting history on more recent citizenship applications. President Trump celebrated the arrests in a post on Truth Social on Monday.

The charges arrive days after the Justice Department sued to stop California from counting ballots cast by as many as 20,000 registrants it suspects are not citizens, CalMatters and KQED reported, and a department official said all 28 defendants are on that list. The Los Angeles Times reported that several of those arrested said they were shocked and confused, and some told the paper they had believed they were allowed to register. The office of California Attorney General Rob Bonta said the 28 people represent a tiny fraction of the state's roughly 23 million registered voters, CalMatters reported. David Becker of the Center for Election Innovation & Research told CalMatters the cases amount to about 0.0001% of California voters. Votebeat, which reviewed federal cases earlier this fall, reported finding no sign of organized efforts to cheat in U.S. elections. Early voting is underway in California ahead of the Nov. 3 midterm elections. Whether any of the 28 cast a ballot that affected a result has not been established.

Mississippi attorney general rules police shooting that killed 1-year-old Kohen Wiley was justified

Mississippi Attorney General Lynn Fitch's office said on Monday, Oct. 19, that a Senatobia police officer was justified in using force when he fired at a car in a Walmart parking lot on June 14, killing 1-year-old Kohen Wiley, PBS NewsHour and NBC News reported. The Guardian reported that officers were responding to an alleged shoplifting call and fired into the vehicle of Vellesiya Wiley, the boy's mother. Police have said the driver, a friend of the mother, drove toward officers and nearly struck one, according to NBC News. The finding means the officer will not face state criminal charges, the Mississippi Free Press reported. Action News 5 identified the officer as Hunter Foster through a records request. The family's lawyer, Ben Crump, rejected the decision and called for the body-camera footage to be released, PBS NewsHour reported. Vellesiya Wiley has said she lifted her son to show officers he was in the car before he was hit. The officer's lawyer, Martin Zummach, said the driver had tried to kill his client. The footage has not been made public.

Rays beat Guardians 7-3 to reach the World Series against the Brewers, guaranteeing a first-time champion

The Tampa Bay Rays beat the Cleveland Guardians 7-3 at Tropicana Field on Monday night, Oct. 19, to win the American League Championship Series four games to two, NBC News and ESPN reported. Junior Caminero hit a two-run homer in the seventh inning and Yandy Díaz added another two-run shot in the eighth after Cleveland had pulled within reach, according to Yahoo Sports and NBC Sports. Starter Freddy Peralta was named series MVP, ESPN reported. The Rays will face the Milwaukee Brewers, who swept the Los Angeles Dodgers in the National League Championship Series, NPR reported. Neither franchise has won a World Series, so this year's champion will be a first-time winner, NPR and CBS Sports noted. ABC News reported that the loss extends Cleveland's long title drought. Fox News reported that some Brewers fans are cashing in a decade-old $1,000 ticket promotion for World Series seats.

Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts expected to miss the rest of the season after blood clots reach his lungs

Philadelphia Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts is expected to miss the rest of the 2026 season after tests found blood clots in his leg and lungs, NBC News, the Los Angeles Times and The Guardian reported on Monday, Oct. 19. The team and his agent, Nicole Lynn, said the diagnosis was upgraded from a superficial clot in his left calf to a deep vein thrombosis with bilateral pulmonary emboli, ESPN reported. PBS NewsHour, citing an Associated Press source, said he is expected to sit out the remaining games. ESPN said treatment with blood thinners is expected to keep him off the field for at least three months. The Eagles said he is clinically stable and expect a full recovery, CNN reported. Hurts last played on Oct. 11 in a 19-17 loss to the Jacksonville Jaguars in London, and CNN reported that the clots may trace to a calf injury from an Oct. 4 game against the Los Angeles Rams. Backup Tanner McKee started in his place against Carolina.

Law · Supreme Court✓ 7 outlets · argument dates not set

Supreme Court takes up the home distilling ban and a Maryland school admissions case, and rejects a 9/11 plea appeal

The Supreme Court on Monday, Oct. 19, agreed to decide whether the federal ban on distilling liquor at home is constitutional, CBS News and The Hill reported. Reuters said the ban dates to 1868. NBC News reported that the case follows a split between appeals courts: the Fifth Circuit ruled for hobby distillers in April, while the Sixth Circuit sided with the government. The justices also agreed to hear a challenge over race and admissions to selective middle school programs in a Maryland county, The New York Times and NBC News reported. The case follows the court's 2023 ruling that ended affirmative action in college admissions, NBC News reported.

In a separate order, the court turned away an appeal from Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, the accused mastermind of the Sept. 11 attacks, and two other defendants who sought to revive plea deals that would have spared them the death penalty, NBC News, CBS News and The Guardian reported. Axios reported that the decision leaves the long-delayed military commission prosecution at Guantánamo Bay in place. The court did not explain its order, The New York Times reported. Dates for arguments in the two new cases have not been set.
