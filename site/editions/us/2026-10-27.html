Justice Department charges James Comey a third time, this time over alleged sharing of national defense information in 2016

NBC News reports the former FBI director has been charged for a third time, and FinancialJuice, relaying a court document, reports the allegation concerns national defense information in 2016.

Federal prosecutors have charged former FBI Director James Comey for a third time, NBC News reported on Monday, Oct. 26, saying the charge is transmission of national defense information. FinancialJuice, relaying a court document, reported that the Justice Department charged him with sharing national defense information in 2016, and Bloomberg Television carried a segment headlined that the new charges against the former FBI chief were unsealed by the court. The New York Times headlined its story that the department brought more charges against Comey accusing him of leaks. NPR reported that the Trump administration is attempting to prosecute him again for what it calls a decade-old leak of classified information, and CBS News reported earlier in the day that the department was taking steps to charge him. CNBC, Axios and the Guardian also published stories on Monday reporting the new case.

Comey answered the charge publicly. FinancialJuice reported that he wrote on Instagram that he is still innocent and that his lawyers will defend him and fight the latest charge in court, and The Hill headlined his reaction as saying it would be funny if it were not an appalling abuse of the Justice Department. The New York Times reported that his lawyers said the case centers on an issue that had already been investigated and had not led to charges. FinancialJuice, citing a court filing, reported that Comey seeks dismissal of the case. It also quoted a Justice Department spokesperson as saying the case was filed to stop the statute of limitations from running out and that the investigation continues. The reports cited give no plea or trial date.

Appeals court declines the administration's request to intervene in California's mail-ballot lawsuit, 2-1

A federal appeals court on Monday, Oct. 26, turned down the Trump administration's request that it immediately order California to set aside certain mail ballots, the New York Times reported, saying the panel voted 2-1. Because the vote was 2-1, one panel member dissented. CNN headlined that the court rejected the Justice Department's demand to set aside 20,000 California mail ballots, CBS News headlined that the court dismissed the request, and the Guardian headlined that the court blocked the bid to stop California from counting mail-in ballots. FinancialJuice reported that the ruling concerned ballots from suspected noncitizens. Democracy Docket had reported earlier that the department took the case to the appeals court before the district court judge had ruled on its request. CBS News reported that over the weekend Attorney General Todd Blanche insisted on "Face the Nation" that the department was within its rights to press the matter, and the network's explainer described the legal battle over California's election process. The department separately asked Washington state on Monday to set aside ballots, according to PBS NewsHour, NBC News and the Guardian.

Trump offers $1,000,000 to anyone whose report of voter fraud leads to an arrest and conviction, days before the midterm elections

President Trump wrote on Truth Social on Monday, Oct. 26, that anybody reporting voter fraud in the past elections, or in the upcoming 2026 midterm election, will be paid $1,000,000 upon the arrest and conviction of those who created the fraud, according to the text of the post archived by trumpstruth.org. NBC News and Reuters headlined the offer as a $1 million reward for tips that lead to convictions, and the Guardian and ABC News headlined that it covers reports about past or upcoming elections. The Hill and the New York Post reported the offer came ahead of the midterm elections, with the Post counting eight days to the vote. The post does not say where the money would come from.

FTC and 20 states sue Dollar General, alleging the discount chain overcharged shoppers at checkout

The Federal Trade Commission and 20 states sued Dollar General on Monday, Oct. 26, alleging the retailer overcharged shoppers, CNBC reported. CBS News reported that the FTC says the chain overcharged people at checkout for years. The Associated Press described the allegations as deceptive pricing, and Reuters headlined that the FTC and the states allege the company overcharged shoppers. The Guardian reported that the suit cites its 2025 investigation and alleges a pattern of inaccurate pricing that affects millions of customers. The reports cited do not state a penalty or a company response.

Melania Trump-brokered ceasefire takes effect in Russian-controlled Oleshky as 1,700 to 2,000 people remain

A localized ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine in the Russian-controlled city of Oleshky, brokered by US first lady Melania Trump, took effect early on Tuesday, Oct. 27, Anadolu reported, giving a start of 0100 GMT. The Associated Press and the Economic Times also reported that the truce began. The New York Times reported on Oct. 26 that many people left in the city, where bombardment is constant, have run out of food. FinancialJuice relayed the Russian Defence Ministry as saying Russia suspended strikes on Kherson as the ceasefire started.

Kyiv Post estimated that 1,700 to 2,000 people remain in Oleshky, many of them elderly, and wrote before the start that neither Kyiv, Moscow nor Washington had made public an independent monitor or detailed arrangements for safe passage. Vatican News reported that an earlier announcement had given Oct. 29 as the start date. Reuters quoted a Ukrainian who fled the city as deeply sceptical of the ceasefire, and ANSA reported that the Kremlin called the truce a possible precedent for other initiatives.
