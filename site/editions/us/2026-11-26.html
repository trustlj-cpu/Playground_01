Judge Blocks the FCC’s Early Review of Disney’s Local Network Station Licenses in Eight Cities, Reports Say

A federal judge ruled late Wednesday that the commission cannot move ahead with an unusually early look at the licenses, which Disney calls an attack on free speech; reports differ on whether the order is a preliminary injunction or a temporary restraining order.

A federal judge in Washington late Wednesday blocked the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) from moving ahead with an unusually early review of the broadcast licenses held by Disney’s local television stations, according to reports published that night. The judge is U.S. District Judge Loren L. AliKhan, and one report says the order covers eight stations in cities including New York, Los Angeles and Chicago. A broadcast license is the government’s permission to use the public airwaves, and stations ask to renew theirs on a set schedule, so a license renewal review pulled forward by the regulator is rare. The reports describe the order differently: one calls it a preliminary injunction, a court order that halts an action while a lawsuit is decided, and another calls it a temporary restraining order, a faster and usually shorter emergency block. One report says the judge found that the administration and the commission illegally sought to punish the network in a free speech case, a characterization that does not appear in the others.

Disney, which owns the network, has called the early review an attack on free speech by the Trump administration. Earlier reports this autumn said the commission announced the reviews in April and had not ordered an early review in more than 50 years. At an October hearing the judge heard arguments on Disney’s request and did not say when she would rule, and the commission argued then that an injunction would hobble its investigation of alleged discrimination by Disney and that the dispute belonged in a federal appeals court. Disney had lost an earlier bid for a quick hearing in August. The commission’s response to Wednesday’s ruling, including whether it will file an appeal, was not reported, and the order’s exact scope and duration have not been confirmed.

Macy’s Postpones the Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday Because of an Approaching Storm, a First in Its History, Reports Say

Macy’s postponed the 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade on Wednesday because of a powerful storm moving toward the East Coast, and the procession is now set for Saturday, according to reports. Reports say it is the first time the parade has been postponed because of weather. Sources familiar with the decision said New York City officials, the NYPD and Macy’s made the call together. The announcement came hours after crews had inflated the parade’s giant balloons, one report says, and a major storm is expected to reach the East Coast on Thursday. The parade is watched by tens of millions of people, including crowds along the route in Manhattan. Another report says the crews who prepared for the anniversary procession will have to wait several days to rest, because the setup has to be undone. Earlier forecasts had warned of heavy rain, strong winds and coastal flooding. Reports did not say whether the route, the performers or the broadcast would change for Saturday, and no forecast for that day was detailed.

Steelers Cut Aaron Rodgers Hours After He Criticized Coach Mike McCarthy in an Interview Following His Benching, Reports Say

The Pittsburgh Steelers cut quarterback Aaron Rodgers on Wednesday night, hours after he criticized head coach Mike McCarthy in an interview following his benching, according to reports. Rodgers, a four-time NFL MVP, had been benched in favor of backup Mason Rudolph ahead of Friday’s home game against Denver. In the interview, one report says on a sports talk show, he said McCarthy fell asleep in team meetings and called the coach “unhealthy” or “unwell,” though reports word the remark differently. He also said he would not return to the team.

Accounts differ on how he learned of the benching: one report says teammate Pat Freiermuth told him by phone, while another says general manager Omar Khan called him directly. One report says a Steelers teammate showed support for Rodgers afterward. The team’s own explanation for the move was not described, and early word that the team was expected to release him came before reports that it had done so. Rodgers’ next step was not reported.

FCC Approves SpaceX Plan for Up to 1 Million Orbital Data Center Satellites Over Objections From Amazon and Others, Reports Say

The FCC authorized SpaceX on Wednesday to deploy up to 1 million satellites carrying AI chips, a plan the agency’s announcement ties to the company’s “Starmind” initiative, according to reports. The satellites would form an orbital data center, a computing facility placed in space instead of on the ground, and Amazon and other groups had opposed the proposal, one report says. Google and Blue Origin are also pursuing the idea of putting AI data centers in space, another report says.

A data center is a building full of computers that store and process information, and moving that work to orbit is the point of the plan. The approval is permission to deploy, not a launch schedule: reports did not say when any satellites would fly, how many would go up in a first phase, or what conditions the commission attached. SpaceX is owned by Elon Musk. The objections raised by Amazon and the other groups were not detailed.

Trump Blames the Nov. 3 Election Results for a Stock Slide, Though Wednesday’s Closes Were Mixed, Reports Say

President Trump blamed the Nov. 3 midterm elections for a falling stock market in a Truth Social post on Wednesday, writing that stocks were “down sharply” because of the results and that, had he been on the ballot, the market and Americans’ 401-k’s “would be BOOMING.” Reports say he blamed the volatile market on results that will put Democrats in control of Congress in January, and one report notes he had taken credit for Dow gains two days earlier.

Wednesday’s closing levels were mixed rather than sharply lower. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 50,182.95, up 0.33%, while the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62% to 23,585.80. One report says the market’s retreat in recent days has collided with Trump’s push to reinstate Nvidia’s chief executive and with his other policies, a link that has not been confirmed. The post did not name any specific policy or company.
