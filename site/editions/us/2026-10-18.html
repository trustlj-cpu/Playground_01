Trump defends pausing new Russia sanctions as Zelenskyy, senators of both parties object

A day after signing the waiver, the president called it “a very good decision”; Ukraine’s leader called it a reward for Putin.

President Trump on Saturday defended his executive order putting on hold the new Russia sanctions in the Lindsey Graham Act, calling it “a very good decision” that would be “good for the country” and “good for the world,” NBC News and Fox Business reported. Asked to explain it, he added that reporters did not yet know what his decision was and would “figure it out.” The order, signed Friday, says withholding the measures is in the “national interests of the United States” and gives no further detail, NBC News and NPR reported. The law, which Trump signed last month, targets Russian officials, oligarchs and financial institutions and lets the president sanction major buyers of Russian oil and gas; the waiver came two days before the law’s Oct. 18 deadline, according to NPR and NBC. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the move was a 60-day pause to “analyse” the sanctions’ impact on the global economy, RTÉ and TheJournal.ie reported. NBC said the pause followed a deal with Vladimir Putin last week to release hundreds of thousands of tons of Russian diesel onto the global market as fuel prices climb.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the decision unfortunate and urged supporters to back the sanctions, saying blocking them would encourage Russia to keep up its assault, NPR, CBC and PBS reported; CNBC quoted him calling the move “wrong.” He also said restrictions on Ukraine’s military use of SpaceX’s Starlink appeared deliberate rather than technical, Bloomberg and CBS News reported, adding that Ukraine could not use Starlink or Starshield to shoot down jet-powered Shahed drones. In Washington, the criticism crossed party lines, Axios and Fox Business reported. Senator Richard Blumenthal, a Democrat who helped draft the bill, wrote on X that the waivers had “everything to do with sucking up to Vladimir Putin,” according to The Hill and Axios. Republican Senator Thom Tillis called the move “beyond irresponsible,” Senator Ted Cruz called it “a mistake,” and Nikki Haley called it “a terrible misjudgment of Putin,” Fox Business reported. Senator Darline Graham of South Carolina, appointed to the seat of her late brother, Lindsey, for whom the law is named, said she would personally “keep the pressure on Russia” because she does not trust Putin, but that using the law is “up to him,” The Hill and The Philadelphia Inquirer reported.

Truck drives through a youth soccer game in Wichita, killing a 6-year-old boy and a man

A truck drove through a youth soccer game at the Stryker Sports Complex in Wichita, Kansas, on Saturday, killing a 6-year-old boy and a 35-year-old man, KWCH and CBS News reported, citing police. Eleven other people were taken to hospitals with injuries that were not life-threatening, according to CBS News and ABC7 Chicago. Police said the driver, a 34-year-old woman from Garden City, was in custody and was cooperating, and that two juveniles were inside the vehicle, NewsNation and ABC News reported. The two people killed were both from McPherson and were not related, police said. Police said it was not yet clear whether the crash was intentional, Fox News and CBS News reported. No charges had been announced as of Saturday night. Police described the scene as a mass-casualty incident, NewsNation and WAFB reported.

Alligator nearly 12 feet long kills 71-year-old fisherman at a Florida lake

Steven Hamilton, 71, of Winter Garden, Florida, was killed Friday by an alligator nearly 12 feet long while fishing at Dr. Bradford Memorial Park on Lake Apopka, CNN and WFTV reported. Police said the attack happened around 5 p.m.; witnesses told reporters he had caught a bass and was holding it up for a photo when the alligator grabbed his leg and pulled him into the water. The fire chief said several other alligators in the water kept first responders from going in right away, and his body was recovered about two hours later, according to CNN and Local 12. State wildlife trappers caught and killed the animal. The outlets said it was the second fatal alligator attack in Florida this year. Police and the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission are investigating together, the reports said.

Man shot dead in a dispute on a Midtown Manhattan subway platform; gunman flees

A man believed to be in his 30s was shot and killed Saturday afternoon on the northbound E train platform at the 53rd Street–Seventh Avenue station in Midtown Manhattan, NBC News, ABC7 New York and Gothamist reported. The NYPD said the shooting, at about 12:21 p.m., followed a dispute between two men that moved from the platform onto a train and back; the gunman shot the victim once in the chest and fled on foot down Seventh Avenue, according to the reports. The victim was taken to Bellevue Hospital, where he was pronounced dead. Police said the attack was not random and released images of a person of interest wearing red clothing, a black vest and a black face mask, Fox 5 New York reported. No arrest had been announced.

No Kings rallies shift from protest to early voting with events across the country

The coalition behind the anti-Trump No Kings protests held events in cities across the country on Saturday focused on getting people to vote early before the Nov. 3 election, NPR, The Washington Post and The Washington Times reported. Al Jazeera said more than 3,000 events were planned, and Fox 5 DC described hundreds of rallies from Washington and New York to Atlanta, Minneapolis and Los Angeles. Ezra Levin of Indivisible told NPR the earlier rallies were meant to send a message and that the aim now is turnout. Organizers called the events nonpartisan and asked people not to bring partisan signs, since some included marches to polling places, according to Indivisible and Fox 5 DC. Crowds varied; the AP reported a band playing for what appeared to be a few hundred people at the Lincoln Memorial.
