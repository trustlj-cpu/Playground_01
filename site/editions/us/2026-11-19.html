Trump, Xi and Putin Meet Together for the First Time in Shenzhen, With Nuclear Arms, Ukraine and Iran Reportedly on the Agenda

The leaders of the United States, China and Russia sat down as a group for the first time; no agreement or official readout had been released as of early Thursday.

President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin met together in Shenzhen, China, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation, or APEC, summit. It was the first trilateral meeting of the three leaders. Xi and Putin greeted Trump in China ahead of the meeting, and before it Xi and Putin criticized Western protectionism, the use of trade barriers to shield home industries, and the sanctions that the United States and its allies have placed on other countries. Ukraine and Iran were expected to come up, along with nuclear weapons.

Trump has repeatedly put nonproliferation, the effort to limit the spread of nuclear weapons, near the top of his agenda and has pushed for a three-way nuclear deal. Russia and China have shown little interest in it. The meeting is a show of three powers at one table, and analysts disagree about how much weight it carries: some call it a statement that the three shape the world order, others point to conflicting agendas and a weakened Trump. No joint statement, agreement or official readout had been released as of early Thursday morning. The next things to learn are what each government says, if anything, was agreed, and whether Moscow or Beijing responds publicly to the nuclear proposal.

Mary Peltola Leads Alaska's Senate Count and Media Outlets Project Her Ahead; the Result Is Not Yet Certified

Democrat Mary Peltola defeated Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan, a two-term incumbent, in Alaska's Senate race, according to the vote count and projections by news organizations. Democrats are now claiming 56 Senate seats. The state has not yet certified the result. Certification, the official sign-off after ballots are verified and challenges are settled, is scheduled for next week, so the count remains unofficial until then. A report citing unnamed officials says Justice Department scrutiny of the Alaska Senate race continues despite Peltola's win, and a second report says the department is investigating. Those accounts do not lay out the scope or basis of the inquiry, and the department's own description of it is not known. Separately, Republican Rep. Nick Begich III was reported to have narrowly beaten an independent challenger for Alaska's House seat, also in the unofficial count. The open questions are the certification date, whether any challenge is filed, and what the Justice Department is examining.

House Democrats Formally Nominate Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker; the Full House Votes in January

House Democrats formally nominated Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York as their candidate for speaker of the House on Wednesday. The full House, Republicans included, is scheduled to vote on Jan. 3 to choose its leader for the new Congress. If he is elected, Jeffries would be the first Black speaker.

A nomination by the party caucus, the meeting of all of a party's members, is only the first step. The speaker is chosen by a vote of the whole House, so Jeffries needs enough votes on the floor, not just inside his party. Some progressive Democrats voiced concerns, and one report describes defections among them in the party vote. His task once elected is to hold together a majority that includes rival factions. What is not yet known is how many votes he will have on Jan. 3.

Anthropic Prices Its IPO at $83 a Share, Reports Say, With Trading Due to Begin Thursday on Nasdaq

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI model, priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, according to reports, above the range that had been projected. An initial public offering, or IPO, is a company's first sale of shares to the public. One report says the company raised more than $100 billion and calls it a record for an IPO. Shares are scheduled to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday.

Estimates of the company's valuation, the total value the market puts on all of its shares, vary widely in coverage, so no single figure is settled. Commentary around the debut has focused on what a public listing means for how AI companies are governed and who controls them. The offering price is set before trading, and the market price can move well away from it once shares change hands. What remains to be seen is how the shares trade on their first day and what the final size of the raise is once company filings confirm it.

A Report Says the FAA Oversaw and Approved Repair Work Before the Nationwide Ground Stop; the Cause Remains Unknown

The cause of last week's nationwide ground stop, an order that holds planes at the gate across the country, has not been established. One report says the Federal Aviation Administration oversaw and signed off on repair work before the stoppage; that account rests on a single report. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has continued to blame the contractor L3Harris even though the cause remains unknown.

The stoppage came as Duffy was pushing to upgrade aviation systems quickly, and Senate Republicans are reportedly wary of his $50 billion funding request for the FAA. A run of digital outages in recent days has left many Americans uneasy, though those outages may not be linked to each other or to the ground stop. The open questions are what failed, whether the repair work played any role, and whether Congress will fund the upgrade.
