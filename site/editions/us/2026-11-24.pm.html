Lawmakers of Both Parties Criticize Trump's Role in Jensen Huang's Return to Nvidia as the U.S. Blacklists Megaspeed and Tech Stocks Slide Again

New since the morning edition: criticism has spread across party lines, Megaspeed has been added to a federal trade blacklist, and Nvidia shares fell about 5% on Tuesday, one report says.

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday after President Trump pressed for his return, and the reaction in Washington has now crossed party lines. Republicans and Democrats have criticized the decision and the president's part in it. Sen. Ruben Gallego, a Democrat mentioned as a possible presidential candidate, warned that it teaches a 'bad lesson for corporate America,' and Rep. Ro Khanna, another Democrat, has also objected. Trump has said he convinced the board to bring Huang back and has said Huang is not a security threat. One report says Huang is still due to testify before Congress next week; no date has been confirmed here. The company's shares had jumped about 31% on Monday, which is why the next move in the stock drew attention on Tuesday.

The administration also added Megaspeed, the Singapore-based firm at the center of the Nvidia controversy, to the entity list, a federal trade blacklist that restricts exports to named companies. One report says the listing came after weeks of disagreement among senior officials over whether to do it. On Tuesday a tech sell-off resumed. One report says Nvidia shares fell about 5% and that shares of Anthropic dropped below their $83 IPO price. The S&P 500 closed down 0.50% and the Nasdaq Composite closed down 1.48%, a day after both surged on Huang's return. No named source in the reporting reviewed says why stocks fell on Tuesday, so the slide cannot be tied to the political reaction. What comes next is Huang's appearance before Congress and whether lawmakers seek documents or hearings on the president's role in the board's decision.

Army Now Says It Will Detonate the Iowa Lead Azide at 9 a.m. Local Time Wednesday, the Day Before Thanksgiving, After a Court Cleared the Way

The Army will detonate the unstable lead azide stored at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown at 9 a.m. local time on Wednesday, the day before Thanksgiving, according to officials who spoke in Iowa on Tuesday. That settles a question left open this morning, when no time had been confirmed. Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle said at a news conference that a controlled detonation is 'preferable for public health and safety' to leaving the material in place or trying to move it. The announcement follows Monday's decision by a federal appeals court not to block the blast, and the president's earlier statement that he would defer to the court. The stockpile is 68 barrels holding about 9,500 pounds of the compound, which the Army says has become too unstable to move or treat any other way. Officials insist the plan is necessary, while residents, lawmakers and environmental groups have said it was not explained to them properly. The blast is expected to send up a plume of dust containing lead, and officials earlier told nearby residents to expect noise and possible window damage and to wipe down outdoor surfaces. One account has put the possible reach of the dust at 100 miles, a figure that has not been confirmed. It is also not clear whether the president has formally lifted the hold he announced Monday, or whether the groups that sued will ask the court for an injunction before Wednesday. What matters next is what residents are told overnight and what air and soil testing shows after the blast.

A Federal Judge Unseals More Than 160 Pages of Court Documents on the Search of Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington Home, and One Report Says the Warrant Sought Evidence Going Back to 1980

A federal judge on Tuesday unsealed more than 160 pages of court documents tied to the search of Rep. Ilhan Omar's home in Washington, D.C. One report says the search warrant sought evidence going back to 1980. Another says the records show how Justice Department lawyers and a judge debated the constitutional questions raised by searching a sitting member of Congress's home. A warrant is a court's written permission for investigators to search a place, and a judge approves it only after finding probable cause that evidence of a crime will be found there.

The unsealing is new since this morning, when the only item on the search was a report that the Justice Department said the House's top lawyer 'refused to be involved' ahead of it. The reports reviewed do not say what investigators were looking for, whether Omar has been accused of a crime or whether any charge is planned. Omar's response to the unsealed pages has not been found here. What comes next is whether more documents are released and whether Omar or House leaders respond.

Storms and Flooding Threaten Thanksgiving Travel for Tens of Millions While California Braces for Near-Record Tides, Reports Say

Storm systems across the country are expected to bring periods of heavy rain and strong winds before Thanksgiving, which could disrupt travel for tens of millions of people, one report says. Another forecasts a nor'easter, a coastal storm that tracks up the Eastern Seaboard with heavy rain, wind and sometimes snow, bringing weather trouble to millions around the holiday. The reports reviewed do not give rainfall totals or say which airports and highways will be hit hardest, and forecasts of this kind can shift in the final days.

In California, high tides reaching two or more feet above normal may cause flooding this week, forecasters have warned, and a storm threatens to make it worse. The so-called king tides are among the highest of the year, and the main danger is along the coast, where low-lying areas near the shore are most exposed. The tides are expected to peak in the days ahead, and local officials are the source to watch for flood warnings and road closures.
