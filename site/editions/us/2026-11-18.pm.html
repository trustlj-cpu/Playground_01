Duffy Asks Congress for $50 Billion to Prevent Another Ground Stop and Meets Skepticism, Including From Senate Republicans

The transportation secretary pitched Senate Republicans on the money after the recent nationwide ground stop. Lawmakers’ reception was skeptical, and the cause of the ground stop has not been established.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy asked Congress for $50 billion to modernize the air traffic system and prevent another nationwide ground stop, and the request met doubt on Capitol Hill. A ground stop is an FAA order that holds planes at their departure airports. Duffy pitched Senate Republicans on the money on Tuesday, and the request was met with yelling and skepticism in Congress. Duffy later said, “I’ll take what I can get,” and the FAA’s chief also addressed criticism of the agency while pushing the package. Congress would have to approve any such money through appropriations, the bills that fund the government. For scale, $50 billion works out to roughly $150 for every person in the United States.

The ground stop came during a stretch of outages that hit banks, air travel and other systems, and the root cause has not been established. Federal officials have said they believe a router, the device that directs data traffic, was involved, and the FAA halted installation of that brand of router. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the outages are not a coordinated attack by a nation-state, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there is zero evidence that they are linked. One report says current and former officials describe a push by Duffy to finish modernization quickly around the time of the ground stop; that report does not establish that the push caused the disruption. One report says Vice President JD Vance scrapped his schedule to address the outages, and another says former White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said the White House needs to “calm the temperature.” Citigroup chief executive Jane Fraser said in one interview that she expects more tech outages. Whether the Senate will take up the $50 billion request has not been announced.

Trump, Xi and Putin Are Set to Meet Together for the First Time After Xi Greets Trump at the Airport in China

President Trump arrived in China this week for a summit of Asia-Pacific leaders, and President Xi Jinping greeted him at the airport; one report called the welcome rare. Chinese officials said Trump, Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin will meet together on Thursday in Shenzhen, the first time the three have sat down as a group. A meeting of three leaders is called trilateral, as opposed to the usual two-way talks. Putin arrived earlier in the week and held talks with Xi, and the Kremlin said the two had useful discussions. Putin said he expects a big potential benefit from meeting Trump and Xi. What the three will discuss has not been announced. One preview says arms control, the negotiated limits on stockpiles of weapons such as nuclear arms, is a likely subject, and another says the talks may touch on Iran; neither is confirmed. One report says the prospect of the meeting has left some U.S. allies uneasy. Rubio said, according to one live report, that Trump is serious about meeting North Korea’s Kim Jong-un. The meeting follows Trump’s stops in the Philippines and a Quad gathering earlier this week, and it comes as new U.S. rules on AI chips bound for China strain relations. Whether the leaders will issue any joint statement has not been announced.

Anthropic’s Stock Market Debut Is Set for This Week as One Report Says the Offering Could Grow on Investor Demand

Anthropic is set to begin trading on the public stock market this week in what is expected to be the largest debut in market history. The first sale of a company’s shares to the public is called an initial public offering, or IPO. One report says the company is considering increasing the size of the offering because investor demand has surged; the company has not confirmed that. The valuation, meaning the total price the market puts on a company, is not settled: one analysis puts Anthropic’s worth above $1 trillion, other estimates differ, and no single figure is confirmed. For scale, $1 trillion is $1,000 billion.

Anthropic will be by far the largest publicly traded public benefit corporation, a legal form that lets a company weigh a stated public mission alongside profit, according to analysis of the listing. One report says Amazon and Google would together own nearly 30% of the company after the IPO, a figure that rests on that report alone. One report says some everyday investors, known as retail investors, have doubts about taking part, while another says their enthusiasm could rival the SpaceX debut. The first trading day, the opening price and the final offering price have not been confirmed.

House Democrats Formally Nominate Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker, Setting Up a Full House Vote in January

House Democrats formally nominated Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York for speaker of the House on Wednesday, the party’s choice to lead the chamber when the new Congress convenes. If the full House elects him in January, Jeffries would be the first Black speaker in U.S. history. A caucus, the meeting of a party’s members, picks the nominee, but the speaker is chosen by a vote of the whole House, so the nomination does not make Jeffries speaker. The vote comes a day after he endorsed expanding Medicare and worked to shore up support among members.

One report says seven incoming Democratic members, described as progressives, defected from Jeffries in the nomination vote, and that it was their first major move since winning their elections; those details are not confirmed. Democrats are projected to hold the House majority, though one report says seven House races remain uncalled two weeks after the election, so the final size of the majority is not set. The date of the full House vote and Jeffries’ vote total have not been confirmed.

House Democrats Prepare Wide-Ranging Investigations of the Trump Administration, With the Incoming Oversight Chair Targeting the President’s Inner Circle First

House Democrats are preparing a broad set of investigations of President Trump, his family and top administration officials, with Rep. Robert Garcia, the incoming chairman of the House Oversight Committee, aiming his first inquiries at the president’s inner circle. Garcia has said he wants to investigate the president’s sons, the vice president and others, and on Wednesday Oversight Democrats sent about a dozen letters to Trump relatives and allies asking them to preserve records. The Oversight Committee is the House’s main investigative panel, and its chairman can issue a subpoena, a legal order compelling someone to hand over documents or testify. Garcia is expected to take the gavel when the new Congress convenes in January.

Also on Wednesday, White House counsel Will Scharf effectively told incoming Democratic chairs to start over, arguing that the executive is under no obligation to respond to letters from ranking members. One report says the planned inquiries would also look at Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and policies related to Nvidia. No subpoenas have been confirmed issued, and the full list of targets has not been announced.
