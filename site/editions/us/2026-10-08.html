Oil jumps 3.6% after blasts in the Saudi capital. Nasdaq falls 1.25%, but bonds rally and the 10-year yield eases to 5.231%

Stocks slid, led by technology, as crude surged on reports of blasts in the Saudi capital. Yet the Treasury's 30-year auction drew solid demand, and the 10-year yield fell 4.6 basis points to 5.231%.

Oil did the damage on Thursday. Early reports from the Saudi capital described explosions in Riyadh, and the Houthis claimed a new attack on the city's airport; a witness cited by the Associated Press reported an evacuation, and Reuters said Lufthansa and Indian carriers suspended flights to Riyadh. Riyadh has disputed Houthi claims in earlier episodes, and the reports we reviewed did not independently confirm the damage. Crude did not wait. West Texas Intermediate, the U.S. benchmark, or WTI, finished up about 3.6% near $91.5 a barrel, by DailyDrop's close data. NBC reported that Brent, the global benchmark, touched $105 at its peak, a figure we could not reconcile with the settlement. Behind the move sits the larger Iran war: Reuters and The Washington Post report that Tehran is widening attacks around the Strait of Hormuz and vowing to close more shipping routes, and tanker risk is rising with them. Gold edged up 0.2% to about $4,157 an ounce. The backdrop is a war that began Feb. 28 and has kept oil near or above $90 for weeks. On Wednesday Vice President Vance said Iran must cut enrichment in a meaningful way to end it, a softer line than before; Thursday's blasts showed how quickly such hope can fade. Every barrel added to the price lands on inflation, which is why a Gulf headline now moves the Treasury market as much as the oil market.

Stocks followed the oil. The S&P 500 fell 0.47% to 7,765.36 and the Nasdaq dropped 1.25% to 27,193.34. The Dow, which holds fewer technology names, rose 0.10% to 51,231.64. Reuters framed the morning as Wall Street slipping on higher yields and oil, which together stoke inflation fears. By the afternoon the bond market had turned. A 30-year Treasury auction found solid demand, Reuters reported, and the benchmark 10-year yield closed at 5.231%, down 4.6 basis points on the day and well below Wednesday's 24-year intraday high of 5.35%. The relief was partly political. President Trump said again that the United States will not attack Iran before the midterm elections, according to Politico, CBS and The Washington Post, which takes some pressure off crude, although The New York Times reports the Pentagon is still drawing up strike plans. The Fed's minutes this week said another rate hike was likely by year end; September inflation data arrive Oct. 14 and the next Fed meeting is Oct. 28. The week's open question is simple: can bonds keep calming while oil rises? Nasdaq's 1.25% drop was concentrated in large technology shares, the group most sensitive to long-term rates, and it shows why a yield spike hurts them first. A calmer Treasury market limited the damage to the S&P 500, though no index could shake the oil headline for long.

Isaias becomes a Category 2 hurricane. Landfall is forecast for Friday or Saturday, and Gulf oil is shutting in

Hurricane Isaias reached Category 2 strength on Thursday, according to CBS News, and is forecast to come ashore on the northern Gulf Coast late Friday or on Saturday. ABC News and Reuters also reported a strengthening storm, and The Guardian said millions of people are under warnings, with dangerous storm surge in the forecast. NBC News reported a shut-in of Gulf of Mexico oil output, a fresh worry for gasoline prices on a day when crude already jumped more than 3%. PBS NewsHour examined how El Niño is shaping the season. A hurricane warning means hurricane conditions are expected within 36 hours, so Thursday night was the last easy window to prepare. We did not re-check the exact reach of the warnings against the National Hurricane Center text; use its latest advisory and your county's orders. Landfall strength and timing are forecasts and shift with each advisory.

Word of the day
Shut-inEnergy

Oil and gas that is deliberately stopped from flowing from a well or platform, usually ahead of a storm, so crews can leave and equipment is not damaged. Output returns in days or weeks. A shut-in is a forecast of lost supply, which is why traders react before the storm arrives.

A man is shot by federal immigration agents in New York City. The mayor says a child was in the car

Federal immigration agents shot a man in New York City on Thursday, according to NBC, The New York Times, The Guardian, CBS and ABC. Reuters reported the mayor's account that the man was shot in a car with a child present. CBS, citing law-enforcement sources, said the man was hospitalized; the New York Police Department described a person shot during an interaction with federal agents.

What led to the shooting, the man's condition and the agents' own account were not consistently reported when we checked, so we are leaving them out. This is not the only case in the news: NBC reports a family has filed a claim over a father fatally shot by ICE, and the Los Angeles Times reports on conditions at an Adelanto detention facility. Video and the agency's statement will decide how this one reads on Friday.

MilitaryPress ✓ 8 outlets · date not announced

The Pentagon says the firing-squad execution of the Fort Hood shooter will be public and livestreamed

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the execution of Nidal Malik Hasan, convicted in the 2009 Fort Hood shooting, will be open to the public, and a Pentagon official said it will be livestreamed. AP, NBC, Politico, The Washington Post, USA Today, The Guardian, CBS and ABC all reported it. President Trump approved a firing squad on Oct. 6 at Hegseth's recommendation, the Pentagon said. A military jury convicted Hasan in 2013 of killing 13 people.

No date has been announced, and the Army secretary sets the time and place. Reporting from Oct. 6 said it would be the first U.S. military execution since 1961. Expect legal challenges to the method and to the plan to broadcast it, coming a week after the failed execution of Christa Pike in Tennessee reopened the national debate.

Politics · WorldWorld · Press ✓ 6 outlets · Pentagon plans per NYT only

Trump promises no attack on Iran before the midterms, as Tehran widens its Hormuz attacks

President Trump said the United States will not attack Iran before the November midterms, Politico, CBS, The Guardian and The Washington Post reported. The Post separately reported that Tehran is scaling up its attacks around the Strait of Hormuz, and Reuters said tanker risks are rising as Iran vows to block more routes and the U.S. adds sanctions on Iran's shadow fleet. The New York Times reported that the Pentagon has drawn up new strike plans while Trump hesitates. A Democratic senator told The Guardian that Hegseth is hiding the war's costs from the public.

The promise followed a Wednesday night rally in San Antonio for Ken Paxton, where USA Today reported protesters interrupted the president, and he called for a Paxton–Talarico debate. NBC reports that Democrat James Talarico has been off the trail with the flu for 12 days, a line of attack for Trump and Paxton. Hours earlier, Trump had said Iran could strike Los Angeles or San Diego, then added that "we protect our cities."
