Anthropic makes its Nasdaq IPO filing public, and CNBC reports an $80.7 billion net loss for the first half of 2026

FinancialJuice relayed that Anthropic applied to list on the Nasdaq under the symbol ANT, and CNBC’s headline put the AI lab’s first-half 2026 net loss at $80.7 billion.

Anthropic made its S-1 registration statement public on Monday, Oct. 26, CNBC reported in live coverage of the filing. PBS NewsHour, carrying an Associated Press story, headlined it a filing for potentially the biggest initial public offering ever and said the chief executive warns of AI’s existential risk to humanity. FinancialJuice relayed from the filing that Anthropic has applied to list its Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT. FinancialJuice also relayed first-half revenue of $16.3 billion, against $1.2 billion a year earlier, and a net loss of $80.7 billion, a loss figure that also appears in CNBC’s headline. FinancialJuice further relayed the filing’s warnings that Anthropic may not achieve or sustain profitability, that its costs will rise over time and losses will continue, and that it may be required to seek additional financing sooner than it anticipates.

The filing also lists large commitments for compute, the processing capacity used to train and run AI systems. FinancialJuice relayed that Anthropic will spend up to $114.5 billion with Google through 2033 and up to $120.5 billion on compute from SpaceXAI and Nvidia. CNBC reported that Anthropic has agreed to spend $3.1 billion a month on capacity from SpaceX’s AI division. Fortune reported that much of Anthropic’s revenue lacks long-term commitments, because customers can reduce usage or leave on little notice. FinancialJuice relayed a Wall Street Journal report that Anthropic is targeting the week of Nov. 16 for the IPO. CNBC reported that the S&P 500 closed at 7,676.77, up 0.7%, and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,169.38, up 0.82%, with technology stocks leading after the filing. The reports cited give no share count, price range or valuation.

Microsoft offers voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly a quarter of its workforce, as it spends more on AI

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, CNBC reported on Monday, Oct. 26, in a story that tied the offer to an efficiency push as AI spending grows. CBS News reported that roughly a quarter of Microsoft’s workforce is eligible for what the company calls a voluntary exit offer. BNN Bloomberg reported that the offer covers staff on select teams in the United States, Canada, India and Britain at vice president level and below, that some groups, including certain data center, sales and marketing workers, are excluded, and that eligible employees must decide in early December and would leave on Dec. 31. BNN Bloomberg also reported that the terms resemble Microsoft’s standard severance. BNN Bloomberg reported that Microsoft has not said how many people it expects to accept. CNBC reported that around 30% of the employees eligible for an earlier buyout offer in April took it. A Bloomberg Television Closing Bell segment was headlined that Microsoft rose on the buyouts as it retools its workforce for AI.

Judge rules the Justice Department unlawfully fired prosecutor Maurene Comey and orders her reinstated

A federal judge ruled on Monday, Oct. 26, that the Trump administration unlawfully fired Maurene Comey, a former federal prosecutor and the daughter of former FBI Director James Comey, according to the headlines of CNBC, CBS News and The Hill. NBC News reported that the judge ordered the Justice Department to reinstate her.

The material cited here does not say whether the Justice Department has responded or filed an appeal.

Meta launches a $35 billion stock offering to fund AI spending, and Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom could buy shares

Meta launched a $35 billion stock offering to fund its AI expansion, Reuters reported on Monday, Oct. 26. MarketWatch reported that Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom could buy Meta shares as part of the offering, and Yahoo Finance reported that the three chipmakers indicated interest in up to $5 billion in aggregate. CNBC and the Financial Times headlined the plan at $95 billion, and Yahoo Finance reported that Meta also plans a separate $60 billion program in 2027.

Yahoo Finance reported that the separate $60 billion program is planned for 2027, which CNBC and the Financial Times counted together with the $35 billion as a $95 billion plan in their headlines. CNBC reported that investors were watching Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft earnings this week after Meta’s disappointing results the week before. The reports cited give no share count, offering price or Meta share-price move.
