Power Is Slowly Restored After a Thanksgiving Storm Cut Electricity to Nearly 1 Million Customers, With About 490,000 Still Dark Friday Afternoon, Reports Say

Heavy rain and wind from Virginia to New York flooded streets and toppled trees; the downpour began easing Friday as crews worked through the outages.

A Thanksgiving Day storm that dumped heavy rain and strong winds from Virginia to New York knocked out power to nearly 1 million customers at its peak, and hundreds of thousands were still without electricity on Friday. More than 700,000 people were in the dark early Friday as wet snow and freezing rain fell on parts of the East Coast. By Friday afternoon the count had fallen to roughly 490,000 customers, according to the tracking site poweroutage.us, with New Jersey and Pennsylvania among the hardest-hit states. Storms also hit the West Coast. A utility counts each metered account as one customer, so the number of people affected is larger than the number of customers.

The weather has begun to ease. Heavy rain over flooded East Coast communities started letting up on Friday, and power is being restored slowly to hundreds of thousands of customers. Streets flooded and trees came down, flights were delayed, and some families canceled or postponed their Thanksgiving dinners, moving them to Friday, Saturday and beyond. When every customer will have power back has not been announced.

Trump Officials Schedule a Dec. 31 Planning Commission Meeting to Advance a D.C. Golf Course Overhaul and Other Projects Before Democrats Take Congress, Reports Say

The Trump administration has called a special meeting of the National Capital Planning Commission for Dec. 31 to move forward a golf course overhaul in Washington and the president's other redevelopment priorities. A special meeting is one held outside the regular schedule. The timing is the point: President Trump will lose control of the commission once Democrats take control of Congress in January, and the meeting is meant to take place before that happens. The commission is the federal body that reviews building and land plans in the capital region, and it could give final approval to some of the projects Trump has prized. Dec. 31 falls in the lame-duck period, the stretch between an election and the day new members take office, when outgoing leaders still hold full power. Democrats will also lead the appropriations committees, which write the spending bills that fund federal agencies, from January. Which projects will be on the agenda, and whether the commission will approve any of them that day, has not been announced.

Wind Dislodges a Tarp Hiding Where Trump's Name Was Removed From the Kennedy Center, Briefly Showing a Blank Wall, Reports Say

High winds dislodged a section of the tarp that covers the front of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, briefly exposing the blank wall where President Trump's name once hung. The covering has hidden the facade since workers took the name down in June under a court order, and for one stormy moment some in Washington reveled in the sight.

U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper ruled on May 29 that the Kennedy Center board had no authority to rename the building on its own: Congress gave it its name, and only Congress can change it. The letters came down on June 13 to meet the court's deadline. Tarp and scaffolding went up and have stayed; a spokesperson has said they remain while crews do maintenance on the marble and the soffit panels, the underside of the roof overhang. Critics have questioned that explanation, and the administration's appeals are still pending. When the tarp will come off has not been announced.

Steelers Beat the Broncos 27-5 With Mason Rudolph at Quarterback as the Released Aaron Rodgers Sends a Message Into the Broadcast Defending Himself, Reports Say

Pittsburgh beat Denver 27-5 on Friday in the first game since the Steelers released quarterback Aaron Rodgers, with Mason Rudolph starting in his place. The game, streamed on Amazon, came at the end of a chaotic week. Rodgers was cut after a public rant on The Pat McAfee Show in which he accused head coach Mike McCarthy of sleeping through meetings, among other charges. During the game, a statement Rodgers sent to broadcaster Al Michaels was read on air.

In that message, Rodgers said McCarthy never called him and that he had been barred from the Steelers' facility before the interview, according to Michaels. The fallout has spread: Rodgers's brother Jordan called the alleged meeting behavior "unacceptable and unprecedented" and asked whether McCarthy should stay as head coach, while McCarthy's oldest daughter, Alex, answered Rodgers on Instagram. Former Steelers coach Mike Tomlin called the situation a "dumpster fire" and said Rodgers was beloved by his teammates, and Tom Brady criticized Rodgers for breaking locker-room sanctity. No change to McCarthy's job has been announced.

Princess Kate's Authorized Documentary 'Heal' Offers a Glimpse of Her Recovery From Cancer Since Her 2024 Diagnosis, Reports Say

"Heal," a 30-minute authorized documentary released this week, offers glimpses into the recovery of Catherine, the Princess of Wales, since her 2024 cancer diagnosis and treatment. Authorized means the film was made with her cooperation. A clip from the film reveals new details about her cancer journey, which she has long kept private.

Catherine disclosed her cancer diagnosis in March 2024 and said in January 2025 that she was in remission. Remission means the signs of the cancer have gone or are under control, which is not the same as a cure. What she says about her treatment and recovery beyond the clip has not been confirmed.
