A Thanksgiving Nor'easter Leaves More Than 900,000 Customers Without Power and Cancels Over 400 Flights Across the East, Reports Say

A powerful coastal storm brought flooding, high winds and widespread outages to the Eastern Seaboard on Thanksgiving, and hundreds of flights were canceled.

A powerful nor'easter, a coastal storm that pulls moisture off the Atlantic and drives it ashore with strong winds, struck the East Coast on Thanksgiving Day with torrential rain, flooding and gusts strong enough to drop trees onto houses. By Thursday night more than 900,000 utility customers were without power, with Virginia, Maryland and Pennsylvania accounting for more than half of the outages, and one tally put the peak above 1 million. More than 2,000 flights were delayed and more than 400 were canceled nationwide, with the Washington and New York areas hit hardest, and the major airlines issued travel waivers so passengers could rebook without fees. New York declared a state of emergency ahead of the storm, and in Maryland nearly 2 million residents of Montgomery and Prince George's counties were asked to conserve water after the storm disrupted power.

The weather also upended the holiday's biggest set piece. Macy's moved its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday, the first time severe weather has forced a reschedule in the parade's history, leaving marching bands from around the country scrambling over lodging and travel costs; one Texas town raised money to keep its high school band in the lineup. President Trump, who spent the holiday at Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, signed disaster declarations for areas hit by severe weather, which open the door to federal aid. Rough weather reached from California to New York this week, and a section of the pier in Pacifica, California, collapsed into the sea. Conditions were forecast to improve gradually overnight in the Mid-Atlantic, though gusty winds were expected to continue along parts of the coast on Friday, and crews will spend the weekend restoring power.

Elon Musk Pledges Two SpaceX Shares for Every Child in America, a Gift Worth About $18 Billion, but the Company Ties Its Share to Active Trump Accounts, Reports Say

Elon Musk said on X that every child in America will receive two SpaceX shares, one from the company and one from him personally, a gift that reports value at about $18 billion. With roughly 72 million Americans under 18 and SpaceX stock near $127 a share, two shares come to about $254 per child. The company's own statement ties its share to active Trump Accounts, the federal savings accounts opened for children, so a child without an account would not receive one. SpaceX's transfers are to begin in December and Musk's personal shares are due in June, and he said he would give each child another share every year without saying how those would be distributed. The pledge comes days after Michael Dell said that about 90% of the Trump Accounts he funded remain inactive and unclaimed, and as the technology sector faces intense public and political scrutiny. Deposits in the accounts had passed $9 billion earlier this week. The gift's value moves with SpaceX's share price, and the total depends on how many accounts are active when the transfers are made.

Nvidia Halts Chip Sales in Four Southeast Asian Countries Under a New U.S. Rule on China Access While Trump Says Jensen Huang Is Not a Security Threat, Reports Say

Nvidia has stopped selling its chips to customers in four Southeast Asian countries after Washington tightened export controls aimed at China. The new rule, issued earlier this month, would require an export license for companies that give Chinese customers remote access to advanced U.S. processors from data centers abroad, and operators get 90 days to license the computing capacity they already provide. Nvidia's interim chief executive, Colette Kress, said on Nov. 18 that the company had already stopped shipping licensed Hopper chips, an earlier generation of its data-center processors, to China, a line that brought in $2.4 billion last quarter.

The halt lands as Jensen Huang returns to the chief executive's job this week, a move that has drawn allegations of Chinese spying over which U.S. officials are divided and for which no finding has been announced. President Trump insists Huang is not a security threat. Trump is also due to visit TSMC's Phoenix plant, one of the largest makers of advanced semiconductors, including chips designed by Nvidia.

Trump Says Putin Can Attend the G20 Summit in Miami if He Wants but Doubts He Will, and Urges Him to Pull Back From Aggression in Europe, Reports Say

President Trump said on Thanksgiving that Russian President Vladimir Putin is welcome at the G20 leaders' summit in Miami on Dec. 14-15, saying, "If it were up to me, I'd like to have him," but that he does not know whether Putin will come and doubts Putin would want to. He added that some people would not like to see Putin there, said Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky "hate each other," and urged Putin to end "military aggression" in Europe. The Kremlin said no decision has been made on Putin's attendance.

The remarks come as the United States has delayed a troop posture review, an assessment of how many American forces to keep in Europe, and as Zelensky says Washington has proposed a new round of three-way peace talks before Christmas. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has pressed for Zelensky to be invited to Miami, and it is not known whether he will be. Zelensky said on Thursday he is working with European partners to line up additional weapons packages for Ukraine.

Eagles Erase a 21-Point Deficit to Beat the Cowboys on a 59-Yard Field Goal, the Biggest Thanksgiving Comeback in NFL History, Reports Say

The Philadelphia Eagles erased a 21-point deficit to beat the Dallas Cowboys on Thanksgiving, with kicker Jake Elliott's 59-yard field goal late in the fourth quarter sealing the largest comeback in the holiday's NFL history and the Eagles' second straight win. Down three scores, Philadelphia rallied in the fourth quarter to flip the game.

In the night game, the Chicago Bears beat the Detroit Lions in a back-and-forth contest decided by late heroics that ended Detroit's winning run. Bears tight end Colston Loveland was carted off with a knee injury in the first quarter, and the team has not announced how serious it is.
