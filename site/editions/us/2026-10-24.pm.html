Elections · Alaska Senate✓ 10 outlets · no court ruling on the suit or the challenges has been reported

Peltola and a voters’ rights group sue to challenge the Justice Department’s bid to block Alaska Senate certification if Sen. Dan Sullivan loses

New since Thursday’s filing: Democrat Mary Peltola and a voters’ rights group have sued to challenge the Justice Department’s bid, and legal experts quoted by the New York Times call it dubious.

Democrat Mary Peltola and a voters’ rights group have filed lawsuits challenging the Justice Department’s bid to block certification of Alaska’s U.S. Senate result, CNBC reported on Saturday, Oct. 24. Peltola, who is running for the Alaska Senate seat, called the department’s suit a “desperate attempt” for Republicans to “cling to power,” The Hill reported, and USA Today’s headline quoted her calling it “corrupt” and “desperate.” The New York Times reported that legal experts see the department’s effort as dubious, writing that no obvious legal authority supports a pre-emptive effort to block certification, and said on Saturday that, with moves in Alaska and California, the department has stepped up its interventions in the midterm elections. CNBC noted that the Alaska race is one of several that could decide whether Republicans keep their slim Senate majority.

The department sued on Thursday, Oct. 22, in the U.S. District Court for the District of Alaska to keep election officials from certifying the result if Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan loses next month, NBC News, CBS News, The Hill and the Guardian reported, and President Donald Trump said on Friday, Oct. 23, that he directed the filing, The Hill and PBS NewsHour reported. ABC News said the filing cites confusion among candidates with similar names, and the Guardian said the department claims there could be “confusion” about two Republicans on the ballot named Dan Sullivan. CBS News reported that the department argues Sullivan would have won had the other Sullivan’s votes been counted for him, and PBS NewsHour described the second candidate as a retired schoolteacher whom Republicans accuse of being a Democratic plant meant to siphon away votes. The New York Times called the filing highly unusual, and the Financial Times reported that the department argues the similar names could “taint” the result.

World · Jordan attack✓ 6 outlets · no arrest or claim of responsibility has been reported

Gunmen kill at least 7 at a Greek Orthodox church funeral in Jordan, and Fox News reports the toll has risen to 9

Gunmen opened fire at a Greek Orthodox church in Karak, Jordan, during a funeral on Friday, Oct. 23, killing at least 7 people, Fox News, PBS NewsHour, NPR and the Guardian reported. PBS NewsHour first reported 34 wounded, while NPR, the Guardian and the Associated Press put the number of wounded at 40, and the Guardian attributed the figures to authorities. Images broadcast on Jordanian state television show two gunmen who appear to be holding pistols approaching the church and firing, and PBS NewsHour said a third man who appears to hold a semiautomatic rifle then opens fire. Reuters reported that a survivor said an assailant pointed a handgun at him. Fox News reported that King Abdullah II called the shooting an act of terrorism and vowed the “harshest retaliation.” On Saturday, Oct. 24, Fox News reported that the death toll had risen to 9 and that officials blamed “extremist” Islamist ideology.

Elections · Georgia governor✓ 3 outlets · the rally’s location and attendance are not reported

Trump says he will hold a Georgia rally honoring Republican billionaire Rick Jackson, who is walking a Trump tightrope in his first campaign

Republican billionaire Rick Jackson, 72, is walking a Trump tightrope in his quest to lead Georgia, PBS NewsHour and AP reported on Saturday, Oct. 24. PBS NewsHour described Jackson as a healthcare magnate and investor who brings a vastly different persona to his first campaign, and said his path differs from Trump’s. Unlike Trump, who was born to wealth, Jackson spent his early years bouncing through foster homes before launching his first company as a young man, PBS NewsHour reported.

President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social on Saturday that he would be doing a “BIG RALLY today in the Great State of Georgia, in honor of Mike Collins, one of the hardest working and most respected Congressmen in D.C., and the phenomenal Rick Jackson, who will hopefully be Georgia’s next Governor.” PBS NewsHour framed the campaign as a balancing act between Jackson’s own persona and the president whose support he seeks.

Tech · Ukraine war✓ 10 outlets · who ordered the slowdown is disputed; Musk’s account is reported by two outlets

Ukraine says Starlink and Starshield speeds are restored after throttling, and Musk says he was behind the restriction

Ukraine said on Saturday, Oct. 24, that speeds on Starlink and Starshield have been restored after limits that hampered its military, Axios, the BBC, CNBC and PBS NewsHour reported. Axios said the Ukrainian defense ministry stated that the speed of the Musk-owned satellite system had been fully restored after two weeks of restrictions, while PBS NewsHour put the disruption at a week and said the restoration follows a new U.S. diplomatic push to end the war. Reuters’ headline said the systems are operating at 400 kph after a week of throttling. The Hill reported that Starshield, SpaceX’s military-grade version of Starlink, supports Ukraine’s drone operations and communications, and NPR reported that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said normal operating speeds were restored after more than a week of disruptions. The Guardian reported that Zelenskyy thanked U.S. allies for restoring a system it described as key to air defense and frontline logistics.

KSL News reported that Elon Musk said he was behind the Starlink speed restriction, and ANI quoted Musk as saying the Ukrainian military violated Starlink’s terms of service and that it cannot be used for offensive drone strikes. On Friday, Oct. 23, The Hill had reported that President Donald Trump denied speaking to Musk about Starlink but said he would if it was not fixed, and Reuters reported that Trump said he would fix any Starlink access problem in Ukraine.

World · France protests✓ 7 outlets · turnout figures differ between police, the government and unions

More than 400,000 march across France as Paris clashes erupt, with 70,000 police deployed

More than 400,000 people protested across France on Saturday, Oct. 24, as clashes erupted in Paris, PBS NewsHour reported. The Guardian reported that the government estimated at least 400,000 took part while trade unions said the number was double that, and described largely peaceful marches in support of schoolchildren’s demands for more teachers and repairs to dilapidated buildings. PBS NewsHour reported that Paris police put turnout in the capital at 110,000, with restrictions confining the march to the city’s southeastern edge, away from the center. NBC News reported that thousands of students and workers marched in Paris and other cities over school funding and soaring living costs, and that Paris shut the Louvre and other museums.

CBS News reported that about 70,000 police were deployed on Saturday, and the New York Times reported that the force of police and security officers aimed to avert the arson and looting that shocked the country last weekend. CBS News reported that 3,000 shops were damaged or looted a week earlier. Fox News reported that Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said nearly 1,000 people had been arrested. The Hill reported that the NFL closed its fan zone in Paris, while the Saints-Steelers game remains scheduled for Sunday.
