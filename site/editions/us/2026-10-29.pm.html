Alphabet suffers its worst day since its IPO, shedding about $480 billion in value

Shares of Google's parent closed about 13% lower after it reported a quarter in which profit fell and heavy spending pushed free cash flow below zero.

Alphabet, the parent of Google, lost about 13% of its stock value on Thursday, its worst day since it went public in 2004. The drop erased close to $480 billion of market value, the second-largest one-day wipeout for a U.S.-listed company after Nvidia's loss of about $593 billion in January 2025. Shares fell as much as 15.8% during the session. The company reported Wednesday after the market closed that revenue rose about 25%, but earnings per share came in at $2.12, down from $2.87 a year earlier and well below the roughly $3 analysts expected. Spending is the heart of it. The company's capital spending, the money a company puts into data centers and equipment, reached $51.36 billion in the quarter, and free cash flow, the cash left after paying for that, turned negative at about $9 billion. An $8.7 billion paper loss on the company's stake in SpaceX, a loss that exists on the books but has not been realized in a sale, also dragged on the bottom line.

The rest of the market held up better. The S&P 500 closed down 0.30% at 7,720.39 and the Dow slipped 0.25%. Microsoft, which also reported Wednesday, gained. Because Alphabet is one of the most widely held companies in index funds, the fall registered in many retirement accounts. Alphabet's results were not weak everywhere: Google Cloud beat revenue estimates. What investors punished was the bill, since spending kept climbing while cash flow turned negative and this quarter's cash burn was larger than forecast. It is not yet known whether Friday brings a rebound or more selling, and the final tally of the value lost varies by a few billion dollars depending on how it is counted.

Polymarket halts trading for nearly an hour after a vendor compromise, days before the midterms

Polymarket, the prediction market where people trade on the outcome of elections and other events, halted trading on its international platform on Thursday for nearly an hour. The company said the cause was a compromise at a third-party vendor, and that users who were affected will be made whole, meaning it will cover what they lost. The outage came days before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, when trading on the platform is expected to surge. Polymarket has not said how many users were affected, how much money was involved, or which vendor was compromised. The halt landed in a week of pressure on the industry: on Wednesday, Utah filed criminal charges against Kalshi, Polymarket's biggest rival, and two of its executives. Betting on the midterm races has become a large market on both platforms. Trading resumed after the halt. A full account of what happened, and whether any funds actually left user wallets, has not been published.

Federal judge blocks Medicaid rule forcing sick recipients to prove they are medically frail

A federal judge ruled Thursday that chronically ill people on Medicaid will not be held to new federal guidance on proving they are medically frail, a status that exempts them from the program's work requirement. The guidance came from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services and is tied to the 2025 budget law known as the One Big Beautiful Bill. Twenty-five states sued over it in June, arguing it would make it too hard for people with serious health conditions to keep their coverage.

Under the law, states must put work requirements in place by Jan. 1, 2027, and people who cannot show they qualify for an exemption risk losing coverage. The ruling addresses the contested guidance, not the work requirement itself, which remains law. The administration could appeal, and it is not yet clear how states that had begun building systems around the guidance will change course.

US expels a Cuban diplomat after a shouting match at the UN over the embargo

The United States has expelled a Cuban diplomat after a confrontation at the United Nations on Tuesday. Mike Waltz, the U.S. ambassador to the UN, said the Cuban official shoved or tried to assault a member of the American delegation, and said Secretary of State Marco Rubio signed the order requiring the diplomat to leave within 24 hours. Cuba disputes that account. Its mission says a U.S. staffer crowded the Cuban diplomat while filming and that a phone was pushed away, and Havana has complained to Secretary-General António Guterres.

The row began when Waltz argued against the annual resolution condemning the U.S. embargo on Cuba and the two delegations traded shouts on the Assembly floor. The General Assembly takes up that resolution every year. No break in diplomatic relations has been announced, and Cuba has not said whether it will respond in kind.

California high-speed rail chief Ian Choudri resigns ahead of a key board vote

Ian Choudri, chief executive of the California High-Speed Rail Authority since 2024, resigned Thursday, leaving abruptly ahead of a critical board vote on a key portion of the route. His exit comes weeks after state investigators questioned $600,000 in agency spending on travel.

On Oct. 9 the board voted 7-2 to strip Choudri of his contracting authority, the power to sign and manage contracts on his own, and to require the agency's attorneys to approve contract changes. Assembly Minority Leader Alexandra Macedo, a Republican, had called for him to be fired. The authority is building a line meant to connect Southern California and the Bay Area, a project that has faced years of cost increases and delays. Who will lead the agency in the meantime has not been announced.
