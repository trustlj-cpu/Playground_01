Justice · PoliticsPress ✓ 12 outlets · subject of the probe single-source (CBS News)

Federal agents search Rep. Ilhan Omar’s Washington home in a criminal probe three weeks before the midterms. Democrats call it an abuse of power

The Justice Department says it approved the warrant in an ongoing investigation. CBS News sources say it centers on immigration paperwork from Omar’s childhood. Omar calls it political targeting, and we saw no report of charges.

Federal agents searched the Washington home of Rep. Ilhan Omar, a Minnesota Democrat, on Monday morning, Oct. 12, in what the Justice Department called part of an ongoing criminal investigation. NBC News, CBS News, NPR, PBS NewsHour, ABC News, The Washington Post, Politico, USA Today, the Guardian, The New York Times and the Associated Press all reported it. Agents from Homeland Security Investigations, the investigative arm of Immigration and Customs Enforcement, carried out the search, The Washington Post and the Guardian reported, and PBS saw them carrying boxes from the house. NPR reported that the Justice Department said it had approved the warrant. Early accounts differed on the label: Fox News, relayed by Reuters, called it an immigration raid, while the Justice Department and the AP described a criminal investigation. What the investigation covers is less settled. CBS News, citing sources, reported that a yearslong probe centers on immigration paperwork completed by Omar’s late father when she was a child and is tied to potential false statements and immigration fraud, and that Attorney General Blanche knew of the search personally. We saw no second outlet confirm those details. Omar, born in Somalia, is a U.S. citizen and the first Somali American in Congress, CBS noted.

Omar called the search “an attempt to target political enemies,” NPR reported, and said she would not “be silenced,” according to PBS, which also reported that she accused the administration of political intimidation. House Democratic leaders condemned it as an abuse of power, PBS and the Guardian reported. The timing is the sharpest point of dispute. The Times and PBS noted that the Justice Department has long avoided overt investigative steps close to an election, and this search came inside the 60-day window before the Nov. 3 midterms. Trump called Omar “totally crooked” and said “they’re looking at her very carefully” before leaving the White House for Ohio, The Hill reported. At his rally that night the crowd chanted “deport” as he attacked her, NBC News said. New York’s mayor, Zohran Mamdani, said her citizenship “should not be weaponized,” The Hill reported. A search warrant is not a finding of guilt. The Guardian described a fraud investigation, citing AP and Fox reports, but in the coverage we reviewed the Justice Department had not laid out the alleged crime or who else is involved. Watch for a Justice Department statement, a charge or court filing, any challenge by Omar’s lawyers, and how both parties use the case in the last three weeks of the campaign.

Russia · EnergyPress ✓ 9 outlets · volumes are Trump’s and Russia’s own figures

Trump defends his Russian diesel deal as lawmakers in both parties object. Kyiv says Russia keeps hitting its energy sites

President Trump defended his decision to suspend sanctions on Russian diesel on Monday, calling it “the right choice for the American people,” CBS News and ABC News reported. He said Friday on Truth Social that Russia would “immediately supply” more than 300,000 tons of diesel to world markets, another 500,000 tons in November and 1 million tons “immediately thereafter,” CBS reported. Senators, including Republicans, objected, ABC and Reuters reported, and NBC News described a bipartisan backlash. U.S. diesel averages above $6 a gallon, CBS said, which explains the push. Analysts told PBS that the volumes are small and unlikely to move prices much, while the revenue helps Moscow pay for the war. Reuters reported, citing sources, that U.S. officials hope the deal will “soften” the Kremlin on peace talks, and that Russia says it could export up to 3.5 million tons a month by year-end. Meanwhile the Kremlin rejected Volodymyr Zelensky’s idea of a 60-day ceasefire and said it wants an overall peace deal, Reuters reported. Zelensky said Russia was not honoring the energy ceasefire Trump announced Sunday, CBS, PBS and AP reported, and one headline counted six Ukrainian energy sites hit since. Oil rose Monday, NBC said, then slipped early Tuesday, Reuters and CNBC said, as Russian diesel prospects offset Gulf risks.

Markets · AIPress ✓ 9 outlets · no U.S. closing figures confirmed

Asia’s chip stocks plunge after a Wall Street sell-off, and the IMF names an AI share crash among the top risks to the financial system

South Korea’s Kospi fell more than 5% on Tuesday as chip shares sold off on worries about the AI boom, the Associated Press reported, and CNBC said the index dropped below 6,000. SK Hynix and SoftBank shares plunged, CNBC reported, and Reuters said Asian stocks dived ahead of U.S. bank earnings. The slide followed a dip in U.S. chip stocks on Monday that MarketWatch tied partly to remarks by Broadcom’s chief executive, who at a conference in Singapore likened some AI investors to gamblers. Reuters separately quoted him saying capital, not memory chips, is the biggest hurdle to AI growth. MarketWatch and AP described mixed trading on Wall Street on Monday: the Dow gained, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq slipped, and oil rose. Stock futures were mixed early Tuesday, Yahoo Finance and CNBC reported.

In its twice-yearly Global Financial Stability Report, the IMF said a crash in AI share prices and a disorderly jump in government bond yields are among the main threats to the financial system, the Guardian, Reuters, CNBC and Bloomberg reported. The fund still sees AI investment holding 2026 global growth near 3%, Reuters and CNBC said; CNBC gave 3.1%. BlackRock turned bullish on short-dated Treasuries and cut its view on U.S. stocks, Bloomberg and Reuters reported, with Bloomberg noting yields at their highest in more than two decades. Big U.S. banks start reporting earnings Tuesday, Bloomberg said. Treasury yields eased as energy prices fell, CNBC reported. Watch whether the chip sell-off reaches U.S. trading and what bank executives say about consumers.

Middle East · SecurityPress ✓ 7 outlets · U.S. decision not seen

Houthi strikes close Riyadh’s main airport and kill at least 13. Trump says he is weighing joining Saudi Arabia against them

Houthi missile and drone strikes on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed at least 13 people, one of them an American, and wounded hundreds over the weekend, CBS News, PBS NewsHour and NBC News reported. The Houthis, an Iran-backed armed movement from Yemen, claimed the attacks, CBS said. The airport remained closed, CBS reported. Saudi officials told the AP, via PBS, that the Houthis first launched drones to clear a path for cruise and ballistic missiles, and the kingdom is investigating why its air defenses failed. Reuters reported that the Saudi warning system stayed silent before the attacks.

Saudi Arabia has vowed to retaliate and wants the United States to join, CBS News reported, and CBS said Trump is considering U.S. strikes on the Houthis; we saw no decision. Airlines cancelled flights to Saudi Arabia, Reuters reported, and the U.S. embassy urged “extreme caution” at Saudi airports, CBS said. Turkey, Pakistan, Egypt and Saudi Arabia condemned the attacks, Reuters reported. Oil slipped early Tuesday as traders weighed the conflict against more Russian diesel, CNBC said. Watch for Saudi retaliation, an American decision and the date the airport reopens.

Justice · MilitaryPress ✓ 8 outlets · execution date not confirmed

Speaker Johnson and the Army’s archbishop oppose the Pentagon’s plan to livestream a firing-squad execution

House Speaker Mike Johnson said he opposes Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s plan to livestream the firing-squad execution of Nidal Hasan, the Fort Hood shooter, and warned that the video could become a “recruiting tool” for terrorists, The Washington Post and CBS News reported. CBS said Republican concerns are mounting and that Trump had approved of a livestream but suggested the decision may not be final. Archbishop Timothy Broglio, who oversees Catholic ministry to the U.S. military, said Catholic service members assigned to the firing squad should ask to be excused because the plan conflicts with church teaching, the Guardian, CBS News, ABC News, Reuters and Politico reported. CBS also reported that Pope Leo XIV weighed in on executions amid the plan; we did not see what he said. Hasan killed 13 people at Fort Hood in 2009. We saw no confirmed execution date.

The debate lands as released 911 calls show how confusion delayed help after Christa Pike’s botched execution, CBS News, ABC News, PBS and The New York Times reported. The first call from inside the prison came at 8:40 p.m., and about 30 minutes passed before officials verified it, the Times and CBS said. ABC reported that one of Pike’s lawyers called 911 to say she was “still alive and breathing” and needed immediate care. CBS reported that Pike is back in prison and able to talk. The mechanics of execution are in public view just as the Pentagon weighs putting one on camera. Watch whether the Pentagon confirms the livestream, names a date or backs away.
