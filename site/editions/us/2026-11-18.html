Mary Peltola Leads Alaska’s Senate Count Over Sen. Dan Sullivan, Media Outlets Project; Results Are Not Yet Certified

News organizations projected late Tuesday and early Wednesday that Democrat Mary Peltola is ahead of the Republican incumbent. Democrats claim the seat would lift them to 56 seats, pending certification.

Democrat Mary Peltola leads Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan, a two-term incumbent, in the unofficial count in Alaska’s U.S. Senate race, and news organizations projected her ahead in calls made late Tuesday and early Wednesday. A projection is a call by news organizations based on counted ballots and statistical models. It is not an official result, and Alaska’s results are not yet certified, meaning the state’s election officials have not signed off on final counts. Only certified results make a winner official. Peltola, a former U.S. representative, would be the first Alaska Native to serve in the Senate if the lead holds and is certified, according to coverage. Democrats say the seat would be their ninth Senate pickup this year and give them 56 seats in the 100-member chamber. That tally is the party’s own claim, pending certification, and depends on the Alaska projection holding. In plain terms, Democrats say they would hold 56 of every 100 Senate seats.

The size of a caucus matters because of Senate rules. A simple majority is 51 seats, enough to confirm nominees, while ending a filibuster on most legislation takes 60 votes. If Democrats’ claimed 56 seats were certified, they would be five above a bare majority and four short of the filibuster threshold on their own. The projection also lands as Democrats sort out their own leadership, with a contest for Senate majority leader reported to be dividing the party. Whether Sullivan has conceded, when Alaska will certify its results and whether any count could still change have not been confirmed. Senators are not seated until the new Congress convenes in January, so no change in Senate control takes effect today.

FAA Pauses Juniper Router Installations After the Nationwide Ground Stop; the Root Cause Has Not Been Established

The Federal Aviation Administration has halted installation of Juniper Networks routers after the nationwide ground stop, an agency spokeswoman confirmed. A ground stop is an FAA order that holds planes at their departure airports. Federal officials said they believe a Juniper router caused a data overload, meaning more traffic than the device could handle, that disrupted the system, but the root cause has not been established. One person familiar with the investigation said the FAA oversaw and signed off on repair work before the ground stop, and that blaming contractor L3Harris overlooked the agency’s role. That account rests on a single anonymous source and is unconfirmed. The ground stop came amid outages that have hit banks, air travel, the National Security Agency, Microsoft and Apple since last Thursday. Apple has restored its services. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the outages are not a cyberattack and not a coordinated effort by a nation-state. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there is “zero” evidence the outages are linked, and also said the rush to secure systems may be causing failures. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has vowed to modernize the aging air traffic system.

Nvidia Reports First Earnings Since Huang’s Exit as Its Board Says It Does Not Intend to Bring Him Back

Nvidia reported earnings on Tuesday, its first since Jensen Huang left as chief executive. Reports describe strong sales and a solid outlook, and one report says the results beat analyst estimates, the average forecasts of Wall Street analysts. Colette Kress is serving as interim chief executive, according to one report. Nvidia’s stock fell ahead of the report. The company’s board chair said the board does not intend to bring Huang back. In his first statement since resigning, Huang said he would be honored to return if the board asked, and that he is committed to cooperating with the board’s independent investigation.

The company is under scrutiny on several fronts. Huang agreed to appear for a House interview in December, according to a report, in connection with what coverage describes as an affair scandal; the allegations themselves have not been confirmed. Nvidia also formed a committee to oversee its national security and export policies. One report relaying an unnamed account says the Securities and Exchange Commission is looking at Nvidia; the agency has not confirmed that.

New U.S. Rules Target China’s Remote Access to Advanced AI Chips and Rattle Southeast Asia

The Trump administration has issued an order aimed at restricting China’s access to high-end AI chips, including through remote access, where a company rents computing power in data centers abroad instead of buying the chips. The new rules could put billions of dollars of investment in Southeast Asia at risk, according to reporting from the region. Nvidia has reportedly warned chip buyers of legal risk tied to China AI access, and according to anonymous sources in one report it will not sell chips to buyers that rent more than 50 percent of their GPUs, the processors that train AI models, to China. One report quotes a Chinese official, Guo, as saying China will protect the rights and interests of its firms.

Washington also faces pressure on how it enforces existing export controls, the rules limiting what technology can be shipped abroad. The administration said it is investigating Megaspeed, described as an Nvidia partner, after criticism that the Commerce Department had been too slow; U.S. agencies said the investigation began in 2024. Nvidia has formed a committee to oversee export controls. One report calls the curbs a dilemma for Xi Jinping ahead of a planned meeting with President Trump. Whether the rules will change before then has not been confirmed.

Jeffries Backs Medicare Expansion Before House Democrats’ Speaker Vote While a Senate Leadership Contest Reportedly Divides the Party

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries endorsed expanding Medicare on the eve of a vote by House Democrats on their speaker nominee, according to reports. He backed lowering the Medicare eligibility age, currently 65, covering all children and adding vision and hearing coverage. Another report says he was working to shore up support before the vote. A brief wire item quotes Jeffries as saying Democrats are discussing whether to keep the House’s China panel next year.

In the Senate, one report says a contest between Sens. Chuck Schumer and Chris Van Hollen to be majority leader, the party’s top Senate post, is sowing division in a party preparing for the new Congress. The majority leader controls the floor schedule, so the winner will shape how Democrats use their expanded caucus. The date of the Senate leadership vote has not been confirmed.
