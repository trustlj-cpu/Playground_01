Shooting at Oregon elementary school kills three children and a teacher

Police and news outlets say the suspect was a 16-year-old former student; a bystander who stopped him has been identified.

A gunman opened fire at Silver Rail Elementary School in Oregon on Thursday, October 15, killing three children and a teacher, according to reports from The New York Times, The Guardian, NBC News and the Associated Press. One outlet described the victims as three boys and a female teacher. The shooting was the lead item in newsletters and morning rundowns from several of those outlets by early Friday, and by Saturday morning it remained the most heavily covered story in the American press.

By Friday, NBC News reported, citing sources, that the suspect was a 16-year-old whose mother had purchased the gun used in the attack. The Associated Press reported that police said the teenager was a former student of the school. NBC’s Friday morning rundown said the teen suspect was dead, but the reports reviewed here do not agree on, or fully explain, how the suspect died, and that point is not confirmed. A separate report identified a bystander, described by one outlet as “heroic,” who stopped the suspect.

Local detail came from families. One outlet interviewed the mother of a fifth-grade student, who described the moment her son told her what had happened at the school. The New York Times placed Silver Rail Elementary on what it called a “grim list” of American school shootings. No outlet reviewed for this edition has reported a motive, and none has said whether anyone beyond the suspect faces criminal charges in connection with how the gun was obtained.

Several basic facts are still open. Official names of the victims had not been published in full in the reports reviewed, the source of the NBC account about the gun is anonymous, and the AP’s description of the suspect as a former student rests on police statements that other outlets repeated. Expect local police and school district officials to release more information through the weekend, including the timeline inside the building and the identities of those killed.

Trump suspends new Russia sanctions after diesel purchase deal

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday suspending sweeping new sanctions against Russia that Congress had passed, according to NBC News and The New York Times. NBC reported that the order cited “national interests” and followed a deal to purchase diesel. The Times reported that the sanctions were part of legislation named after Senator Lindsey Graham, the South Carolina Republican, and described the order as exempting Russia from that legislation. The terms of the diesel deal, including its size, seller and price, were not detailed in the summaries reviewed and are not confirmed. The same day, NBC reported that Mr. Trump called Ukraine’s resumption of strikes on Russian oil refineries a “disgrace.” Neither outlet reported a response from Congress in the material reviewed, and whether lawmakers can override the order was not addressed. Both outlets published the news Friday evening, Eastern time.

Homeland Security chief calls Ilhan Omar probe a “strong case”

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Friday that the investigation of Representative Ilhan Omar, a Minnesota Democrat, is a “strong case,” CBS News reported. The department’s remarks gave more detail about the search of her home. The New York Times reported that federal investigators seized the phone of Ms. Omar’s sister before the search of the lawmaker’s home, and that the criminal investigation concerns her immigration process.

No charges against Ms. Omar had been reported in the material reviewed. The evidence behind Mr. Mullin’s description has not been made public, and neither outlet reported a response from Ms. Omar in the summaries reviewed. The Times placed the seizure of the phone before the home search, a sequence CBS did not address. Both reports were published Friday.

Justice Department asks judge in golf course case to step aside

The Justice Department demanded that U.S. District Judge Ana C. Reyes recuse herself from a lawsuit over a golf course, The New York Times reported Friday evening. The Times described the demand as an escalation of an existing standoff with the judge. Two Times-linked listings carried the story; no second independent outlet was found in the material reviewed.

The same paper reported that an appeals court panel questioned the department’s claim that Mr. Trump owns White House records, with three judges appearing unconvinced that a federal law requiring the president to preserve such records does not apply. No ruling on either matter had been reported. The recusal request will be decided by a court; the date of any decision was not stated.

Politics · Midterms✓ 2 outlets · early-vote readings preliminary

Trump rallies in Georgia as midterm campaigning narrows

President Donald Trump held a rally in Georgia on Friday for Republican Mike Collins, whom the Guardian described as struggling, and PBS NewsHour said the stop came despite party divisions in the battleground state. The Guardian reported that Mr. Trump falsely claimed Senator Jon Ossoff’s election was illegitimate. CBS News reported early voting data in North Carolina, Georgia and Florida showing signs it called potentially promising for Democrats.

Elsewhere on the trail, Democrat Troy Jackson told Senator Susan Collins to “shut up” during a Thursday debate in Maine, The Hill and Google News listings reported, and Alaska’s Senate candidates clashed over the Iran war and prices, per NBC News. The New York Times reported Meta withdrew $25 million from its midterms push, and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez transferred $1 million to help Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan.
