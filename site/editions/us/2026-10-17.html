Teen gunman kills three children and a teacher at a Bend, Oregon, elementary school

Police named the shooter as a 16-year-old former student; a bystander shot him dead inside the school, outlets reported.

A gunman opened fire at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon, on Thursday afternoon, killing three children and a teacher, NBC News, CNN, ABC News and the Associated Press reported. The children were 5, 8 and 11 years old, and a 7-year-old child and a 54-year-old teacher were taken to a hospital in critical condition, according to those reports. Police said the first call described an unknown man shooting into a car at the school, and that officers arrived at about 1:02 p.m., Oregon Public Broadcasting reported. By the time they got inside, the shooter had been shot dead. CNN said the attack took place in a special needs classroom. Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz named two of the victims on Friday, Yahoo News reported: Rhoam Summer, a 5-year-old student, and Megan Lambert, a 25-year-old teacher. KPTV reported that police declined to release more names. The FBI and the Oregon State Police joined the investigation, OPB and KSL reported.

On Friday, police identified the shooter as Griffin J. John II, 16, who ABC News reported had attended Silver Rail as a special needs student and was not enrolled in the district. Krantz said he used a Maverick 88 pump-action 12-gauge shotgun. CBS News, citing two senior law enforcement officials, reported that his mother bought the shotgun around his birthday in August; ABC News and the AP carried similar accounts from sources. Krantz would not address it. “How the suspect got the weapon and ammunition remains part of the active criminal investigation,” he said. CBS noted that a 16-year-old cannot buy a shotgun in Oregon but may possess one provided by a parent, and CNN reported that the mother declined to comment. NBC News identified the man who confronted and killed the gunman as Kaylub Giamanco; police described him only as a 36-year-old man and did not confirm his name. Police said the motive remains under investigation.

Trump waives the new Graham Act sanctions on Russia two days before they take effect

President Trump signed an executive order late Friday waiving the Russia penalties in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, ABC News, CNBC, The Hill, RFE/RL and The Washington Post reported. Most of the law’s provisions were due to take effect on Oct. 18. The order says withholding the measures is in the “national interest” of the United States and gives no further detail, RFE/RL reported. According to those reports, it waives a requirement for tariffs of up to 500% on Russian goods and for tariffs of up to 100% on the top five buyers of Russian oil and gas, while leaving earlier sanctions in place. Trump signed the act in September; it is named for the late Senator Lindsey Graham of South Carolina, who died in July. RFE/RL called it the second sanctions waiver in two weeks, after Treasury issued a temporary license on Oct. 9 allowing Russian diesel exports under a deal Trump and Vladimir Putin announced. Earlier in the week, a bipartisan group of senators had urged him to rescind that diesel deal, the Kyiv Independent reported.

Homeland Security chief calls the Ilhan Omar probe a “strong case” headed to a grand jury

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Friday on the Charlie Kirk Show that the investigation of Representative Ilhan Omar, a Minnesota Democrat, is a “strong case,” CBS News reported. “There was multiple other warrants served on that same day. We had a judge sign it, and we expect to be able to take the case to a grand jury,” he said, referring to Monday’s search of her Washington home. The Associated Press reported that agents seized Ms. Omar’s phone and that more than a dozen grand jury subpoenas went out, some to relatives, from a Minnesota grand jury examining possible fraud in her immigration paperwork. CBS said the documents date to her childhood and were completed by her late father. Mr. Mullin also raised the possibility of a racketeering case reaching back decades, CBS reported. Ms. Omar has not been charged; she has called the search an “attempt to target political enemies and weaponize federal law enforcement,” the AP and Newsweek reported.

Justice Department asks Judge Ana Reyes to step aside from the golf course case

The Justice Department filed a motion Friday asking U.S. District Judge Ana C. Reyes to recuse herself from the lawsuit over tree removal at East Potomac Golf Links in Washington, The Washington Times, Newsweek and RedState reported. The motion, signed by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward Jr., cites the appearance of partiality and points to her treatment of government lawyers at an Oct. 12 hearing, her order that senior Interior officials testify on Oct. 19 and her remarks in other cases, according to those reports. Judge Reyes had earlier halted tree-cutting until Oct. 26 after about 500 trees were removed, the AP reported. She asked the plaintiffs, the D.C. Preservation League, to respond by Sunday.

Separately on Friday, a three-judge appeals court panel heard the administration’s challenge to the Presidential Records Act, and The New York Times and NPR reported that the judges appeared skeptical. Justice Department lawyer John Bailey called the 1978 law an “unconstitutional intrusion on the presidency.” No ruling was reported in either case.

Trump rallies in Dalton, Georgia, for Mike Collins as early turnout runs high

President Trump held a rally in Dalton, Georgia, on Friday for Representative Mike Collins, the Republican challenging Senator Jon Ossoff, Georgia Public Broadcasting, NBC News and KTVU reported. Mr. Trump called the senator “Open Borders Ossoff,” compared him to Pee-wee Herman, and suggested that Mr. Ossoff had paid protesters who interrupted the speech, Newsweek reported. He also repeated that he won Georgia in 2020. GPB said the rally also featured Rick Jackson, the Republican candidate for governor. One report cited polls showing Mr. Ossoff ahead by 8 to 10 points.

CNN reported early voting data showing signs of a Democratic advantage in two states. More than 500,000 Georgians had voted by the third day of early voting, CBS Atlanta reported, citing the secretary of state’s office. In North Carolina, 201,609 people voted in person on the first day, and registered Democrats outvoted registered Republicans by nearly 28 points that day, CBS17 reported.
