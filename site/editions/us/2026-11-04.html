Democrats Win Control of Congress, Ending the Republican Trifecta

Overnight race calls flipped Senate seats in Texas, Kansas, Ohio, Iowa, Maine and South Carolina, and the House is projected to follow.

Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in Tuesday’s midterms, ending the Republican trifecta that had given the party the White House, the House and the Senate. Race calls overnight put the Senate in Democratic hands after the party flipped seats in Texas, Kansas, Ohio, Iowa, Maine and South Carolina. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico defeated Ken Paxton. In Ohio, former Sen. Sherrod Brown unseated Sen. Jon Husted. In Kansas, the Rev. Adam Hamilton beat Sen. Roger Marshall, and in Iowa, Josh Turek won a Republican-held seat. In Maine, Troy Jackson defeated five-term Sen. Susan Collins in one of the hardest-fought contests of the cycle, and in South Carolina, Annie Andrews beat Sen. Darline Graham in a major upset. Around 10 p.m. Eastern, a projection that Democrats will take the House next year followed, which gives the party the power to investigate the administration.

Republicans held some of the most-watched seats. Sen. Ashley Moody won the special election in Florida for Marco Rubio’s old seat, and Sen. Pete Ricketts fended off independent Dan Osborn in Nebraska. Democrats also held ground they were defending: Jon Ossoff, the incumbent, won Georgia over Rep. Mike Collins, Abdul El-Sayed defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan, and Rep. Chris Pappas of New Hampshire will become the Senate’s first openly gay man. In the House, Republican Reps. Max Miller of Ohio and Tom Kean of New Jersey lost re-election. Voters cited high prices and the war in Iran as drivers of their discontent, and the backlash reached into deep red states. President Trump said he is ‘willing to accept those results.’ Still unsettled are the final seat totals in both chambers, the margins in races still being counted, and the first targets of Democratic investigations. The new Congress is not seated until January, so Republicans keep control of both chambers until then.

Texas Turns Blue: Talarico Wins Senate Seat, Hinojosa Ousts Gov. Abbott

Texas elected Democrats to its two biggest offices. Democrat James Talarico won the Senate seat, defeating Ken Paxton, in the first Democratic Senate victory there since 1988. Austin state Rep. Gina Hinojosa defeated Gov. Greg Abbott, a three-term Republican, and is set to become the state’s first Democratic governor since 1995 and its first Latina governor. The gubernatorial result was called as a stunning upset. The pattern repeated in other battleground states. In Ohio, Democrat Amy Acton beat Republican businessman Vivek Ramaswamy. In Georgia, Keisha Lance Bottoms won the governor’s race, flipping the office. In Nevada, Attorney General Aaron Ford defeated Republican Gov. Joe Lombardo and is set to become the state’s first Black governor. Rob Sand won in Iowa. Deb Haaland won New Mexico and will be the nation’s first Native American woman governor, and Arizona Gov. Katie Hobbs was re-elected over Rep. Andy Biggs. Republicans kept Florida, where Byron Donalds, endorsed by Trump, won and will be the state’s first Black governor. Final vote margins in several of these races have not been certified.

Incoming Democratic Chairs Tell Trump and Republicans to ‘Lawyer Up’

Rep. Robert Garcia, who is set to become chair of the House oversight committee, told President Trump and Republicans to ‘lawyer up’ after Tuesday’s midterm defeats. Sen. Ron Wyden signaled that subpoenas could follow. A subpoena is a legal order that compels a person or agency to hand over documents or testify. Democrats’ projected House majority gives them that power beginning next year, along with control of which committee investigations get started.

Democrats won the House with a vow to combat the president, and the Senate flipped as well. Trump said he is ‘willing to accept those results.’ The oversight chair does not take the gavel until the new Congress is seated in January, and no subpoenas have been issued. Which topics Democrats take up first, and which officials or companies would receive requests, has not been announced.

S&P 500 and Nasdaq Close Higher on Election Day While the Dow Slips

U.S. stocks closed mixed on Tuesday, before the first polls closed at 6 p.m. Eastern. The S&P 500 rose 0.36% to 7,568.92 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.83% to 25,372.01, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.38% to 51,786.72. The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.952%, up 0.058 percentage points, or about 5.8 basis points. A higher yield means the government pays more to borrow, which feeds through to mortgage and loan rates.

Earlier in the day, stock futures had fallen and oil rebounded on worries that the United States and Iran remain far apart on a peace deal. Market analysts called oil the biggest binary risk, meaning an outcome that can swing prices sharply either way. After the close, crypto dipped on the Democratic wins, and a Binance hack dented optimism, as Anthropic disclosed $895 billion in spending commitments as its IPO nears. How stocks react to the new balance of power in Washington will not be clear until trading opens later today.

Trump Says US Will End Military Aid to Israel by Year’s End

President Trump said Tuesday that the United States will end military aid to Israel by the end of the year, a surprise announcement that would halt about $4 billion in annual funding. The aid has figured in midterm campaign debates. Military aid is money and weapons the U.S. government provides to another country’s armed forces.

The announcement came about 12:15 p.m. Eastern, hours before polls closed in the first states. Details on how the cutoff would work, whether Congress would need to act, and how Israel will respond were not available. The statement arrived the same day Trump told an interview that the U.S. has no immediate plan to strike Iran, and the day Democrats won Congress.
