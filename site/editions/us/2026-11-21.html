Wall Street's Slide Deepens: S&P 500 Drops 3.7% and Nasdaq 5.28% on Friday, With Reports Saying the S&P and Dow Are Now in a Correction

A $10,000 stake in an S&P 500 index fund was worth about $9,630 by the closing bell, and reports say the Nasdaq Composite has turned negative for 2026.

U.S. stocks fell hard on Friday, Nov. 20, extending a week of heavy losses in technology shares. The S&P 500 closed at 6,910.39, down 3.7%. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 22,617.73, down 5.28%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 48,147.33, down 1.93%. In plain terms, $10,000 in an S&P 500 index fund was worth about $9,630 at the close. It was the second heavy day in a row: the S&P 500 had fallen 2.02% on Thursday, so a fund that lost both days is down about 5.6%. Reports say the S&P 500 and the Dow have now entered a correction, meaning each sits more than 10% below a recent high, and one report says the Nasdaq Composite has turned negative for the year. Another report says the Nasdaq had its worst week since 2020. Index funds hold the whole basket of big companies, so a drop like this reaches retirement accounts, not only traders.

Chipmaker Nvidia was again at the center. One report says its shares plunged 14% on Friday, extending its longest weekly losing streak since the 2008 financial crisis. Separately, unnamed sources have said the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia's accounting; neither the agency nor the company has been reported confirming it. Shares of Anthropic, which recently listed on the stock market, fell nearly 6% in premarket trading on Friday, a report said, and another report says they have slid closer to the IPO price. No named official or analyst has been reported giving a single cause for Friday's fall, so the reasons for the selloff are not established. Markets are closed for the weekend, and the next trading session is Monday, Nov. 23.

New York Mayor Mamdani Proposes Pushing Back the Rikers Island Closing Deadline From 2027 to 2032 to Build Smaller Borough-Based Jails

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani proposed on Friday a five-year extension for closing Rikers Island, the city's notorious jail complex. His plan moves the deadline from 2027 to 2032, giving the city more time to build the borough-based jails meant to replace it. The idea is that people held in custody would eventually be housed in smaller jails in the city's boroughs rather than on the island. A deadline in this case is the date by which the city has committed to stop holding people on Rikers. Moving it from 2027 to 2032 means the island stays in use about five years longer than the current schedule says. The proposal is a plan from the mayor, not a finished decision. What approvals or votes it needs, and what the new jails will cost, have not been reported.

Jury Finds Jonathan Rinderknecht Guilty of Arson in the Palisades Fire at His Second Trial, After the First Ended in a Hung Jury

A Los Angeles jury on Friday found Jonathan Rinderknecht, a 30-year-old former Uber driver, guilty of arson in the Palisades Fire, the deadly 2025 blaze that devastated parts of the city. He was convicted on two counts. The verdict came in a retrial. The first trial, almost five months ago, ended without a verdict because jurors could not agree, known as a hung jury, and the judge declared a mistrial.

Arson means setting a fire on purpose. Prosecutors accused Rinderknecht of starting the blaze that grew into the Palisades Fire, and the guilty verdict answers that question for this case. When he will be sentenced and what sentence he faces have not been reported, and neither has whether he will appeal.

Judge Approves Bayer's $7.25 Billion Settlement of Claims That Its Roundup Weedkiller Caused Cancer

A court on Friday approved a $7.25 billion settlement in which Bayer, the German company that owns the Roundup weedkiller, resolves claims that the product causes cancer. One report says the company faced 65,000 claims from people who said they developed cancer after using it.

A settlement is an agreement to end lawsuits without a trial verdict, so the approval does not by itself decide whether the weedkiller causes cancer. How the money will be divided among the people who filed claims, when payments begin, and how the company responded to the approval have not been reported.

Trump Says He Will Penalize U.S. Companies That Build Data Centers Abroad, Threatening a 'Tremendous Tax'

President Trump said on Friday that he plans to stop U.S. companies from building data centers in other countries, or to hit them with what he called a 'tremendous tax.' A data center is a large building packed with computers that store data and run artificial intelligence software, and U.S. technology companies are building them around the world.

No details have been announced: which companies would be covered, what the tax rate would be, which countries are involved, and whether it would take an executive order or a new law. A statement of intent is not a rule, and until written text appears nothing has changed for projects already under way. A separate report says U.S. data center construction costs are up 13% in 2026.
