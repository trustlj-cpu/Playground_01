Meta Ordered to Pay $25 Billion Penalty in New Mexico Privacy Case

The penalty, one of the largest ever levied against the company, stems from a lawsuit over a data privacy scandal at Facebook.

The New York Times reported on Wednesday that Meta was ordered to pay $25 billion for deceiving users on privacy, and described the penalty as one of the largest ever levied against the technology giant. ABC News carried the same account. According to the Times, the penalty stemmed from a lawsuit brought by New Mexico, which claimed that the company misled the people who use its platforms about how their personal information was handled. The amount, $25 billion, was the figure given by every outlet that covered the ruling on Wednesday.

CNBC reported that Meta is to pay $25 billion in penalties tied to the Cambridge Analytica scandal in New Mexico. Axios and the Los Angeles Times reported that the penalty was for misleading Facebook users about data privacy, and said the lawsuit concerned a privacy scandal that Facebook faced more than eight years ago. The Times and ABC News dated their reports to Wednesday, October 21, and CNBC's report was published the same day. Separately, Bloomberg Television's Asia Trade program listed in its headline for Thursday that Meta raised its AI spending outlook and announced a new class of stock; that program did not tie those announcements to the New Mexico ruling. None of the reports reviewed said whether Meta plans to appeal, or when the money would be due.

Putin Hosts Trump Envoy for New Ukraine Talks, Frees American Stephen Hubbard

Reuters reported that Russian President Vladimir Putin hosted a Trump administration envoy for new Ukraine peace talks and that Russia freed a US citizen held in the country. A separate Reuters report said the envoy, Steve Witkoff, had arrived in St Petersburg for the talks with Putin. AP News reported that Russia released the detained American, Stephen Hubbard, during the visit by the envoy, and that the release came while Witkoff was in the country. Reuters and AP News both reported the release as happening during the Witkoff visit, which was the first public detail of an outcome from the trip. A headline carried by several outlets said the Kremlin sees little substance in Ukraine peace ideas. The reports reviewed did not say whether any agreement on Ukraine was reached during the talks, and no joint statement was cited. The reports also did not describe the terms of Hubbard's release.

Supreme Court Keeps Pause on Trump Policy Restricting Care for Transgender Inmates

NPR reported that the Supreme Court on Wednesday upheld a pause on the Trump administration's prison policy aimed at transgender inmates, ruling that the administration has to continue providing gender-affirming care. NBC News reported that the court said the administration cannot enforce a new policy that restricts treatment for transgender prison inmates.

Both outlets framed the order as a rejection of the administration's request to put the policy into effect. NPR's report said the administration has to keep providing gender-affirming care while the pause stays in place. The reports reviewed did not detail how each justice voted, and they did not say when the underlying case would be decided on the merits.

Trump Administration Asks Supreme Court to Allow Big Bend Border Wall Construction

The Hill and the New York Times reported that the Trump administration asked the Supreme Court on Wednesday to clear the way for border wall construction in the Big Bend area. The request puts a dispute over the project before the justices. Neither outlet's summary said when the court might act on it, and the court had not responded in the reports reviewed.

The reports reviewed did not describe the terms of the lower court order the administration wants lifted. No response from opponents of the project was included in the summaries.

Chemical Tank Explosion in Toledo Prompts Evacuation of Nearly 1,000 People

The Guardian US reported that a chemical tank explosion in Toledo, Ohio, prompted a mass evacuation. Officials said the explosion released an airborne irritant that can cause burning of the eyes, nose and mouth. The New York Times reported that nearly 1,000 people were told to leave the area and that others were urged to shelter in place while a hazardous chemical was dealt with.

Both reports were published on Wednesday, October 21. The summaries reviewed did not give a cause for the explosion, did not say whether anyone was injured, and did not say when residents could return.
