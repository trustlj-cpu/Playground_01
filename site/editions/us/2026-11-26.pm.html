Nvidia Faces a Credit-Rating Review and a Senate Records Demand Days After Jensen Huang’s Return as CEO, Reports Say

S&P Global put Nvidia’s AA credit rating on negative watch, Sen. Cory Booker asked for records of contacts with federal officials by Dec. 16, and Vice President JD Vance said Huang is under no investigation.

Nvidia’s board brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive on Monday, Nov. 23, about 11 days after he resigned following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman alleged to be a Chinese intelligence asset. The board vote was unanimous, and the company said it had weighed input from senior U.S. officials. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and fronts the company that supplies most of the chips used to train artificial intelligence, which is why the episode has drawn attention far beyond its boardroom. The stock jumped about 31% that day, the largest one-day gain on record, but the rally has faded. Shares fell nearly 6% on Tuesday and dropped again Wednesday as a wider tech sell-off took hold. Late Wednesday S&P Global placed Nvidia’s AA credit rating on CreditWatch with negative implications, which means a one-notch downgrade is possible within 90 days. The agency said the exit and return of a chief executive does not settle its questions about how the board oversees executive conduct, and it plans to meet the board and management.

Washington is pressing from both directions. Sen. Cory Booker, the top Democrat on the Senate Judiciary antitrust subcommittee, wrote Wednesday to Nvidia board Chair Harvey Jones asking for details of the company’s communications with federal officials, including any talk of President Trump’s role in the reinstatement, which Trump has said he was personally involved in. He set a Dec. 16 deadline and asked Nvidia to preserve records back to Jan. 1. Vice President JD Vance said in an interview that the board made the decision, that the administration was told Huang is under no investigation, and that Huang does not need a pardon, a day after Trump called a pardon an option. Vance added that Huang will take export controls very seriously. Nvidia’s chips sit at the center of a fight over how much advanced technology China can buy, and no federal inquiry into Huang has been confirmed. On Thursday Denny’s said it had restored the plaque at the San Jose booth where Nvidia was conceived.

Iowa Shelter-in-Place Order Is Lifted and the EPA Reports No Airborne Lead After the Army Destroys About 5 Tons of Unstable Explosive, Reports Say

The Army destroyed about five tons of aging lead azide, a sensitive explosive used in detonators, in a controlled detonation Wednesday at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, and federal monitors have so far found no lead in the air. The Environmental Protection Agency said its real-time monitors read zero airborne lead before, during and after the blast. The shelter-in-place order for nearby residents was lifted that evening after about 12 hours, far shorter than experts had expected. The Army called the operation a huge success and said the risk of spontaneous explosions at the site has been eliminated. The material, held in 68 barrels in two bunkers, had been judged unstable in a March safety review. The all-clear covers the air, not the ground. Army crews are still collecting surface samples, and the first laboratory results are expected Friday. Some residents and at least one doctor want longer-term testing of soil, water and animals. Before the blast, local officials had advised people to rinse outdoor surfaces and wipe down children’s play equipment, and the Army’s model had forecast a plume drifting north. Vice President JD Vance, visiting troops in Ohio, defended the operation and said the problem had been left for years, blaming the Obama administration for kicking the can down the road.

Trump Calls the Pre-Election GDP Figure “Fake” and Demands a Probe After Third-Quarter Growth Is Revised Up to 4.3%, Reports Say

President Trump demanded an investigation of the Commerce Department on Thursday after the government revised third-quarter economic growth up to an annual rate of 4.3% from an earlier 3.4%. In Thanksgiving posts on Truth Social he called the earlier figure, released before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, “fake” and a “record setting error,” and suggested the mistake was deliberate. He offered no evidence. The Bureau of Economic Analysis, the Commerce Department agency that compiles gross domestic product, attributed the 0.9-point increase mainly to fuller source data on business investment and consumer spending. The agency publishes revisions on a schedule set in advance, and a first estimate is not meant to be final.

The White House said the upward revision shows Trump’s policies are delivering results. The posts come three weeks after midterm elections that will put Democrats in control of Congress in January, and Trump has spent the week blaming the election for market declines. Which agency, if any, would carry out the investigation he asked for has not been reported.

Altria Explores a Cash Bid for Imperial Brands, Possibly With a Breakup, as Imperial Shares Jump as Much as 17.6%, Reports Say

Altria, the maker of Marlboro in the United States, said Thursday it is considering a cash offer for Imperial Brands, the British owner of Winston and other cigarette brands, and is in talks with a third party about buying part of Imperial’s business, which points to a possible breakup. Under British takeover rules Altria has until Dec. 24 to make a firm offer or walk away. Imperial said it has not received an approach or a proposal. Its London-listed shares rose as much as 17.6% to 3,562 pence, giving it a market value of about £26.77 billion, or $35.07 billion.

Another report said Japan Tobacco is weighing a bid for Imperial, possibly with a partner, which neither company has confirmed. An RBC analyst said a joint offer from Altria, which sells cigarettes only in North America, and Japan Tobacco would sidestep antitrust objections because their businesses barely overlap. Last week Imperial announced plans to cut as many as 4,600 jobs, nearly a fifth of its workforce.

European Leaders Choose Spain’s Pablo Hernández de Cos to Succeed Christine Lagarde as European Central Bank President, Reports Say

European leaders on Thursday chose Pablo Hernández de Cos, 55, a former governor of the Bank of Spain who now heads the Bank for International Settlements, to be the next president of the European Central Bank. He succeeds Christine Lagarde, who is leaving ahead of the end of her eight-year term. His only rival was Klaas Knot, a former Dutch central bank governor, and the Netherlands stepped aside once Hernández de Cos had the backing of at least 16 governments representing about 65% of the euro area’s population. The choice still needs review by the European Parliament and the bank’s governing council, steps described as formalities.

The ECB sets interest rates for the countries that share the euro. Analysts describe Hernández de Cos as a monetary dove, meaning an official who leans toward keeping rates lower to support growth. He takes over while consumer prices in the euro area remain well above the bank’s 2% target, and two other seats on its executive board are due to open.
