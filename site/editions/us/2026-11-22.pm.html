Democrats Projected to Win a House Majority of at Least 258 Seats, the Largest Since 1992, as Chuck Hubbard Is Projected to Flip a North Carolina District After a Recount

Hubbard, a former journalist, is projected to have ousted 11-term Republican Rep. Virginia Foxx, the chair of the House Rules Committee, leading by 330 votes after a recount; Foxx has conceded, but the results are not yet certified.

Democrats are projected to have won a House majority of at least 258 seats, according to projections on Sunday, Nov. 22, which would be the biggest the party has held since 1992, when it also won 258, and the largest of this century. The projection came when Chuck Hubbard, a Democrat and former journalist, was projected ahead in North Carolina's 5th Congressional District, a flip of a seat that Republican Rep. Virginia Foxx had held for 11 terms. None of these results is certified yet. A recount, which is a second tally of the ballots in a race close enough that officials want to be sure the first count was right, changed only one vote and left Hubbard leading by 330 votes. Foxx has since conceded, but the race is not yet certified. Forecasters had rated the race as likely Republican before Election Day. The House has 435 seats and 218 make a majority, so 258 would be 40 seats above the line, or about 59 of every 100 seats. It is the product of the midterm elections, the national vote held halfway through a president's term, in which Democrats are projected to have flipped dozens of Republican-held seats: as of Nov. 19 the count of projected flips stood at 46, with Democrats leading in three more races.

Foxx chaired the House Rules Committee, the panel that sets the terms of debate for bills on the floor, so her projected defeat would end a long tenure at the top of one of the chamber's most powerful committees. The size of the projected majority matters beyond bragging rights because the party in control sets the agenda, runs the committees that write bills and send out letters and subpoenas, and can afford to lose more of its own members on a close vote. Reports say Democrats are preparing for clashes with the president over investigations and health care. The seat total could still move as the last races are certified, and Democrats lead in a few contests that have not been called; certified results are the only final word. The new House is sworn in when the new Congress is seated in January. Still open are the final seat count, who will lead each committee, and how the White House and the Senate respond to the new balance of power.

Senator Presses Lutnick on How China Got Nvidia's Top Chips, and Bessent Says the Administration Needs Answers, as Reports Describe an Unconfirmed Accounting Probe

Sen. John Kennedy, Republican of Louisiana, urged scrutiny of Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Sunday, Nov. 22, over what he described as China evidently getting around export controls to obtain Nvidia's most advanced chips. Export controls are U.S. rules that limit which chips can be sold to China. Kennedy called the reported affair of Jensen Huang "the most expensive sex since Helen of Troy." Reports say Huang has stepped down as chief executive of Nvidia after a reported relationship with Alice Huang, whom reports describe as a former executive at Megaspeed, a Nvidia customer that reports say bought more than $4 billion of the company's chips in about two years. U.S. intelligence officials reportedly suspected her of working for Beijing, which denies it, and Megaspeed denies smuggling chips. One unconfirmed report says the Commerce Department dropped a probe of Megaspeed in 2025 after Nvidia's lawyer contacted its agents. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday that the administration has no answers yet on Nvidia's chip sales to China and needs clarity, including which level of Nvidia's business knew and who led compliance, the team that makes sure sales follow the law. He said he is confident congressional committees are moving swiftly to get answers. Separately, a report published Friday says Nvidia is said to face a Securities and Exchange Commission probe into its accounting practices, with the questions arising amid revelations about questionable chip sales in Asia. No filing or statement from the company or the commission confirming that probe has been reported, so it remains unconfirmed. Still open are whether any chips actually reached China through Megaspeed, what Nvidia's leaders knew, what the commission is examining, and who will run the company.

Trump Orders Federal Contractors to Check With the White House Before Responding to Any Congressional Inquiry

President Trump signed an executive order on Saturday, Nov. 21, directing federal contractors to check with the White House before responding to an inquiry from Congress. An executive order is a written directive from the president to the executive branch that carries legal force without a vote in Congress. Contractors, the companies that do paid work for federal agencies, often receive letters and document requests from committees looking into how government money is spent. The order arrives as Democrats prepare to take over the House with a projected majority, their largest since 1992, and are expected to open wide-ranging congressional oversight, the investigations and information demands through which Congress checks the executive branch. Reports describe it as the administration's latest effort to resist that oversight and say it anticipates a flood of Democratic requests.

The text's exact reach has not been fully described: which contractors and which kinds of requests are covered, whether a company may still answer a committee after checking in, and what happens to one that responds directly. What to watch next are any legal challenge, the reaction of congressional Democrats who will soon chair the committees, and whether companies change how they handle requests from Capitol Hill.

The Army Says It Plans to Detonate Nearly 5 Tons of Lead Azide in Iowa as Soon as Monday, Reports Say, Though a Court Pause Is Reported and the Timing Is Unclear

The Army says it will go ahead with a planned detonation of nearly 5 tons of lead azide at a facility in southeastern Iowa, a blast that local officials have said could come Monday, Nov. 23, or Tuesday, even though reports describe court orders pausing the plan. Lead azide is an explosive used to set off other explosives, and blowing up a stockpile of it is expected to send a plume of toxic lead dust into the nearby community. The Army says it will rely on an emergency exclusion for munitions and explosives to the Environmental Protection Agency's permit rules. Environmental groups say that is a maneuver to get around a court order and to avoid public demands for safety and transparency.

The Army had hired a firm to turn the explosives into harmless waste but then dropped that approach in favor of a controlled blast, according to an account published Saturday, and critics say the blast could scatter lead dust across the state. Not settled are the exact time of the detonation, what the Army must still do before it acts, and whether the courts will step in again.

Mexico Says It Detained Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, "El Chapo Isidro," an FBI Most Wanted Fugitive, and Reports Describe a Burst of Violence in Sinaloa

Mexican authorities say they detained Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro," in a special forces raid in Guasave, Sinaloa, late Friday, Nov. 20, according to reports published Saturday, Nov. 21. Meza Flores is described as the head of a cartel in northern Sinaloa state, a criminal organization that traffics drugs and fights rivals for territory and routes, and he has long been at odds with rivals in the Sinaloa Cartel. The FBI had listed him among its Ten Most Wanted fugitives. The arrest set off a burst of violence in Sinaloa, reports say.

What the reports leave open is how many people were hurt or killed in that violence, which charges he faces in Mexico, and whether the United States will ask for his extradition, the legal process of sending a suspect to another country to stand trial. Watch for statements from Mexican federal officials and for any move by his group's rivals in the state.
