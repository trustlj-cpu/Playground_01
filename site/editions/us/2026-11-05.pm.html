Tech Rout Wipes Out Wednesday's Gains as S&P 500 Falls 4.4% and Nasdaq 5.5%

Chip and AI stocks led a broad selloff on Thursday, a day after the S&P 500 gained 1.58%. The cause of the drop has not been established.

Wall Street gave back all of Wednesday's gains on Thursday. The S&P 500, the benchmark index of 500 large U.S. companies, fell 4.4% to 7,353.73. The Nasdaq Composite, which is heavy with technology stocks, dropped 5.5% to 24,315.00, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 3.4% to 51,523.06. A day earlier the S&P 500 had risen 1.58% and the Nasdaq 1.44%, so Thursday's fall was larger than all of Wednesday's gain. Chipmakers took the worst of it: Nvidia, Micron and Qualcomm were among the hardest hit, and Qualcomm slid about 9% in the morning after a weak profit forecast. Investors bought Treasuries, a traditional shelter in selloffs, which pushed yields, the return on those bonds, lower. The VIX, a gauge of how large a swing traders expect in stocks, moved more than 11 points from its low to its high for the day, its widest one-day range since April 9.

No single cause is confirmed. Several explanations are in circulation, and none has been established: that a Democratic-controlled Congress could slow the spending behind the AI build-out, the race to construct chips and data centers for artificial intelligence, and that part of the move is a rotation, a shift of money out of one corner of the market and into others, which began with semiconductors on Wednesday. Voters approved dozens of datacenter moratorium measures on Tuesday, and on Thursday OpenAI pledged to pay for the electricity its data centers use, publish construction plans and seek community input, but nothing confirms that either event moved prices. Whether Friday brings a rebound or more selling is not known. The new Congress does not take office until January, and no law touching AI spending has changed.

Newsom Commutes All 548 California Death Sentences to Life Without Parole

Gov. Gavin Newsom commuted the death sentences of all 548 people on California's death row on Thursday, converting each to life in prison without parole. A commutation is an act of clemency that reduces a sentence without erasing the conviction, so none of those inmates is released. Newsom called state-sanctioned killing incompatible with the values of the nation's most populous state. The order covers about a quarter of all death sentences in the United States, and because it is permanent, no later governor can carry out those executions. The order does not repeal California's death penalty, and prosecutors can still seek it in future cases. Newsom leaves office in January, and the order is among his last acts as governor. It is not yet known whether prosecutors or victims' families will challenge the commutations in court, or whether lawmakers will respond.

Count Favors Raman Over Bass in Los Angeles Mayor's Race; No Certified Result or Concession

Los Angeles City Council member Nithya Raman led Mayor Karen Bass, who was seeking a second term, in the vote count released through Thursday in the mayor's race. Raman, a democratic socialist who campaigned against what she called the city's broken status quo, led by about 26,000 votes, roughly 3 percentage points, in the count released through Thursday. Both are Democrats. Bass had led earlier in the count, and late-counted ballots moved the result.

A projection is a call made from the votes counted and the ballots still outstanding. It is not an official result. There is no certified result and Bass has not conceded. Los Angeles County has to certify the count, a process that takes weeks, before any win is official. The next things to watch are the certified totals and whether Bass concedes.

Harmeet Dhillon Resigns as Head of the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division

Harmeet Dhillon resigned Thursday as assistant attorney general for civil rights, the post that leads the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, Attorney General Todd Blanche announced. She had held the job for about 18 months and is a longtime ally of President Trump. Hers is the first senior departure from the department since Tuesday's midterm losses. Dhillon said the White House had asked her to wait until after the midterms before leaving.

The Civil Rights Division enforces federal law on voting rights, discrimination, policing and hate crimes, and Dhillon's time in the job was contentious inside and outside the department. Beyond her own account, no reason for the timing has been confirmed, and a successor is not yet confirmed.

Russell Brand Convicted of Rape and Sexual Assault by London Jury

A jury in London found Russell Brand, 51, guilty on Thursday of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. He faced seven charges in all: three of rape, three of sexual assault and one of attempted indecent assault. The jury cleared him of three of the counts. The allegations date back more than 20 years, to the height of his career as a comedian and actor.

Brand denied the charges. He testified that he had been obsessed with sex as a young man and had led a promiscuous life but had never forced anyone to do something against their will. The prosecutor described him as a ruthless, calculating, manipulative "sexual predator." The judge said he will receive a prison term, and sentencing is expected Monday. What sentence he will receive, and whether he will appeal, are not yet known.
