Israel's President Taps Opposition Leader Gadi Eisenkot to Try to Form a Government, a Setback for Netanyahu

Isaac Herzog gave the former military chief 28 days to assemble a coalition after a late-October election in which neither bloc won a majority.

Israeli President Isaac Herzog announced on Saturday, Nov. 7, that he will task Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief who leads the opposition Yashar party, with forming Israel's next government. It is a setback for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, whose coalition lost its majority in the late-October election. Herzog had until Nov. 11 to decide and announced his choice four days early. Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor Eisenkot's won a clear majority of the Knesset, Israel's 120-seat parliament, and a government needs the backing of at least 61 members. The president's office is largely ceremonial, but in this one step he decides who gets the first chance, which is why his consultations with party leaders this week drew so much attention. At the end of them, more parties recommended Eisenkot than Netanyahu; reports differ on the exact seat totals behind each. The ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism, a longtime Netanyahu ally, declined to recommend anyone, and the Arab-led Joint List said it would back Eisenkot.

Under Israeli law Eisenkot now has 28 days to put together a coalition, a governing alliance of parties that together hold a majority of seats, and the president can extend that by up to 14 more days. If he fails, the task can pass to another lawmaker, most likely Netanyahu, and repeated failure could eventually force the Knesset to dissolve and send voters back to the polls. Netanyahu stays on as head of a caretaker government, which keeps running day-to-day affairs, until a new coalition is sworn in. Eisenkot has said he will not serve in a government led by Netanyahu, even under a rotation deal in which the two would swap the top job. The hard part is arithmetic: the recommendations came from separate parties with different goals, and it is not settled whether they will sit together in one cabinet, or whether Eisenkot would rely on Arab-led parties. Next: Eisenkot's first coalition talks, and whether any party that backed Netanyahu changes sides.

Dana White Says He Will Meet Trump in Washington on Monday About the White House Press Secretary Job

Dana White, the chief executive of the UFC, said he will meet President Trump in Washington on Monday, Nov. 9, to discuss Trump's offer of the White House press secretary job, without saying whether he will accept. Trump made the offer in a Truth Social post on Saturday, urging White to take the job and asking him to bring UFC fighters along as assistants, several of whom he named. The press secretary is the president's chief spokesperson, the person who briefs reporters on the administration's positions, and the post has been largely vacant since Karoline Leavitt stepped aside in August. White is a longtime Trump supporter who worked with the president to host a UFC event at the White House. No acceptance has been announced. The offer came one day after Trump named Stephen Miller White House chief of staff, and during a period of transition for the White House after Republicans' losses in Tuesday's midterm elections. The job does not require Senate confirmation, a vote by the Senate that many top administration posts need. Still open: White's answer, when he would start if he says yes, and what role the fighters Trump mentioned would play.

Berkshire Hathaway Spends a Record $9.6 Billion on Buybacks in the Third Quarter as Operating Earnings Fall

Berkshire Hathaway reported third-quarter results on Saturday, Nov. 7, and said it spent about $9.6 billion buying back its own shares, the largest quarterly repurchase in its history. Buybacks more than doubled from the earlier pace while operating earnings, the profit from its day-to-day businesses before investment gains and losses, declined, and overall profit fell as well. Weaker insurance underwriting, the profit or loss from writing policies before counting investment income, weighed on the operating result. Greg Abel, who now runs the company as chief executive, was a net seller of stocks in the quarter, meaning Berkshire sold more shares of other companies than it bought. A buyback shrinks the number of Berkshire shares outstanding, so each remaining share is a slightly larger slice of the company. Berkshire's stock has outperformed the S&P 500 this week and so far this year. Next: whether Abel keeps buying back stock at this pace.

House Republicans Seek a Briefing From Nvidia's Jensen Huang on Reports Tying Him to an Alleged Chinese Spy

Senior House Republicans have asked Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang to brief Congress on reports that he had a close relationship with an executive whom U.S. authorities believe worked for China's intelligence services. Reports on Friday night, citing U.S. officials, said American intelligence believed the relationship was romantic; neither Huang nor Nvidia has confirmed that description, and no U.S. agency has said so on the record. The executive, Alice Huang of the Singapore-based company Megaspeed, is not related to Jensen Huang. Huang has said his interactions with her were “all completely normal and not unusual” and that the government never told him of its suspicions. Nvidia has said earlier allegations of chip diversion were “thoroughly investigated and debunked last year.” China's foreign ministry called the claims “groundless,” and President Trump has voiced support for Huang. Next: whether Huang agrees to brief lawmakers and whether any committee opens a formal inquiry.

Kansas Attorney General Kris Kobach Trails Democrat Chris Mann by 1.2 Points in the Unofficial Count

Kansas Attorney General Kris Kobach, a Republican, trails in his bid for a second term in the unofficial count as of Saturday, Nov. 7, and the result is not certified. Democrat Chris Mann, a former prosecutor and police officer, led with 50.6 percent of the vote to Kobach's 49.4 percent, a margin of 1.2 percentage points. Mann had narrowly lost to Kobach in the previous race for the office, and he declared victory on Wednesday, when Kobach had not conceded.

A projection is a news organization's call of the winner based on counted votes; it is not the official result. Kansas counties must still finish their canvasses, the formal checks of the vote count, before the results are certified. As attorney general, Kobach is the state's top lawyer, the office that handles lawsuits for the state and advises its agencies. Next: certification of the result and whether Kobach concedes.
