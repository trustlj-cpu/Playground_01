Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang faces a House briefing request and a board investigation over his ties to an executive U.S. officials are reported to view as a Chinese spy

Huang has disclosed an affair with Alice Huang, an executive at the data-center firm Megaspeed; the claims about her are allegations, and no charge against him has been reported.

Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang is under pressure from lawmakers, investors and his own board after disclosing an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, an executive at the data-center company Megaspeed whom U.S. intelligence officials are reported to have identified as a Chinese asset, meaning someone secretly working for Beijing. Those claims about her are allegations: no charge against either person has been reported. Three House Republican committee chairs, Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, asked Huang to attend a closed-door briefing on November 18 about his dealings with her and about how Nvidia protects itself against foreign espionage. The letter is a voluntary request, not a subpoena, the legal order that compels testimony, although reports say some Republicans have considered issuing one. Huang’s deadline to accept the date or propose another was the end of Wednesday, and Nvidia has not said whether he will attend. On Tuesday the company said independent members of its board had hired a former acting U.S. attorney, working with outside lawyers, to investigate.

The fallout is spreading beyond the company. Larry Kudlow, a commentator and former White House economic adviser, has called on Huang to resign, and a senior Republican on the Senate Banking Committee has done the same. The briefing would reportedly fall the day after Nvidia reports quarterly earnings. The case also touches export policy, because Nvidia’s most advanced AI chips are restricted for sale to China and Megaspeed has been the subject of a Commerce Department probe. Sen. Michael Bennet, a Colorado Democrat, is pressing intelligence and Commerce officials over why that probe was reportedly paused last year. One report says a classified memo about the relationship was sent to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick; officials have not confirmed it. What the board’s investigation will find, whether Huang accepts the November 18 date and whether any law was broken are all unknown.

House shelves the Hegseth impeachment in a 211-204 vote as 11 Democrats miss the roll call

The House voted 211 to 204 on Tuesday evening to refer Rep. Thomas Massie’s articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, a procedural move that effectively ends the effort for now. Massie, a Kentucky Republican who is leaving Congress, was the only Republican to vote with Democrats. Eleven Democrats did not vote; several said a traffic incident and a police escort delayed them, and the absences drew criticism inside their party. The resolution accused Hegseth of abusing his office in military action in Iran, Yemen and Venezuela. Hegseth called the push an attack on the troops, and President Trump praised him as a “Great American Patriot.” Democrats can try again next year. On Wednesday, Veterans Day, Hegseth and senior staff stopped at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington National Cemetery, where an Army sentinel told the group to keep “atmospheric silence and respect.” Reports describe the guard as loudly admonishing the group; what prompted it is not detailed. Massie says he will use his remaining weeks in office to force more difficult votes on his own party.

Sanders says he won’t back Schumer to lead Senate Democrats and would support Van Hollen if he runs

Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont said Tuesday he will not support Sen. Chuck Schumer of New York as leader of Senate Democrats. “I like Chuck … but the time is now for change,” Sanders said. On Wednesday he went further and said he would support Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland if Van Hollen runs. Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan has also called for new leadership, though neither she nor Sanders named an alternative on Tuesday. Van Hollen, who has declined to commit to Schumer, is weighing a bid and has not announced one. Sen. Raphael Warnock of Georgia has dodged questions about whether he would back Schumer, and the Machinists union has urged Schumer to step aside. Senate Democrats are expected to choose their leaders on November 18 and 19. How many senators in the caucus, the group of a party’s senators who meet to choose leaders, will vote against Schumer is not known.

Missing 11-month-old found alive after being buried in a dog crate for what authorities believe was about 24 hours in South Carolina

An 11-month-old boy reported missing in South Carolina was found alive Tuesday afternoon, buried in a dog crate, after what authorities believe was about 24 hours underground. Investigators say the boy disappeared Monday while his grandmother ran errands and that his father had taken him. Dozens of first responders searched the countryside before he was found “clinging to life.” A sheriff called the discovery joyful. The father has been arrested, according to a report. The boy’s medical condition, the charges against the father and what led to the burial are not confirmed, and the investigation is continuing.

U.S. · Immigration✓ 3 outlets · ICE’s reasons and the boy’s current location are disputed

Federal agents take a Haitian 8-year-old from his uncle at a San Diego immigration check-in

An 8-year-old Haitian boy, a third grader at Fay Elementary School in San Diego’s City Heights neighborhood, was taken into federal custody last week when his uncle brought him to a routine immigration check-in. He had been living with his uncle’s family since his father was deported last year, and he had lost the Temporary Protected Status that once let many Haitians stay legally. Immigration officials sent him to a shelter, where, according to his family, “he cried and just begged to come home.” San Diego’s congressional delegation and the school district have demanded answers, and the district says it will provide counselors to students.

Accounts differ on why he was taken. Immigration and Customs Enforcement has said, according to local reports, that the uncle could not establish legal guardianship and that the boy was treated as an unaccompanied child, a minor without a parent or legal guardian present. The school district says its records list the uncle as an authorized caregiver. Accounts of where the boy is now also conflict: some lawmakers said he was unaccounted for, while the district and an advocacy group say he is in the custody of a federal refugee-resettlement office. Whether he faces deportation proceedings has not been announced.
