Gunman Kills 6, Wounds 15 in Krakow's Main Square; Interior Minister Says Suspect Appeared Drawn to Far-Right Ideology

A man in military-style clothing drove into the square in Poland's second-largest city and fired at random on Saturday afternoon. The suspect's identity, motive and final custody have not been confirmed.

A gunman opened fire on people in the center of Krakow, Poland, around 3 p.m. local time on Saturday, Nov. 7, killing six and wounding 15, according to the latest tally. The dead are four women and two men. The shooter, wearing military-style clothing, drove into the Main Square, the medieval market square at the heart of the old town and one of the city's busiest tourist destinations, and fired at random at people there. Krakow is Poland's second-largest city. Prime Minister Donald Tusk headed to Krakow after the attack.

The toll rose through the day. Early reports counted five dead and 12 wounded; the later tally is six dead and 15 wounded, and the numbers can still change. Poland's interior minister said there are signs the suspect was drawn to far-right ideology. That is an early description, not a finding: the suspect's identity, a specific motive and his final custody have not been confirmed, and it has not been said how many of the 15 wounded are seriously hurt. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned the shooting and offered condolences to the families of those killed. Still to come: confirmation of who the suspect is and where he is held, an official account of why he attacked, and any change to the casualty count.

Trump Publicly Offers Dana White, the UFC's Chief Executive, the White House Press Secretary Job

President Trump said on Saturday, Nov. 7, that he is offering the job of White House press secretary to Dana White, the president and chief executive of the UFC, the mixed martial arts promotion. He made the offer publicly in a Truth Social post, urging White to take the job. The press secretary is the president's chief spokesperson, the person who briefs reporters on the administration's positions; the job does not require Senate confirmation. It was not clear on Saturday whether White had accepted, and White's own response has not been reported. The offer came one day after Trump named Stephen Miller White House chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles. Still open: whether White takes the job and when any start date would be.

Trump Directs Justice Department to Investigate Democrats He Says Threaten Impeachment of the Attorney General and Others

President Trump said on Saturday, Nov. 7, in a Truth Social post that Democrats are threatening a number of people, including the Attorney General of the United States, other administration figures and Republican donors, with impeachment and other punishment, even though he says they admit they have no reason to do so. He said no impeachable acts have been committed and directed the Justice Department to investigate. Impeachment is the House of Representatives' power to bring formal charges against a federal official; a Senate trial then decides whether to remove the person.

The post came after Republicans lost ground in the Nov. 3 midterm elections. The Justice Department had not announced an investigation as of Saturday, and the post leaves open which Democrats and which statements Trump has in mind.

Full Data Show Heart Drug Pelacarsen No Better Than Placebo as a Second Drug Is Also Reported to Fail

Full results released on Saturday, Nov. 7, show that pelacarsen, a drug aimed at an inherited heart-disease risk that cardiologists had high hopes for, did no better than a placebo, an inactive treatment used as a comparison, on every measure. A second heart drug, a promising cholesterol treatment, was also reported to have failed to prevent heart attacks or stroke; details of that trial have not been confirmed.

In a placebo-controlled trial, one group of patients gets the drug and another gets an inactive look-alike, and researchers count health events in each group; “no better than placebo” means the two groups fared the same. Cardiologists are now asking whether the fault lay in how the studies were designed or in the underlying ideas about what drives heart disease. Not yet known: the full trial numbers, what the drugs' makers will do next, and whether any follow-up analysis finds a benefit in a smaller group of patients.

Two Senate Races Remain Unsettled as Colom Holds an Unofficial Lead in Mississippi and Peltola Sues Alaska

In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom has kept his lead over Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in the unofficial count as more provisional ballots and mail ballots were reported across the state. The tally has not been certified, and Hyde-Smith has said she is not backing down. The closeness has put Mississippi's voter rolls under scrutiny after a cleanup of inactive registrations, with some voters reporting problems casting ballots.

In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola's campaign sued the state, a new court filing shows, to get records of absentee ballots that the Division of Elections rejected; the campaign says it asked for the records on multiple occasions. Exact margins in the chambers remain unclear because some races have not been called. Next: further vote reports in Mississippi, the court's handling of the Alaska records request, and certification of both states' results.
