Isaias comes ashore in the Florida Panhandle as a Category 2 hurricane. Nearly three-quarters of Gulf oil output is shut in

The first Atlantic hurricane of the season weakened from a major Category 3 before landfall near Destin on Friday night, but forecasters still warned of life-threatening storm surge. Damage reports were not confirmed, and oil traders get their first look when futures reopen.

Hurricane Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida, at about 8:30 p.m. local time on Friday as a Category 2 storm with 105 mph winds, NBC News reported, and Politico described a dangerous hurricane coming ashore along the Gulf Coast. Earlier in the day Isaias had been a major Category 3 with winds up to 120 mph, according to PBS NewsHour and CBS News, before it weakened on its approach. NPR called it the season’s first Atlantic hurricane. The National Hurricane Center, as relayed by DW and NBC, still warned of life-threatening storm surge, extreme winds and possible tornadoes, and CBS had put the surge at up to 9 feet. Florida and Alabama declared states of emergency, NBC reported, and ABC said several Panhandle counties ordered evacuations. By late evening NBC’s correspondents described conditions deteriorating fast in Pensacola and across the Panhandle as the eye moved onshore. We have not seen a confirmed count of damage, injuries or deaths, so we are not reporting one. The Financial Times said the storm was headed toward Florida, Alabama, Georgia and Mississippi, so the danger now moves inland with rain, wind and power outages.

The storm also hit the oil patch. Bloomberg reported that nearly three-quarters of oil production in the U.S. Gulf of Mexico was shut in, and CBS said Chevron and Shell were halting output and evacuating offshore platforms. CNBC and MarketWatch noted that the hurricane arrives when fuel markets are already tight because of the wars in Eastern Europe and the Middle East. Bloomberg said Gulf Coast crude prices rose. West Texas Intermediate settled at $91.85 on Friday, and diesel is the sensitive spot: prices are near a record, the Japan Times reported, 24 days before the midterm elections. Delta Air Lines separately cut its 2026 profit forecast because a $6 billion jump in fuel costs outweighed higher fares, Reuters reported, a sign of how fuel prices already reach travelers and shippers. Producers usually restart within days if platforms are undamaged, but we found no official restart timetable. PBS explained why forecasts swung this week: a strong El Niño, whose upper winds tear storms apart, was fighting the Gulf’s unusually warm water over the hurricane’s strength. AFP Fact Check separately reported that a meteorologist retracted an AI-generated hurricane map that had spread online. Treat unsourced tracks and screenshots with caution and follow the Hurricane Center and your county emergency office. Saturday’s questions are practical. Where does the center track after landfall, how much rain falls inland, how many homes lose power, and how quickly do offshore platforms come back? State and local damage assessments will answer the last two. Quotes and figures here are as reported by the outlets named; the Hurricane Center’s own advisories were not re-read.

Trump says Russia will ship diesel to the U.S. after a Putin call. Zelensky calls it an “investment in war”

President Trump said Friday that Vladimir Putin agreed in a phone call to release 300,000 tons of diesel immediately and millions more over coming months, CBS, Reuters, NBC, NPR, The Washington Post and The New York Times reported. The aim is lower pump prices before the Nov. 3 elections. The Times noted that Russia has an export ban on diesel, and the Post said the volume is small next to U.S. demand but lines Washington up against Ukraine’s strikes on Russian refineries. Zelensky called the plan an “investment in war,” Reuters reported. Livemint said the U.S. is temporarily easing sanctions; we saw no Treasury notice. Reuters added that earlier diesel moves have not yet lowered prices.

World · LawPress ✓ 9 outlets · reactions as reported

The U.S. sanctions the International Criminal Court itself, hours after Navi Pillay wins the Nobel Peace Prize

The Treasury Department designated the International Criminal Court for sanctions on Friday, Reuters, AP, CBS, The Washington Post, The Guardian and USA Today reported, going beyond earlier penalties on more than a dozen of its judges and other officials. The Post said the measures aim to cut the court off from the global financial system and threaten punishment for countries that do business with it. CBS reported that Secretary of State Marco Rubio vowed to dismantle the court “brick by brick” unless it changes. The move came hours after the Nobel committee awarded the Peace Prize to Pillay, a former ICC judge, saying international law is “under political attack,” PBS reported.

Canada, Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom issued a joint statement backing the court, and Japan’s prime minister voiced “deep concern,” the Japan Times said. The ICC prosecutor said the work will continue, per Reuters. Trump said Norway bears an “indelible stain” for not awarding him the prize, NBC and Reuters reported. Watch how banks respond, and whether allies shield the court.

Military · JusticePress ✓ 10 outlets · execution date not confirmed

The Pentagon plans to livestream the firing-squad execution of the Fort Hood shooter. Vance says he would not watch

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the Pentagon will livestream the execution of Nidal Hasan, the former Army psychiatrist who killed 13 people at Fort Hood in 2009, CBS, NBC and The New York Times reported. CBS said the execution by firing squad is planned for December. Reuters noted that a government execution has never been broadcast and the military has not used a firing squad since 1945. Republicans split: Vice President JD Vance said he “is not going to be watching” and cast doubt that it would go ahead, PBS and the Times reported, while Rep. Adam Smith, a Democrat, called it “barbaric and gratuitous.”

Trump said Hasan “deserves” the firing squad, USA Today reported, but has not said whether it will be streamed, according to Reuters. PBS and the Times quoted legal experts who see a narrow path to challenge a public execution. Guardian reporting described condemnation abroad. The Pentagon has not changed its plan in the coverage we saw.

White HousePress ✓ 9 outlets · formal announcement not seen

Trump picks conservative commentator Katie Zacharia as his next press secretary

A White House official confirmed that Katie Zacharia was offered the job of press secretary, ABC, NBC, The Guardian, Politico, Reuters, USA Today, CBS and The Washington Post reported. She would replace Karoline Leavitt, who left the post at the end of August after giving birth to her second child, The Guardian said. The New York Times described Zacharia as a conservative commentator who briefly worked at the Department of Homeland Security. Outlets differ on her recent media roles, so we leave those out.

We did not see a formal announcement of a start date. The first briefing, and how she handles access for reporters, will set the tone for the final weeks before the midterms.
