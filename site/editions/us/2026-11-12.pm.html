Nvidia Announces Jensen Huang Has Resigned as CEO; Colette Kress Takes Over as Interim Chief

Nvidia’s board said Thursday that Huang is out effective immediately, hours after reports based on unnamed sources said directors were preparing to remove him; shares fell in trading.

Nvidia’s board of directors announced on Thursday, Nov. 12, that Jensen Huang has resigned as chief executive, effective immediately. Colette Kress, the company’s chief financial officer, the executive who oversees its accounts and its reporting to investors, is serving as interim chief executive, a temporary leader, while the board searches for a permanent successor. The announcement settles what was left open this morning, when reports built on unnamed sources said directors were preparing to remove him and the company had said nothing. Huang, who co-founded Nvidia, had acknowledged an affair earlier this week, and the board had opened an independent review. Nvidia has not said why he resigned. A second relationship, with an executive who reported to him, has been reported by a single outlet citing unnamed sources, and no on-the-record confirmation has been published. Nvidia shares fell in Thursday trading. The company is the leading maker of the chips that power artificial intelligence, which is why a change at the top reached well beyond one boardroom.

President Trump said Thursday that Tim Cook of Apple should take over at Nvidia; Nvidia has named no candidate. Sen. Elizabeth Warren said the resignation “does not absolve him.” On Wednesday, Rep. Krishnamoorthi said Huang should face a subpoena, a legal order that compels testimony. One news report says Huang has agreed to testify before Congress; no confirmation from him, the company or a committee has been published. What changed since this morning is simple: the board has acted and the title is vacant. What remains unknown is who will run Nvidia permanently, how long the review will take and what it has found, and whether Huang will appear before lawmakers.

Brooklyn Collapse Death Toll Rises to Eight, Including Two Children, as Search Continues

The number of people killed when a three-story apartment building collapsed in Flatbush, Brooklyn, on Wednesday evening has risen to eight, up from two at last count this morning. Mayor Zohran Mamdani told a news conference Thursday that two of those killed were children. The number of injured is reported inconsistently, with accounts giving 17, 18 and 19; the figure is conflicting and may change as hospitals report. Crews were still working through the rubble on Thursday, using excavation to open the site, and had not declared the search finished. The building stood beside a construction site with an open excavation, a dug-out pit. One report says city officials issued a stop-work order at that site after the collapse; the city has not said the work caused it. Brooklyn’s district attorney has said his office, the Department of Buildings and law enforcement are examining whether any criminal conduct played a part, which is a review, not a finding. Still unknown: the names of the dead, how many people were inside, why the structure failed, and when a cause may be known.

Trump Says U.S. Does Not Need an Iran Deal, Moves Vance Off Talks to Focus on What He Calls Fraud

President Trump said Thursday that the United States does not need a deal with Iran and that he is pulling Vice President JD Vance off the Iran negotiations. In a social media post he wrote, “We don’t need a deal with Iran,” and said Vance will instead concentrate on what he called corruption and crime in Democratic-run cities and states. He named Minnesota, California and Illinois and claimed that billions of dollars are stolen from government programs every month; that figure is Trump’s own claim, not an official tally.

The reversal comes a day after Trump used his Veterans Day speech to defend the war with Iran. Vance had been leading the administration’s public push on Iran negotiations. It is not yet clear whether anyone else will take over negotiations, how Tehran will respond, or what happens to any remaining contact between the two governments.

Putin Holds Two-Hour Talks With Witkoff and Kushner; Kremlin Calls Them Productive as the U.S. Pair Heads to Dublin to See Zelensky

Russian President Vladimir Putin received U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Moscow on Thursday, and the meeting lasted about two hours. Yuri Ushakov, the Kremlin’s foreign-policy aide, said the conversation was “substantive, constructive, frank and productive.” One report says the envoys asked Putin to include Europe in the peace talks, and says the Kremlin wants the negotiations widened to cover European security arrangements; neither point has been confirmed by the Kremlin or the White House on the record. No agreement or timetable has been announced.

From Moscow the envoys were due to travel to Dublin to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, who said he has been briefing European leaders on earlier meetings in Abu Dhabi. Ukraine also said its military satellite internet service was disrupted again on Thursday. The war is approaching its fifth year, and what Moscow and Kyiv will accept remains unknown.

One Killed and Four Wounded in Shooting Around a Washington Gym; Suspect Found Dead

One person was killed and four were wounded in a shooting Thursday morning in Northwest Washington, D.C., police said. The shooting took place at a gym and two nearby locations around a condominium building in a busy neighborhood, and for a time police told people nearby to stay inside. Authorities said the suspect was a member of the gym. A federal law enforcement official said the response shifted from an active-shooter response to a barricaded-subject response, in which officers surround a building and work to end a standoff without rushing in.

The suspect was later found dead of a gunshot wound after barricading in a building, police said. One of the wounded was reported to be in critical condition, a term doctors use when injuries are life-threatening. No motive has been announced, and officials have not yet said how the suspect died beyond the gunshot wound.
