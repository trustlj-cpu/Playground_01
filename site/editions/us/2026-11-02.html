Final Senate polls put Democrats ahead or level in seven battleground states, but most leads sit inside the margin of error

Siena's last surveys of Michigan, Texas, Maine, Ohio, Kansas, Alaska and Iowa show Democratic candidates leading or effectively tied a day before voters decide.

The last big round of Senate polling before Election Day puts Democrats ahead or effectively tied in all seven battleground states surveyed, though most of the leads are small. Siena pollsters questioned likely voters, the people judged most probable to cast a ballot, from Oct. 23 through Nov. 1, and the results came out Sunday. In Michigan, Democrat Abdul El-Sayed leads Republican Mike Rogers by 11 points. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico leads Attorney General Ken Paxton 50% to 43%. In Maine, Troy Jackson leads Sen. Susan Collins 51% to 45%. Democrats also lead by 5 points in both Ohio and Kansas. In Alaska, Mary Peltola leads Sen. Dan Sullivan 48% to 46%, and in Iowa, Josh Turek leads Ashley Hinson 48% to 47%. Republicans now hold 53 of the Senate's 100 seats, so Democrats need a net gain of four seats to take control.

Every poll carries a margin of error, the range of uncertainty that comes from asking a sample of voters instead of all of them. In these surveys it runs from about 3.4 points in Texas to 5 points in Alaska. A lead usually has to be about twice that range to count as clear, and only Michigan's 11 points and Texas's 7 points pass that test. Anything smaller is a statistical tie, which is why the one-point Iowa gap and the two-point Alaska gap say little about who is ahead. Alaska voters also decide Tuesday whether to scrap the state's ranked-choice voting, the system that counts voters' second choices when no candidate wins a majority. Polls measure opinion before the vote, and turnout, which no survey can see in advance, will settle which side they have right. Polls begin to close Tuesday evening, and the tightest races may not be called that night.

Indirect US-Iran talks in Qatar enter a third day as Tehran offers to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if Washington eases its blockade and sanctions

Negotiators from Iran and the United States were in a third day of indirect talks in Doha on Sunday, with Qatar carrying messages between the sides because they are not meeting face to face. Iran's proposal would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway that carries a large share of the world's oil, within two days after Washington lifts its blockade of Iranian oil shipments, stops holding up the release of Iranian assets and ends sanctions on Iranian oil exports. Qatar is trying to arrange the release of more than $6 billion of those assets. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Qatar had proposed a five-day implementation period and that the sides had reached a version that cuts it to two days. Iran also wants recognition of the shipping routes it has discussed with Oman. President Trump said Saturday the talks were “going fine but you know they're not very reliable.” Washington had not publicly answered Iran's proposal by Monday morning. Violence at sea continued while the talks ran. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Navy said Sunday that two tankers “exploded” after hitting mines on what it called an unauthorized route through the strait, and that one of them, a supertanker, caught fire. It accused the ships of switching off their tracking systems and operating with U.S. military support. There was no independent confirmation of the vessels' identities or of the cause, and a separate UK maritime alert the same day described a vessel struck by a projectile without saying whether it was one of the two. A Monday statement attributed to Iran, that the strait cannot reopen before U.S. sanctions are lifted, had not been confirmed by a second source.

Trump closes his midterm push with rallies in Iowa and Kansas after canceling and then restoring both

President Trump held two rallies Sunday, the first at the Five Flags Event Center in Dubuque, Iowa, and the second at an airport in Salina, Kansas, in the last weekend before Tuesday's midterm elections. In Dubuque he appeared with Rep. Ashley Hinson, the Republican nominee for Senate, who faces Democrat Josh Turek. In Salina he appeared with Sen. Roger Marshall, who faces Democrat Adam Hamilton, a challenger trying to become the first Kansas Democrat in 94 years to win a Senate seat. The White House had canceled both stops Thursday, and Trump had said he did not need to go to Kansas because Marshall was “doing very well,” before the schedule was restored.

In Iowa, Trump also turned to Michigan's Senate race. He repeatedly called Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed a “jihadist” and said he would love for Rep. Haley Stevens to switch parties after she endorsed Republican Mike Rogers on Sunday. El-Sayed did not address Stevens's endorsement Sunday. On Truth Social, Trump urged Iowa and Kansas Republicans to vote early in person, which is open in both states through Monday, and to “swamp” Democrats with turnout.

Flash floods force evacuations in Sedona and Pagosa Springs as the Southwest takes another major storm in two weeks

Heavy rain set off flash floods across the Four Corners region on Sunday, forcing evacuations in Arizona and Colorado a little over a week after earlier storms flooded the same area. In Sedona, Coconino County placed evacuation zones 8, 9 and 10 along Oak Creek under a “go now” order at about 10 a.m. Oak Creek read 15.06 feet at Sedona, above its 14-foot flood stage, which is the level at which water begins to threaten people and property. About 100 homes sit in the zone, 19 had been evacuated by Sunday morning, and the state said crews had made six rooftop rescues by afternoon. Sedona Red Rock High School opened as a shelter, and no deaths or injuries had been confirmed.

The larger threat was in southwestern Colorado. In Pagosa Springs, more than 1,000 homes in Archuleta County were under mandatory evacuation orders, many of them also evacuated for last week's storm, Sheriff Mike Le Roux said. The San Juan River was forecast to crest above 20 feet, higher than the 17.8 feet it reached in the 1911 flood that destroyed much of the town. Highway 160 was set to close to traffic entering Pagosa Springs. Forecasters said a plume of Pacific moisture known as a pineapple express was feeding the storm, with 1 to 4 inches of rain expected in places.

Kimi Antonelli, 20, clinches the Formula 1 title with four races left, becoming the youngest world champion ever

Kimi Antonelli clinched the Formula 1 drivers' championship Sunday at the Mexico City Grand Prix, finishing third behind Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton. At 20, the Italian is the youngest world champion in the sport's history, beating the record Sebastian Vettel set in 2010 at 23 years and 133 days. He is the first Italian champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953, and he won it with four races still to run. Verstappen won the race, with Hamilton second, 0.799 seconds behind, and Antonelli 4.006 seconds back.

The race was chaotic. A crash on lap 44 involving Valtteri Bottas brought out the safety car, which slows the field behind a lead car, and later a red flag, which stops the race while the track is cleared. Antonelli had become the youngest driver to lead the championship in March, when he won the Japanese Grand Prix at 19, and he won the U.S. Grand Prix in October. The season has four races left.
