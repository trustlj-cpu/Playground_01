Transportation Secretary Duffy Says Contractor Work Replacing Old Copper Wiring Set Off the Nationwide Ground Stop; Whether Faulty Equipment or Human Error Is to Blame Is Unresolved

Sean Duffy says early findings point to a problem during a telecommunications upgrade at a remote site, and Congress is split over whether to investigate or to spend.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Saturday that early findings tie Friday’s nationwide ground stop to an equipment problem during a telecommunications upgrade at a remote location, where a contractor, L3Harris, was replacing old technology that included copper wiring. A ground stop is an order that keeps planes on the ground until air traffic control clears them to leave, and this one began Friday afternoon and halted departures at airports across the country. Duffy said it was not a cyberattack and that the root cause was not a foreign actor. He also said it is not yet clear whether a faulty router or plain incompetence was responsible. By Saturday he said the system was back at full operations, but crews and aircraft were out of position, and tracking sites counted nearly 3,800 canceled flights as of Saturday morning.

The failure has turned into a fight in Washington. Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin have called for a $50 billion overhaul of the air traffic system and said Congress has put off the money for years. Democrats pushed back on that demand and pledged to investigate the ground stop. House Speaker Mike Johnson said the opposite: “We don’t really need any investigation … we need further investment.” The last nationwide ground stop came in 2023. What failed at the remote site, who signed off on the work and whether any inquiry will be opened have not been settled.

Bomb Found at an Illinois Ethanol Plant Is Rendered Safe After Evacuations in Peoria; the FBI Is Investigating and Officials Call It a Credible Threat

Authorities found a bomb Friday afternoon at the BioUrja Renewables ethanol plant in Peoria, Illinois, an industrial site along the Illinois River, and an evacuation order covered everyone within a one-mile radius. Local events were canceled while investigators worked. Officials said the device was rendered safe, meaning it was disarmed so that it could no longer detonate, and they called it a “credible threat.” The FBI is investigating. Peoria police said Saturday that officers searched the facility for hours, found no additional devices and lifted the evacuation order. Who left the device, why they chose the plant and how it got inside have not been made public.

Elon Musk Says in a Post That Nvidia Will Lose “Critical Deals” Without Jensen Huang and Backs His Return as Chief Executive; Nvidia Has Announced No Change

Elon Musk wrote in a Saturday post that Nvidia will lose “critical deals” without Jensen Huang, and he backed a petition to bring Huang back as chief executive, days after Huang left the company. Nvidia has not said why he left. Reports of extramarital relationships, including one with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy, surfaced around the time of his exit; those allegations have not been proven. A separate report citing unnamed sources says there was a second relationship, which is unconfirmed. Other technology leaders have said in posts and in letters to Nvidia’s board that Huang cannot be replaced. One report says a petition to reinstate him passed 100,000 signatures, and another says large shareholders and about 3,000 employees want him back; neither count has been independently verified.

The stakes reach beyond one company. At the end of July Nvidia held stakes worth $48 billion in 229 private companies, and some start-up founders and investors say they fear smaller checks without him. One report says some Asian governments moved to confirm chip supply commitments after his exit. Nvidia has not announced any change to its leadership, and a report that it approached another executive to succeed him is unconfirmed.

Sen. Jeff Merkley Says He Will Back Chris Van Hollen’s Challenge to Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic Leader as Van Hollen Launches a Campaign Website

Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland announced Thursday night that he will challenge Chuck Schumer of New York to lead Senate Democrats in the next Congress, setting up what one account calls the first contested race for the post since 1994. Sen. Jeff Merkley of Oregon said Friday that he will back Van Hollen. Van Hollen has launched a campaign website and will appear on the Sunday shows. Schumer’s allies say they expect him to win more than 40 votes in the caucus, the meeting of all the party’s senators, and call the challenge a distraction.

Schumer’s allies argue the contest is really about whether to end the filibuster, the rule that lets a minority of senators block most bills unless 60 vote to end debate. Van Hollen’s backers say Democrats need new voices after this month’s elections. The date of the leadership vote and the number of senators who support each man have not been made public.

Judge Says Inquiry Into Justice Department Lawyers in the East Potomac Park Golf Course Case Will Proceed as the Government Plans a Sunday Closure

A federal judge said Saturday that an inquiry into the conduct of Justice Department lawyers in the East Potomac Park golf course case will go forward, even though the administration’s attorneys have objected to a special master reviewing their conduct. The case is over the Trump administration’s plan to close the park and the historic public course in Washington, D.C., and remake it. At a long hearing Friday the judge signaled she was not ready to let the closure proceed, at least for now, and she was expected to rule over the weekend.

President Trump, who calls the course “Trash Island,” posted Saturday that the Interior Department will make it the best golf course in the world and that Judge Ana Reyes does not want it fixed quickly and cheaply. The government has said it will close the park Sunday. It was not clear Saturday whether the judge would act before then.
