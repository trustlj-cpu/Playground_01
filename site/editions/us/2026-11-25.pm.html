Trump Says a Pardon for Nvidia’s Huang Is “an Option,” Vance Says He Needs None, and a Fight Over Chip Sales to China Grows, Reports Say

Trump reportedly called a pardon an option he does not expect to need, while Vice President Vance said he was assured Huang is not under investigation and the attorney general’s answer a day earlier was less direct, days after the chipmaker’s board brought its chief executive back.

President Trump said a pardon for Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang is “always an option,” according to reports published Tuesday and Wednesday, while indicating he does not expect one to be needed. A pardon is a president’s power to forgive a federal crime, and no report says Huang has been charged with anything. Vice President Vance said Wednesday that Huang does not need a pardon and that Attorney General Todd Blanche assured him Huang is not under investigation. Blanche, asked on Tuesday, did not directly say whether Nvidia or Huang is being investigated, so the accounts conflict, and none has been backed with documents. Vance also said he supports the reinstatement, citing assurances from top officials.

The reinstatement has turned into a fight over what chips China can buy. At the center are export controls, the rules that limit which advanced chips can be sold abroad: one report describes a dispute in Washington over China’s access to chips, and another says Trump’s backing of Huang is a setback for China hawks, the officials and lawmakers who favor tighter limits. Some Republicans have condemned the move, one report says, and Democrats have criticized both Nvidia and Trump. Unnamed officials told one report that Trump pushed for the reinstatement over economic concerns, which has not been confirmed on the record. Huang is expected to give testimony to a Senate committee, but a date has not been announced.

Ábrego García Lands in Spain After Choosing to Leave the U.S., Reports Say, and One Report Says He Hopes to Return After Trump

Kilmar Ábrego García, the Salvadoran migrant whose March 2025 removal to El Salvador became a national test of presidential power over immigration, has landed in Spain, according to reports published Wednesday. Immigration agents met him at the airport before he left, one report says. The departure is described as his own choice: reports use the words voluntary and self-deporting, meaning he left on his own rather than being put on a plane by agents. One report quotes him saying he feels free of everything evil, and another says he left happy. Why he chose Spain has not been stated. Spain’s prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, has leaned heavily into his contrasts with Trump, one report notes, though no report says that played a part. The case began in March 2025, when government agents sent Ábrego García to a prison in El Salvador even though an immigration judge had ruled in 2019 that he could not be removed there. Government lawyers called the removal an administrative error, and he was returned on June 6, 2025, to face federal charges in Tennessee. A federal judge ordered him freed from immigration detention in December 2025, and a U.S. official told one report that a judge had ordered him out of the country by Tuesday. One report says he hopes to return to the United States after Trump. What happens to the Tennessee criminal case, and whether he can lawfully come back, has not been reported.

Army Declares the Iowa Lead Azide Detonation a Success; One Report Says the EPA Found Zero Airborne Lead, Not Yet Confirmed Elsewhere

The U.S. Army destroyed its stockpile of lead azide, an unstable explosive compound, at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown on Wednesday and called the controlled detonation a success. Lead azide is a chemical used to set off other explosives, and the stockpile was reported at nearly 10,000 pounds, or about 5 tons. The Army insisted before the blast that the plan was necessary. Officials had warned of a possible toxic plume of lead dust, and reports say some residents were urged to evacuate while others were told to shelter in place. After the blast, one report says the Environmental Protection Agency recorded zero airborne lead, a reading no second report has confirmed.

Whether the area is fully safe is not settled. Officials are examining air, water and soil samples for contamination, a process that will take some time, one report says, and the Army has set out measures to limit lead dust. Lead is a toxic metal, and the main worry after a blast like this is dust that settles on land and water. No results for water or soil had been reported as of Wednesday afternoon, so the single early air reading, carried by one report, is the only measurement described so far.

Another Hot Inflation Reading Keeps the Fed on Track to Raise Interest Rates Again in December, Reports Say

A new reading of inflation, the pace at which prices rise, came in hot again, and it keeps the Federal Reserve on track to raise interest rates at its December meeting, according to reports published Wednesday. The Fed is widely expected to raise rates next month, and one report says the central bank has itself said inflation has not gotten any better ahead of that vote. A rate hike makes borrowing more expensive, which is meant to slow spending and cool price growth. The size of the expected increase was not given.

Stocks ended mixed. The S&P 500 closed down 0.25% at 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62% to 23,585.80, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.33% to 50,182.95. The 10-year Treasury yield, which moves opposite to bond prices, slid on the day, one report says. The exact date of the Fed’s December decision was not in the reports.

A Thanksgiving Storm Is Expected to Bring Heavy Rain, Wind and Coastal Flooding to the East Coast, Reports Say

A storm developing offshore is expected to move inland over North Carolina and grind northeast through Thanksgiving Day, bringing heavy rain and flooding to the East Coast, according to forecasts reported Wednesday. Coastal flooding, the pushing of seawater onto low-lying land during high tides and strong onshore winds, is among the risks. One report on New York City carries rain, wind and flooding updates for Thursday’s Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, though no change to the parade was reported.

One report says the storm system threatens more than 250 million people with intense winds, heavy rain and flooding, a figure that comes from that report alone. Timing and rainfall totals for individual cities were not given. The storm is separate from the record king tides on the California coast covered below.
