Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO 11 Days After He Resigned, With Trump Saying He Convinced the Board, as Lawmakers of Both Parties Object and Stocks Rally

The board had said Huang would not return; Trump says he persuaded it, lawmakers of both parties are demanding answers, and Wall Street closed sharply higher.

Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday, Nov. 23, 11 days after he resigned on Nov. 12 following revelations of multiple extramarital relationships. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress had been serving as interim CEO, the stand-in who runs a company until a permanent leader is chosen. The company's board of directors, the body that answers to shareholders and picks the chief executive, had said earlier that Huang would not be coming back. President Trump, who pressed for his return, said Monday that he spoke to the board and “convinced” it, and he said Huang is not a security threat. Huang told employees on his first day back that he would be a “better CEO,” and staff greeted him with applause. Wall Street rallied. At the close the S&P 500 stood at 7,237.17, up 4.73%, the Nasdaq Composite at 24,089.56, up 6.51%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 49,595.31, up 3.01%. Nvidia's own shares jumped about 31%, and one report puts the one-day gain in the company's market value at nearly $1 trillion.

Lawmakers of both parties objected within hours. Republican senators criticized the decision, and two Democrats, Rep. Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, and Sen. Chris Van Hollen, raised corruption concerns about Trump's role. Several lawmakers called the move outrageous, some have called for Huang to testify before Congress, and one report says he is still due to appear next week. California's attorney general, Rob Bonta, said the reinstatement was “wrong” and “shocking.” The reversal comes as questions continue over how China obtained Nvidia's most advanced chips despite export controls, the U.S. limits on selling sensitive technology abroad. Why the board changed its position, whether the government is reviewing Huang's return and when any hearing will be held have not been settled.

Commerce Department Adds Singapore Chip Firm Megaspeed, a Nvidia Customer at the Center of the Chip Scandal, to a Trade Blacklist

The U.S. Commerce Department added Megaspeed, a Singapore-based chip company described as a Nvidia customer at the center of the scandal around the chipmaker, to the entity list on Monday, Nov. 23, according to reports. The entity list is a trade blacklist: American companies generally need a government license to ship certain technology to a listed firm, and such licenses are often denied. An earlier report had said the listing would come later in the day, and one account describes weeks of sparring among top administration officials before it was made. The move arrives as lawmakers press for answers on how China obtained Nvidia's top chips despite export controls, the limits on selling advanced technology abroad. The reports do not say what specific conduct led to the listing, whether Megaspeed will contest it or whether Nvidia faces any penalty.

Trump Tells the Army to Hold Off on Detonating a Stockpile of Unstable Explosive Chemicals at an Iowa Munitions Plant

President Trump told the Army on Monday, Nov. 23, to hold off on detonating a cache of unstable explosive chemicals stored at an Iowa munitions plant, after the Army had said it would go ahead. The Army had planned to destroy about 5 tons of the material, which includes lead azide, a compound used to set off other explosives, arguing that freezing temperatures could risk an accidental explosion if it stayed in storage. One account says the delay is meant to allow a court to approve the plan; other accounts did not spell out what the pause depends on.

The Army had said it would use emergency provisions to carry out the detonation, and one report says it intended to do so without a federal environmental permit. When the chemicals will be destroyed, how they will be kept safe in the meantime and whether a court will be asked to rule on the plan have not been announced.

Zelenskyy Says Ukrainian Naval Drones Hit Two Russian Ships Off Libya and One Sank, as Libyan Officials Describe a Suspected Drone Attack on a Russian Ship at Benghazi

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Monday, Nov. 23, that Ukraine hit two Russian ships off the coast of Libya with naval drones, unmanned boats that carry explosives, and that one of them sank. He called the operation “exemplary.” Libyan officials had said Sunday, Nov. 22, that a Russian ship was hit at the naval base in Benghazi in a suspected Ukrainian drone attack. The accounts differ on details: the Libyan description covers one ship at the port, while Zelenskyy's covers two vessels and a sinking.

A strike at a Libyan port would be far from the front line in Ukraine and from the Black Sea, where earlier naval attacks took place. The identity of the vessels, the extent of the damage, any casualties and any Russian response had not been reported.
