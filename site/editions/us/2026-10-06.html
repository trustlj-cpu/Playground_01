The S&P 500 closes above 7,800 for the first time. The trade deficit jumps to $105.6 billion on record imports

A first record since mid-August came as Treasury yields and oil eased. Google signed for 3.6 gigawatts of nuclear-backed power, and Constellation Energy shares jumped.

The S&P 500 rose 0.58% on Tuesday to 7,818.93, its first record close since mid-August and its first finish above 7,800, according to CNN and CNBC. The Nasdaq Composite added 0.45% to a record 27,599.79. The Dow gained 253.38 points, or 0.49%, to 51,521.28. The lift came from the bond market. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 5.27% from 5.31% on Monday, by the Federal Reserve's daily series, about 7 basis points below last week's peak, the highest since 2002. Oil slipped, with Brent hovering near $100, as more crude moved out of the Gulf, which eased some of the inflation worry that has driven yields up since the Fed's September hike. Technology led again. Not every stock joined in: CNBC noted that much of the market still looks oversold even as the index sets records, a sign the gains rest on a narrow group of large companies.

The morning's data cut the other way. The U.S. trade deficit widened 13.7% in August to $105.6 billion, the Commerce Department said, the largest since March 2025 and wider than the $102.0 billion economists had expected in a Reuters poll. Imports rose 4.3% to a record $420.8 billion; exports rose 1.4% to $315.2 billion. Much of the import surge is equipment for AI data centers, and it will likely subtract from third-quarter GDP, despite tariffs meant to shrink the gap. The same AI buildout moved the day's biggest stock. Google agreed to buy about 3.6 gigawatts of power from Constellation Energy on the PJM grid under a 20-year deal, and Constellation plans more than $4.3 billion of upgrades at 11 nuclear units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, with the first new capacity due by 2028 (Reuters). Neither company disclosed the price. Constellation shares closed up about 12% by Bloomberg's count; other tallies put the gain near 14%.

Media · BusinessSource ✓ Company closing announcement · Press ✓ 6 outlets

Paramount closes its $110 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery. David Ellison now runs CBS, CNN and two studios

Paramount Skydance completed its purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery on Tuesday, ending a fight that began with a hostile bid last December and ran through a bidding war with Netflix, a shareholder vote in April and a lawsuit by state attorneys general. The deal, valued at about $110 billion, pays $31 a share in cash; Netflix had offered $27.75 before stepping back. The combined company will be called Skydance Corp., Variety reported, and starts life carrying roughly $80 billion of debt. The antitrust suit was settled last month with concessions: an editorial independence board meant to protect CNN and CBS News, and a commitment to keep releasing a set number of films a year, backed by about $1.5 billion of extra film spending over five years, according to the Philadelphia Inquirer and Reuters. Ellison said his focus "now turns to the future." The open questions are about the newsrooms, the cost cuts needed to pay down that debt, and how many theatrical releases survive the merger of two studio lots.

Trump says Iran could 'take out' Los Angeles and San Diego. California's leaders call it a threat; his team says it was a warning

At a campaign rally in Grand Island, Neb., on Monday night, President Trump defended higher gas prices from the Iran war as "a small price to pay" for security, then said Iran could "take out a city," naming Los Angeles and San Diego. Parts of the crowd applauded. On Tuesday Gov. Gavin Newsom called the president "deranged and dangerous," Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass said the words could compromise the city's safety, and San Diego Mayor Todd Gloria wrote that his city is "not collateral damage." Trump's Rapid Response account said critics had it wrong and that he was warning about what Tehran could do with a nuclear weapon. He did not mention nuclear weapons at the rally.

By afternoon Trump was in Maryland for a different message. At Sparrows Point near Baltimore, Anduril said it will spend $3.7 billion on a shipyard to build parts for Virginia-class submarines, and the Navy awarded it a contract worth up to $2.9 billion, paid as production targets are met: $6.6 billion in all, about 3,100 jobs. Democratic Gov. Wes Moore called it the state's largest private investment in more than a decade. The midterms are four weeks away.

France's school protests reach 256,000 people by the government's count. Police fire tear gas in Paris, Lyon and Strasbourg

Protests that began at one high school outside Paris in mid-September became the largest day yet on Tuesday. The Interior Ministry put turnout at 256,000 nationwide, including 56,000 in Paris, according to AP. Teachers, parents and older workers joined the students. Most marches were peaceful, but police fired tear gas outside high schools and at small groups in Paris, Strasbourg, Lyon and Rennes, Reuters reported. More than 6,100 people have been arrested since the movement began, most of them minors. Counts of shut or blockaded schools vary widely, from several hundred by the ministry's figures to about 2,000 claimed by a student union.

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu told parliament that officials must listen to students, condemned what he called "ultra violence" against schools, promised that police misconduct would be investigated and called claims of police racism a lie. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said the response was proportionate in almost all cases. The protests began over school funding, but they now carry the weight of France's budget squeeze and its debt, which UBS's chief executive said needs "hard measures."

Kenya confirms its first Ebola case. The patient, back from Congo, died in Nairobi; 28 contacts are in isolation

Kenya's health ministry said Tuesday that a Kenyan man who had lived in Congo for about seven years tested positive for Ebola and died late Monday at Nairobi Hospital, where he had been isolated. He had been ill for about a month and was treated at several hospitals before traveling by road to Kampala, Uganda, and flying to Nairobi, arriving Saturday afternoon. Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale said 28 contacts, including relatives and health workers, have been identified and will stay in isolation for 21 days. Another 23 passengers from the same flight are being traced. Kenya's border points are on high alert.

Kenya is the fourth country to record a case tied to the epidemic in eastern Congo, the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, with at least 8,400 cases and more than 4,000 deaths, according to AP. Kenya had tested 267 samples since May, all negative until now. The Africa CDC said it was sending a senior team to Nairobi at once. Nairobi is East Africa's main air hub, which is why one imported case counts for more here than in a smaller country.
