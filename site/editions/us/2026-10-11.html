Isaias weakens to a post-tropical storm after killing four. About 670,000 customers are without power in four states

The hurricane that came ashore near Destin on Friday night spared the Florida Panhandle its worst, but flash flooding is now the danger inland. An insurance analyst expects single-digit billions in insured losses, and Gulf oil platforms have no restart date.

Hurricane Isaias killed at least four people in Florida and Alabama and left about 670,000 customers without power in Florida, Alabama, Georgia and Mississippi, according to a wire report carried by GMA News that cited local authorities and electricity-monitoring services. CBS News and NBC News also put the toll at four. The storm came ashore near Destin on Friday night as a Category 2 hurricane and weakened within hours to a post-tropical cyclone, CBS reported. A local station, WVTM, said a tree fell on a Birmingham, Alabama, home overnight and killed one person, and another Alabama outlet reported two deaths in the state; we have not seen all four deaths itemized. The New York Times described towns near landfall as relieved: beachside communities lost trees and power but avoided catastrophic flooding. The danger has moved inland, where the Times said parts of the Southeast face flash floods and NBC reported millions still under threat of torrential rain, flooding and tornadoes. NPR noted numerous downed trees. Forecasters leaned heavily on artificial-intelligence weather models this week, the Times reported, and on Saturday it asked whether the forecasts held up. Bloomberg Intelligence’s insurance analyst told Bloomberg Television that insured losses could reach the single-digit billions of dollars, with Florida’s stronger building codes likely to limit damage. That is an analyst’s estimate, not a tally.

Oil is the open question. Bloomberg reported on Friday that nearly three-quarters of Gulf of Mexico crude output was shut in ahead of the storm, and we found no official restart timetable. Futures trading reopens Sunday evening. West Texas Intermediate closed Friday at $91.85 a barrel, and the Japan Times has reported diesel prices near a record. Two other storms are running alongside it. CBS reported that Hurricane Simon strengthened into a Category 4 storm aimed at Mexico, and the Los Angeles Times said Tropical Storm Rachel’s surf drove residents out in Long Beach and shut beaches in Newport Beach, in Southern California. Life went on in Isaias’s path: Georgia and Alabama still played their football game Saturday night, Yahoo Sports and USA Today reported, and ESPN said President Trump skipped it because of the hurricane. Sunday’s questions are practical. How fast does the rain move out, how many homes get power back, are more deaths confirmed, and when do offshore platforms restart? Treat unsourced maps and screenshots with care, and follow the National Weather Service and your county emergency office.

Nine dead in a mass shooting in Erie, Pennsylvania, including the gunman

Nine people were dead after a shooting at what appeared to be a home in Erie, Pennsylvania, on Friday night, city officials said Saturday. Gov. Josh Shapiro called it a mass shooting, The Hill reported, and AP said the suspected shooter was among the dead; USA Today’s headline counted the suspect in the nine. The New York Times and The Washington Post first reported eight dead, so early counts differ from the later one. Police called it a “horrific scene” with two small children among the victims, WTAE reported, and ABC News said a pregnant woman was among them. The Post said authorities had given few details. We have not seen the victims’ names, the shooter’s identity, a motive or how the weapon was obtained, and we are not reporting any of them. Erie is a Lake Erie port city of roughly 90,000 people in the state’s northwest corner. It was one of two family killings this weekend: Georgia police said five relatives died in an apparent murder-suicide, The Guardian, The New York Times and USA Today reported. Watch for the victims’ names, a police briefing and the shooter’s identity.

Post-tropical cycloneWeather

A storm that has lost the warm core and tidy shape of a hurricane but can still bring strong wind, heavy rain and flooding. Isaias weakened to one within hours of landfall, which is why forecasters kept warning about floods after the winds eased.

Middle East · SecurityPress ✓ 9 outlets · attribution to the Houthis from Saudi and coalition statements

A strike on Riyadh’s airport kills 12 and wounds more than 300. Trump says he is weighing U.S. involvement against the Houthis

Saudi authorities said an attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday killed 12 people and wounded more than 300, NPR, CBS, AP and Politico reported. NPR said the dead included four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, a Palestinian, an Egyptian, a Jordanian and a U.S. citizen. The Japan Times called it the deadliest attack in the kingdom in more than a decade and said no warning alert sounded. The Saudi-led coalition blamed Yemen’s Houthis and called it a war crime, and Livemint counted it as their third strike on the airport in a week.

NPR reported that Trump is weighing joining the fight against the Houthis, and Livemint quoted him calling the strikes “not acceptable.” Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attack, and the U.N. secretary-general did too, according to FinancialJuice. We saw no U.S. decision. Reuters said major Saudi conferences will go ahead. The attack comes while a Saudi-led offensive against the Houthis is in its second week, NPR said, and The Washington Post tied it to the wider Iran war.

Ukraine war · EnergyPress ✓ 8 outlets · casualty count from Ukrainian officials

Trump says Ukraine should “get a new president” after Zelensky condemns the diesel deal. A Russian bombing kills at least 12 in Zaporizhzhia

President Trump said Saturday that it is time for Ukraine to get a new president and that Volodymyr Zelensky “wants to make problems for the world,” The Washington Post and the BBC reported. Politico said he warned Zelensky to stop attacks on Russian oil refineries, and FinancialJuice said he called for a Ukrainian election. CBS reported that Ukraine says it will not stop hitting Russian oil facilities. Zelensky wrote on Telegram that Russia “thanked” Washington for easing its diesel export sanctions by dropping five guided bombs on homes in Zaporizhzhia, with 12 dead including three children; The Globe and Mail also reported at least 12 killed.

Republicans objected to the deal, The Hill reported, and Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick, a Pennsylvania Republican, said a bipartisan bill barring U.S. purchases of Russian oil is coming. Germany said it will keep its Russia sanctions, Reuters reported, and a Kremlin aide called the Trump–Putin call “very friendly.” Trump said Friday that Russia would send 300,000 tons of diesel at once and more in coming months; we have seen no cargo or Treasury notice.

Media · LawPress ✓ 8 outlets · networks’ response not seen

The Justice Department opens an antitrust investigation of five TV networks over their halt to pool coverage of Trump

The Justice Department said it is investigating Fox News, CNN, ABC, CBS and NBC for possible violations of federal antitrust law after they stopped supplying shared pool coverage of President Trump, NBC News, The Washington Post, The New York Times, The Guardian and Reuters reported. The Guardian said the networks stopped last month after CNN, MS Now and Politico were barred from the White House. The Times said the networks were notified Friday. The Post called it a sharp escalation in the administration’s use of its powers against perceived enemies in the press.

Antitrust law bars competitors from agreeing to restrain trade. Whether a joint decision to stop supplying footage fits that definition is the legal question, and we have seen no ruling or charge. The coverage we reviewed carried no response from the networks. Watch for their statements, reaction from press-freedom groups, and whether the White House restores access.
