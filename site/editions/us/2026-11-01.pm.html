Federal judge dismisses Justice Department suit that sought to block Alaska's Senate result if Republican Dan Sullivan loses

Two days before the vote, U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason throws out the case built around a second candidate named Dan Sullivan, and the senator says he will accept the result.

A federal judge in Alaska dismissed the Justice Department's lawsuit against the state on Sunday, ending a case brought at President Trump's direction that asked the court to keep Alaska from certifying a Senate winner if the Republican incumbent, Dan Sullivan, loses. U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason issued the dismissal in federal district court; her full reasoning has not been made public in the material we could confirm. The government's argument was that a second candidate on the ballot, also named Dan Sullivan, could confuse voters, draw votes away from the senator and help Democrat Mary Peltola, a former congresswoman. The suit also asked that certain ballots be set apart and preserved. Election Day is Tuesday.

Sullivan said in a Sunday television interview, “I'm going to accept the election results,” even if he loses, while also suggesting the election could be “stolen.” He has accused Peltola of lying about her campaign's involvement with the second Sullivan's candidacy; Peltola's campaign has denied it, and no court has ruled on the charge. On Truth Social, Trump wrote that the second candidate has “DISAPPEARED”; no official has confirmed that. An FBI agent has visited a donor to the second Sullivan's campaign at a boat dealership in Petersburg, the fishing town where the candidate lives. Alaskans also vote Tuesday on whether to scrap ranked-choice voting. What remains open is whether the Justice Department appeals, and how the two Sullivans split the vote before certification of the result.

Sen. Chris Murphy hospitalized after a stroke; his office says he is awake and responding well to treatment

Connecticut Sen. Chris Murphy, 53, had a stroke on Saturday and is being treated at Johns Hopkins Hospital, his office said Sunday. His chief of staff, Allison Herwitt, said in a statement that Murphy began to feel numbness in his arm that afternoon at his son's hockey game and was taken to a local hospital, where it was found that he had suffered a stroke. A stroke happens when blood flow to part of the brain is cut off or a vessel in the brain bursts, and numbness on one side of the body is a common warning sign. The office described him as “awake and alert,” “responding well to treatment” and expected to make a full recovery. Murphy, one of the most visible Democrats in the Senate and a name mentioned as a possible 2028 presidential candidate, had been traveling the country to campaign for Democrats ahead of Tuesday's midterm elections. How long he will stay in the hospital has not been announced.

Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens endorses Republican Mike Rogers for Michigan's Senate seat, spurning her party's nominee two days before the vote

Rep. Haley Stevens, a Michigan Democrat, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers for the U.S. Senate on Sunday, turning on the nominee who beat her in the Democratic primary election, Abdul El-Sayed. She appeared with Rogers at Perfecting Church in Detroit, praised his support for Black-owned businesses and then posted her endorsement, writing that El-Sayed had said she was no longer welcome. She had been scheduled to attend a unity event with El-Sayed that day, and the two had feuded over a campaign ad Stevens called anti-Semitic. “I'm not turning my back on Democrats,” she said in Detroit. Michigan is a battleground race that could help decide control of the Senate.

The reaction was swift. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called the move “a despicable betrayal,” the Michigan Democratic Party said it “stands united and fully behind Abdul El-Sayed,” and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani rebuked her. Some members of her staff announced they were leaving her office as of Nov. 1. El-Sayed said her endorsement did not matter and that he was focused on issues in his state. The Republican National Committee said Michigan “should take notice,” and Trump called her endorsement “BIG” and praised her bravery in a post on Truth Social.

Learjet bound from Austin to the Bahamas crashes in Palm Beach, Florida, with nine people aboard, the FAA says

A Learjet carrying nine people crashed in Palm Beach, Florida, on Sunday, the Federal Aviation Administration, or FAA, said. The jet had taken off from Austin, Texas, and was headed to the Bahamas. It came down near homes in a South Florida neighborhood. The FAA has not said who was aboard, how many of the nine were killed or hurt, what caused the crash or whether anyone on the ground was injured. A Learjet is a small twin-engine business jet. Investigators from the FAA and the National Transportation Safety Board handle civil aviation accidents in the United States, and findings on cause usually take months.

Crypto · Security✓ 4 outlets · How the funds were taken, how much of the loss has been replaced and how the rest will be covered are unconfirmed

Customers pull $3.64 billion from Binance as the exchange reopens after a $6.3 billion hack

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, resumed normal operations on Sunday after a theft of about $6.3 billion in digital tokens, and customers immediately began pulling money out. Customers took out a net $3.64 billion, measured as net outflows, or 2.1% of the $171 billion in assets under management on the platform. The company had halted withdrawals after Thursday's hack to investigate and stabilize its systems. Binance says it has replaced most of what was stolen with its own funds; the exact share is not settled.

The FBI has linked the theft to North Korea. The size of Sunday's outflow shows how nervous customers were. The company has not said how the rest of the loss will be covered, and how the attackers got in has not been made public. Monday's withdrawal figures will show whether the run has ended.
