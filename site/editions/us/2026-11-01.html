One dead, six wounded in “targeted” shooting at a Spirit Halloween store in Fairfield, California; suspect still sought

Police call Friday night's attack at a Solano County mall a targeted shooting by one suspect; a pregnant mother of four was killed, and no arrest had been announced.

A gunman opened fire inside a Spirit Halloween store at the Solano Town Center mall in Fairfield, California, at about 8:30 p.m. Friday, the evening before Halloween. One woman was killed and six other people were wounded. Police identified the woman who died as Constance Hages, 31; her family told police she was pregnant, and she was the mother of four children. The wounded were taken to local hospitals, and police said at least three were in critical condition, a hospital term for patients whose vital signs are unstable and whose lives are at immediate risk. Fairfield, a city of about 120,000 people, sits in Solano County between San Francisco and Sacramento.

Fairfield police said the shooting appears to stem from a targeted incident involving one suspect inside the store. In plain terms, officers believe the gunman was after a specific person or people rather than firing at random, though police have not said who that was or whether the intended target was among the victims. The FBI's Sacramento office sent an evidence response team to help process the scene, and officers searched for the shooter overnight. As of the latest police statement on Saturday, no arrest had been announced, and police had not released a description of the suspect or a motive. A separate shooting at a Spirit Halloween store in Madison Heights, Michigan, killed a 21-year-old man the same night; no connection between the two has been made public. Fairfield police are asking anyone with video or information to call 707-428-7600.

Valkyries beat Dream 68-63 in the first Game 7 in WNBA history to win their first title in just their second season

The Golden State Valkyries are WNBA champions after beating the Atlanta Dream 68-63 on Saturday night at Chase Center in San Francisco, in the first Game 7 the league's Finals have ever produced. Gabby Williams was held to two points at halftime and then scored 28 in the second half to finish with 30, and she was named Finals MVP. The win gave Golden State the series 4-3 and a championship in only the franchise's second season. Game 7 meant one result decided the title, with no second chance for either side. For Atlanta, the loss extends a title drought: the Dream were playing in the Finals for the fourth time in franchise history and still have no championship. Angel Reese, the Dream's star forward, scored 8 points, only one of them in the fourth quarter, and shot 12 for 45 over the last four games of the series. Trump calls $50 million contract for his former lawyer Alina Habba unacceptable; she denies any link to a billionaire's pardon

President Trump said Saturday night that a contract paying his former lawyer Alina Habba more than $50 million is unacceptable and far too much. He spoke to reporters at a Halloween party at Mar-a-Lago. The contract itself has not been released. The deal Trump objected to would pay Habba more than $50 million over four years, including bonuses, from a company set up by the family of Venezuelan banker Julio Herrera Velutini, with Habba as its general counsel, the company's top in-house lawyer; that figure has not been officially confirmed. Trump pardoned Herrera Velutini on Jan. 15, 2026 and issued an amended clemency order five days later.

Habba has called the suggestion that her job helped win the pardon false. A White House official said, “Alina Habba had nothing to do with this presidential pardon.” Herrera Velutini's daughter, a U.S. citizen, gave $3.5 million to a Trump-connected super PAC, a political committee that can raise unlimited sums but may not coordinate with a campaign. Trump's own remarks treated the contract as real, but the $50 million figure has not been independently verified, and no evidence has been made public that the pay was tied to the pardon.

Newsom warns California's federal judges that the administration may seek warrants to seize ballots before midterm results are certified

Gov. Gavin Newsom wrote Saturday to the chief judges of California's four federal district courts, saying he has “grave concern” that federal agents may seek a search warrant to seize ballots, election records or certified voting technology while an election is underway and before the results are certified. Certification is the formal step in which election officials confirm the final count. Newsom said he is not aware of any pending warrant related to the election, and he asked the judges to examine any such request closely. He attached legal guidance from the University of California, Berkeley's Edley Center on Law & Democracy on how judges can evaluate warrants aimed at election equipment.

The letter lands in the middle of a legal fight between the Justice Department and California over the administration's claim that about 20,000 noncitizens are registered to vote; California says the department relied on “suspect data.” A federal judge recently refused to order California to set aside 20,000 ballots, citing a pattern of attempted election interference by the administration. The worry is not purely hypothetical: a Riverside County sheriff seized about 650,000 ballots in February, and the California Supreme Court later ordered them returned. Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 3.

Degreaser mistaken for sugar burns five people at a North Carolina Chili's: three customers and two employees

Five people were burned at a Chili's Grill & Bar in Forest City, North Carolina, this week after a granulated degreaser was used in margarita preparation in place of sugar. Three customers suffered burns on and around their mouths when they drank the margaritas, and two employees who tasted the drinks had similar symptoms, health officials said. A contractor who was power-washing the outside of the restaurant had handed an employee the degreaser, which went into an unlabeled container that was left in the bar area. A later employee used that container to prepare the margarita station, believing it was sugar.

The Foothills Health District suspended the restaurant's food service permit and ordered staff to clean and sanitize affected equipment and surfaces; the permit was reinstated after inspectors verified that the corrective steps were done. Chili's has called it an isolated situation. Health officials have not said how severe the burns were, and accounts differ on whether the drinks were made Wednesday or Thursday. Industrial degreasers are often caustic, meaning they burn skin and the lining of the mouth and throat on contact.
