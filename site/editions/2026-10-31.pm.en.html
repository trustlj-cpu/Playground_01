Korea-US-Japan foreign ministers meet in Atlanta, reaffirm resolve for North Korea’s complete denuclearization… Arctic-Pacific Partnership launched

The foreign ministers of the three countries sat down together again after 40 days. They urged North Korea to ease tensions and spoke with one voice on peace in the Taiwan Strait.

The foreign ministers of Korea, the United States and Japan met in Atlanta, in the United States, on the 30th (local time) and reaffirmed their firm resolve for the complete denuclearization of North Korea. It was the first time the three ministers had met face to face in 40 days. The meeting was scheduled to fit the G20 programme held in the United States. Earlier on the 30th, the foreign affairs authorities of the three countries had separately discussed how to respond to North Korea’s ballistic missile launch. At the meeting the three countries said they would continue efforts toward denuclearization of the Korean Peninsula and urged North Korea to lower tensions. They also agreed that peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait are important. There is a new framework for cooperation as well. Agreeing on Arctic maritime cooperation, they announced the launch of an ‘Arctic-Pacific Partnership’.

The emphasis on the same sentence differed from country to country. Korea put weight on the need for dialogue between North Korea and the United States, while the United States put containing China first. Foreign Minister Cho Hyun and US Secretary of State Rubio are also reported to have discussed visa issues. With a dispute between the ruling and opposition parties at home continuing over the delay in the issuance of a US visa to lawmaker Jin Sung-joon, attention is drawn to this point. However, the specifics of the visa discussion were not made public. Reaffirming the resolve for denuclearization is wording repeated at every Korea-US-Japan meeting, so what is new this time is the Arctic cooperation framework. What projects and budget will fill the partnership, and how North Korea will react to this meeting, have not yet been confirmed.

50-odd Samsung presidents meet Sam Altman to discuss AI strategy… SK Chairman Chey Tae-won also joins lunch

About 50 presidents of Samsung Electronics invited OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman on the 31st and held an ‘AI Future Strategy’ meeting. The presidents are said to have asked Altman how the next ten years of AI would unfold. Samsung says it will use the occasion to push faster on AX, shifting the way the whole company works to be centred on AI. Around this schedule Altman also had lunch with SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won. The two are reported to have talked about cooperation on HBM and AI data centers. HBM is high-performance memory that sits beside an AI chip and moves data quickly. The more OpenAI grows its AI services, the more demand for this memory grows too, so OpenAI is an important customer for both Samsung and SK Hynix. The meeting came just after the KOSPI fell 3.48% on the Alphabet shock. At a time when doubts were growing over whether AI investment returns as real revenue, the head of an AI company met the domestic chip industry directly. However, no specific supply contract or investment amount was announced at either event. The scope and timing of the cooperation must await official announcements the two companies will make themselves.

NEC special counsel conducts additional search and seizure at National Election Commission… alleged corruption in 14 billion won-range election equipment supply

The NEC special counsel on the 30th conducted additional searches at the National Election Commission and the homes of its staff. It is looking into allegations that, in the process of supplying election equipment worth some 14 billion won, equipment whose performance fell short of the standard was passed as acceptable. A search and seizure is a procedure in which investigators obtain a court warrant and take away material that may serve as evidence. On the same day the special counsel asked the NEC to preserve in their current state the ballots taken out of Olympic Park. On the 28th a public verification of ballots from the June 3 local elections was held at the handball stadium in Olympic Park in Seoul, and the special counsel’s position is that the ballots that went through verification should be kept separately in preparation for further detailed forensic examination.

Political circles pushed back at once. The Democratic Party criticized People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyeok for inciting claims of election fraud, calling it a bid to prolong Jang’s political life. The People Power Party argued that all the ballots verified at Olympic Park should be preserved as evidence. The request to preserve the ballots does not mean manipulation was confirmed; it is storage in preparation for forensic examination. Which firm’s equipment is at issue, and who has been booked, has not yet been made public. The results of analyzing the seized material and the schedule for detailed forensic examination will set the direction of the next stage of the investigation.

Fallout from police search and seizure… Ajou University pediatric emergency center head resigns: ‘Medical staff assumed to be potential criminals’

The head of the pediatric emergency medical center at Ajou University Hospital submitted a resignation on the 30th. It is a protest against the police search of the center. The center head objected, saying the investigation assumed medical staff to be potential criminals. Police belatedly visited the hospital and bowed their heads in apology, but could not stop the resignation.

The pediatric emergency medicine community criticized the search again the same day. An emergency room sees patients who are racing against time, so a search can halt care and further shrink the essential medical care field, they said. They demanded that search and seizure in emergency rooms be limited. On the other side is the need for an investigation to establish the truth of the case. The medical community’s right to provide care and the investigators’ pursuit of the truth have found no meeting point. Whether the center head’s resignation will be accepted, and whether police will change their search procedures, have not yet been decided.

Ruling party: ‘Cho Hee-de parliamentary investigation to focus on conduct during Dec. 3 insurrection’… opposition: ‘petty revenge’

The Democratic Party said on the 30th that it would focus the parliamentary investigation of Chief Justice Cho Hee-de on the Chief Justice’s conduct during the Dec. 3 insurrection. A parliamentary investigation is a system in which the National Assembly itself investigates a specific matter. The ruling party’s position is that it will establish what really happened at the late-night meeting at the time, and it plans to process the investigation plan on the 12th of next month. It said the results of its Shincheonji inquiry would be released next week.

The opposition objects, calling it petty revenge. It says a matter that even the special counsel judged to warrant no charge is being dragged into a parliamentary investigation again. The ruling party draws a line, saying the target of the inquiry is not the outcome of trials but conduct at the time of martial law. Even if the plan passes, fresh disputes are expected over the choice of witnesses and the investigation period. How the Supreme Court will respond to the investigation has not yet emerged.
