Domestic politics · SPD · CoalitionPress ✓ 14 outlets · one claim only in Tagesspiegel

Schweitzer takes over the Labor Ministry, Merz demands speed on pensions, Pistorius does not want to become SPD chair

The chancellor speaks of the most far-reaching reforms in decades, while the SPD and the Union argue over the tone after the resignation of Bärbel Bas. Who will lead the party is open; no break-up of the coalition has been reported.

Alexander Schweitzer is the new federal labor minister. Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier handed him the certificate of appointment on Tuesday (Die Zeit, Süddeutsche Zeitung, MDR, Der Spiegel, Tagesschau). The former minister-president of Rhineland-Palatinate thus sits in the federal cabinet. His predecessor Bärbel Bas, who had announced her resignation, was dismissed from office beforehand; Tagesspiegel calls it a probably unprecedented first in the history of the republic that this happened behind closed doors (single source).

The chancellor gave the new minister no grace period. At the Employers’ Day, Friedrich Merz said he had “no intention of becoming the nation’s darling” (Der Spiegel, n-tv, FAZ). According to Deutsche Welle, he announced the most far-reaching reforms in decades. Schweitzer is to bring the agreed draft bills into the cabinet quickly (Handelsblatt, Die Zeit), and Merz held to ending deduction-free early retirement (Die Zeit); according to Handelsblatt he considers the end of the “pension at 63” fair. FAZ reports another statement: the country is currently experiencing the biggest push toward modernization. Previous governments had let pensions drag, he said according to Der Spiegel; the wait had already been “too long” for him (Handelsblatt).

Schweitzer replied that decisions are needed and tied the pension law to a condition (n-tv); the brief report does not say which. The CDU expects him to stick to the pension decisions he had partly criticized earlier (Die Zeit). SPD General Secretary Tim Klüssendorf does not see the coalition in danger, but questions individual measures (Die Zeit). Klingbeil and Bavaria’s minister-president Söder, according to Handelsblatt, commit to the pension reform; Tagesschau lists three issues on which it is stalling.

At the same time the dispute over the tone after the resignation goes on. Klüssendorf accuses the Union of a lack of consideration for Bas (Die Zeit). Union politicians insist they did not attack her personally; SPD chair Lars Klingbeil, according to Süddeutsche Zeitung, could not name any concrete counterexamples. Merz rejected the accusations from the SPD (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt); according to Rheinische Post he spoke of “unfair claims” and demanded an apology from Manuela Schwesig. BILD reports that Klingbeil and Merz clashed over the Bas case (single source). T-Online describes defiance in the Union after the fall of Bas.

In the SPD the search for a new leadership begins. The party wants to elect its entire top anew, Klingbeil leaves his future as chair open, and the Juso chair sees an opportunity in the reshuffle (FAZ). Defense Minister Boris Pistorius ruled out a candidacy (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt, Stern). Ulf Kämpfer, SPD chair in Schleswig-Holstein, floated Schwesig for a dual leadership (Der Spiegel); Brandenburg’s minister-president Dietmar Woidke named Rehlinger and Schwesig (Die Zeit).

What remains open is who leads the party, when the party conference takes place and whether the pension reform comes as fast as Merz demands. Today the chancellor gives a government statement in the Bundestag on the EU summit (Der Spiegel, Handelsblatt); it will show whether he tones down his language toward the SPD. Tagesschau asks whether the resignation is a chance for a fresh start in the coalition, and notes that nothing is certain.

Economy · Prices · Business cyclePress ✓ 10 outlets · figure of 3.3% per Die Zeit

Inflation rises to 3.3 percent in September, the IMF raises its forecast for the German economy to 1.3 percent

The inflation rate in September was above three percent for the first time since late 2023 (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt, BILD). According to Die Zeit it rose to 3.3 percent, the highest level since December 2023. Energy prices are the driver, above all fuel and heating oil (Handelsblatt, Die Zeit); BILD puts the increase for fuel at 37 percent. Experts expect inflation to stay high despite the new fuel rebate (Handelsblatt). Schwäbische Zeitung writes that prices at the pump are barely falling despite the tax cut of 17 cents.

At the same time the International Monetary Fund raises its forecast for the German economy: 1.3 percent growth in 2026, almost double what was expected in July (Handelsblatt, Die Zeit, Tagesschau). That matches the federal government’s estimate (Handelsblatt). According to Die Zeit the driver is not industry. Worldwide the AI boom supports growth, which is concentrating in ever fewer countries (Handelsblatt, FAZ); Germany benefits, while developing countries are being left behind (Handelsblatt). The IMF warns of a correction in the AI market, of the war and of high debt (Reuters, Handelsblatt). Finance Minister Klingbeil is, after all, traveling to the IMF meeting, according to Handelsblatt.

On fuel prices FAZ sees no quick relief: neither the release of reserves nor Trump’s diesel deal with Russia is pushing down the price at the filling station noticeably. According to Süddeutsche Zeitung, France is releasing ten million barrels of oil and diesel from state reserves to lower prices (single source).

Domestic politics · Saxony-Anhalt · BerlinPress ✓ 13 outlets · election date open

CDU wants to walk out of the chamber when AfD candidate Siegmund is elected, in Berlin SPD and Greens interrupt exploratory talks with the Left Party

In Saxony-Anhalt the CDU parliamentary group wants to walk out of the chamber at the election of the minister-president if AfD politician Ulrich Siegmund stands for election (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt, Süddeutsche Zeitung, Der Spiegel, Die Welt, BILD, MDR). AfD leader Weidel mocked the plan as a “top-notch kindergarten” (T-Online, Die Zeit, Tagesspiegel). Der Spiegel writes that the maneuver could outmaneuver Siegmund or help him to power at the decisive moment. The reports gave no election date. The Institute of the German Economy warns of an “explosive government mixture” because individual AfD members of parliament could go their own way (Handelsblatt, single source). In Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, AfD and SPD are nominating their own candidates for the post of state parliament president (Die Zeit). Today the state parliament meets in Magdeburg for a special session on the war in Ukraine (Die Zeit).

In Berlin, SPD and Greens interrupted the exploratory talks with the Left Party after the Left Party parliamentary group had admitted the controversial member of parliament Vanessa Emde (Die Zeit, Der Spiegel, FAZ, Tagesschau, Handelsblatt, rbb24). According to FAZ, Emde has so far not acknowledged Israel’s right to exist. Green Party chair Franziska Brantner considers the pause right, saying the Left Party did not stick to the agreement (Der Spiegel). Greens lead candidate Graf said one has to assess whether last week’s agreement still holds (FAZ). The Berlin Left Party showed itself surprised and said it would sign the agreed principles (Die Zeit, Morgenpost). Left Party chair Elif Eralp also rejected any interference from Merz (FAZ, Die Welt, Tagesspiegel). A meeting of Vonovia and the Left Party fell through at short notice (Der Spiegel, Handelsblatt), and an SPD district association is coming out against a coalition with the Left Party and the Greens (Die Zeit).

Security · Justice · PolandPress ✓ 8 outlets · number of arrests inconsistent

Nord Stream trial begins in Hamburg, after the Starlink fire in Poland further suspects are arrested in Germany

Today the trial over the blowing up of the Nord Stream pipelines begins before the Higher Regional Court in Hamburg, about four years after the explosions (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt, Der Spiegel). The accused is the Ukrainian Serhii K.; the Federal Prosecutor’s Office considers him the mastermind (Handelsblatt), which the paper calls the trial of the year. He is said to have acted as an officer of a Ukrainian commando and has stayed silent so far. According to Der Spiegel, what is central is on whose orders he acted and whether the pipes were a civilian or a legitimate military target. Deutsche Welle points to questions about Kyiv’s role and about German aid to Ukraine.

In the Starlink case in Poland the number of arrests is rising. On September 23 a ground station that also supplies Ukrainian forces burned (Handelsblatt, Tagesschau). FAZ reports a third suspect in Germany, Reuters, citing the broadcaster RMF FM, a fourth; the figures thus differ. According to Die Zeit, explosive devices were found on people arrested in Poland. Warsaw sees Russia behind it (FAZ, Der Spiegel); FAZ writes that the pattern of the incident points to Russia. Foreign Minister Wadephul said Germany is in Russia’s crosshairs and called for closer cooperation (FAZ, Tagesschau). The authorities have so far not published evidence of Russian responsibility. Notz calls for a European intelligence alliance against sabotage (Die Zeit), and Baden-Württemberg’s interior minister Hagel warns of Germany’s “Sleeping Beauty slumber” (Der Spiegel). At the same time a cyberattack hit a contractor of the Fehmarnbelt Tunnel (Die Zeit, NDR, BILD), and Tagesschau reports cyberattacks on hundreds of French supermarkets.

Sport · Health · PoliticsPress ✓ 11 outlets · details of the tax incomplete

Bayern draw 3:3 in Stavanger, the coalition agrees on a sugar tax, Nvidia buys into Doctolib

In the league phase of the Champions League, FC Bayern Munich could not get beyond a 3:3 at Viking Stavanger (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, FAZ, sportschau.de, Transfermarkt). The Norwegians took an early lead (Die Zeit, T-Online), Harry Kane scored twice within five minutes to make it 2:2 (T-Online, Der Spiegel), then Michael Olise was shown a red card. Coach Kompany called it “incredibly dumb” (FAZ, SPORT1), and Kane demanded that Olise take responsibility (Die Welt). Playing a man down, Bayern conceded a late equalizer (FAZ). RB Leipzig narrowly beat PSV Eindhoven, with Gruda scoring the winner (Die Zeit, FAZ, T-Online). In Milan, Bruges won at Inter, with German striker Nicolò Tresoldi scoring three goals (Der Spiegel, sportschau.de, FAZ). The German women’s team won their friendly against Japan, Bühl scoring the winning goal (Die Zeit, FAZ, Süddeutsche Zeitung). Today Stuttgart and Dortmund, among others, play (Die Zeit). The €120 million Eurojackpot goes to North Rhine-Westphalia (Die Zeit, FAZ, n-tv).

The coalition has settled the dispute over the sugar tax (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, Süddeutsche Zeitung, Handelsblatt, Tagesschau). According to media reports, a two-tier model is to apply from July 1, 2027 (Tagesschau); according to Die Welt the agreement came after a veto by the chancellor. The cabinet wants to approve the plans today (Berliner Morgenpost, Handelsblatt). The tax is to hit heavily sweetened drinks (Der Spiegel); according to Der Spiegel the details were fought over for a long time. Hesse’s minister-president Boris Rhein calls the tax a “pricey tax” (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt).
