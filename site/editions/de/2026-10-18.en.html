Left Party, Greens and SPD announce breakthrough in exploratory talks in Berlin

Four weeks after the election to the House of Representatives, the exploratory talks among the red-green-red parties are over; a coalition agreement, though, is not yet in hand.

Four weeks after the election to the Berlin House of Representatives, the Left Party, the Greens and the SPD have concluded their exploratory talks and announced a breakthrough. That is what Tagesschau and Die Zeit report; a search on Google News also shows the item from Saturday afternoon. Die Zeit had already reported an agreement at midday, using the word “apparently”, and Tagesschau published its item in the late afternoon. The capital may thus be facing a three-party alliance, which in this constellation is called red-green-red.

So far, one point above all is known on substance: according to Die Zeit, the three parties plan the socialization of housing in Berlin. How far it is meant to go, which holdings would be affected and by what procedure it could proceed is not apparent from the reports at hand. The topic is politically charged because it affects tenants, landlords and investors alike and has been debated in the city for years. Exploratory talks are a preliminary conversation in which parties sound out whether negotiations make sense at all; they are not binding.

At the same time, pressure from outside is growing. On Saturday, at the Deutschlandtag of the Junge Union, Federal Chancellor Friedrich Merz explicitly warned the SPD and the Greens against talks with the Left Party. “Stop going down this path,” he said, according to Die Zeit and Handelsblatt. The Left Party itself is under pressure in the debate: party leader Schwerdtner rejected accusations of antisemitism at a party conference, with which the party has been confronted for weeks, according to Die Zeit and Handelsblatt.

What comes next is foreseeable but not decided. The exploratory talks would have to be followed by coalition negotiations, in which ministries, finances and legislative projects are settled; after that, party bodies or members usually vote their approval before the House of Representatives elects a Governing Mayor. The reports contain neither a timetable nor any statement on who would govern the city. It is also open how the SPD and the Greens will deal with the warning from the Chancellery and the CDU. For Berliners, the state of rents and housing policy therefore remains the most important test for now.

The timing is politically notable: the breakthrough becomes known on the same day on which the Federal Chancellor warns against exactly this cooperation, and the reports set the two events side by side. For national politics, this gives Berlin a signalling effect, because the SPD and the Greens would have to show how far they are willing to engage with the Left Party, while the Union stresses its distance. Whether the Berlin state branches withstand the pressure cannot be read from the reports.

Domestic · Union and SPD✓ 3 outlets · quotes from Tagesschau, Handelsblatt, Die Zeit

At the Junge Union’s Deutschlandtag, Merz defends his reform course and warns the SPD

At the Deutschlandtag of the Junge Union on Saturday, Federal Chancellor Friedrich Merz defended a tight reform course and set limits for his coalition partner, the SPD. According to reports by Tagesschau, Handelsblatt and Die Zeit, he wants to continue the coalition but attaches conditions to its survival: he does not want to preserve it at any price. Without reforms, he said according to Handelsblatt, he sees no electoral prospects for the CDU at the federal level; on pensions, long-term care and health insurance, something has to move. “They are stirring up the country, they are stirring up our party,” Handelsblatt quotes him as saying, with a view to the debates about the welfare state. At the previous appearance before the Junge Union there had been a row over pensions, according to Handelsblatt; this time the visit went more calmly. At the same time, Merz attacked the SPD politician Manuela Schwesig, who had previously accused the Union of inhumane behavior toward a politician who had resigned; the “disparagement,” he said, was not acceptable. Die Zeit also reports that Merz declared the defense of freedom the top priority and called for more setting of priorities. Which specific draft bills he means and by when they are to come is not stated in the reports.

Iran again reports explosion on a supertanker in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran has again reported an explosion on a supertanker. Die Zeit and Handelsblatt report that attacks on oil tankers and other ships keep occurring in the Strait of Hormuz. Another report says a supertanker ran into a sea mine in the strait; it is counted in the review of three outlets. Who laid the mine and how severe the damage is are not stated in the reports at hand.

The strait is regarded as one of the most important sea routes for crude oil. That is reflected in prices: US crude WTI closed at $99.02 on Friday, according to market data, a gain of 2.61 percent. The Iranian accounts of the explosion are not independently confirmed in the reports. For Germany the situation is relevant because heating oil, diesel and petrol depend on the world market price and haulage firms are already complaining about high fuel prices, as a Die Zeit report on charging infrastructure for electric trucks suggests. It says that high fuel prices make electric trucks more interesting for some haulage firms. Whether and when the situation in the strait will calm down cannot be inferred from the reports; they cover individual incidents, not an overall tally of the attacks since the start of the conflict.

World · Russia and Ukraine✓ 3 outlets · background of the decision unconfirmed

Trump suspends sanctions on Russia, Zelensky demands 70 billion euros from the EU

US President Donald Trump is not letting sanctions against Russia that were only just adopted take effect, citing the “national interest.” Tagesschau and Die Zeit report this, and the item also appears on Google News. Russia welcomed the step as a “historic moment,” as Die Zeit writes; a Russian negotiator commented accordingly. Tagesschau recalls that Trump had previously made a controversial diesel deal with Russia.

At the same time, Ukrainian President Zelensky is demanding a further 70 billion euros from the EU to defend against the Russian attacks, according to Deutsche Welle. At the EU summit, according to a review of reports, the question is looming whether support for Ukraine is crumbling. No decisions on this are available in the reports. It is also open how long the suspension of the US sanctions is to apply and what Washington expects from Moscow in return. For Germany and the EU the question is doubly delicate: they bear a large share of the aid, and they must expect the negotiating situation to shift without their involvement. So far the reports document only the suspension and the reactions to it, no new talks.

Panorama · Violence✓ 2 outlets · background of the crime still unclear

Knife attack in a care home for the elderly in North Rhine-Westphalia: the suspect was in a wheelchair

A knife attack has taken place in a care home for the elderly in North Rhine-Westphalia. Die Zeit reports that the suspect was in a wheelchair; Google News carries the item under the title “Attack in German care home claimed one life.” According to the reports, there is no reliable information on the motive, the identity of the suspect or the age of the victims; investigations are under way. What happened in a home where people expect protection and care will occupy above all the police and the home supervisory authority in the coming days.

A second sad piece of news comes from Bavaria: a firefighter died during a firefighters’ run at the ski-flying hill in Oberstdorf in the Oberallgäu. Der Spiegel reports that participants had to climb more than 1,000 steps in full gear; Google News also carries the item. How the death came about is not known. Such runs are regarded as a stress test, and the organizers now face questions about medical care and safety. According to the reports, both cases are isolated incidents; there is no connection between them.
