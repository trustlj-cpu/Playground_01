Tax estimate: federal government expects about 4.1 billion euros more in 2027, Klingbeil sees no new room to maneuver

The federal government can expect more tax revenue in 2027 than first anticipated; Finance Minister Klingbeil nevertheless draws no relief from it.

The Tax Estimation Working Group presented its new forecast on Thursday. According to a dpa report carried by Tagesspiegel, it expects about 4.1 billion euros more in tax revenue for the federal government in 2027; for the federal government, the states and the municipalities together, the figure is just under eleven billion euros more. For the first time, the revenue of all levels of government is expected to exceed one trillion euros next year. For the years through 2030, Spiegel cites additional revenue of 43.3 billion euros. Tagesschau sums up the report by saying that, despite current crises, the federal government can expect more tax revenue next year than first anticipated, but headlines it “More money, but no new room to maneuver.”

Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil (SPD) draws no budget leeway from the estimate. Zeit Online and Handelsblatt report under the headline “Klingbeil: no relief for the budget” that the surplus changes nothing about the situation of the federal budget; Handelsblatt also writes that the extra tax revenue is not enough to cut the electricity tax. According to Tagesspiegel, Klingbeil sees no new room to maneuver for 2027, but no new gaps either. Zeit Online recalls that hopes had been high above all in the CDU/CSU: more tax revenue was supposed to pay for relief on the electricity tax. FAZ describes disillusionment at the minister’s financial reckoning and asks where the catch in the estimate lies; Handelsblatt speaks of a missing unexpected tax windfall for the coalition.

In Montenegro, Merz tells the Western Balkans he will personally back their EU accession

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has pledged personal support on their path into the EU to the six countries of the Western Balkans at a meeting in Montenegro, report Zeit Online and Handelsblatt. According to dpa reports carried by Neue Westfälische, he assured the governments that they could count on Germany and on him personally, but at the same time demanded more reforms; the political will, he said, must come from the countries themselves. According to these reports, Merz called Montenegro the country closest to accession; negotiations are likely to be concluded next year at the latest. Berliner Zeitung quotes him as saying that the Balkans must not become a playground for the great powers, and Handelsblatt headlines a second report “Merz warns of power struggle over the Western Balkans.” The Berlin Process, under which the countries meet, was launched in 2014 under Angela Merkel; Merz and France’s President Emmanuel Macron have also launched an initiative to speed up admission.

Federal prosecutors arrest alleged Russian spy in Brandenburg, Merz expects more such cases

An alleged spy for Russia was arrested in Brandenburg on Thursday, report Tagesschau, Zeit Online, Handelsblatt and FAZ. According to the dpa report in Tagesspiegel, he is a Ukrainian whom the Federal Prosecutor’s Office had arrested in the Oder-Spree district. He is accused of agent activity for an intelligence service. He is said to have gathered information for a Russian intelligence service on the production of tanks, ammunition and explosives as well as on personnel and military installations on the NATO eastern flank; some of it was publicly accessible. According to the Federal Prosecutor’s Office, contact with the intelligence service ran through an intermediary and dated back to July 2025 at the latest. Tips came from the Office for the Protection of the Constitution.

Handelsblatt reports that Chancellor Merz warns of increasing espionage attacks; according to Tagesspiegel, he called the arrest a success and expects such cases more often in the coming months and years. The man is to be brought before the investigating judge at the Federal Court of Justice in Karlsruhe, who will decide on pretrial detention.

At the Conference of Minister-Presidents, the states demand a fuel price cap from January 1 and a quick cut in the electricity tax

The Conference of Minister-Presidents has called on the federal government to promptly present a proposal for the announced fuel price cap. Zeit Online and Handelsblatt headline their reports “States press for fuel price cap from January 1.” According to the dpa report carried by news.de, the states also urged a quick cut in the electricity tax for everyone. According to Zeit Online, the SPD heads of government Manuela Schwesig of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Anke Rehlinger of Saarland are demanding that Economy Minister Katherina Reiche (CDU) flesh out the cap; it is to take effect on January 1, 2027, with rules in Luxembourg and Belgium as the model.

According to these reports, Reiche had previously voiced skepticism about the cap favored by the SPD. As offsetting funding, the states demand a national windfall tax if the EU does not agree soon; they no longer want to take part financially in another fuel rebate. According to news.de, the coalition had agreed in mid-September on a fuel rebate from October and a temporary cap from January at the latest. Zeit Online also reports that Thuringia is demanding that carbon pricing be suspended.

Bosch spins off its automotive business and reorganizes the group, 62,500 employees move to new companies

The foundation-owned group Bosch is reorganizing. As Zeit Online, Handelsblatt, FAZ and Börsen-Zeitung report, its largest division, Mobility, is being separated from Robert Bosch GmbH and run as an independent subsidiary. Handelsblatt quotes new group CEO Christian Fischer as saying “I want to radically simplify Bosch.” FAZ writes of a restructuring because of the auto crisis. Zeit Online notes that Mobility remains the group’s largest division.

According to the company’s announcement on Thursday in Gerlingen, carried by Börsen-Zeitung, the restructuring affects 62,500 employees in Germany. Besides Mobility, Bosch Digital, Global Business Services and Global Real Estate are also to be moved into companies of their own; the four units are to remain in the group as wholly owned subsidiaries. Bosch says pay and working conditions are to stay unchanged. A sale of the automotive division is not planned, according to the reports.
