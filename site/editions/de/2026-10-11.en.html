After the diesel dispute, Trump calls for a new president for Ukraine; Kyiv and London back Zelensky, Berlin sticks to the sanctions

The dispute over the diesel trade with Moscow escalates on Saturday. Republicans in Congress object, Russia praises the words, and in Ukraine 20 to 21 people die in Russian attacks. What Trump intends to do remains open.

On Saturday, US President Donald Trump openly attacked Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. “It is time for Ukraine to get a new president,” he said, according to consistent reports from the BBC, DW, Die Zeit, Tagesschau, FAZ, Süddeutsche Zeitung and Der Spiegel; according to DW, the country is to hold an election for that. The occasion is the dispute over the diesel trade with Russia that we reported yesterday. Zelensky had called it an “investment in the war.” According to Italian media (Il Sole 24 Ore, La Stampa), Trump also demanded that Kyiv stop its attacks on Russian refineries.

The background is domestic politics. On Friday evening, after a phone call with Putin, Trump announced on Truth Social that Russia would “immediately” deliver more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel, and later three million. So far the figures come only from him; the Kremlin named no volumes. Fuel prices in the US weigh on him ahead of the midterm elections on November 3, and Washington is easing the sanctions pressure on Moscow for that reason. Zelensky sees this as a gift to Putin; according to ABC, Trump held Kyiv partly responsible for the rising diesel prices in the US.

The Ukrainian government rejected the move and is backing Zelensky, report Die Zeit and Handelsblatt. According to La Vanguardia, Zelensky said gifts to Putin would bring “not peace, but more terror.” Support came from London: Burnham assured Zelensky of “complete solidarity” (Straits Times, ANSA). In Moscow the reaction was different. Kirill Dmitriev, Putin’s economic envoy, called Trump’s remark “iconic and historic” (ANSA, EADaily). Opposition is also stirring in Trump’s own party: Republican lawmakers reject the diesel deal and the words about Zelensky, reports The Hill.

The federal government is sticking to its line. According to Reuters and DW, Germany is holding on to the sanctions against Russia. According to Der Spiegel and FinancialJuice, Russia has partly lifted its diesel export ban. Experts doubt that this will make anything cheaper at the pump: the FAZ speaks of a “symbolic act” that will barely change prices; Der Spiegel, too, quotes a female expert with doubts.

Meanwhile the war went on. According to France 24 and Straits Times, 20 to 21 people died in Russian attacks on Saturday, mostly in Zaporizhzhia; Reuters had first reported seven dead. According to Reuters, negotiators from the US, Ukraine and European states met in Miami while Trump announced the diesel deal. This week the Kremlin dampened expectations that peace talks will resume soon (Reuters).

What Trump specifically intends to do against Zelensky is not documented in the reports at hand: no plan, deadline or step by the US government is named anywhere. The delivery volumes, too, still come only from Trump. The markets are closed until Monday; WTI closed on Friday at $91.85, up 0.39 percent. For Germany, what counts is whether the allies hold their line on sanctions and how Berlin and Brussels answer Trump’s demand for a change of power in Kyiv. A comment from the federal government on this demand could not be found up to early Sunday.

Houthi attack on Riyadh airport: twelve dead, 309 injured, Trump considers taking part in Saudi strikes

In an attack on King Khalid Airport in Riyadh on Saturday, at least 12 people died and 309 were injured, according to the Saudi civil aviation authority (Reuters, AP, DW, Tagesschau, Die Zeit, Handelsblatt, Al Jazeera). At first there was talk of two dead and several injured. The Saudi-led military alliance holds the Houthi militia in Yemen responsible and calls it a war crime; Business Times calls it the deadliest Saudi attack in ten years. The alliance also reports an intercepted missile near al-Kharj and a projectile near Dammam airport.

The attack falls within the war with Iran, in which the Houthis act as an Iran-backed militia. According to Reuters, airlines cancelled more connections, and the airport suspended operations. Major conferences in Saudi Arabia are still to take place, according to the organizers (Reuters). The Yemeni government army reports advances against the Houthis (Al Jazeera); this is not independently confirmed.

US Secretary of State Rubio said an American was killed in the attacks. Trump called the attack “unacceptable” and, according to Al Jazeera and Mint, is considering taking part in Saudi strikes against the Houthis; nothing has been decided. UN Secretary-General Guterres condemned the attack. According to Der Spiegel, the federal government advises against flights over Riyadh. In the Strait of Hormuz an oil tanker caught fire after being hit by an unknown projectile; the crew is unharmed, according to UKMTO (Der Spiegel, FinancialJuice). It is not yet confirmed who hit the tanker, or whether it is connected to the attack on Riyadh.

Merz also rejects the new EU budget draft, Seehofer accuses him of a “massive loss of credibility,” pressure grows ahead of the CDU retreat

Chancellor Merz sees “no basis for an agreement” in the new proposal by the Irish Council presidency for the EU budget for 2028 to 2034 and regards an agreement as “far off” (Tagesschau, Die Zeit, Handelsblatt, dpa). The draft provides for total spending of around 1.826 trillion euros. According to dpa, that is about 141 billion euros less than in the Commission’s draft; t-online gives 159 billion. According to Handelsblatt, net payers such as Germany consider it “simply unaffordable.” The heads of state and government confer in Brussels on October 15 and 16.

At the same time, pressure on the chancellor at home is growing. Former CSU leader Horst Seehofer attests to a “massive loss of credibility” on his part (Die Zeit, Stern, n-tv, t-online); the source of the quotes is a BILD report. Handelsblatt writes that pressure is growing ahead of the CDU Presidium’s retreat, and according to Tagesschau Merz faces a difficult task. In Berlin, exploratory talks among the SPD, the Greens and the Left are causing annoyance: Minister of State for Culture Weimer calls red-green-red “a vote of no confidence against our coalition” (FAZ, Tagesschau). Former chancellor Schröder is isolated in the SPD after his visit to Putin; the Upper Palatinate district demands his expulsion (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt). An end to the coalition cannot be found in the reports.

Law · TaxesPress ✓ 5 outlets · ruling not expected before autumn

Karlsruhe examines inheritance tax from Monday: may business heirs pay less than everyone else?

The Federal Constitutional Court will hear cases on inheritance tax starting Monday (Der Spiegel, LTO, Epoch Times, Freitag, Finanzwende). One proceeding goes back to a referral from the Federal Fiscal Court, which holds the exemption of business assets to be unconstitutional. A second was initiated by the Bavarian state government. It considers the tax-free allowances too low because property prices have risen. According to the reports, the exceptions for business heirs cost the state about 8.8 billion euros in 2024.

Economists such as Ifo head Clemens Fuest call for lower rates with fewer exceptions. The Association of Family Entrepreneurs warns of consequences for investment and jobs; the DIHK points to 500,000 jobs at risk under a heavier burden, an association calculation without independent verification. A ruling is expected for the autumn (LTO); an exact date was not given in the reports. For heirs of small estates, nothing changes at first.

Augsburg scores after eight seconds, Bayern take only one point, Leipzig beat Frankfurt 6-0, and Lange quits in Hawaii

FC Augsburg scored the fastest goal in Bundesliga history after eight seconds against FC Bayern (Die Zeit, Der Spiegel, Straits Times, CNA). Der Spiegel writes that goalkeeper Manuel Neuer was to blame. Bayern then managed a draw; according to Der Spiegel, four goals were disallowed for offside, and the BBC speaks of a late goal that averted a defeat. The FAZ counts almost 30 shots on goal. In Leipzig versus Frankfurt, Christopher Nkunku scored three times in the 6-0 (Die Zeit, FAZ, Der Spiegel); the Frankfurt coach said, according to Express: “One has to be ashamed.”

Triathlete Patrick Lange had to retire with cramps in his last Ironman in Hawaii (Der Spiegel, FAZ, Die Zeit, n-tv). In the 2. Bundesliga, Nuremberg and Wolfsburg drew 4-4, with three goals scored in stoppage time (Die Zeit, Eurosport). Pro cyclist Paul Seixas won the Tour of Lombardy as the youngest winner in history (Der Spiegel, Sportschau).
