Domestic politics · SPD · CoalitionPress ✓ 12 outlets · reasons per Bas

Bärbel Bas resigns as labor minister and SPD chair, Schweitzer to succeed her at the ministry today, Klingbeil wants the party leadership re-elected at the turn of the year

The minister cites the death of her stepdaughter and public attacks, including from the coalition partner. The SPD faces a question of power, and Klingbeil remains vice chancellor and finance minister. An end to the coalition has not been reported.

Bärbel Bas gave up all her offices on Monday morning: that of federal labor minister and the SPD chair, which she shared with Lars Klingbeil (Reuters, Tagesschau, Der Spiegel, Süddeutsche Zeitung, Handelsblatt, Die Zeit). She cites the death of her stepdaughter and public attacks on her, including from the coalition partner (Süddeutsche Zeitung, Die Zeit). “Today a chapter ends for me, but not the story,” her statement says (Die Zeit). She said she cannot find the strength to carry on at the moment (FAZ, Handelsblatt).

Her successor at the ministry is to be Alexander Schweitzer, the former minister-president of Rhineland-Palatinate (n-tv, Der Spiegel, Tagesschau, T-Online). According to Handelsblatt and Die Zeit, he is to be appointed today, Tuesday, and receive his certificate of appointment. Klingbeil promised that the new minister would hit the ground running and that the pension reform would stay on schedule (Handelsblatt). Die Zeit writes that Bas is leaving her successor a large reform package.

Klingbeil, who is vice chancellor and federal finance minister, wants to have the SPD leadership re-elected at an early party conference at the turn of the year (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit). He wants to stay in both government posts even if he gave up the chair; whether he will run again he left open (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit). Defense Minister Boris Pistorius dodged the question of his own ambitions for the party leadership (Der Spiegel, Handelsblatt). Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania’s Minister-President Manuela Schwesig said coalition talks took priority (Handelsblatt). Klingbeil cancelled a trip to India because of the turmoil in Berlin (Reuters).

Klingbeil lamented a “poisoned” climate in federal politics that “breaks people” (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt) and criticized the CDU (T-Online). Handelsblatt writes that digs from the Union are said to have led to the resignation; so far this is supported only by Bas’s statement and the paper’s assessment. Commentaries in Der Spiegel and Tagesschau ask whether conduct in politics has become too unforgiving, and urge the Union and the SPD to pull themselves together.

For the Union the resignation comes at a delicate moment: the CDU Presidium met in retreat on Monday to discuss the party’s slump (Der Spiegel, Handelsblatt). Handelsblatt sees the coalition shaken and Klingbeil at risk.

It is open who will lead the SPD in future, when the party conference will take place and whether the coalition will present the pension reform as promised. Schweitzer’s appointment follows today; after that it will become clear whether he takes up the reform package as Klingbeil promises.

Foreign policy · EU · Russia · USAPress ✓ 12 outlets · Moscow’s consent not documented

EU imposes record sanctions on Russian war enablers, Trump defends diesel deal with Moscow, Kremlin does not confirm energy ceasefire

On Monday the EU foreign ministers put more people and organizations on a sanctions list over the war against Ukraine in one go than ever before (Tagesschau, Die Zeit, Süddeutsche Zeitung, Handelsblatt). Reuters counts almost 1,600 new entries. Tagesschau writes that it looks like a response to Trump but was not planned that way.

Trump defended his diesel deal with Russia even though lawmakers of both parties voice criticism (Reuters, Der Spiegel). According to Der Spiegel, senators warn of a possible violation of the law and of consequences for Ukraine. Russia’s Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said Russia could export up to 3.5 million tonnes of diesel a month by the end of the year (Reuters, FinancialJuice). Trump’s envoy Jared Kushner called the deal an attempt to put Moscow in a more accommodating mood in the peace talks (Reuters, FinancialJuice). Experts doubt that Russia can deliver significant volumes (FAZ).

According to Kyiv, the “energy ceasefire” announced by Trump is not holding: Russia is continuing to attack energy facilities (Reuters, AP, PBS). The Kremlin rejects Zelensky’s idea of a 60-day ceasefire and wants a comprehensive agreement (Reuters); according to FAZ, it did not confirm the energy ceasefire. Chancellor Merz and Zelensky spoke by phone and called for a ceasefire for energy facilities (Handelsblatt, Federal Government). At the same time the EU is expanding accession negotiations with Ukraine and Moldova (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt).

Domestic politics · CDU · Saxony-AnhaltPress ✓ 8 outlets · financing open

CDU wants to cut the electricity tax for everyone, Klingbeil doubts the financing, in Saxony-Anhalt the CDU parliamentary group considers walking out of the chamber during the vote for minister-president

After the CDU Presidium’s retreat in Berlin, Chancellor Friedrich Merz announced that the electricity tax would be cut for everyone to the European minimum (Die Zeit, Tagesschau, FAZ, Der Spiegel). Der Spiegel writes that this relieves consumers and makes electric cars and heat pumps more economical, but also names reasons for skepticism. Klingbeil, responsible as finance minister, doubts the financing (Handelsblatt, Deutschlandfunk). A commentary in Handelsblatt notes that of the threatened revolt by the CDU minister-presidents after the election defeats in the East, only “five meager pages” remained; neither the government’s personnel nor its policy is changing. FAZ reads from Merz’s appearance the message “We have understood.”

In Saxony-Anhalt the CDU is suspected of having voted for an AfD state parliament speaker (Die Zeit). In the vote for minister-president, the parliamentary group is considering leaving the chamber together so as not to risk any votes for the AfD candidate Ulrich Siegmund (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, BILD, Süddeutsche Zeitung). The reports gave no date for the vote.

Security · Poland · GermanyPress ✓ 8 outlets · attribution per Poland

Fire at Starlink station in Poland: two suspects arrested in Germany, Warsaw blames Russia

At the end of September a ground station of the Starlink satellite network, which also supplies the Ukrainian military with internet, burned in Poland (Tagesschau, Der Spiegel). Prime Minister Donald Tusk announced on Monday that two suspects had been arrested in Germany (FAZ, Die Zeit). According to Reuters they are two Turks, according to Der Spiegel Turkish citizens; the arrests were made on Poland’s behalf (FAZ).

Warsaw sees Russian intelligence services behind the fire and regards Russia’s responsibility as established (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, Reuters). In the reports at hand Poland has not published any evidence. On Monday Reuters also reported that the federal interior minister warns of a “shadow war” against Germany that goes beyond the threats of the Cold War.

Sport · Business · LawPress ✓ 8 outlets · timing of the ranking inconsistent

Zverev to become No. 1 in the world tennis rankings, Audi sells almost a fifth fewer cars in the third quarter, Constitutional Court hears case on inheritance tax

Alexander Zverev is set to become No. 1 in the world tennis rankings (Die Zeit, DW, BILD, Eurosport). The trigger was Carlos Alcaraz’s surprise exit from the Masters tournament in Shanghai, where he lost in the third round to Bergs (Eurosport). According to sportschau.de, Zverev is the first German man at the top since Boris Becker. When the first ranking with him on top will appear is not consistent across the reports: BILD speaks of the coming weeks, FAZ names the tournament in Vienna as the first opportunity.

Audi delivered almost a fifth fewer cars in the third quarter, mainly because of China and the USA and because of model changeovers (Handelsblatt, Die Zeit, Der Spiegel, n-tv). Volkswagen also reports lower sales again (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt). In Karlsruhe the Federal Constitutional Court began a two-day hearing on the inheritance tax (Handelsblatt, Tagesschau): on the first day it dealt with a complaint from Bavaria over whether the tax should be a matter for the states; it is also examining whether business heirs pay too little tax. Family business owners and the German Chambers of Industry and Commerce (DIHK) reject higher levies (Der Spiegel).
