Berlin and Paris want to soften the combustion-engine ban

Merz and Macron agree on the sidelines of the EU summit: more room for petrol and diesel cars, plus an auto package with an eye on China – but the details and the approval of the other member states are still outstanding.

Germany and France have agreed to soften the combustion-engine ban. Both governments announced this on Friday, as Tagesschau reports; WEB.DE and other outlets also report an agreement between Chancellor Friedrich Merz and President Emmanuel Macron. In total, the news appeared in four outlets in our review, making it one of the most widely circulated reports of the day in Germany.

The background is the EU rule under which, from 2035, only new cars that emit no CO2 in operation are to be newly registered in the European Union. German carmakers in particular, but also parts of the French industry, have long pushed for more flexibility: demand for electric cars is growing more slowly than hoped, competition from China is fierce, and manufacturers have to invest billions in two drivetrain worlds at once. That the two largest economies in the EU are now taking a joint position gives the cause political weight.

How far the loosening goes is not conclusively settled according to the reports at hand. One headline speaks of “more combustion engines, less China” and cites new details of an auto pact between Merz and Macron; what exactly it contains is so far described by this one source only, so we are not reproducing any figures or years. All that is clear: it is an understanding between two governments, not a decision by the EU.

That requires the Commission, the European Parliament and a majority of the member states. Environmental groups and countries that bet early on electric mobility are likely to announce resistance; manufacturers, in turn, demand planning certainty and warn against back-and-forth on the rules. For employees and suppliers in Germany a great deal is at stake, for buyers at first very little: cars that can be bought today are not affected by the agreement. We are keeping an eye on the negotiations and will report back as soon as a text is available that several sources confirm.

ECB apparently gives UniCredit the green light on Commerzbank

The European Central Bank has apparently allowed the Italian major bank UniCredit to increase its stake in Commerzbank – up to and including a full takeover. That is what several headlines on Friday say; in the review the topic appeared in seven outlets, making it the most widely circulated business topic of the day. One title is cautious (“apparently allows”), another more definite (“gives green light for full takeover”). We have no official statement from the banks involved or from the supervisor. For Commerzbank, the second-largest private bank in the country after Deutsche Bank, the supervisor’s approval would be an important step, but not yet a takeover: the supervisory permission merely allows UniCredit to cross the threshold; whether and at what price it actually buys shares or makes an offer is open. In the past the federal government has voiced skepticism about a sale, and the works council fears for jobs. Whether anything has changed since then is not apparent from the reports at hand. For customers nothing changes at first. For investors and employees, what matters is how UniCredit’s leadership proceeds now and whether Berlin announces resistance. We will follow up as soon as one of the banks or the ECB speaks for itself.

Zelensky courts EU billions in Brussels – and Trump piles on

At the EU summit in Brussels, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky appealed for further aid, according to reports by Der Spiegel and Google News. He is demanding, according to several outlets, around 70 billion euros from the EU; Ukraine is reportedly struggling to close its budget hole for the coming year. That is a demand, not a decision. At the same time, one outlet asks whether support for Ukraine in Europe is beginning to crumble – an assessment, not an established fact.

The summit is weighed down by a dispute with Washington: according to Handelsblatt, US President Donald Trump has renewed his criticism of Zelensky while the EU wants to de-escalate. Trump also said he was not responsible for a throttling of the Starlink satellite internet that is putting Ukraine in a bind (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt). Tagesschau reports, immediately before Zelensky’s appearance, that Russia is placing further Western-owned companies under Kremlin control.

Pope asks Putin for a ceasefire in Oleshky

Pope Leo XIV has called on Vladimir Putin to allow a ceasefire in the sealed-off frontline city of Oleshky in southern Ukraine. He is concerned about the situation there, report Die Zeit, Handelsblatt and Google News. Whether the Kremlin responds is open.

In Poland, authorities are examining a lead to the Turkish mafia in the case of the fire at a Starlink internet station, according to Der Spiegel reports. The attack was apparently meant to weaken the Ukrainian army; Poland had initially held Russia responsible. Both readings are so far lines of investigation, not findings.

Junge Union: Winkel stays chief, Wüst attacks talks with the Left Party

Johannes Winkel remains chair of the Junge Union. He was re-elected on Friday evening at the Deutschlandtag of the youth organization of the CDU and CSU (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit). Beforehand, North Rhine-Westphalia’s Minister-President Hendrik Wüst had called on the SPD and the Greens to end the exploratory talks with the Left Party in Berlin and had warned against the Left Party and the AfD (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt, Google News).

Meanwhile the leadership of the Left Party’s parliamentary group in Berlin distanced itself from the member of parliament Vanessa Emde, who had drawn attention with controversial statements about Israel. In Saxony-Anhalt, the BSW leadership wants to recommend abstaining on Siegmund in the vote for minister-president.
