Middle East · Saudi ArabiaPress ✓ 12 outlets · damage to Aramco unconfirmed

Houthis hit Riyadh airport a second time, terminal in flames, Economy Minister Reiche stuck there, Dammam also a target

After twelve deaths on Saturday, another projectile strikes the airport of the Saudi capital on Sunday. The Houthis threaten further attacks, and airlines and governments warn against travel. How Washington will respond remains open.

King Khalid Airport in Riyadh has been hit for the second time within two days. On Saturday, at least twelve people died in a missile attack, according to the Saudi civil aviation authority, and 309 were injured (Reuters, AP, Tagesschau, Die Zeit, Handelsblatt). On Sunday, another unidentified projectile struck, according to the Saudi-led military alliance (BBC, PBS, 1News). According to 1News and Detik, it hit the passenger terminal, where a fire broke out. The civil aviation authority reports one dead and eleven injured for Sunday (FinancialJuice); Indian media report the death of an Indian national (Hindustan Times, Livemint).

The first attack is regarded as the worst of its kind: Business Times calls it the deadliest Saudi attack in ten years, and the alliance speaks of a war crime (DW, Business Times). Many of the injured are foreigners, including, according to Economic Times, eleven Indians and, according to Inquirer, four Filipinos. Airlines cancelled connections. According to the Financial Times, oil company chiefs cancelled their attendance at an energy congress in Riyadh; the organizers had previously wanted to hold major conferences regardless (Reuters).

The Houthis, an Iran-backed militia in Yemen, claimed responsibility for the attacks (Reuters, Die Zeit). They also said they had hit the airport of Dammam and facilities of the oil company Aramco in the east of the country (FinancialJuice). The alliance reports an intercepted drone there; Handelsblatt and FinancialJuice confirm Dammam as a target. Damage to Aramco is not independently confirmed. The Houthis again warned airlines and travelers against using Saudi airports (Reuters, Die Zeit, CBS). According to Handelsblatt and Die Zeit, Saudi Arabia attacked the airport of Sanaa; according to Reuters, that report goes back to a television station run by the Houthis.

Germany is affected too. Economy Minister Katherina Reiche could not leave Riyadh as planned because of the attacks (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, Handelsblatt). The reports did not say how and when she will return. According to Der Spiegel, the federal government has advised against flights over Riyadh since Saturday. The British embassy warns against all non-essential travel to Jeddah, Dammam and all Saudi airports except Medina (FinancialJuice, post on X); Canada advises against Riyadh airport (Global News).

US President Trump said after the first attack, according to Der Spiegel: “We are acting quickly.” Which steps he means was not reported. The Iran war remains the backdrop: Der Spiegel sees in the attacks the danger of an open war, SRF an attack on Saudi Arabia’s business model as a hub for travelers and conferences. According to Saudi state media, the foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia and Iran spoke about the situation (FinancialJuice).

Three things count for Monday: whether Riyadh airport reopens, how the oil price reacts, and whether Washington announces strikes. Saudi shares fell (Reuters), and the oil price rose in Sunday-evening trading (Bloomberg). The last market close was Friday: WTI $91.85, up 0.39 percent. Heating oil and diesel can get more expensive in Germany; Xetra opens at 9 a.m.

Foreign policy · USA · UkrainePress ✓ 12 outlets · Moscow’s consent not documented

Trump announces an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine, Kyiv awaits details, Russia hits a power plant

The background is the dispute over the diesel trade with Moscow. On Friday Trump announced Russian diesel volumes, Zelensky called it an “investment in the war,” and on Saturday Trump demanded a new president in Kyiv. Berlin then sided with Zelensky (Reuters).

On Sunday at about 2:30 p.m. UTC, Trump declared that Russia and Ukraine had agreed on an “energy ceasefire,” “with immediate effect” (Reuters, USA Today, PBS, Tagesschau, Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, FAZ). Zelensky first said it was news to him (NBC, Der Spiegel). Later he welcomed the announcement and awaited details from Washington (Reuters, Straits Times). At the same time, Kyiv reported a Russian strike on a power plant in Kharkiv (ABC, Ukrainska Pravda, Tagesschau).

Ukraine set one condition: it will stop its attacks on Russian refineries if Russia stops its attacks on energy facilities (Reuters). In Russian attacks, six people died on Sunday in two cities (Reuters). Whether the Kremlin agrees to the ceasefire is not clear from the reports at hand. Britain’s Prime Minister Burnham, Canada’s Prime Minister Carney and EU Commission President von der Leyen support the idea of an energy ceasefire (FinancialJuice, Evening Standard). Foreign Minister Wadephul calls for more pressure on Putin after Trump’s remarks (Die Zeit).

The Financial Times reports that the US is threatening Kyiv with a halt to weapons deliveries if the attacks on refineries continue; this is confirmed only by that source. A Republican senator considers Trump’s diesel deal with Russia possibly unlawful (Reuters). In an ABC interview, Zelensky offered elections if Russia agrees to a 60-day ceasefire (Reuters).

Domestic politics · CoalitionPress ✓ 6 outlets · outcome of the retreat open

CDU leadership meets in retreat on the way out of the crisis, Söder wants to strengthen Merz and urges dissenters to leave, nine state ministers protest the sugar tax

The CDU Presidium withdraws to a retreat in Berlin to discuss consequences after the election debacle in the East (Der Spiegel, Handelsblatt, Tagesschau). General Secretary Franziska Hoppermann plays down expectations of the retreat (Die Zeit). Tagesschau sees Chancellor Merz facing a difficult task; the FAZ writes that the party must make a virtue of necessity. No results were available by early Monday.

CSU leader Markus Söder said he wants to keep strengthening the chancellor (Die Zeit). On the ARD show “Caren Miosga” he urged CDU dissenters to leave the party and called a behavior “pretty shabby” (Die Zeit, WELT); the reports did not say whom he means. The coalition is under pressure (Tagesschau): CDU politician Gordon Schnieder rejects an SPD proposal on pensions (Die Zeit). Nine CDU state ministers wrote to Merz and reject the planned sugar tax (Die Zeit, Handelsblatt). An end to the coalition cannot be found in the reports.

Foreign policy · PolandPress ✓ 9 outlets · suspect’s identity not reported

Poland: arrest after alleged plot to attack Prime Minister Tusk, security agencies convene crisis meeting

Polish police have received indications of a possible attack on Prime Minister Donald Tusk and the defense minister, by their own account (Reuters, AP). Security agencies convened a crisis meeting (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, Handelsblatt). In the evening BBC, Tagesschau, Der Spiegel and the Süddeutsche Zeitung reported that a suspect had been arrested. Who he is and how far his plans had progressed was not stated in the reports.

Tusk had said earlier that knife attacks on teenagers in Poland were driven by the glorification of violence online (BBC). Former president Andrzej Duda spoke of “a big fuss,” according to Onet. On the same day Tusk’s camp lost the mayoral election in Kraków: an independent candidate beat Tusk’s candidate (Notes from Poland, Rzeczpospolita, WP).

Sport · BundesligaPress ✓ 8 outlets · scores per match reports

Cologne wins the Rhine derby, Gladbach fans riot, Freiburg takes the lead in the table, Union coach Härtel resigns, Verstappen wins in Singapore

1. FC Köln beat Borussia Mönchengladbach 2-1 in the Rhine derby, decided by a goal from Said El Mala (FAZ, Die Zeit, Der Spiegel). Gladbach fans rioted, and the match was briefly interrupted because firecrackers and rockets were thrown (Die Zeit, Express, Der Spiegel). SC Freiburg won short-handed 2-1 against Schalke 04, substitute Goto scored late, and Freiburg displaced Borussia Dortmund from the top (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, FAZ, Eurosport, sport.de). Jens Härtel resigned as U19 coach of Union Berlin after allegations over discriminatory remarks and a DFB penalty motion (Die Zeit, WELT, n-tv, T-Online).

In the Premier League, Manchester City won at FC Liverpool with a numerical advantage, and the home fans booed City after the ruling in the financial scandal (Der Spiegel, FAZ, SPORT1). According to Transfermarkt, Erling Haaland scored in a fifth game in a row. In Formula 1, Max Verstappen won in Singapore; Kimi Antonelli has his first match point for the title at the next race in Austin (Der Spiegel, Die Zeit, FAZ, BBC). Lewis Hamilton suffered a bout of weakness after the race (BILD, Speedweek).
