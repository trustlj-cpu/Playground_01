Berlin SPD and Greens vote for coalition negotiations with the Left Party – SPD state executive board backs it 26:3

A small Greens party convention follows with a broad majority; the Left Party, as the election winner, has not yet voted.

The Berlin SPD and the Berlin Greens decided on Monday to enter coalition negotiations with the Left Party. Tagesschau, Handelsblatt and FAZ report this. The SPD state executive board approved first; Handelsblatt cites a vote of 26:3. The Greens followed in the evening: at a small party convention, a broad majority of the 45 delegates voted for negotiations, according to Handelsblatt; officially there were two votes against, later there was talk of probably four, because some ballot cards had been overlooked. The vote by the Left Party, which won the election, is still pending, according to Tagesschau; Handelsblatt writes that the party is still taking its time. The basis is the exploratory paper by the lead candidates Elif Eralp (Left Party), Graf (Greens) and Steffen Krach (SPD), presented on Saturday. According to Handelsblatt, the vote was preceded by weeks of criticism: first the Union attacked the possible alliance, then resistance grew in the SPD parliamentary group.

The decision comes amid a debate over the role of the Left Party. Economic Affairs Senator Franziska Giffey of the SPD warned, according to Handelsblatt, of the consequences of the socialization of housing. Greens leader Felix Banaszak criticized comparisons between the AfD and the Left Party, according to the FAZ live blog. With the decisions, the three parties move from exploratory talks to formal negotiations; there is no coalition agreement, and the party bodies would still have to approve one. The reports give no date for the start of the talks.

France imposes local curfews, Macron rejects Interior Minister Nuñez’s resignation

After the looting and street battles of the weekend, the French government announced locally limited curfews on Monday. Tagesschau, SRF, NZZ and Handelsblatt report this. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu admitted, according to the NZZ: “On Saturday we lost control.” According to SRF, a curfew for minors applies in Lille, Douai, Wattignies and Denain; in two Paris districts a nighttime curfew applies from 11 p.m. Monday to 6 a.m. Tuesday. The NZZ counts at least 3,000 looted shops and almost 1,000 injured police officers. Handelsblatt reports that the Yellow Vests called for new protests on Saturday. Tagesschau reports that Federal Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt blamed two groups for the riots in the neighboring country. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez, who is accused of having reacted too late, had offered his resignation after the riots. President Emmanuel Macron rejected it, according to the NZZ, and left it to Lecornu to calm the situation. The figures on injuries and damage differ depending on the source and the time of the report; the government had given other figures at the weekend than the NZZ now cites.

IG Metall: Volkswagen plans to freeze salaries until 2030

According to IG Metall, the Volkswagen group plans to freeze its employees’ salaries until 2030. Zeit, Handelsblatt, Tagesschau and FAZ report this on Monday. According to ZDF, the works councils passed on an instruction from the group’s management board under which HR managers worldwide are to fix current salary levels and suspend increases. The instruction has been in effect since the beginning of October and, according to the union, covers not only base salaries but also variable pay and special payments. IG Metall fears real wage losses of at least ten percent. Volkswagen does not comment on the reports; the works council calls the plan unrealistic and announces tough resistance. According to the reports, collective bargaining talks are to begin at the end of November, and the union is demanding five percent more pay. The group has terminated several company-level collective agreements as of December 31, 2026.

Federal government and Nvidia sign a declaration of intent on industrial AI at the investment summit

The federal government and the US chipmaker Nvidia signed a declaration of intent on cooperation in artificial intelligence at the first “Invest in Germany” summit in Berlin. Tagesschau, Zeit, Spiegel, Handelsblatt and Süddeutsche Zeitung report this. According to Zeit, the Economics and Research Ministries signed the non-binding declaration; at its center is an initiative for “Physical AI”, meaning AI for robots and factories. Federal Chancellor Friedrich Merz called on companies to make their data available, according to Spiegel; Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang warned against giving knowledge away abroad. The Süddeutsche Zeitung headlines its report with Huang’s sentence “Rebuild Germany” and recalls that he had criticized Germany beforehand. Handelsblatt quotes him as saying German industry would get “no further chance”. Before the summit, Handelsblatt had reported a three-digit million sum from federal funds; there is no confirmation.

Roche puts new investments in Germany on ice and accuses the Merz government of breaking trust

The Basel-based pharmaceutical group Roche is halting all new investment decisions in Germany for now. WirtschaftsWoche, Blick and 20 Minuten report this. Germany head Daniel Steiners justifies the step with the federal government’s planned austerity measures in health care; politics has “destroyed a great deal of trust”, 20 Minuten quotes him as saying. According to WirtschaftsWoche, it is mainly about the plans to raise the statutory manufacturer rebate for many patent-protected drugs from 7 to 15.5 percent from January 2027. Projects already approved are not affected, according to the report, including the expansion of the Penzberg site in Bavaria with more than 600 million euros. Roche employs around 18,000 people in Germany; no job cuts have been announced so far.
