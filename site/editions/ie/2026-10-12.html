Trump announces a Russia–Ukraine “energy ceasefire”; Kyiv awaits details and a Kharkiv power plant is hit

Zelenskyy called the announcement “news to me” but said Ukraine would agree if Russia does. Hours later Kyiv said an energy facility in Kharkiv had just been struck.

On Sunday afternoon President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that Russia and Ukraine had agreed an “energy ceasefire”, a halt to attacks on each other's power and fuel infrastructure, according to Reuters, the BBC, Bloomberg, CNBC, NOTUS and the Irish Examiner. Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, told Axios the declaration was “news to me” and said Kyiv was waiting for details from Washington. He added that Ukraine would agree if Russia accepts, and told ABC he would organise elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire (Reuters). Ukraine also said it would stop strikes on Russian refineries if Russia stops its attacks on Ukrainian energy (Reuters).

Shortly afterwards the Kyiv Independent and other outlets reported that Russia had struck a power plant in Kharkiv, and Ukrainska Pravda quoted Zelenskyy saying Ukraine was ready to agree but that an energy facility there had just been hit. Reuters reported Russian attacks that killed six people in two Ukrainian cities. The Financial Times reported that Washington had threatened to block arms for Ukraine over its attacks on Russian refineries, and that Trump faces Republican opposition to his diesel deal with Vladimir Putin; Reuters reported a Republican senator saying the deal may be illegal. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Canada's Mark Carney backed the call for an energy truce, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen backed Zelenskyy's call for a ceasefire. Moscow's response was not confirmed in the reports seen, and neither was any reaction from the Irish Government.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · Sunday casualty figure is single-source

Second missile attack in two days targets Riyadh airport as oil executives pull out of energy congress

Saudi authorities said Saturday's strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 12 and injured 309, according to AP and FinancialJuice, citing the civil aviation authority. On Sunday the airport was targeted again: the BBC reported a second strike in two days and the Saudi-led coalition said air defences intercepted a missile near the airport. FinancialJuice, citing Saudi civil aviation, reported one dead and 11 wounded on Sunday, a figure no other outlet in our pool had carried. The Irish Times reported that an Irishman was among the injured on Saturday.

The Houthis claimed responsibility and renewed a warning to airlines and travellers against using Saudi airports (Reuters). AP reported that the airport's closure was extended, and the FT reported that oil company chiefs pulled out of an energy congress in Riyadh. Reuters said Saudi stocks slid. The UK embassy told British citizens to avoid all but essential travel to Jeddah (FinancialJuice). Irish and European markets reopen today; WTI closed on Friday at US$91.85.

Ireland · Public sector payPress ✓ 3 outlets · positions attributed to those named

Public service strike on Wednesday “can be avoided”, says a minister, as the Dáil sitting is cancelled

RTÉ reported on Sunday that a minister said the public service strike planned for Wednesday 14 October can be avoided. The Irish Examiner reported that more than 100,000 workers are expected to take part, including teachers in the INTO and ASTI, nurses and health workers, and that the HSE is bracing for “widespread disruption”, cancelling non-urgent services and asking non-union staff to cross picket lines. The Irish Times reported on Saturday that the Dáil sitting planned for Wednesday has been cancelled because of the strike.

The dispute is over a new public sector pay agreement. Unions say the last deal left pay about 5% behind inflation, and want pay to be the first item in talks. The Examiner reported that Public Expenditure Minister Jack Chambers has set aside €1.2bn in Budget 27 for a deal, and that the Taoiseach, Micheál Martin, said the Government will not accept unilateral preconditions. A second strike day is planned for 21 October, and teachers have also announced two 24-hour strikes.

Inagh-Kilnamona win a first Clare senior hurling title as club finals fill the weekend

Inagh-Kilnamona were crowned Clare senior hurling champions for the first time on Sunday, according to the Irish Independent, the Irish Examiner and RTÉ. In Tipperary, Thurles Sarsfields won the county title again, with the former Tipperary hurler Pádraic Maher guiding the club, the Independent and RTÉ reported. The Independent said Rathnure ended St Martin's reign in the Wexford hurling final.

In football, the Independent reported that Portarlington won a sixth Laois title in seven years, Tullamore completed a four-in-a-row in Offaly, Four Roads won five in a row in Roscommon and Clonduff ended a 26-year wait in Down. The Leitrim final between Ballinamore and Fenagh goes to a replay, and in Kerry, Spa beat East Kerry in a quarter-final with a last-gasp two-pointer.

Burnham says it is “clear” Russia interfered in the 2016 Brexit vote

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said on Sunday that it is clear Russia interfered in the 2016 Brexit referendum, according to RTÉ, the Guardian, Reuters and the Financial Times. The headlines we have seen do not set out new evidence, and DailyDrop will not guess at it.

The claim has been disputed for years. In 2020 a UK parliamentary committee said the issue had not been properly examined, and the government of the day said it had seen no evidence of successful Russian interference in the referendum, according to reports at the time. Burnham made his remarks as he also backed Trump's call for an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine.
