Ireland · Public sector payPress ✓ 6 outlets · cost and next steps not yet confirmed

Schools shut and hospital services hit as 300,000 public sector workers strike for a day; union leaders meet today

Minister Jack Chambers said late on Tuesday that pay can “absolutely” be the starting point for talks, which RTÉ called an apparent shift in position, and the four largest unions now weigh their next move.

More than 300,000 public sector workers, among them nurses, doctors, teachers and civil servants, stopped work on Wednesday over delays in agreeing a new public sector pay deal, RTÉ reported. The Journal also spoke of around 300,000, and the Belfast Telegraph and ITV headlined “hundreds of thousands” in a 24-hour strike. RTÉ said virtually every primary and post-primary school would stay closed as teachers and SNAs joined the one-day stoppage, and the Irish Examiner's live coverage described schools, hospitals, local authorities and government services across the State being hit. How many hospital appointments or operations were lost was not in the reports seen, so DailyDrop gives no figure. On the same day an email threatening schools was sent to more than 150 of them, the Irish Times reported; the Irish Independent called it a “threatening email”, and the coverage RTÉ carried said gardaí were assessing how credible it was. The headlines seen report no incident.

The stoppage followed a late change of tone. As reported at the weekend, the dispute turned on whether pay should head the agenda; on Tuesday night RTÉ quoted Mr Chambers saying pay can “absolutely” be where negotiations on a new deal begin. By early Thursday RTÉ said the leaders of the four largest public sector unions would meet to assess the strike's impact and discuss their next steps. The Irish Independent reported that unions are weighing escalation, including regional strikes aimed at ministers' constituencies; it described that as on the cards, not as a plan. A second strike day on 21 October was reported earlier in the week, and the Wednesday and Thursday reports seen do not say whether it stands. Opinion split as expected: the Aontú leader argued in the Journal that the Government's long spell in office has brought the country to a standstill, while Mary Regan in the Independent wrote that the Government wants to show it made every effort to get unions to the table. Both are views, not findings.

Ireland · CrimePress ✓ 4 outlets · motive is a family member's claim

Gardaí search premises in Waterford as a son says the fire that killed four relatives was over a €20,000 drug debt

Four members of one family, two parents and two of their children, died when their home in Waterford was set alight on Tuesday morning, the Irish Times and the Irish Independent reported. The Times named the parents as Phil and Kim English. RTÉ reported on Wednesday that gardaí had carried out searches in connection with the murders of the four. The Independent said it was the second time in less than a month that the house had been targeted in what it called drug-debt intimidation, and that the family were “living in fear” and had “asked to be moved”.

On Wednesday night the Irish Examiner and RTÉ reported one of the sons saying the attack was over a €20,000 drug debt owed by someone else. That is a relative's account, and the headlines seen do not show gardaí confirming a motive; the Examiner quoted them calling the inquiry “intense in nature and very active”. A vigil in Birchwood drew hundreds, according to waterford-news.ie.

Markets · United StatesPress ✓ 3 outlets · closing levels not confirmed

Hot US inflation and a chip rout hit Wall Street; Asian shares fall as Fed officials play down a rush to raise rates

US consumer prices rose 0.6% in September from August and 3.7% over the year, Bloomberg reported from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, with petrol prices up 9.1% on the month. Reuters said prices accelerated on dearer fuel and that core inflation was firmer. Wall Street fell on Wednesday, led by technology. Bloomberg reported that chip shares tumbled, that the S&P 500's longest spell of calm since 2020 was over, and that the Russell 2000 index of smaller companies entered a correction, 11% below its peak. Bank of America also slid after a charge of about $1.2 billion, which Bloomberg called a fine. Treasuries rallied as investors sought safety. DailyDrop has no two-source closing levels for the indices and gives none.

Markets are betting the Federal Reserve will keep raising rates; the question is how fast. Reuters carried Fed official Daly saying hikes are likely needed but not urgently, and Bowman saying there is time to wait and see, and reported the dollar retreating from an 18-month high as those remarks tempered bets. Asia took the hit on Thursday. Reuters said AI-linked trades crumbled, the FT said the sell-off deepened with Korean and Japanese AI stocks hit, and Bloomberg said the Taiwan dollar and Korean won led regional currency losses. Japan's finance minister Satsuki Katayama warned speculators she is ready to act again on the yen. The ISEQ close and the ECB euro rate for Wednesday are not confirmed.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · no reason given for the curbs

Ukraine says SpaceX has curbed Starlink for its army; Musk points to Washington as Zelensky visits London

A presidential aide said SpaceX had limited Starlink operations in Ukraine, disrupting military missions, Reuters reported, and Reuters added that Kyiv accused the company of crippling drone missions as ties with the US worsen. The BBC reported Elon Musk saying the US government may be behind the curbs on Ukraine's use of Starlink; Reuters headlined him as saying Mr Trump is “ultimately” responsible for any limits on Starshield. Reuters wrote that Europe has no quick fix, and Poland's Sikorski asked Mr Musk whether he is playing politics. Reuters also said shares in the satellite operator Eutelsat jumped.

President Zelensky was in London on Wednesday, where the BBC said Prime Minister Andy Burnham welcomed him to Downing Street, and he met King Charles, according to AOL and London Now. Reuters quoted him saying it is “painful” when Mr Trump chooses a different side. The FT, relayed by Reuters, reported that US negotiators floated unfreezing some Russian assets to restart peace talks and that European officials balked; no second outlet had that. Italy's prime minister Meloni called the US-Russia diesel deal a “very risky choice”.

World · GulfPress ✓ 3 outlets · cause of the sinking not confirmed

A cargo ship sinks near Hormuz after being hit; Houthi attack leaves Aramco headquarters badly damaged, sources say

A container ship sank near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile, Reuters reported, citing sources. Bloomberg said a cargo ship sank in the strait after a strike set off a fire and the crew were evacuated, according to the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore. The reports do not name the vessel or say who fired. Reuters separately reported officials saying Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks on shipping before the US vote. A post on Reddit said Iran sank two ships; the two news outlets describe one.

In Saudi Arabia, Reuters, citing sources, said the Aramco headquarters suffered extensive damage in a Houthi attack, and Bloomberg said the Houthis had launched attacks across the country that hit it. AP reported residents of Riyadh hearing new explosions, and Reuters said the Houthis vowed more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities. Oil moved both ways on the headlines: Bloomberg said it gained on Wednesday and Reuters said prices fell early on Thursday as investors weighed supply and the tensions. No price is given because DailyDrop could not confirm one in two sources.
