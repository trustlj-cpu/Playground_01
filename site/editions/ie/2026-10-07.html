Budget 2027: higher-rate threshold rises €2,500 to €46,500 and main tax credits go up €125

Simon Harris and Jack Chambers presented an €8.5bn package on Tuesday. RTÉ described the tax measures as larger than expected, though outlets give different totals.

Finance Minister Simon Harris told the Dáil on Tuesday that the point at which the 40% rate of income tax starts will rise by €2,500, from €44,000 to €46,500 for a single person, according to the Irish Times, the Irish Examiner, TheJournal and RTÉ. The main personal, employee and earned income tax credits each rise by €125, and a Universal Social Charge band ceiling goes up by €1,600 to €30,300. Home carer credit rises by €100. The Irish Times and Raisin report that the standard rate of capital gains tax falls from 33% to 31% for disposals from 7 October.

RTÉ called the tax package larger than expected. Outlets differ on its size: the Irish Times put tax reductions at €1.65 billion, while Raisin gave €1.3 billion for income tax alone. Davy had pencilled in €1.5 billion of tax measures. Public Expenditure Minister Jack Chambers set spending growth at 6% for 2027, according to RTÉ. Sinn Féin called the income tax changes an "insult to workers", according to coverage of the Dáil debate.

Fuel excise cuts extended to the end of February, and carbon tax on heating cut

Harris said the reduced excise rates, 27 cent a litre on petrol and 32 cent on diesel, will stay fully in place until 28 February 2027 and then be restored in four phases, with full restoration on 30 June 2027, according to RTÉ, the Irish Times, Agriland and the Connacht Tribune. The reduced NORA levy runs to 31 December and the enhanced diesel rebate scheme stays until year end.

The carbon tax on home heating oil and gas goes back to its 2023 level and will not rise again in the lifetime of the Government, Harris said. The fuel allowance rises by €5 to €43 a week. The Government did not bring back one-off energy credits, according to the Irish Times. Fuel protest leaders warned that action "will go ahead" because the budget did not lower pump prices, the Irish Examiner reported.

Help to Buy rises to €35,000 but second-hand stamp duty relief is dropped at the last hour

The maximum Help to Buy support for first-time buyers rises from €30,000 to €35,000, according to the Irish Examiner, TheJournal, the Irish Times and Raisin. It applies to new homes. A proposal to scrap stamp duty for first-time buyers of second-hand homes was dropped at the last hour, the Irish Examiner reported, and the rise in the grant was described as a compromise.

Harris said the €5,000 increase would have a "negligible" effect on house prices. The Irish Times carried an opinion piece in August calling the scheme a money pit that benefits higher earners. For renters, the rent tax credit rises by €150 to €1,150 for a single person and €2,300 for a couple from January, according to the Irish Times and Raisin.

Economy · Public financesPress ✓ 4 outlets · Government response attributed

Fiscal watchdog says the budget puts public finances on a worse trajectory

The Irish Fiscal Advisory Council said on Tuesday that the budget puts the public finances on a "worse trajectory" by overshooting spending limits and relying on high-risk corporation tax, according to RTÉ, the Irish Times, TheJournal and the Irish Examiner. It said the speed limit set at 5% in 2021 has been exceeded repeatedly, with actual spending growth of nearly 9% in 2026.

The Council estimated that about six of every seven euros of corporation tax would be spent, much of it on current spending, and noted that three firms pay nearly half of the total. Excluding excess corporation tax, it projected deficits growing from €12 billion in 2026 to €20 billion in 2030. Taoiseach Micheál Martin disagreed, citing the 6% rise in expenditure and a €1.6 billion unallocated contingency. Harris said reliance on corporation tax was "a real concern" and the Council was right to say so.

Society · BudgetPress ✓ 4 outlets · childcare figures per Irish Times and Irish Examiner

Welfare up €10 a week, a new €500 disability payment and childcare fees capped at €550 a month

Weekly social welfare rates rise by €10, and a €500 cost-of-disability payment is introduced, according to RTÉ, the Irish Times, TheJournal and the Irish Examiner. The Irish Times and Irish Examiner reported that the National Childcare Scheme gets an extra €65 million, with maximum monthly fees falling to €550 from next September and the universal subsidy rising from €2.14 to €2.50 an hour.

Inheritance tax thresholds rise in all three categories: parent to child goes from €400,000 to €420,000, according to the Irish Times. Cigarettes go to €20 a pack, TheJournal reported. A critic told the Irish Examiner that a targeted €400 energy credit for low-income households was refused. TheJournal also carried a check on which measures were repeats of earlier announcements.
