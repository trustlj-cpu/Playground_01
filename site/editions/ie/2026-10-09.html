Oil jumps about 5% as Iran steps up attacks on tankers in Hormuz; WTI closes up 3.6%

Brent touched about US$105 and WTI closed at US$91.50. The rise is a reminder that the fuel excise cuts only soften the pump price, not the oil price.

Brent rose about 4.8% to roughly US$105.02 on Thursday and WTI traded near US$92.69, up about 5%, according to Oilprice.com, which tied the jump to reports of Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. It cited Reuters in saying that a US Navy-led information outlet had counted a dozen attacks between 28 September and 2 October. WTI closed at US$91.50, up 3.60%, according to market data.

Oilprice.com also reported that US Gulf Coast operators had started shutting in production and preparing refineries ahead of an approaching storm. DailyDrop found the Hormuz and storm details in that one outlet, so they are attributed. For Ireland, oil sets the price before tax. The Government extended the fuel excise cuts to the end of February on Tuesday, but a rise of this size would still feed into pump prices within weeks.

Public sector unions plan national strikes on 14 and 21 October as pay talks stall

Unions have planned one-day national strikes on Wednesday 14 and Wednesday 21 October, according to the Irish Examiner, RTÉ and Fórsa, as unions and the Government could not agree an agenda for pay talks. The last public pay deal expired in June, and unions say it left pay behind inflation.

Fórsa, the largest public service union, said 96.6% of members who voted backed industrial action, on a turnout of 72.6%. SIPTU and the INMO reported similar majorities. The Irish Times reported on Wednesday that Taoiseach Micheál Martin called the dispute "unwarranted given our willingness to talk". Chambers has said any deal must be "sustainable and affordable".

Bakhurst calls talks with minister "constructive" but does not know if RTÉ's €20m will be restored

RTÉ Director General Kevin Bakhurst and chairman Terence O'Rourke met Culture Minister Patrick O'Donovan on Thursday afternoon, according to the Irish Examiner. Bakhurst called the talks "constructive". Asked whether RTÉ would get back the €20 million cut from its 2027 funding, he said: "I don't know. We'll see." He said he had confidence in the minister.

The Irish Examiner also reported that a senior Government source said a solution could come from reforms to enforcement and collection of the TV licence fee. The Public Accounts Committee will ask RTÉ and the Department to appear in the coming weeks. The Irish Times carried an analysis saying RTÉ could be forced to cut services, programming and staff if there is no political resolution.

Opposition parties lay into the budget: "insult", "KitKat budget" and "not a win"

Sinn Féin's Pearse Doherty called the income tax changes an "insult to workers" and said most workers would miss out on the €750 tax break, which goes mainly to higher earners, according to RTÉ and TheJournal. Mary Lou McDonald said the budget lacked imagination and ambition, the Irish Times reported.

Labour called the €10 welfare rise "not a win" that would not carry people through winter, according to RTÉ, and its leader Ivana Bacik called it a "KitKat budget from a TikTok Tánaiste". Ged Nash described Harris and Chambers as "Soundbite Simon and JackGPT". The Social Democrats' Cian O'Callaghan said promised help with energy bills had not arrived. The Government rejects these claims, which are political opinion, not findings.

Euro slips below 1.13 as the French-German bond gap widens, and a US 30-year auction hits 5.618%

FXStreet reported that the euro sold off hard as the gap between French and German bond yields widened, with 1.1300 giving way for the first time since May 2025. StoneX said the gap briefly reached about 150 basis points, its widest since the euro debt crisis, before narrowing to about 140. Investors are questioning whether France can rein in its budget deficit.

RoboForex put EUR/USD at 1.1190 on 8 October and at 1.1230 on 9 October, adding that a US 30-year bond auction had produced a yield of 5.618%, the highest since August 2000. The levels differ by time of day, and DailyDrop has not confirmed a closing rate, so the right-hand ear box shows a dash. A weaker euro makes dollar purchases dearer for Irish households.
