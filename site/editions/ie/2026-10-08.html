European shares fall 1.1% and banks slump as bond yields climb; Dublin was down 1.3% at midday

Higher oil and borrowing costs ended a three-day rally. Deutsche Bank lost 5%, and Irish banks fell more than 2% at midday.

The STOXX 600 finished Wednesday 1.1% lower at 629.95 after three days of gains, according to Sharecast, which said bond yields and oil were rising again. Lenders were hit hardest. Deutsche Bank fell 5%, and Intesa Sanpaolo, UniCredit and BNP Paribas lost about 4% each, as fears of contagion from French and Italian government debt weighed. Trading Economics reported that 10-year yields rose 10 basis points in Italy and 15 in France, while the German Bund was flat.

In Dublin the ISEQ was down 198 points, or 1.3%, to 14,272 at 2.45pm, RTÉ reported. AIB was down 2.5% at €11.08 and Bank of Ireland 2.3% at €18.81. DailyDrop could not confirm the official close, so the left-hand ear box shows a dash. Roic.ai linked the retreat to oil above US$100 and French fiscal worries. The euro market story matters in Ireland because banks make up a large part of the index.

RTÉ funding cut by €20m for 2027 as the broadcaster rejects the minister's reasons

The Government confirmed that RTÉ will receive €240 million in 2027, €20 million less than the €260 million it expected under its multi-annual funding deal, according to the Irish Examiner. Culture Minister Patrick O'Donovan said the decision reflected dissatisfaction with progress on reducing staff numbers, and that there was "very little scope" in a departmental funding package of €848 million.

RTÉ Director General Kevin Bakhurst told staff: "We completely reject the assertion that the pace of reform and transformation within RTÉ has been slow." The Irish Examiner reported that the cut could affect RTÉ's five-year strategy and a planned move in Cork. DailyDrop has seen the reports from one outlet only.

US 10-year yield touches its highest since 2002 as Wall Street retreats from its record

The benchmark 10-year US Treasury yield jumped to 5.364%, its highest since 2002, before easing after strong demand at an auction, according to market reports. The 30-year yield rose to about 5.725%, a peak since May 2002. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq each fell about 0.22% on Wednesday after record highs the day before, and the Dow lost 341 points, or 0.67%, according to the Associated Press report carried by the Coeur d'Alene Press and other outlets.

The Russell 2000 of smaller companies dropped 1.31%. The S&P 500 closed at 7,801.77. Reports differ on the exact S&P figure, one giving 7,775.04, so DailyDrop uses the closing level supplied by market data. For Ireland the yield rise matters because euro bond yields tend to follow, and Irish pensions hold US assets.

Fed minutes show unanimous support for September's rate rise, with differing views on further tightening

Minutes of the Federal Reserve's September meeting, released on Wednesday, showed that policymakers unanimously backed the rate increase, the first in about three years, according to reports. Officials expressed differing views on the risks that would require tighter policy.

The minutes arrived the same day yields hit 20-year highs. Weak September payrolls of 29,000, reported last week, have cooled expectations of another rise in October, according to an earlier RoboForex note. The Fed meets again in November. For Irish households the link is indirect: the ECB sets euro rates, but Fed moves shift the dollar and global bond yields.

Markets · CommoditiesPress ✓ 2 outlets · closing levels from market data

Gold falls 1.1% and oil reverses lower as yields climb

Gold fell 1.11% to US$4,140.7 an ounce on Wednesday as rising yields made non-interest-paying assets less attractive. WTI crude closed at US$88.28, down 1.30%, after oil had been higher at the open. Brent briefly traded above US$102 before settling at US$100.20, according to market reports.

Earlier in the day, RTÉ's midday market update and Roic.ai had pointed to oil above US$100 as a drag on shares. The reversal in oil suggests that traders were reluctant to push prices up further while demand fears grow.
