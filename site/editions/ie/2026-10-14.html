Ireland · Public sector payPress ✓ 6 outlets · scale of the walk-out is from headlines only

Schools shut and services disrupted as the public sector strike begins, while a minister says pay can open talks

RTÉ reported schools closed as teachers joined the 24-hour stoppage that began at the start of Wednesday. Late on Tuesday, Minister Jack Chambers said negotiations with the unions can begin with pay.

RTÉ reported just after midnight that the public sector strike had begun, with widespread disruption, and a little later that schools were shut as teachers joined it. The broadcaster had run an explainer on Tuesday asking what would be hit, and followed it with an “At a glance” guide to the effects. Headlines in the Belfast Telegraph and on ITVX speak of hundreds of thousands of public sector workers staging a 24-hour stoppage. DailyDrop saw only those headlines, so it treats that scale as their description and not as a count. The headlines seen do not list every union or service involved.

The Government’s tone shifted late on Tuesday. RTÉ quoted Jack Chambers saying talks with unions can “absolutely” begin with pay; the Irish Examiner headlined that pay can be the starting point, and the Irish Times reported the Minister saying pay can be discussed as a “priority”. Earlier in the week the Examiner had the unions pressing for pay to come first. It also reported that union confidence in the industrial relations process is “severely damaged”, so the offer does not by itself end the dispute. RTÉ added that the Government will be warned of the strike’s effect on the wider economy, and that farming groups are criticising payment delays linked to the strikes. The Journal’s FactCheck asked whether recent public sector pay rises have outstripped the cost of living; DailyDrop has seen the question, not the answer.

Ireland · CrimePress ✓ 3 outlets · the gang link is a garda belief; no charges seen

Gardaí carry out raids in Waterford after a family dies in a petrol bomb attack on their home

The Irish Times reported on Tuesday evening that gardaí believe the deaths of a Waterford family in a petrol bomb attack are linked to a local drugs gang. The paper also reported that the targeted family had suffered other vandalism attacks in recent years. The Irish Examiner described the horror of the arson attack as neighbours recalled it and named the children who died. DailyDrop does not repeat the names, and the headlines seen do not give the number of dead. Early on Wednesday RTÉ reported gardaí carrying out raids in Waterford after the fatal fire.

Three things are not yet clear from the reports seen: who carried out the attack, whether anyone has been arrested, and what the evidence is for the gang link. A police belief reported by a newspaper is a line of inquiry, not a finding, and the Examiner calls the attack arson without naming anyone. Where the headlines differ on the word, petrol bomb or arson, DailyDrop uses the Irish Times wording.

World · VaticanPress ✓ 3 outlets · type of cancer and treatment plan not in the headlines seen

Pope Leo is diagnosed with lung cancer after surgery to remove part of his right lung

AP reported on Tuesday evening that Pope Leo XIV has been diagnosed with lung cancer after surgery to remove part of his right lung. Headlines in the news feed attributed the announcement to the Vatican. Earlier on Tuesday the Guardian reported that he had been admitted to hospital for an operation to remove a growth on a lung, and a Reuters report, which DailyDrop saw only as a web address, said he would enter hospital that day for surgery on a lung nodule.

The headlines seen do not say what type of cancer it is, how advanced it is, or how long recovery and any further treatment will take, and DailyDrop will not guess. Leo was elected in 2025 and is the first pope born in the United States. His diagnosis will draw close attention to how the Vatican reports on his health.

Ireland’s women prepare for the World Cup play-off against Poland as Carla Ward warns the Poles will want revenge

The Irish Times reported manager Carla Ward striking “only positive notes” ahead of the World Cup play-off against Poland. The Independent wrote that Ireland are chasing a second successive World Cup place, and called Cork’s Denise O’Sullivan a local hero “on Leeside” as the Poles lie in wait. World · Middle EastPress ✓ 6 outlets · damage reports differ

Houthi attacks hit Saudi Aramco’s headquarters in Dhahran as the Saudi-led coalition strikes oil depots near Sanaa

Bloomberg and FinancialJuice reported on Tuesday that Aramco’s headquarters in Dhahran was hit during Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia; Reuters, citing sources, had reported loud booms near the site. The Saudi state news agency, citing an energy ministry official, said an attack on an Aramco building caused a limited fire and no casualties. Iranian media said the headquarters was evacuated, which the Saudi account does not confirm. Saudi civil defence said debris from intercepted missiles injured four people in Riyadh and slightly damaged 15 homes.

In Yemen, the Guardian, the BBC and the FT reported the Saudi-led coalition striking oil or fuel depots and Houthi targets near the capital, Sanaa, after Reuters reported it ordering an oil facility there evacuated. Reuters quoted Donald Trump saying the Houthis are trying to drive up oil prices before the US midterm elections, and reported oil rising as traders weigh Saudi supply risk. A Bloomberg video headline warned that a wider conflict could push oil to $120.
