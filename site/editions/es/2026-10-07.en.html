Maricarmen Abascal dies, the woman whose eviction changed the campaign

She was 87. She had been evicted on Sept. 23 from the Retiro home where she had lived since 1956 and was about to return. Her case prompted the housing decrees, their defeat in Congress and the early election.

María del Carmen Abascal died on Wednesday aged 87, according to El País, La Vanguardia and Europa Press. The family had not made the cause of death public by the close of this edition. According to El País, she died hours before signing a new eight-year rental contract that would have allowed her to recover her home. International outlets picked up the news.

Her story began as a neighborhood case. On Sept. 23, the company Urbagestión carried out, at the fourth attempt, the eviction from the home in Madrid’s Retiro district where she had lived since 1956 under a legacy rent contract (renta antigua). She had reduced mobility and a pension of €1,350. The eviction went ahead despite opposition from the Sindicato de Inquilinas (tenants’ union) and a UN request to suspend it. Images of the eviction set off protests across the country and a protest camp (acampada) in the Puerta del Sol that on Wednesday reached its twelfth day.

On Sept. 28, her lawyer announced an agreement with the owner so that she could return to the home. But her case had already left the neighborhood. The Government approved two housing decrees in response; Congress struck them down on Oct. 2 with the votes of the PP, Vox and Junts; and on Oct. 5 Pedro Sánchez called elections for Nov. 29. On Tuesday, the Council of Ministers approved the decrees again, which the press was already calling the “Maricarmen decrees.”

At the Sol camp, the news was received with sadness. “We are not going to stop, we are going to keep going,” the campers told Europa Press. Left-wing leaders lamented that she could not live her last years in her home. El Mundo reported that part of the left is turning her death into an electoral icon, with phrases such as “it is not a death, it is a murder.”

The texts approved on Tuesday, made known this Wednesday, go beyond her case. They require administrations to cover the unpaid rent of vulnerable tenants to prevent their eviction, according to El País and Europa Press. They crack down on speculative buying and include companies with more than ten flats, La Vanguardia reported. One of the decrees comes into force on Thursday; the one on rental extensions would do so on Nov. 15, according to ABC.

Four days ago a hoax announcing her death circulated on social media; fact-checkers debunked it at the time. This time the news is confirmed by several outlets. Her name will stay in the campaign: the general strike for housing now has a date, Nov. 11, and the Government has until Nov. 14 for the Permanent Deputation (Diputación Permanente) to validate the decrees.

General strike over housing set for Nov. 11

CCOO and UGT set Nov. 11 as the date of the general strike for housing, according to La Vanguardia and El Mundo. They call to “end speculation” and to put a check on rising prices. ELA announced that the strike will also be held in Euskadi and Navarre, Europa Press reported. In Catalonia, unions and social organizations speak of a “historic” strike, according to ABC.

The date falls two days before the start of the election campaign. Not everyone is joining. USO, Fetico and civil-service unions are distancing themselves because they see “political connotations,” according to ABC. El Mundo estimates that the organizations rejecting the call have more than half a million members between them. Doctors’ unions, for their part, called off their indefinite strike after the early election was called.

Weather · CataloniaPress ✓ 4 outlets · search under way

Body found in flooded tunnel as search continues for a boy in Granollers

Firefighters on Wednesday found the body of a 43-year-old man inside a vehicle in a flooded tunnel in Les Franqueses del Vallès, according to El Mundo. Firefighters and the Mossos widened the search for Rami, a 12-year-old boy missing in Granollers during the storm.

Rodalies had a chaotic morning: drivers refused to run trains for lack of safety after the rains, La Vanguardia, El País and ABC reported. Rodalies denied there was a safety problem and president Salvador Illa announced an investigation, according to El Mundo. Service recovered only in part.

Health · WorldPress ✓ 3 outlets · Russian version unverified

Russia denies a second case of plague. WHO offers help and Mongolia watches the border

Moscow called suspicions of a second case of plague “false information,” while the United States asked its citizens not to travel to Russia, according to El Mundo. The World Health Organization offered technical help to Russia and asked for more information, El País reported.

Mongolia ordered health monitoring measures on its border with Russia over the possible outbreak of pneumonic plague, according to Europa Press. The case began on Monday with the death of a researcher at a high-security laboratory in Siberia. There are no confirmed cases outside Russia.

ElectionsPress ✓ 3 outlets · candidacies not settled

Más Madrid proposes Ada Colau as the Frente Amplio candidate

Más Madrid proposed that Ada Colau be the Frente Amplio’s candidate for prime minister and head the list in Barcelona, according to El País. El Mundo reports that this route brings Podemos closer, although the Madrid list is still disputed between the parties. Europa Press picked up the appeal to Podemos to understand “the historic moment” and join the Frente Amplio with “a single ballot.”

In the PP, La Vanguardia reports that Feijóo is weighing Xavier García Albiol as his candidate for Barcelona. The stock market had a bad day: the IBEX 35 fell 1.68%, to 19,118 points, in line with falls in Frankfurt and Paris.
