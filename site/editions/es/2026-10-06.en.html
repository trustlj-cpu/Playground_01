Constitutional Court orders amnesty for procés embezzlement. Llarena lifts Puigdemont’s arrest warrant

By seven votes to five, the court annuls the Supreme Court’s refusal to apply the amnesty. Hours later, the judge in the procés case voids the national arrest warrants. Puigdemont welcomes the decision, but does not say when he will return.

The full bench of the Constitutional Court on Tuesday upheld the amparo appeal of former Catalan minister Dolors Bassa and annulled the rulings with which the Supreme Court refused in 2024 to apply the amnesty law to the crime of embezzlement. The vote was seven to five, according to the press. The court obliges the Supreme Court’s Criminal Chamber to issue a new ruling declaring that crime amnestied. Formally, the judgment resolves only Bassa’s case, but it sets a doctrine that reaches the rest of the procés leaders.

The consequence came within hours. Judge Pablo Llarena, the investigating magistrate in the case at the Supreme Court, lifted the national arrest warrant hanging over Carles Puigdemont, according to Europa Press, La Vanguardia and El Mundo. He made the same decision for Toni Comín and Lluís Puig. Puigdemont has lived in Belgium since 2017. In theory, he can now return to Spain without being arrested at the border.

The five dissenting justices signed a very harsh dissenting opinion. They argue that the Constitutional Court “usurps” the Supreme Court’s role by deciding how the amnesty is applied, according to El Mundo. The rapporteur was the court’s president, Cándido Conde-Pumpido, as El País reported on Monday. The clash between the two courts goes back a long way: the Supreme Court held that the procés leaders obtained a personal benefit from the public money, and for that reason excluded embezzlement from the amnesty.

Puigdemont spoke in the afternoon in Waterloo. He celebrated the “victory of the amnesty,” but warned that “nothing is being solved today” in the political conflict, according to Europa Press. He avoided saying when he will return, El Mundo reported. Junts spoke of a victory for the independence movement. Vox said the ruling shows the support of the “institutions” for Sánchez’s campaign, La Vanguardia reported.

The ruling comes the day after the election call and changes the board in Catalonia. A return by Puigdemont before Nov. 29 could mobilize both blocs. For the PP and Vox, the amnesty will be a central campaign argument. For the PSOE, it is the closing of a chapter that was born of its 2023 investiture pact with Junts, the same party that brought down the housing decrees last week.

Steps remain. The Supreme Court’s Criminal Chamber will have to issue the new ruling the Constitutional Court demands for Bassa, and the same doctrine will be applied to the others convicted of embezzlement. Llarena has already applied it to those who were still outside Spain. El Mundo describes the ruling as the endorsement of the “Pumpido Constitutional Court,” and the phrase anticipates the tone of the campaign: the PP and Vox will put the spotlight on the court, and the PSOE will try to bring the conversation back to housing. None of the parties has yet said how the ruling will affect their pacts after Nov. 29.

Government approves the housing decrees again. Now the Permanent Deputation decides

The Council of Ministers on Tuesday approved for a second time the housing decrees that Congress struck down on Oct. 2. Expansión describes them as nearly identical. They include €10 billion in financing for first-home purchases, according to La Vanguardia and El Mundo, and bring back the anti-eviction shield, according to El País. The second decree extends rental contracts.

They must now be validated by Congress’s Permanent Deputation, which remains in place while the Cortes are dissolved. The PNV announced it will vote in favor of the first decree and against the second, the one extending rentals, according to Europa Press and Expansión. Isabel Díaz Ayuso warned that she will use “all legal instruments” against the decrees if they are validated “with some kind of trick,” El País and ABC reported. The date of the vote is not yet known.

WeatherPress ✓ 4 outlets · toll still provisional

No respite from the rains in Catalonia. Search continues for two missing people

Torrential rains continued over Catalonia on Tuesday and prevented a return to normal, according to La Vanguardia. ABC and El Mundo report two people missing because of the storm, and that the Generalitat is considering asking for a disaster zone declaration. In Granollers, the municipality hardest hit on Monday, the cleanup began.

The warning is not limited to Catalonia. Europa Press reported that rain and storms have half the country on alert. In Madrid, a downpour flooded the housing protest camp in the Puerta del Sol in the afternoon, according to ABC. A meteorologist quoted by La Vanguardia speaks of a tropical cyclone formation process “never seen” in the Mediterranean.

Student revolt spreads across France. Brussels calls for a proportionate response

Thousands of students demonstrated again on Tuesday in Paris, Marseille and other cities. El Mundo puts the number of demonstrators backing the students at more than 250,000. There was a heavy police deployment, according to ABC. High schools have been blocked for two weeks in some cities, El Mundo reported.

French intelligence attributes part of the unrest to the radical left, according to ABC. The European Commission called for a “proportionate” response and rejected violence, Europa Press reported. The crisis adds to the pressure on French debt, which weighs on the euro. In Madrid, the IBEX 35 rose 0.75%, to 19,444.2 points.

ElectionsPress ✓ 4 outlets · lists not yet closed

Pre-campaign begins: PSOE seeks the tactical vote and PP headquarters (Génova) distances itself from Ayuso

With 54 days to go before the vote, the parties are on the move. Oriol Junqueras announced that Gabriel Rufián will be ERC’s candidate for Congress, according to Europa Press. Pedro Sánchez will open the campaign on Saturday in Dos Hermanas (Seville) alongside María Jesús Montero, according to ABC and El Mundo. The PP circulated a video mocking an injury to minister Óscar Puente, El País reported.

The PP leadership distanced itself from Isabel Díaz Ayuso: it called postal voting “well safeguarded,” while she insisted that “everyone has to go to the ballot box,” according to El Mundo. On the left, Mónica García’s party demands to head the Madrid list. And Vox asked the Supreme Court to suspend the vote of the descendants of exiles as well, El País reported. The PSOE, for its part, is trying to attract the left’s tactical vote in the medium-sized and small provinces, according to El Mundo.
