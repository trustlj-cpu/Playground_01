Constitutional Court rejects the Ombudsman’s appeal over the grandchildren law and keeps the vote suspension in place

The court considers Ángel Gabilondo’s challenge premature and leaves the matter with the Supreme Court, as criticism between the two courts grows

The Constitutional Court has rejected the appeal filed by the Ombudsman, Ángel Gabilondo, against the Supreme Court over the so-called grandchildren law, and keeps in place the suspension of the vote that the Supreme Court had ordered, according to El Mundo, 20minutos and La Vanguardia on Wednesday. La Vanguardia specifies that the high court does not admit Gabilondo’s challenge for processing because it considers it premature. El Mundo adds that the progressive justices Ramón Sáez and Juan Carlos Campo, a former Justice Minister under Pedro Sánchez, are the only ones backing the Ombudsman’s appeal. El País quotes one of the arguments of the order, according to which “administrative ineffectiveness is no basis for admitting an amparo,” and notes that the ruling concludes that the case remains open at the Supreme Court. As a result, the matter returns to the ordinary route without the Constitutional Court having assessed the substance of the request, according to the four outlets.

El País also devotes an analysis to what it describes as the clash between Marchena and Conde-Pumpido over the amnesty. According to the paper, with the new order of the Criminal Chamber it is clear that the Supreme Court and the Constitutional Court are trading criticism and reproaches, holding opposing positions on how the amnesty should be applied. The same outlet explains that the Constitutional Court’s order insists that the case is not exhausted in the ordinary courts and that, therefore, an amparo is not yet possible. 20minutos stresses that the vote suspension handed down by the Supreme Court remains in force after the appeal was rejected. La Vanguardia attributes the inadmissibility to the fact that the Ombudsman’s petition was filed before the Supreme Court had ruled. None of the outlets consulted reports that the Supreme Court has yet set a date to issue its decision on the substance.

New Mexico judge orders Meta to pay $25 billion for deceiving users over privacy

A New Mexico judge ruled on Wednesday that Meta, the parent company of Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, must pay a fine of $25 billion for deceiving users with its privacy policies, ABC reported. El País, which calls the penalty historic, reports that the judge considers it proven that Facebook deceived users with its privacy policies up to 43 million times. The two papers agree in placing the ruling in the state of New Mexico and in saying that the amount of $25 billion is what the judgment sets for the group run by Mark Zuckerberg. ABC sums up that the penalty is based on having misled users about how their personal data was handled. El País adds the calculation of the 43 million occasions on which, according to the judge, the deception took place. Neither outlet reports, in the coverage published up to the early hours of Thursday, a response from Meta or says whether the company will appeal the ruling. Reuters reported separately, in the early hours of Thursday, that Meta has reached an agreement to keep Zuckerberg in California amid concerns over a possible tax on great fortunes, without linking it to this ruling.

Technology · Security✓ 3 outlets · no confirmed mass attack is reported

Two critical vulnerabilities found with AI force an update of PHP, the base of up to 70% of websites

The creators of PHP, technology that powers up to 70% of the world’s websites, have disclosed a serious security flaw, according to El Mundo, El País and Expansión. El País specifies that these are two critical vulnerabilities detected with the help of artificial intelligence and that they cause multiple failures on thousands of web pages. The developers of the programming language have released updates to fix the errors, according to the same paper. El Mundo headlines that AI detects a flaw that puts up to 70% of the world’s web pages at risk, while El País speaks of thousands of sites affected by multiple failures.

The three outlets place the finding in the PHP programming language and agree that patches have already been published by its maintainers. The coverage consulted gives no figure for sites that have suffered attacks and names no specific company as affected. Nor does it detail which artificial intelligence tool was used to locate the flaws. The outlets do not indicate a mandatory update schedule for administrators, so applying the patches depends on each website.

Economy · Business✓ 3 outlets · no agreement or timetable announced

Elon Musk backs SpaceX and Tesla studying a merger

The founder and chief executive of SpaceX and Tesla, Elon Musk, has voiced his support for a merger between the two companies, according to Europa Press and ABC. Both outlets report that the companies are studying the deal to create a technology giant. Bloomberg reported on Wednesday that SpaceX and Tesla are discussing a possible merger and that Musk backs it in an interview, and linked the announcement to SpaceX’s quarterly results.

According to Bloomberg, US stock futures rose after SpaceX reported better-than-expected third-quarter results, which eased doubts about whether global spending on artificial intelligence was sustainable. Bloomberg adds that Musk’s comments on the merger fueled the move. Reuters reported separately that Tesla aims to sell its Cybercab robotaxis before the end of the year despite a federal investigation. None of the outlets consulted offers terms, a date or a structure for the deal, or says that the boards of directors have voted.

Spain · Public order✓ 3 outlets · exact number of arrests and injuries still to be confirmed

Police charges and at least two arrests in A Coruña at the protest against an event with Santiago Abascal

More than a thousand people gathered on Wednesday in María Pita square, in A Coruña, at the call of nationalist groups, to protest against an event with Santiago Abascal, according to Europa Press and El Mundo. Both outlets speak of disturbances and police charges against the demonstrators. La Voz de Galicia raises the tally to at least two arrests after the incidents.

The three outlets place the events on the afternoon of October 21, around the event of the Vox leader. The coverage consulted gives no official figure for injuries or the full police report. Nor does it specify the charges on which the two detainees were arrested or whether Abascal’s event took place at the scheduled time.
