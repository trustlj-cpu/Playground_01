Spain · ImmigrationPress ✓ 7 outlets · the figures for those cleared do not match

Police clear El Guano, the last major migrant settlement in Ceuta, and the Government declares the large camps dismantled

The Sunday operation is reported with conflicting figures: some 500 people cleared according to Europa Press, around 150 according to other outlets. Felipe VI is scheduled to visit Ceuta and Melilla this week.

The National Police cleared the esplanade of El Guano on Sunday, next to the beach of the same name in Ceuta, the last major irregular settlement of migrants in the autonomous city, according to ABC, La Vanguardia, El Mundo, 20minutos and Europa Press. Europa Press reports a deployment of more than 350 officers and some 500 people cleared; eldiario.es and CeutaTV speak of around 150 people, most of them minors according to CeutaTV, which describes an operation of barely 45 minutes. The figures do not add up, and we have not seen an official tally that clears them up.

Minister Ángel Víctor Torres, coordinator of the operation’s unified command, declared the large settlements dismantled and said the Police will keep operating in the outlying neighborhoods to locate those sleeping on the street, according to Infobae and EFE. According to eldiario.es, the Government maintains that everyone will leave Ceuta if they are not entitled to international protection. The same outlet recalls that sources at the Government Delegation said a day earlier that there was room for everyone in the designated settlements, and that the delegate, David de la Encina, met with residents of the outlying neighborhoods, who pointed to migrants being moved into their neighborhoods.

Defense Minister Margarita Robles praised the work of the military in Ceuta in the face of criticism she considers “unfair” (El Mundo) and, in a message to soldiers deployed abroad, said that “Ceuta and Melilla are utterly Spanish” (ABC). Expansión announces Felipe VI’s visit to both cities, and El Confidencial reports a demonstration over Ceuta called in Barcelona for this Monday, National Day.

There is one allegation we cannot take at face value: eldiario.es claims that dozens of migrants were returned to Morocco without any procedure. It is the version of a single outlet, and we have not seen others confirm it or the Government deny it with data. It is also not known how many of those cleared were minors, how many have moved to reception centers or how many have been left on the street.

Ceuta is the European Union’s external border with Morocco, which is why every operation is read in political terms. The laSexta barometer we cited yesterday places immigration among the three issues that weigh most on the Nov. 29 vote. Today it is worth watching whether the Government publishes a tally with figures, whether the people dispersed through the streets reappear in the outlying neighborhoods and what message the King carries on his visit.

Spain · PoliticsPress ✓ 7 outlets · deals still not closed

Rufián will again head ERC’s list on Nov. 29. Podemos and ERC move closer to a Catalan deal and the Broad Front looks for a lead candidate

Gabriel Rufián will again head ERC’s ticket for Congress in the Nov. 29 election, according to El País, ABC, El Mundo, La Vanguardia, 20minutos, Europa Press and Expansión. El Mundo speaks of “peace” with his party, and Nogueras, of Junts, said it was hard for him to decide “whether he bets on Catalonia or on Spain” (ABC).

The decision shifts the chessboard on the left. ABC and 20minutos point out that Podemos and ERC are “very close” to a pact in Catalonia outside the Broad Front, an unprecedented option. Sumar backs Ada Colau and Emilio Delgado to lead the Broad Front and is open to alliances “with anyone who wants to come” (Europa Press), and IU supports Colau (La Vanguardia). Más Madrid proposes Delgado as lead candidate for Madrid (La Vanguardia, El Mundo).

An El Mundo poll, which we have not been able to cross-check with another, gives Feijóo 60% as the future prime minister and puts Abascal almost level with Sánchez and Díaz. Time is running: the coalition registration deadline expires on Oct. 16.

World · HealthPress ✓ 10 outlets · date of the operation not specified

Pope Leo XIV to undergo surgery this week for a lung nodule at a “very early” stage

Pope Leo XIV announced on Sunday that he must undergo surgery for a lung nodule, according to Reuters, NBC, CBS, NPR, PBS, Politico, The Washington Post and USA Today. The Vatican speaks of a nodular lesion, and the Pontiff himself, according to El País and DW, describes it as being at a “very early” stage. The operation will take place “in the coming days” (RTP) or “this week” (NPR), and Infobae reports that it will be at Gemelli hospital in Rome, the center that treated John Paul II and Francis; the Pontiff is 71 years old.

A lung nodule is a small spot on the lung. Many are benign, which is why doctors usually remove them to analyze them. The reports we reviewed do not detail the nature of the nodule or the exact date, and several outlets note that the Pope will remain hospitalized for about a week. Expressions of support are multiplying, according to RTP.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 8 outlets · figures from the Saudi authorities

New attack on Riyadh airport a day after the massacre. The Houthis threaten airlines again

King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh was hit again on Sunday, a day after the attack that left 12 dead and 309 wounded, according to PBS, Europa Press, La Vanguardia and El País. The Saudi civil aviation authority reports one dead and 11 wounded in this second blow (FinancialJuice), and Indian outlets identify the deceased as an Indian national (Livemint, Hindustan Times). Asharq Al-Awsat reports that the Saudi authority speaks of two new attacks on the airports of Riyadh and Dammam. The Houthis claimed the attacks (RTP, RSI) and renewed their warning to airlines and travelers (Reuters).

Kuwait Airways joins the airlines canceling flights (El Mundo), Canada advises against traveling to the airport (Global News) and the EU expressed its solidarity with Riyadh. The Saudi stock exchange fell after the first attack (Reuters) and oil is rising with the attacks, according to Bloomberg. El País warns that the escalation could drag in Trump, and boxer Canelo Álvarez is fighting in Riyadh anyway, according to The Guardian.

MarketsPress ✓ 4 outlets + Friday closing quotes

Stock markets start the week with Riyadh in the background. Wall Street closed higher on Friday and gold rose 1.43%

The IBEX 35 closed on Friday at 19,033.1 points, up 0.55%, and lost 0.27% over the week (Yahoo Finance, Forbes España, OKDiario). In New York, the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points; the Nasdaq, 0.64%, to 27,366.17, and the Dow Jones, 0.83%, to 51,654.95. West Texas crude rose 0.39% to $91.85, and gold 1.43% to $4,216.3 an ounce. The euro is worth $1.1206 according to Friday’s ECB reference rate.

These closes do not reflect this weekend’s attacks in Riyadh, which Bloomberg already links to a rise in crude and a somewhat stronger dollar. This week the Middle East, U.S. inflation and the start of the earnings season will set the tone. For anyone with a variable-rate mortgage, Euribor remains the figure to watch.
