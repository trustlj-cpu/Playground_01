Feijóo demands that Sánchez explain the Moroccan ships in Ceuta and takes the case to the EU and the National Court

The PP leader asks for an urgent appearance in the Permanent Deputation. The King and Queen ended their tour of the autonomous cities in Melilla, 45 days before the Nov. 29 election.

PP president Alberto Núñez Feijóo demanded on Wednesday that Pedro Sánchez appear to explain the entry of Moroccan warships into the waters of Ceuta, according to Europa Press, El País, El Mundo, 20minutos, El Confidencial and La Vanguardia. He asks that it be done in the Permanent Deputation of Congress (El Mundo) and took the matter to the European Union and the National Court (El Mundo, La Vanguardia, Europa Press). El Confidencial reports his accusation that the prime minister “knew and hid it.”

Sánchez had acknowledged on Monday that the ships entered in late July, during the mass arrival of migrants, and had called it a “one-off incident,” as reported by El País, ABC, La Vanguardia and 20minutos. In the sources reviewed we have not seen a Government response to Feijóo’s request. Diario de Cádiz adds a detail that no other outlet reports: that two Navy frigates based at Rota were activated after the ships entered. And El Confidencial says that the judge investigating the storming of Ceuta has been waiting a month for “land, sea and air” images of the border.

The crisis also shows in the electoral lists. Sánchez placed Margarita Robles as number two of the PSOE ticket for Madrid, ahead of Félix Bolaños (El Confidencial, ABC, La Razón, LaSexta, 20minutos); El Mundo links the decision to the Ceuta crisis, but that is its reading, not a confirmed fact. Pollster Narciso Michavila, of GAD3, says in ABC that on Ceuta voters “do not vote out of Spanish nationalism but because of immigration.”

Today it is worth watching whether Sánchez agrees to appear, whether Rabat reacts officially —we have not seen any Moroccan statement in the outlets reviewed— and whether the judge finally receives the images. With the election 45 days away, Ceuta remains a campaign issue.

The King, in Melilla: “We are prepared to defend our values and our freedoms”

Felipe VI and Letizia arrived in Melilla on Wednesday after spending the night in Ceuta, and visited the autonomous city for the first time, according to La Vanguardia, Onda Cero and El Mundo; with it they complete the autonomous map, notes RTVE. In his speech, the King said that Melilla “is Spain” and that, together with Ceuta, it deserves to feel the closeness of Spaniards more often (Europa Press); he recalled that “the EU begins” there (RTVE) and stated that they are “prepared to defend our values and freedoms” (El País, ABC, El Mundo). Melilla’s president, Juan José Imbroda, replied: “Spain and Europe begin here” (ABC).

On Morocco, the King asked to “look to a relationship of good neighborliness” (El Confidencial). 20minutos also attributes a warning to him: “Let no one mistake our convictions for weakness.” On Tuesday, in Ceuta, he had already said that sovereignty and territorial integrity “are not negotiable: they are beyond dispute” (El País). 20minutos summarizes how the Moroccan press treated the trip, with headlines such as “Felipe VI in occupied Ceuta”; we have not seen an official response from Rabat.

In Melilla he was received with Spanish flags and the chant “we shall not be moved” (El País, La Vanguardia). Today it is worth watching whether the trip has a diplomatic echo and how much it weighs in the campaign.

U.S. proposes charging Spanish ships $200,000 per voyage and restricting Spanish exports

The United States proposed on Wednesday to impose $200,000 per voyage on Spanish ships that use its ports and to restrict exports of several Spanish products, according to RTVE, ABC, 20minutos, El Mundo, Expansión, La Vanguardia, Europa Press and Cinco Días. Expansión names olive oil among the products. Washington accuses Spain of harming its maritime trade (Europa Press), and RTVE links the standoff to Spain’s ban on trade with Israel. Europa Press expresses the figure as 180,000 euros.

The outlets do not label the measure the same way: tariff (Expansión, 20minutos), fine (ABC), sanction (El Mundo, La Vanguardia) and fee (Europa Press). None reports it as applied: it is a proposal. The Spanish Government argues that tariffs and the other restrictions fall under the EU’s remit (20minutos). The sources reviewed do not say when it would be decided or what procedure follows.

Madrid teachers begin an open-ended strike and five are detained at the Complutense

Madrid’s public-school teachers began a strike on Wednesday in defense of public education, with a rally at Sol and a march from Atocha, according to 20minutos, Europa Press, El País and Última Hora. The Community put turnout at 44.6% (El Mundo); 20minutos speaks of half the teaching staff. El Mundo describes a standoff “sine die” and quotes a line that sums up the mood: “Stopping for one day is useless. We will hold out as long as it takes.” The government of Isabel Díaz Ayuso called the strike “incomprehensible” (El País) and condemned the promotion of a “political” strike (Europa Press).

The day got complicated at the Complutense University: El País reports five people detained and six injured in a police charge. The Government delegate in Madrid apologized and called for an urgent investigation (20minutos, Libertad Digital), and El Mundo adds that the riot police who charged say they did not receive “clear guidelines” from the Delegation. Today it is worth watching whether the strike continues, whether there is negotiation with the Community and what comes of the investigation.

U.S. inflation rises to 3.7% and a sell-off in chips and AI stocks cools the markets

Consumer prices (CPI) in the United States accelerated in September because of more expensive gasoline, with firmer core inflation, according to Reuters; Expansión and Yahoo put inflation at 3.7%. The figure revived bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike. Daly, of the Fed, told Reuters that interest rates will probably have to go up, but without urgency, while Bowman argued for waiting and seeing before acting (Reuters). Stock futures fell on fears of that hike (Yahoo, Investing.com) and the dollar moved away from its 18-month high (Reuters).

The S&P 500 ended its longest calm streak since 2020 as the AI trade collapsed (Bloomberg). Bank of America slipped after a $1.2 billion fine (Reuters, Bloomberg TV) and chips fell (Bloomberg TV, FT). CoreWeave plunged on doubts about its debt (Investopedia, Globe and Mail, Business Insider). Early in the morning, Reuters spoke of selling in Asia and the FT of a deepening tech decline.

In Madrid, the IBEX 35 was up 0.74% midsession and holding 19,300 points (ABC), although Radio Intereconomía spoke of a standstill at 19,200. We could not confirm the close with two sources, so the IBEX box appears with no figure, as does the euro’s. FinancialJuice logged Brent at $106.06 and WTI at $95.09 at Wednesday’s close, but that is a single source. The IEA warns that the diesel shortage could last well into 2027 (Reuters, Mundo Petróleo).
