Sánchez admits Moroccan warships entered Ceuta’s waters in the July crisis, on the same day the Oct. 12 parade greeted him with boos

He called it an “isolated incident” and did not explain why they entered. The King travels to Ceuta and Melilla this Tuesday, some 47 days before the Nov. 29 election.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez acknowledged on Monday that Moroccan warships entered Ceuta’s waters in late July, coinciding with the mass arrival of migrants in the autonomous city, according to El País, ABC, La Vanguardia, 20minutos and The Straits Times. He called it an “isolated incident,” said it was resolved through contact “at the highest level between the navies” (El País) and stated that “between two neighboring countries that kind of isolated incident happens” (20minutos). He did not explain why the ships entered and said he was pleased that normality is returning to the city (ABC). The Spanish Navy deployed frigates in July and August, according to elDiario.es.

The news, which El País had reported first, reached the National Day parade in Madrid, presided over by the King and Queen with more than 4,000 military personnel according to Europa Press. The event began with a parachute jump carrying a Spanish flag of 24 square meters (20minutos, La Vanguardia), and the presence of Ceuta and of the early election set the mood. Sánchez was received with whistles, boos and insults, according to Europa Press, El País, ABC, La Vanguardia and El Confidencial, and Ayuso said she hoped it would be the president’s “last parade” (20minutos, Europa Press). Santiago Abascal did not attend the events in Madrid because Ceuta “is still invaded” (ABC) and marked Oct. 12 in the city alongside Jordan Bardella of the French party National Rally (La Vanguardia).

The scale of the July crisis is reported with different figures. ABC speaks of more than 80,000 migrants between July 30 and 31; El Economista, of Mexico, of more than 72,000. On Brussels there are two accounts: El País maintains that the EU is closing ranks with Spain and sending Morocco the message that the autonomous cities are part of the Union, and El Mundo asserts that “concern” is growing over the lack of legislative measures, because the Government did not approve the decree it was preparing and will no longer do so in the middle of the pre-campaign. The latter is reported by a single outlet.

The King travels to Ceuta and Melilla this Tuesday, a visit that El País calls “historic” and that France 24 describes as “highly sensitive” for the relationship with Morocco; Libération speaks of tensions still alive in Ceuta. PP and Vox took the protest over the “invasion” of Ceuta to the streets of Barcelona (La Vanguardia, El Mundo), and the Andalusian president, Juanma Moreno, asked in Brussels for protection of this “southern external border of the Union” (El Mundo).

Today it is worth watching whether the Government or the Royal Household give further explanations about the ships, whether Rabat reacts —in the sources reviewed we have not seen an official Moroccan response— and whether the Executive approves measures before the campaign starts. With the Nov. 29 vote some 47 days away, Ceuta is already an electoral issue.

Spain · PoliticsPress ✓ 6 outlets · Podemos has not yet said whether it joins

The Broad Front proposes Ada Colau as its candidate for Prime Minister and leaves the door open to Podemos, which struck a deal with ERC

IU, Comuns, Más Madrid and Movimiento Sumar, the four parties of the Broad Front, proposed on Monday the former mayor of Barcelona, Ada Colau, as their candidate for Prime Minister in the Nov. 29 election, according to Europa Press, El País, La Vanguardia, 20minutos, El Mundo and El Confidencial. The statement does not clarify in which electoral district she will head the list (El País) and keeps the invitation to Podemos to join a unity list (La Vanguardia, El Confidencial).

The decision comes after the pact between Gabriel Rufián, of ERC, and Irene Montero, of Podemos (El País). The Comuns, Colau’s party, expressed their “deep disappointment” with Rufián and accuse him of dividing the left (El Mundo, Europa Press). El Mundo adds that Rufián pressed for Montero to lead the Broad Front; no other outlet reports this. Podemos opened its primaries process on Friday (20minutos). Before the statement, the Comuns had already confirmed that they will run with the Broad Front (El Mundo), and La Vanguardia reports that the parties are negotiating against the clock a formula to bring in Podemos.

Time is short: the coalition registration deadline expires on Oct. 16. If Podemos does not join, it will run with ERC in Catalonia, an unprecedented option that ABC and 20minutos already pointed to on Sunday. Today it is worth watching whether it accepts joining the unity list or runs on its own.

Spain · PoliticsPress ✓ 7 outlets · they disagree on who keeps the vote

The Supreme Court upholds the suspension of the vote of those naturalized under the grandchildren law for Nov. 29

The Supreme Court on Monday upheld the suspension of the vote of those naturalized under the “grandchildren law” for the Nov. 29 election, according to El País, El Mundo, ABC, La Vanguardia, 20minutos, Europa Press and Expansión. The Administrative Chamber rejected the State Attorney’s Office appeal by five votes to one (El Mundo). El País puts the number of people affected at 163,020 and reports the dissenting opinion of magistrate Alicia Millán, who held that the right to vote should prevail. The outlets do not agree on who keeps the vote: La Vanguardia says those registered before Aug. 1, and Clarín only those who prove they are descendants of exiles.

The Government regretted the decision —“it seems that the right to vote is not so relevant to some”— and is studying whether it can appeal to Spain’s Constitutional Court (20minutos, Europa Press). Feijóo welcomed the Supreme Court setting a “limit on the irresponsibility” of the Executive (Europa Press). The strongest effect is felt in Argentina, the country with the most applications (Clarín, La Nación).

World · HealthPress ✓ 8 outlets · it has not been said whether the nodule is malignant

Pope Leo XIV enters the Gemelli this Tuesday to have a lung nodule removed and cancels his audiences until Oct. 20

Pope Leo XIV will enter Rome’s Gemelli hospital this Tuesday to undergo surgery for a lung nodule, according to Al Jazeera, GMA, La Nación, Clarín and ANSA. The Vatican expects about a week of recovery and has not said whether the nodule is malignant (Livemint). The Pontiff is 71 years old (Al Jazeera) and kept his usual schedule on Monday (The Straits Times, Al Jazeera).

The Vatican canceled all audiences until Oct. 20 (ANSA, G1). For now, the trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru that begins on Nov. 6 remains on (Clarín, La Nación), and Argentine president Javier Milei said his Government is willing to “make the necessary adjustments” to the dates. What is missing is the medical bulletin after the operation.

The IBEX 35 rises 1.33% in the National Day session and Wall Street closes slightly lower

The IBEX 35 closed on Monday, with the exchange open despite the National Day, at 19,285.8 points, up 1.33%, according to Yahoo Finance; Valencia Plaza and El Confidencial report the same 1.33% gain and put the index near 19,300. We have not been able to confirm Monday’s Dow Jones, crude and gold with two sources; on Friday the Dow closed at 51,654.95 points, West Texas at $91.85 and gold at $4,216.3 an ounce.
