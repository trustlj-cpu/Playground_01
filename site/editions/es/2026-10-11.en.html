Spain · PoliticsPress ✓ 7 outlets · “hoaxes” and “afraid of losing” are Sánchez’s words

Sánchez opens the pre-campaign on housing: he vows to “reinforce and maintain” the decrees and accuses the PP and Vox of hoaxes about the postal vote

In Dos Hermanas, the prime minister appealed for “social mobilization” in the name of Maricarmen, a symbol of the housing protests. The PP answers with its doubts about the postal vote. There are 49 days left until the vote.

Pedro Sánchez opened the pre-campaign for the Nov. 29 general election on Saturday with a rally in Dos Hermanas (Seville), his stronghold, according to ABC. He promised to “reinforce and maintain” the Government’s housing decrees (Expansión, El Periódico) and pledged that housing policy would “not have an expiration date,” according to Última Hora. Congress had rejected two housing decrees eight days earlier, the first by 172 votes in favor and 178 against, and the second by 166 to 184, with the PP, Vox and Junts voting against, according to EFE. Housing was the centerpiece of the event, according to El País and La Vanguardia.

The prime minister also accused the PP and Vox of spreading hoaxes about the postal vote: “They are afraid of losing” (20minutos). El Confidencial sums up that he is trying in this way to keep his own side from losing heart, and El País attributes to him the phrase “these are the ones who think the fewer people vote, the better.” The PP, for its part, questions that way of voting: Isabel Díaz Ayuso said she fears “foul play” because in 2023 some people did not receive their ballots (El Confidencial, 20minutos), and ABC reports that Alberto Núñez Feijóo is working on a plan called “SSL” to prevent rigging. In the reports we reviewed there is no evidence of fraud: it is a political dispute, not a verified fact.

The event had a name at its center, Maricarmen. El Mundo reports that Sánchez asked that the “social mobilization” in her name “overflow” at the polls in favor of “the progressive majorities,” and La Vanguardia, that he hoisted her “fight for housing” as a banner. El Periódico is following live the reactions to her death, the housing protest camp, the protests and the general strike. We do not detail the circumstances of her death because we have not been able to verify them.

On the substance of the matter, Expansión is already publishing guides for tenants and landlords on the “new regulation” and the “new decree,” with sections on automatic lease renewal and taxation. ABC warns that the decrees and inflation are leading the market “into the perfect storm.” We have not been able to confirm which text is in force today or how the Government would reinforce it, and that is the question Sánchez has not yet answered with figures.

The laSexta barometer indicates that 74.5% of respondents view the early election on Nov. 29 positively, and that housing, the economy and immigration are the three issues that weigh most on the vote. Today, Sunday, and tomorrow, Monday, National Day (Fiesta Nacional), it is worth watching whether the Government spells out the reinforcement of the decrees, whether the PP provides evidence for its suspicions about the postal vote and whether the protests at the camp grow or cool. On Oct. 16 the deadline to register coalitions expires.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 7 outlets · toll from the Saudi authorities

A Houthi attack on Riyadh airport leaves 12 dead and 309 wounded. Trump is weighing joining the offensive against the Houthis

The Saudi civil aviation authority reported that Saturday’s attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh caused at least 12 deaths and 309 injuries, according to AP, NPR, CBS, Al Jazeera and RTVE. The Saudi-led coalition attributed the strike to the Houthis, the rebels of Yemen, and called it a war crime (FinancialJuice). Airport operations were suspended.

U.S. President Donald Trump said it is “something terrible” and, according to NPR and Al Jazeera, is weighing joining the Saudi attacks on the Houthis; no decision has yet been announced. UN Secretary-General António Guterres condemned the assault, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the U.S. denounces it firmly. Among this week’s dead there is, according to Rubio, one American.

The strike adds to the attacks of Oct. 6 and 7 on the airports of Riyadh and Abha, which left three dead and 36 wounded, according to Al Jazeera. Meanwhile, U.S. Central Command said it had disabled a cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman that, by its account, tried to slip past the naval blockade of Iran. Reuters reported that several airlines canceled flights and that the organizers of large conferences in the country say they will go ahead. For Spain, the risk is oil and flights to the Gulf.

Sport · LaLigaPress ✓ 8 outlets · sanction still undecided

Madrid beat Villarreal with Vinicius sent off for pulling a rival’s hair. Mourinho does not face the press

Real Madrid beat Villarreal on matchday eight with a goal from Diomandé, scoring for the first time, and with a sending-off that took over everything: Vinicius saw red for grabbing Saliba by the hair, and had a goal disallowed, according to El País, El Mundo, La Vanguardia, ABC and L'Équipe. Mourinho did not speak to the media after the match (Cadena SER, EFE), and Tchouaméni said: “I don’t understand what is going on with the referees” (El País). Vinicius apologized and, according to L'Équipe, later complained on social media.

Madrid are six points behind Barcelona, who beat Getafe with a goal from Koundé and have eight wins in eight matches (El País, El Mundo). Outlets such as El Mundo and El Desmarque note that Vinicius could miss the Clásico, scheduled in about two weeks; the sanction still depends on the Competition Committee and has not been decided.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 6 outlets · the Kremlin and Kyiv, no independent source

Trump calls for “a new president” for Ukraine. Zelensky: gifts to Putin will bring “more terror”

Donald Trump said on Saturday that “the time has come for Ukraine to have a new president” and demanded that Volodymyr Zelensky stop attacking Russian refineries, according to Politico, USA Today, El Mundo and La Vanguardia. It comes a day after he announced, together with Vladimir Putin, a supply of 300,000 tons of Russian diesel. Zelensky replied that gifts to Putin “will not bring peace, but more terror” (La Vanguardia). Moscow applauded the line, according to Corriere della Sera.

The reaction in the West was one of support for Kyiv: the United Kingdom expressed its “full solidarity” with Zelensky (Straits Times, Le Monde), Germany is maintaining its sanctions on Russia (Reuters) and, according to The Hill, Republican lawmakers reject the diesel deal and Trump’s remarks. A Russian attack on Zaporizhzhia killed seven people, according to the governor (Reuters). For Spain, which imports energy, the risk is more expensive diesel if supply tightens.

MarketsPress ✓ 4 outlets + Friday closing quotes

Wall Street closed higher on Friday and gold rose 1.43%. Stock markets are closed until Monday

The IBEX 35 closed on Friday at 19,033.1 points, up 0.55% (Expansión, TradingView). In New York, the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.54 points; the Nasdaq, 0.64%, to 27,366.17, and the Dow Jones, 0.83%, to 51,654.95, according to market closes. The U.S. ten-year bond closed at 5.244%, up 1.3 basis points; West Texas crude rose 0.39% to $91.85, and gold 1.43% to $4,216.3 an ounce.

The euro ended at $1.1206, down 0.09%. There is no session today, Sunday: the next is Monday. This week the Riyadh attack, which could move crude, U.S. inflation data and the start of the earnings season will set the tone. The newspaper Demócrata warns that the rise in bond yields threatens mortgages, companies and budgets. For anyone with a variable-rate mortgage, Euribor remains the figure to watch.
