Supreme Court applies the amnesty to Junqueras, Romeva, Turull and Bassa after the Constitutional Court’s order

The Criminal Chamber extinguishes the liability for embezzlement of the four leaders of the procés and criticizes the Constitutional Court’s interference.

The Supreme Court applied the amnesty law on Tuesday, October 20, to Oriol Junqueras, Raül Romeva, Jordi Turull and Dolors Bassa, the procés leaders convicted of embezzlement, according to Europa Press and El Mundo. The Criminal Chamber issued an order declaring the criminal liability arising from the judgment extinguished and agreeing to erase their criminal records, according to eldiario.es. The court took on the operative part of the judgment in which the Constitutional Court granted the amparo appeal to Bassa and annulled the Supreme Court orders that had denied her the amnesty, according to El País and eldiario.es. A majority of seven Constitutional Court justices held that the Supreme Court had interpreted the law’s exceptions too broadly, and the ruling was drafted by Cándido Conde-Pumpido, according to eldiario.es. Junqueras was barred from office until 2031 and the other three until 2030, according to that same report. All four had seen their appeals for reconsideration rejected by the Supreme Court in 2024, according to the reports of that year, which cited personal financial gain as the reason for excluding embezzlement from the amnesty.

ABC headlined that the Supreme Court got ahead of the Constitutional Court and reported criticism of Pumpido’s overreach, while eldiario.es reported that the order lashes out at the Constitutional Court’s “interference.” El País published that the Supreme Court resisted to the end and, in another report, that the Constitutional Court is starting to apply the amnesty to the last six procés leaders still pending. Junqueras welcomed the decision in these words: “At last justice has been done where there was only injustice,” according to Europa Press. La Vanguardia reported that the Government welcomed the measure and called it “a cause for celebration.” El Mundo presented the order as the direct consequence of the Constitutional Court’s instruction, which obliged the Supreme Court to issue a new ruling on Bassa and, by extension, on the other three convicted leaders.

Feijóo confirms he is negotiating a Senate formula with Vox: “It would be a mistake to hand sanchismo control”

Alberto Núñez Feijóo admitted on Tuesday, October 20, that the PP is seeking a “formula” with Vox in the Senate because “it would be a mistake to hand sanchismo” control of it, direct or indirect, according to Europa Press and El Mundo. With the deadline for forming coalitions already closed, the options cited by El Español and Infobae are for Vox to forgo running in some constituencies or for the PP to include Vox candidates as independents on its lists. ABC reported that the PP proposes that Vox not run in every constituency in exchange for guaranteeing it regional senators, and El Mundo wrote that Vox is open to facilitating a majority if the PP cedes it senators. The context is the joint PSOE and Frente Amplio candidacy for the Senate, according to El Español. An El Mundo poll from October 19 puts the PP and Vox 6 seats short of full control of the chamber against that left-wing front. eldiario.es links the negotiation to the aim of a three-fifths majority, which would give the right weight in appointing Constitutional Court justices. According to El Español and eldiario.es, the two parties have until October 26 to close the deal before the filing of candidacies expires.

Dana Adam leaves four dead and Sánchez promises to declare a seriously affected zone after visiting Oliva

Dana Adam left four dead: La Vanguardia speaks of three in Valencia and one in Andalusia, while other reports attributed to the Generalitat cite two dead in Oliva, one in Sagunto and another in Cabrera de Mar (Barcelona). El País reported that a farmer was found dead in the Pego-Oliva marsh. RTVE counted more than 2,000 evacuated and almost 500 rescued by the Guardia Civil in the Valencian Community, and El Diario Vasco spoke of more than 1,700 incidents. Europa Press and El Mundo reported that on Tuesday five provinces were still on alert, with Barcelona and Girona on orange for rain, according to the Aemet.

Pedro Sánchez visited Oliva on Tuesday with the president of the Generalitat, Juanfran Pérez Llorca, according to La Vanguardia and El Mundo. He arrived by helicopter, met for about an hour at the forward command post and, according to his team, did not tour the affected areas, according to Libertad Digital and El Diario Alerta, which also report boos from a group of residents. He announced that the next Council of Ministers will approve the declaration of a zone seriously affected by a civil protection emergency and revived the proposal of a “national agreement on the climate emergency,” according to El Mundo. The Generalitat will approve aid, according to those same reports.

Government Delegation to review the police charges against striking teachers as Ayuso rejects 328 more schools and 13,600 more teachers

Madrid teachers on indefinite strike marched on Tuesday, October 20, dressed in black, with green T-shirts underneath, from the Government Delegation to the regional Education Department to reject the previous day’s police charges, according to El País and Infobae. Police charged on Monday in front of the Chamber of Commerce, where the demonstrators were waiting for Isabel Díaz Ayuso, according to Infobae. The strike committee’s spokesman, Andrea Ordóñez, called the charges disproportionate and spoke of police abuse. 20minutos reported that participation is wearing down on the fifth day of the strike.

The Government Delegation in Madrid will investigate the charges, according to El País; Europa Press qualifies that it argues the police action should be reviewed when it is not proportionate and timely, and 20minutos that it will open an investigation if there is a lack of proportionality. Ayuso criticized on X that the Government always blames the police, according to Infobae, and considered the strike’s demands unachievable, among them 328 new schools and 13,600 more teachers, according to ABC. An earlier investigation had already been announced after the charges of October 14 at the Complutense University, according to El Salto and Euronews.

A Google Cloud power failure in London leaves Revolut without card payments and hits other European banks

Revolut suffered an outage on Tuesday, October 20, that stopped its customers from paying by card, making transfers and accessing the app, according to El País, El Mundo and ABC. El Mundo attributed the failure to Google Cloud in Europe, and Europa Press added that the incident also dragged on other European banks. La Vanguardia placed the problems in Spain, the United Kingdom and France. Google explained, according to El Español and eldiario.es, that an electrical incident caused a power cut in one zone of its infrastructure in London.

Revolut said its customers’ money and data were safe, according to El Español, and a spokesperson spoke of a technical incident linked to the Google Cloud outage. Over the course of the afternoon the service gradually returned to normal and Google considered the problem mitigated, according to the same outlets. Neither Google nor Revolut had published the number of customers affected or a full technical explanation.
