U.S. State Department: “Talks on safeguards for Korea’s nuclear sub are the IAEA’s call”… rebuts China’s demand for ‘consensus of all members’

On the 28th the United States rebutted China’s claim that talks on nuclear-material safeguards needed for Korea’s adoption of nuclear-powered submarines first require the consensus of all member states of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA).

The U.S. State Department said that talks on nuclear-material safeguards needed for Korea’s adoption of nuclear-powered submarines (nuclear subs) are a matter for the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA). In a reply to written questions, a State Department spokesperson said the IAEA has established authority to negotiate member states’ implementation of safeguards agreements, including the Article 14 arrangement. An Article 14 arrangement is an agreement under which a country with a comprehensive safeguards agreement exempts nuclear material used for non-weapon military purposes, such as nuclear sub propulsion, from routine safeguards and, in exchange, separately settles with the IAEA how it will be confirmed that the material is not diverted to nuclear weapons. The reply draws a line against China’s claim that these talks first need the agreement of all IAEA member states.

At an IAEA Board of Governors meeting on the 18th, China claimed that U.S.-Korea nuclear sub cooperation involves transferring nuclear material for non-peaceful purposes, and demanded that discussion and agreement at the Board and among all member states come first, rather than leaving it to technical consultations between the Secretariat and the parties concerned. The Korean government told the IAEA in June that it intends to conclude an Article 14 arrangement, and in September formally notified it of the start of consultations. At this Board meeting the IAEA issued its first report on Korea’s nuclear sub, confirmed that reporting obligations were being met, and expressed satisfaction with safeguards cooperation. The government explained its plan to use low-enriched uranium enriched to 20% or less in the nuclear sub, and its position is that fuel procurement requires consultation with the United States. A Ministry of Foreign Affairs official said the parties to the technical consultations are the country seeking to adopt the nuclear sub and the IAEA Secretariat, and that Korea will also communicate transparently with member states. There is no set deadline for the consultations.

Despite all-out effort to block ‘AI cheating,’ 2 electronic devices caught at Pusan National University essay exam and 3 at national health-care professional exams

On the 28th, Pusan National University checked belongings left in the hallway outside the exam rooms at its 2027 academic year early-admission essay exam and found electronic devices belonging to 2 examinees. Separately, 2 examinees were caught holding paper materials such as past exam questions during the test. Mobile phones and smart glasses were barred for 10,291 applicants, who went through metal-detector checks, wore disposable gowns, and had hallway belongings scanned with a radio-wave detector. The people caught will be dealt with under the rules by the Admissions Fairness Management Committee, and the finding of an electronic device alone does not confirm cheating with AI. Kyungpook National University, where 11,301 applied for 550 places in its essay exam, put every examinee through a metal-detector check and inspected all glasses and analog watches, and did not disclose whether anyone was caught. At the national health-care professional exams held the same day at 23 sites across the country, with about 4,000 sitting, 1 tablet PC and 2 wireless earbuds, items that must be submitted in advance, turned up in bags and elsewhere. The Ministry of Health and Welfare decided to invalidate the exams of the 3, and no report confirmed that devices were actually used during the exams.

KOSPI 47.6% below year high… investors hold candlelight rally at Seoul Station, demand temporary ban on short selling

The Korea Investors Federation held an emergency candlelight rally at the Seoul Station plaza on the 28th. Attendance was 20 by police’s unofficial estimate. Jeong Ui-jeong, who heads the group, demanded that the government temporarily ban short selling, create a 100 trillion won market stabilization fund, tighten monitoring of illegal short selling and set up an investor protection agency, and argued that the government should apologize and present measures over the plunge in leveraged ETFs that track Samsung Electronics and SK hynix returns at twice the rate. Short selling is a trade in which an investor borrows shares and sells them first, betting the price will fall, then buys them back cheaper to return them and profit from the difference.

The backdrop is a falling market. The KOSPI closed on the 27th at 4,915.68, 47.6% below its June 19 year high of 9,385.59. It rose 4.34% on the 27th, but sell sidecars were triggered two days in a row, on the 20th and the 23rd. Jeong claimed that of close to 15 million individual investors only about 10% are in profit, and that the amount closed out this year through forced liquidation was 5.4 trillion won. Individual investors’ net purchases of KOSPI shares this year through the 27th total 96.447 trillion won. It is not known whether the government will accept a temporary ban on short selling.

National Intelligence Service director nominee Kang Geum-sil rebuts ‘pro-China’ attacks for the first time: “China special adviser post was an unpaid goodwill honorary title under the Park Geun-hye government”

National Intelligence Service director nominee Kang Geum-sil issued a statement on the 28th that for the first time rebutted the opposition’s ‘pro-China’ attacks over a record of serving as special adviser to a Chinese local government. Kang acknowledged having been appointed to an unpaid honorary position as a goodwill gesture amid improving Korea-China relations at the time, but said the ‘economic adviser management measures’ cited in some reports were news to Kang. The nominee explained that the appointments came in July 2013 and September 2014, right after a major Chinese publisher released an autobiography of President Park Geun-hye (March 2013) and the Chinese Foreign Ministry gave a positive assessment of Korea-China relations in the first year of the Park government (February 2014).

The confirmation hearing request states that the nominee was appointed special adviser to the People’s Government of Qiqihar, Heilongjiang Province in July 2013 and special economic adviser to the Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture in September 2014. At issue is a report that Yanbian’s economic adviser management measures include a requirement to uphold the line and policies of the Chinese Communist Party. Kang denied this and urged restraint, saying baseless allegations could harm national security. Whether this rule was actually applied to Kang has not been independently confirmed. The post of National Intelligence Service director requires going through a National Assembly confirmation hearing.

On talk of another Kim Jong Un trip to China, National Intelligence Service says it is “watching various developments”… Cheong Wa Dae denies a Korea-China summit remark

The National Intelligence Service said on the 28th that it is “watching various developments” when asked about the possibility of another trip to China by State Affairs Commission Chairman Kim Jong Un. It was an answer to a question, and the service did not confirm any specific trip plan or timing. No clear unusual movement pointing to a trip has been confirmed in the border region either. The talk began with a report that President Xi Jinping made, at the Korea-China summit in Shenzhen on the 18th, a remark to President Lee Jae-myung that could be read as signaling a possible trip to China by North Korea’s top leadership. Cheong Wa Dae and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs said there was no remark to the effect reported at the recent Korea-China summit, and that they are closely watching developments in North Korea–China relations.

Chairman Kim crossed the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge by vehicle on the 24th of last month, visited Dandong in Liaoning Province and returned home the same day. The visit came ahead of China’s anniversary of entering the 6·25 Korean War, and the Korean Central News Agency reported that the visit reaffirmed the blood-forged ties between North Korea and China. Cheong Wa Dae and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs said they hope North Korea–China exchanges will proceed in a direction that helps peaceful coexistence on the Korean Peninsula, and will keep up close communication between Korea and China. There is speculation that North Korea and China may be coordinating positions ahead of a resumption of North Korea–U.S. dialogue, but whether a trip is actually being pursued has not been confirmed.
