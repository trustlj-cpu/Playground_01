Jio Platforms sets the terms for India's biggest IPO: up to ₹30,213 crore, about $3.2 billion, with bidding open from 21 October

Mukesh Ambani's telecom and digital arm filed its final prospectus on Wednesday and fixed a price band. The Wall Street Journal and Firstpost say it is aiming for a valuation near $108 billion; the band itself is not in the reports we reviewed.

Jio Platforms, the telecom and digital arm of Reliance Industries, filed its final prospectus on Wednesday 14 October, Reuters and The Hindu BusinessLine report. The issue opens for subscription on Wednesday 21 October and closes on Friday 23 October, and anchor investors bid on Monday 19 October (BusinessLine). Reuters, CNBC and Forbes put the amount at up to $3.2 billion. AajTak gives ₹30,213 crore (about 302 billion rupees), which matches at roughly ₹95 to the dollar. Dainik Bhaskar says about 27 crore (270 million) shares are on offer to raise about ₹30,000 crore, and an AajTak headline mentions a category for employees and Reliance shareholders. Reuters calls it a record for India.

Reuters and TradingView report that the price band was set by Wednesday night, and Reuters adds that Reliance Industries shares rose on the news. No report we reviewed gives the band, so we give no per-share price. The Wall Street Journal and Firstpost say Jio is seeking a valuation of about $108 billion, roughly ₹10.3 lakh crore at the same exchange rate. CNBC describes the company as backed by Meta and Google. AajTak reports that the filing was made in Ahmedabad and that Akash Ambani is leading the issue.

On the use of the money, The Hindu BusinessLine quotes Ambani as saying almost all the proceeds will go to make Jio Platforms net debt-free, with later expansion paid from internal cash flow. That is one outlet and a statement of intent, not a result. Reuters quotes analysts who expect a listing pop, meaning a first-day price above the issue price; that is a forecast. Bloomberg says the early global backers, among them Meta, Alphabet and KKR, have seen modest returns in dollar terms because the rupee has weakened for years (single outlet). Moneycontrol and AajTak carry Akash Ambani's remark that Jio will move to 6G through software upgrades rather than new hardware.

What the reports leave out is as useful as what they include: the price band, the lot size, the listing date, and the split between new shares and shares sold by existing holders. BusinessLine's remark on debt suggests mostly new shares, but we have no prospectus figures. A headline we saw says the issue has drawn bids above $20 billion; we saw no detail, so we do not rely on it. A Hindi headline we saw asks whether Jio recharges could rise by up to 12%; it is a question, not a confirmed plan, and we give it no weight.

Supreme Court voids Yes Bank's ₹8,415-crore AT1 bond write-off; the share falls about 10%

The Supreme Court on Thursday upheld a Bombay High Court order that quashed Yes Bank's write-off of ₹8,415 crore (about 84 billion rupees, $880 million) of AT1 bonds. Reuters says the top court ruled the write-off illegal; LiveLawBiz says it refused to interfere with the High Court order. NDTV Profit calls it a big win for bondholders. CNBC TV18 reports the bank's share fell by about 10%, and The Economic Times says Yes Bank is examining the ruling. The dollar figure matches the rupee amount at about ₹95 to the dollar. The Economic Times says the court upheld the High Court ruling that quashed the write-off, and NDTV Profit says it set the write-off aside; both descriptions point the same way.

None of the reports we reviewed says whether, how or when bondholders will be paid, or what the bank must now set aside. Live Law's headline quotes the court as saying the Reserve Bank of India was a mere bystander while the bank carried on its business clandestinely; we have that from one outlet and do not read more into it. The ruling is a few hours old in most of these reports, and the bank has not published a response beyond saying it is studying the judgment.

Business · BankingPress ✓ 7 outlets · total price differs by outlet

Details of the IDBI Bank sale emerge: Fairfax to buy 60.72% for about ₹53,000 crore; the share falls 7%

The Union Cabinet approved on Wednesday the sale of the government's controlling stake in IDBI Bank to Fairfax Financial Holdings of Canada. Telegraph India and AajTak give the stake as 60.72%. The price is about ₹53,000 crore, or roughly $5.5 billion, according to The Hindu BusinessLine, Telegraph India and CNBC, while Hindustan Times puts it at ₹58,000 crore in its headline; none of the reports we saw explains the gap. Bloomberg calls it a deal of nearly $6 billion. On Thursday IDBI Bank shares fell 7% to 7.5% (CNBC, BusinessLine).

BusinessLine says Fairfax will pay ₹81 a share, above the ₹75.99 fair value set by two independent valuers; that figure comes from one outlet. The Economic Times reports that Fairfax plans to fold Digit and IIFL into the bank, a plan Financial Post also carried on Wednesday. BusinessLine, citing Finance Ministry sources, says the restructuring changes nothing in the rules for the bank or in its customer-facing duties, and that it will keep running government financial-inclusion, social-security and pension schemes. Bloomberg notes the sale gives the Centre some fiscal room while fuel prices rise and foreign inflows slow, and separately says it shows appetite for Indian financial assets and could jump-start banking reform. Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav attacked the sale, and Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman told the opposition not to shed crocodile tears (AajTak).

Politics · CourtsPress ✓ 8 outlets · crowd and police numbers from one outlet

Supreme Court issues notice to the Election Commission on Rahul Gandhi's plea and warns that no one can browbeat the judiciary; CJP marches in Delhi today

The Supreme Court on Thursday issued notice to the Election Commission of India and Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar on a plea by Rahul Gandhi and other INDIA bloc leaders. It challenges the SIR and voter deletions and seeks an inquiry into alleged irregularities (Bar and Bench, Hindustan Times). Hindustan Times adds that the plea followed the Chief Justice's refusal to meet Gandhi and Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge. The bench criticised a protest march to the court, saying nobody can browbeat the judiciary and that it does not sit in an ivory tower (Mint, Hindustan Times). Separately, the court issued notice to the Centre on the denotification of Jantar Mantar as a protest site (Live Law and two other outlets).

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) march at Connaught Place falls today. NDMC has closed Central Park to the public on 16 and 17 October for administrative reasons (Mint), and a court declined to restrain founder Abhijeet Dipke from holding the protest, while Delhi Police told the High Court it would take all measures to control it (three outlets for each). Hindustan Times reports more than 6,000 police on duty and an expected 50,000 protesters; that is one outlet's figure. The BJP calls the opposition's conduct anarchic (Times of India, The Hindu), and the opposition says the Commission has been biased (The Hindu, Hindustan Times).

Markets · WorldPress ✓ 8 outlets · Nifty close from market data; US figures are intraday

Nifty closes 1.66% higher at 22,997 while Asian shares fall on hot US prices and AI-debt fears; the IEA widens its oil release

The Nifty 50 closed Thursday at 22,997.35, up 1.66%, extending a three-day rally, while other Asian markets fell (The Hindu BusinessLine). Moneycontrol and The Times of India had the Sensex up more than 1,000 points during the day. Moneycontrol credits early buying in IT shares; BusinessLine points to a stronger rupee, which it says gained 31 paise to 94.86 against the dollar in early trade. Reuters reports that Asian markets sold off as AI-linked trades crumbled after hot US inflation data, and that the dollar eased from an 18-month high as Fed officials tempered bets on rate hikes. US consumer prices rose faster in September on costlier gasoline, with core inflation firmer, and producer prices posted their biggest rise in 4½ years (Reuters). The Fed's Daly told Reuters that rate hikes are likely needed, though not urgently. Reuters said during Thursday's US session that chip stocks were on course to lose $2 trillion in value and that CoreWeave shares and bonds had slid further on AI debt fears; we have no closing figures for that session.

In oil, the IEA's governing board agreed to speed up the release of the remaining stocks from its March action over the Strait of Hormuz crisis. Reuters says the total will exceed 400 million barrels, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel as soon as possible. Security data cited in several outlets put attacks on shipping around the strait at their highest since the Iran war began. A container ship sank near Hormuz after being hit by a projectile (Reuters, Bloomberg), and five Indian nationals were rescued after a projectile hit a tanker there, with the Indian embassy thanking Omani authorities (The Hindu and one other outlet). FinancialJuice, a single source, put the November WTI contract's settlement near $98 a barrel on Thursday.
