Jio Platforms' anchor book draws record bids of ₹2.5 lakh crore, more than 30 times the ₹8,110 crore it raised from 212 investors

The shares were allotted at ₹1,119, the top of the price band, on Monday, October 19, two days before the IPO opens on October 21.

Jio Platforms raised ₹8,110 crore (₹81.1 billion, about $850 million) from 212 anchor investors on Monday, October 19, two days before its initial public offering (IPO) opens, according to Moneycontrol, The Hindu and Business Standard. The company allotted 7.24 crore (72.4 million) shares at ₹1,119 each, the top of its ₹1,065 to ₹1,119 price band, The Hindu reported. Bids in the anchor book exceeded ₹2.5 lakh crore (₹2.5 trillion, roughly $25 billion), more than 30 times the amount on offer, according to Business Standard and The Hindu, which described the demand as a record. Reuters, citing a source, reported that the anchor quota was nearly 30 times oversubscribed. Moneycontrol's report listed Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) among the investors that received shares.

The public issue is a fresh issue of up to 27 crore shares, and Moneycontrol put its size at ₹30,188 crore (₹301.88 billion, about $3.2 billion). Public bidding opens on Wednesday, October 21, and closes on Friday, October 23, according to the reports. Earlier reports citing Bloomberg had put potential bids above $20 billion before the anchor book closed. Business Standard's headline said the book drew over 30 times demand, and Moneycontrol's headline tied the ₹8,110 crore to a ₹30,188-crore IPO. The reports did not name all 212 investors, and the Reuters figure came from an unnamed source.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio says an India-US trade deal is 'very close' and explains the Russian oil sanctions waiver ahead of talks with Jaishankar in Mumbai

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Monday, October 19, that Washington is 'very close' to finalising a trade deal with India, according to Hindustan Times, Mint and The Times of India. The Daily Jagran reported that he linked the pending deal to the Trump administration's decision to waive sanctions on Russian energy: the waivers were issued over the past few months because of their effect on global supplies and prices, and the trade deal was one of the factors considered. He rejected the idea that the waivers meant buying Russian oil was now acceptable, the Daily Jagran reported, and The Times of India quoted him as saying 'Geography is real' in explaining why some countries struggle to find energy elsewhere. Rubio is due in Mumbai on Tuesday, October 20, for talks with External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar, followed by a visit to the energy hub of Jamnagar in Gujarat, according to the reports. The reports noted that the United States had earlier imposed tariffs of 50 per cent on India, including an extra 25 per cent over its purchases of Russian crude, and that Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal said on October 15 that the first phase of the proposed pact had been finalised and was in legal drafting.

India's foreign ministry rejects 'anti-India' references in OIC resolutions from the Baghdad meeting and says Pakistan orchestrated them

The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) on Monday, October 19, rejected what it called anti-India references in resolutions adopted at the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) foreign ministers' meeting in Baghdad, according to Hindustan Times, The Hindu, The Times of India and India TV. MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said the references to India are 'malicious, biased and false' and were orchestrated by Pakistan, as the reports quoted the statement. He said the MEA was disappointed that the OIC had 'once again allowed itself to be misused by Pakistan for its bigoted agenda'.

The statement said the OIC has no standing on India's internal affairs and that the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh are integral and inalienable parts of India. It alleged repression and killings of civilian protesters in Pakistan-occupied Jammu and Kashmir (PoJK) and said the only issues left concerning Jammu and Kashmir are Pakistan vacating areas it holds and ending its support for cross-border terrorism. It also rejected the Islamophobia references as an attempt to divert attention from Pakistan's record on minorities, as quoted in the reports.

AISA president Neha Bora asks the Delhi High Court to stay a police notice directing her to sign a ₹10,000 peace bond

Neha Bora, president of the All India Students' Association (AISA), moved the Delhi High Court on Monday, October 19, against a Delhi Police notice that asks her to appear before a Special Executive Magistrate and execute a personal bond of ₹10,000 with one surety to keep the peace, according to Hindustan Times, The Hindu, ANI and Scroll.in. ANI reported that the notice was issued on October 18 under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), and that she was asked to appear on October 19. The Hindu reported that AISA said her petition seeks an immediate stay and withdrawal of the notice.

Scroll.in and ANI reported that the petition argues the notice is vague and factually wrong and violates Articles 19 and 21 of the Constitution, and that her lawyers sought an urgent hearing. The reports said she had been detained on October 16 before a 'Lehra Do Tiranga' march at Connaught Place, which was aimed at Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar, and that AISA alleges the notice is meant to curb such protests. Delhi Police were not quoted on the reasons for the notice in the reports.

BJP veteran L. K. Advani, 98, is discharged from Delhi's Indraprastha Apollo Hospital after his health improves

Veteran Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) leader L. K. Advani, 98, was discharged from Indraprastha Apollo Hospital in Delhi on Monday, October 19, after his condition improved, according to The Hindu, Hindustan Times, The Times of India and The Economic Times. The Hindu reported that he had been admitted on October 16 with a chest infection and related lung complications.

Hindustan Times reported that he was admitted to the Delhi hospital over a chest infection and discharged after improvement in his health. The reports did not give a detailed medical bulletin, and no statement on his care at home was reported.
