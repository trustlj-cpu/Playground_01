Voter-roll protests spill onto Delhi streets as government and opposition trade charges

About 150 people were detained in the capital, the defence minister accused Congress of seeking 'regime change through violence', and New Delhi told a US lawmaker to stay out of it.

The dispute over the Election Commission of India's revision of the voter rolls moved from statements to the street on Friday. Delhi witnessed a day of detentions around a march billed as 'Lahra Do Tiranga' (hoist the tricolour). BBC Hindi reported that Abhijeet Dipke and Neha Bora were taken into custody, and a headline carried by two outlets put the number detained in the capital at about 150. Supporters of rival groups were reported to have shouted slogans at each other. The exact count and the circumstances of each detention come from those reports and have not been independently tallied. The political temperature rose the same day. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh, as reported by Hindustan Times, accused Congress of seeking 'regime change through violence', arguing that the protests against the Chief Election Commissioner were not really about electoral rolls but an attempt to weaken institutions. Union minister Pralhad Joshi said Rahul Gandhi was protesting out of desperation. BBC Hindi separately reported that a remark on 'regime change' by lawyer Prashant Bhushan had set the BJP and the opposition against each other. The opposition's position, voiced at a rally in Bihar by INDIA bloc leaders, is that the Election Commission is acting as a puppet of the ruling party. The Hindu carried that account; we found no second outlet for the Bihar rally yet.

Delhi Police are also answering to the courts. BBC Hindi reported that the force gave the Supreme Court its replies on questions about harsh handling of protesters, during hearing of a contempt petition alleging that the court's earlier directions had not been followed. Separately, after the announcement of a tricolour-hoisting at Connaught Place, the police announced several measures, according to the same outlet. The detail of those measures is not yet clear from the reports. The row also crossed borders. Hindustan Times reported that India rejected remarks by US Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal on the revision exercise, calling them 'unwarranted'. The statement is the government's, so its description of the remarks is one side of the story. What to watch next is whether the Supreme Court hearing moves forward, whether the detainees are released without charge, and whether more marches are announced.

Government raises windfall tax on petrol exports, trims it on diesel

The Hindu BusinessLine reported that the government late on Thursday raised the windfall tax on petrol, the export duty rising to ₹2.5 (about US$0.026) a litre from ₹0.5, while the duty on diesel was cut. Hindustan's Hindi business desk carried the same change as effective from Friday. The figure for the diesel cut was cut off in the report we saw, so we do not quote it. The windfall tax is levied on exports of refined fuel, not on what drivers pay at the pump. A higher export duty makes shipping petrol abroad less rewarding for refiners, which can nudge more supply toward the home market. No change in retail petrol or diesel prices was reported by either outlet. With crude near US$99 a barrel on the tape, the revision is a periodic recalibration of an extra-profit levy rather than a one-off shock.

West Asia crisis has turned logistics into 'anyhow, anytime', says DP World chief

DP World's global chief executive told The Hindu that logistics has moved from just-in-time delivery to 'anyhow, anytime' because of the crisis in West Asia, as reported by The Hindu BusinessLine and a news search feed. The main trouble centres on the port of Jebel Ali in Dubai, the ninth-largest port in the world. This is one company's view and the delays are described, not quantified, in the reports we saw.

The same day The Hindu reported, citing trade data, that India sourced more than two-thirds of its August oil imports from just three countries. Russia's share fell to 44% from a historic high in July, while the shares of the other two rose. Concentration of supply matters when shipping routes are disrupted: it exposes refiners to a single chokepoint. A separate report, carried by two outlets, says the rupee is forecast to weaken toward 100 to the dollar even as the RBI pushes back; the tape showed 94.95.

Courts · Kerala✓ 2 outlets · case outcome pending

Pinarayi Vijayan goes to Supreme Court against order for FIR in CMRL case

Hindustan Times and The Hindu reported that Pinarayi Vijayan has moved the Supreme Court against a High Court order directing that an FIR be lodged in the CMRL bribery case. Senior advocate Sidharth Luthra, appearing for Mr. Vijayan, mentioned the plea for urgent listing before a Bench headed by the Chief Justice.

Mentioning is only a request for an early date; the court had not, in the reports we saw, said when it would hear the matter. An FIR is the first step of a criminal investigation, so the question before the court is whether that step should be taken at all. Allegations in the case remain allegations, and Mr. Vijayan has not been found guilty of anything. Readers should treat the High Court's direction and the appeal as separate stages, and wait for the apex court's listing.

Parliament · Delimitation✓ 2 outlets · Centre's proposal text not seen

Kharge asks Modi not to 'bulldoze' delimitation Bill, seeks all-party meeting

The Hindu reported that Mallikarjun Kharge, Leader of Opposition in the Rajya Sabha, told Prime Minister Narendra Modi not to try to bulldoze the Bill again and to call an all-party meeting to discuss delimitation proposals. He said he had first written to the Prime Minister on April 11 asking for such a meeting. Delimitation is the redrawing of constituency boundaries and seat counts, and it is politically sensitive because states fear losing weight in Parliament.

We have not seen the text of the Centre's current proposal, so what the Bill contains is not described here. The letter is the opposition's side. In courts, the Madras High Court said borrowers do not mortgage their constitutional rights to banks. Elsewhere in the news, the Tamil Nadu government launched a semiconductor scheme, and a Visakhapatnam summit heard that investments in Andhra Pradesh have crossed ₹8.67 lakh crore (about US$91 billion) in two years, a claim by ministers that we have from one outlet.
