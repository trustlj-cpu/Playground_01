At least 17 die and 25 are hurt in a stampede at a north Kolkata Durga Puja pandal, and a judge-led SIT is ordered

The crush happened on Saturday night, October 17; the Prime Minister announced ex gratia and the state announced ₹25 lakh in aid and a government job.

Seventeen people died and 25 were injured in a stampede-like crush at a community Durga Puja pandal in north Kolkata on the night of Saturday, October 17, according to reports that were updated repeatedly through the late evening. The first alerts, at about 10 pm local time, spoke only of several people injured in a stampede-like situation. Within roughly twenty minutes the live coverage said several were feared dead and that the injured had been taken to the Calcutta Medical College and Hospital. By the time later reports consolidated the figures, three separate reports carried the same count: 17 dead and 25 hurt. Official reaction followed quickly. Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced ex gratia compensation for the victims, and Amit Shah was reported to be heading to the hospital. Murmu, Amit Shah and Mamata Banerjee all expressed grief, according to six reports. The state's Chief Minister announced aid of ₹25 lakh (2.5 million rupees) and a government job, and three reports say a Special Investigation Team (SIT) headed by a sitting judge will examine how the tragedy happened. Seven outlets carried some version of the story, which makes it the most widely reported event of the night.

Several basic facts are still missing from what we have seen. The headlines do not give the amount of the Prime Minister's ex gratia, and they do not say whether the ₹25 lakh is meant for each family or for each victim. We have not seen the name of the pandal, the time the crush began, the number of people inside, the ages of the dead, or whether any permission or crowd-control plan had been breached. The cause has not been stated by any report we reviewed, and we are not guessing at one. The early reports used softer words, 'several injured' and 'feared dead', and six outlets ran the first wording before the toll was known. That explains why you may have seen different numbers on different screens overnight. Treat 17 dead and 25 hurt as the latest count that more than one report agrees on, not as a final figure. The first things to look for today are the terms of reference of the SIT, the final list of the dead and injured, and any official statement on crowd limits at the remaining puja pandals.

US lawmakers write to the secretary of state over Delhi detentions as India rejects 'interference'

US lawmakers have written to Trump's secretary of state over the detentions of protesters in Delhi, and India has rejected what it called interference, three outlets reported on Saturday, October 17. One report says the letter urges Marco Rubio to raise the handling of the CJP protest during his visit to India. It comes after New Delhi dismissed earlier remarks by Jayapal on the revision of India's electoral rolls, the same reports note. The detentions themselves took place around the 'Lehra Do Tiranga' protest march at Connaught Place. Delhi Police took Neha Bora and Abhijeet Dipke into preventive custody ahead of the march; Neha Bora was released, and Dipke was to be produced before the SEM court after action under the BNSS, two outlets reported. A separate report from the Hindi service of the BBC says Bora came out of custody early in the morning while action was taken against Dipke under sections 126 and 170 of the BNSS. A second strand is the questions being asked in Hindi coverage about why Rubio's India visit did not include Delhi and why External Affairs Minister Jaishankar travelled to Mumbai to meet him. That report quotes experts who describe India-US ties as increasingly transactional. We have not seen the text of the lawmakers' letter.

ICICI Bank's Q2 profit rises 20% to ₹14,843 crore, beating estimates, as the Nifty closes at 23,146.15

ICICI Bank reported a 20% rise in second-quarter net profit to ₹14,843 crore (₹148.43 billion), ahead of analysts' estimates, with net interest income (NII) up 18%, two outlets reported on Saturday, October 17. The results came on Saturday, with markets shut, so the share-price reaction will be visible only when trading resumes on Monday. We have not seen the bank's asset-quality or margin figures, and we do not describe them. The Nifty closed Friday, October 16, at 23,146.15, up 0.65%, the last completed close. The rupee stood at 94.96 to the US dollar in the latest completed reading, down 0.09% on the day. One crore is ten million, so ₹14,843 crore is about 148 billion rupees.

In a separate report, the BBC's Hindi service and a Hindi business feed carried a piece titled on who Prem Vats is, the man said to be buying IDBI Bank for ₹52,000 crore (₹520 billion). The article describes him as known as a value investor, a style built on buying shares priced below estimated worth. We have only that headline and a summary line; the structure, the stake, the approvals and the timeline of the purchase are not in the reports, and we are not filling them in.

Law · Supreme Court✓ 2 outlets on each ruling · full judgment not seen

Supreme Court says children have a fundamental right to access and use the internet; a court panel opens a complaints portal on July violence

The Supreme Court has held that the right to access and use the internet is a fundamental right of every individual, including children, according to two outlets on Saturday, October 17. The court took a practical view that children did not stop using the internet merely because a law prohibits it, the reports say. We have only the headline findings and a line of summary from the reports, not the judgment, so we do not describe the facts of the case or the directions given. On a separate matter, a panel appointed by the Supreme Court has launched a portal for complaints and evidence about the July violence at Jantar Mantar, two outlets reported. The portal is at hpec.nic.in and was launched for the convenience of affected people, including police personnel. The Ministry of Home Affairs said priority will be given to matters involving the use of pellet guns and targeted violence. The reports do not give a deadline for filing complaints or the size of the panel.

The two items are unrelated in subject but both turn on the court's reach: one widens a right, the other builds a channel for evidence about force used at a protest. Anyone relying on the internet ruling should wait for the full text before assuming what it changes.

L.K. Advani, 98, is 'stable' at Apollo Hospitals after admission on Friday evening; Nadda pays a visit

L.K. Advani, the 98-year-old BJP leader, is stable at Apollo Hospitals, and Nadda visited him, three outlets reported on Saturday, October 17. He was taken to the hospital at around 7:30 pm on Friday, October 16, and was undergoing treatment, according to the first report. The Hindi service of the BBC quotes the hospital as saying his condition is stable and that doctors are monitoring his health continuously. The reports do not state what he is being treated for, which ward he is in, or how long he is expected to stay. No bulletin giving test results or a medical diagnosis was in the material we reviewed, so we do not offer one. The Hindi report is dated early on Saturday, and the English reports a few hours later, which suggests the 'stable' description held through the day.

Readers looking for updates should rely on the hospital's own bulletins rather than on forwarded messages, which often spread unverified claims about the health of elderly public figures. We will report any change in the next edition if two outlets confirm it.
