Supreme Court puts Rahul Gandhi's criminal defamation trial on hold over a 2019 remark on Modi, and asks Maharashtra and the complainant to reply

The stay covers further proceedings before the trial court. It does not say whether the remark was defamatory; the Court will first look at why the Bombay High Court left the 2019 summons standing.

The Supreme Court on Tuesday put a criminal defamation trial against Rahul Gandhi on hold. The case concerns a remark he made about Prime Minister Narendra Modi in 2019. The Hindu reports that a bench of Justices Vikram Nath and Aparesh Kumar Singh stopped further proceedings before the trial court in Maharashtra and issued notices to the state government and to the complainant, M. H. Shrishrimal, asking for their replies in four weeks. Mint describes it the same way, and Hindustan Times adds that the Court agreed to examine an order of the Bombay High Court that had refused to quash the summons issued in 2019.

The outlets name the remark differently. Bar and Bench and Mint call it the "commander-in-thief" remark, a play on "commander-in-chief". India Today's headline calls it the "Chowkidar chor hai" case, and Aaj Tak ties the case to the Rafale jet deal, which we have from that outlet alone. All of them describe the same dispute: a private complaint over what Gandhi said about Modi in 2019.

A stay pauses the trial court while the Supreme Court looks at the question before it. It is not an acquittal, and the Court has not said whether the remark was defamatory. The notices simply oblige the other side to answer. Defamation remains a criminal offence in India, which the Supreme Court upheld in 2016, so a politician's speech can lead to a summons, a trial and a long appeal. What the Court has agreed to examine is narrower than the rights and wrongs of the remark: whether the Bombay High Court was right to leave the 2019 summons standing.

It was a heavy day in court for Gandhi. The Hindu reports that an MP-MLA court in Uttar Pradesh, which hears cases against current and former legislators, fixed 26 October to pronounce its verdict in a different defamation case, over remarks he made in 2018 about Amit Shah, then the BJP president and now Union Home Minister, during an election campaign in Bengaluru. We saw that from one outlet. The Supreme Court registry also pointed out 10 defects in the petition he filed with other INDIA bloc leaders against the Chief Election Commissioner, so that case has yet to be listed (The Hindu, The Economic Times, Mint); see the story beside this one. On the political front, Gandhi hosted INDIA bloc leaders for tea at his residence to plan their next step (Dainik Jagran, India Today), and Hindustan Times reports that the opposition announced a "mission to restore democracy" and plans an "India Plus" group. The Economic Times says the daily Delhi marches are pausing for the festive season.

The stay is relief, not vindication, and the dates matter more than the headlines. Replies are due in four weeks, which from Tuesday points to about 10 November; the reports we reviewed do not name a hearing date. The Uttar Pradesh verdict on 26 October comes first.

Registry flags 10 defects in the INDIA bloc's petition against the CEC; the Court defers its Delhi crackdown hearing to Thursday

The Supreme Court registry pointed out 10 defects in the petition that Rahul Gandhi and other INDIA bloc leaders filed against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and the voter-roll revision, The Hindu, The Economic Times and Mint report. Mint cites missing pages and a ₹220 shortfall in court fee (about US$2.30); one Times of India headline counted 14 defects. The petition is not listed until they are cured (The Hindu). The Court also put off its hearing on the Delhi protest crackdown to Thursday because a judge was unwell, and gave Delhi Police and other authorities until Wednesday evening to answer notices in contempt proceedings (Hindustan Times, Live Law). It wants a senior Delhi Police officer to explain the reported assault and detention of women journalists at the protests (Hindustan Times).

Outside court, Delhi Police filed an FIR against 14 people, among them the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) leaders Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka, for trespassing at Jantar Mantar (The Hindu, Hindustan Times). The CJP says they were denied phones and lawyers (Hindustan Times); the police case is for trespass. Jantar Mantar is no longer a protest site, and police named three replacement grounds (Hindustan Times, The Hindu). Delhi High Court asked the Centre to respond on rules for regulating protests and listed the plea for Friday (Hindustan Times). Amit Shah said Congress has been losing elections for decades and the SIR began after 2024 (Hindustan Times, NDTV), while Union minister Piyush Goyal accused Congress of rewarding past election commissioners (Moneycontrol, India Today). Those are political charges, not findings.

World · West AsiaPress ✓ 7 outlets · the Dhahran hit rests on Bloomberg

Indian airlines halt Saudi flights until 17 October as Houthi attacks reach near Aramco's Dhahran headquarters

IndiGo cancelled flights to and from Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam and Madinah until 17 October (Hindustan Times, Mint), and Air India did the same for Riyadh, Dammam and Jeddah (Hindustan Times). Hindustan Times counts four Indian carriers off Riyadh, adding Air India Express and Akasa Air. The Indian embassy said Indians in an emergency can leave Saudi Arabia through neighbouring countries (Hindustan Times), and advised citizens to remain calm and indoors and not to share videos (Mint, The Hindu). Mint reports that the attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport killed an Indian citizen and that four injured Indians remain in intensive care; the Times of India puts the airport attack's toll at 13.

Tuesday brought more. Reuters reported loud booms near Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran, and Bloomberg, citing people with knowledge, said a Houthi missile hit the complex and started a fire in part of it; the hit itself is Bloomberg's report alone. The Houthis warned civilians and staff to leave all Aramco facilities (Mint, Al Jazeera), and the Saudi-led coalition struck an oil depot near Sanaa after ordering an evacuation (Reuters, BBC, The Guardian). Reuters adds that Saudi Arabia's public warning system stayed silent before the airport attacks. Donald Trump said the Houthis were trying to push up oil prices before the US midterm elections (Reuters). A market-news wire put Brent at $105.69 a barrel, down 96 cents, and WTI at $94.14 (FinancialJuice, single source).

Markets · EconomyPress ✓ 5 outlets · rupee close from one outlet

Sensex jumps 1,109 points, its best day in four months, as IT shares surge and the finance minister leaves the door open on capital-gains tax

The BSE Sensex rose 1,109.10 points, or 1.54 per cent, to close at 73,033.78, its best single-day gain in four months, The Hindu reports. NDTV and the Times of India both put the gain above 1,100 points, and the Times of India's closing estimate of investor wealth added is ₹4.12 lakh crore (₹4.12 trillion, about US$43 billion at the day's rupee rate, our conversion), up from ₹3.89 lakh crore earlier in the session. IT led: The Hindu names TCS, Tech Mahindra, Infosys and HDFC Bank among the winners, and Upstox says the Nifty IT index rose about 5 per cent, with HCLTech, whose second-quarter net profit rose 21 per cent to ₹5,132 crore (about US$540 million) (The Hindu), among the leaders. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said she is open to hearing views on scrapping the long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax as Budget 2027 consultations begin (Moneycontrol, NDTV Profit, The Economic Times); the Times of India stresses she made no commitment on LTCG or the securities transaction tax.

The rupee closed 40 paise higher at 95.38 against the dollar, having touched 94.96 in the session (The Hindu, single source for the close). Sitharaman said the RBI will keep working to curb volatility and blamed global uncertainty for the rupee's slide (The Hindu, The Economic Times). The rally ran against the Asian tide: AP reported that South Korea's Kospi sank more than 5 per cent on a chip sell-off over worries about the AI boom. Prices are the other story. Food inflation rose to 7.74 per cent in September, with onion prices more than doubling over the year (Hindustan Times).

Anti-drug activist shot dead in Punjab's Mansa while streaming live on Facebook; the accused is a medical-store owner

Parvinder Singh, known as Jhota (some reports spell the first name Parminder), was shot dead in Mansa on Tuesday while confronting a medical-store owner over the sale of certain drugs. The Hindu reports he was questioning the owner and recording the argument on his phone when he was shot. The Times of India, Moneycontrol and NDTV say the attack was caught on a Facebook live stream, and Hindustan Times reports on a video of his last moments.

Anger followed. Mansa residents held large protests and the family refused to cremate the body, while the opposition corners the AAP government (The Hindu). BJP president Nitin Nabin questioned security in Punjab and targeted the AAP government (Hindustan Times). The accusation that the shop sold drugs illegally is the activist's; the reports we reviewed carry no police finding on it and do not say whether the owner has been arrested.
