Cabinet clears Fairfax's ₹52,878-crore purchase of a controlling stake in IDBI Bank, the largest foreign investment in Indian finance

The Canadian group's bid for the state-owned lender won the Union Cabinet's approval on Wednesday. The finance minister answered Akhilesh Yadav's attack on the sale by blaming the opposition alliance for public sector banks' bad loans.

The Union Cabinet on Wednesday approved the sale of IDBI Bank to Fairfax Financial Holdings, the Canadian investment group, for ₹52,878 crore (about $5.6 billion), The Economic Times and The Hindu report. Both call it the largest foreign direct investment (FDI) ever made in India's financial sector. Reuters and The Globe and Mail describe the purchase as a majority or controlling stake, and Bloomberg calls it the biggest bank deal in India. The Economic Times says the buyer is an arm of Fairfax; The Indian Express carries the Cabinet's own phrase, a "strategic restructuring" of the bank. AajTak put it for Hindi readers in one line: the Cabinet has cleared the sale of a government bank to a Canadian company. The rupee and dollar prices, which The Globe and Mail gives as $5.56 billion, imply a rate of about 95 to the dollar, close to the rupee's level this week, so the two figures appear to describe the same price.

The detail is thin. None of the reports we reviewed gives the size of the stake in per cent, the price per share or a completion date, so we give none. Financial Post carries a Fairfax announcement that it was declared the "strategic buyer" in the auction and plans to contribute Digit Insurance and IIFL Capital to IDBI Bank; we have that from one outlet only, and how such a combination would work is not explained. The auction itself is described only in that announcement, and we have seen no list of rival bidders. In a separate piece The Economic Times counts 13 disinvestments by the Modi government in ten years, a measure of how often the state has sold its holdings.

Politics arrived within hours. Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav went after the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) over the sale, and AajTak reports Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman's reply under a "crocodile tears" headline. News18 reports that she blamed the INDIA bloc, the opposition alliance, for ruining public sector banks through bad loans, a charge aimed at the earlier UPA government. Both sides are arguing about who left the bank in its present state, and that is our reading; neither argument tells a depositor what happens next.

The same Cabinet meeting approved a ₹2,009.61 crore (about $211 million) performance-linked Diwali bonus for 11.40 lakh (1.14 million) railway employees, worth 78 days' pay, and ₹53,428 crore (about $5.6 billion) for a social assistance scheme that also revises disability pension norms (The Hindu, Mint, The Economic Times, Dainik Bhaskar). Bhaskar's headline reads the new norms as a pension for people with 40 per cent disability from birth; we have not seen the notification. Of the three decisions the bank sale is the one whose consequences carry no date. Whether it needs further regulatory clearances, and how long they take, is not covered in anything we saw.

Politics · ProtestPress ✓ 10 outlets

Two CJP leaders go to jail for five days; Rahul Gandhi backs them and the Supreme Court hears the SIR plea today

Delhi Police detained Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) leaders Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka, and Ajinkya Shinde, after they entered Jantar Mantar on Tuesday; Dainik Bhaskar says they were brought to Sansad Marg police station at 3 am. CJP's legal head Ratna Singh called it an arrest, and the police called it preventive (The Hindu, Hindustan Times). A Special Executive Magistrate then sent Das and Ranka to five days of judicial custody in a trespassing case (The Hindu, Mint, Live Law). Rahul Gandhi condemned the custody (The Economic Times) and said waving the tricolour at Jantar Mantar is not a crime. Mamata Banerjee backed the jailed leaders (Hindustan Times), and M.K. Stalin's party offered them legal aid (The Hindu).

Founder Abhijeet Dipke, heckled by a stranger at Delhi airport (NDTV), asked supporters to gather with the tricolour at Connaught Place at 5 pm on Friday 16 October, and for tiranga marches in other cities (ABP News, The Hindu). The Supreme Court has 18 petitions on the protests and detentions before it (Bar and Bench), and the Delhi Metro Rail Corporation told it that protests kept 2.6 million (26 lakh) commuters off trains (Hindustan Times). Today the Court is to hear the plea by Rahul Gandhi and other opposition leaders against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and the SIR (The Hindu).

World · West AsiaPress ✓ 8 outlets · damage described by unnamed sources

Houthi missiles hit Aramco's headquarters; India tells citizens in Saudi Arabia to stay indoors and Indian airlines cancel flights until 17 October

Yemen's Houthi movement struck the headquarters of Saudi Aramco in Dhahran late on Tuesday, and the damage was extensive, Reuters reported, citing sources; Mint and AajTak also report missiles hitting the oil giant. Reuters says the Houthis have vowed more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities, and AP reports that residents of Riyadh heard fresh explosions on Wednesday. India's foreign ministry called the attacks "unacceptable" and urged them to stop (The Economic Times). The embassy told Indians to stay indoors during aerial attacks and not to share videos (Mint), Indian airlines have cancelled flights to Saudi cities until 17 October (The Hindu), and Kerala's NoRKA has opened a help desk for expatriates in Saudi Arabia (The Hindu).

In the Strait of Hormuz, attacks on shipping have reached their highest level since the Iran war began, according to security data cited by The Economic Times. A container ship sank near the strait: Reuters, citing sources, says a projectile hit it, while Iran says, as relayed by The Indian Express, that a naval mine sank a vessel carrying more than 2,500 containers. They may be the same ship; we cannot confirm that. Hindustan Times reports a merchant navy sailor from Uttar Pradesh missing after a tanker was attacked off Oman; our 13 October edition reported two missing Indian seafarers, and the report does not say whether he is one of them. Oil rose on the strikes (Reuters), and the IEA says diesel shortages could last well into 2027 (Reuters).

Economy · PricesPress ✓ 7 outlets · rupee close from one outlet

Wholesale inflation jumps to 11.39 per cent in September; the RBI governor says markets are too gloomy on the rupee

Wholesale price inflation reached 11.39 per cent in September on costlier fuel, food and manufactured goods, The Hindu, Moneycontrol and NDTV Profit report. The Reserve Bank of India's Governor, Sanjay Malhotra, told Moneycontrol that markets are overly pessimistic on the rupee (Moneycontrol, News18), and The Economic Times reports the bank is open to more steps to curb speculation in the currency. The Hindu says the rupee closed at 95.17 to the dollar, up 19 paise, and that shares closed lower on dear crude and rising inflation; at midday Moneycontrol and NDTV Profit had the Nifty 50 below 22,700. We have no second source for either closing level, which is why this edition's ears show a dash.

Abroad, US consumer prices accelerated in September on costlier gasoline, with core inflation firmer, Reuters reports, and Mint puts the annual rate at 3.7 per cent. Fed officials sounded divided on timing: Daly told Reuters rate hikes are likely but not urgent, while Bowman said there is time to wait and see. US shares fell on Wednesday with technology and chip stocks leading (Bloomberg, Reuters), and Bloomberg reports the small-cap Russell 2000 sank into a correction, 11 per cent below its peak. Gold slid towards $4,100 an ounce as hot inflation lifted rate-hike bets (FXStreet). At home, sugar has risen more than 10 per cent in six days (Hindustan Times), and up to 17 per cent by Moneycontrol's count, on festive demand and tight supply.

Law · CrimePress ✓ 10 outlets · sentence as reported from court

Meerut court sentences Muskan Rastogi and Sahil Shukla to death for the "blue drum" murder of her husband

A Meerut court on Wednesday sentenced Muskan Rastogi and her partner Sahil Shukla to death for the murder of her husband, Saurabh Rajput, in the case known as the "blue drum" murder (The Hindu, Hindustan Times, The Times of India, NDTV, Mint). Mint and NDTV report that the judge referred to the Mahabharata and the Gita's idea of dharma while passing sentence. AajTak reports that both convicts broke down on hearing the order, and Hindustan Times quotes their advocate as saying they were shocked.

Under Indian procedure a death sentence from a trial court must be confirmed by the High Court before it can be carried out, and the convicts can appeal; we have seen no report of an appeal yet. The sentence settles the trial, not the questions of law that will follow.
