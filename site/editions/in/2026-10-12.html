Supreme Court orders no illegal detentions and normal metro service, yet Delhi detentions go on as the CJP vows to return to the streets

On a Sunday sitting the Court ordered open metro, rail and internet and no unlawful detentions. Police still held people on the ground, 20 FIRs were filed, and the CJP says its next call is coming.

The Supreme Court sat on Sunday and set rules for how Delhi handles the protests against Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar. Mint, Hindustan Times, The Hindu and LawChakra report that it told the authorities there must be no illegal detention of people gathering peacefully, that metro and rail services must run normally, that the internet must not be cut, and that a Section 163 notice board near the Court must go. Bar and Bench reports that the bench found a prima facie case of contempt over the metro restrictions, and Mint says it issued a contempt notice over the alleged breach of its 9 October order. NDTV quotes the Court as saying the right to protest must stay within the Police Act. LawChakra adds that it issued notice on a habeas corpus plea over the alleged illegal detention of about 7,000 people.

The streets did not follow. Hindustan Times reports that about 100 people were held at Connaught Place after the order, and The Hindu and Hindustan Times say journalists were detained. The Hindu reports that Left parties accuse the police of turning Delhi into a "war zone" and that Rajya Sabha MP John Brittas was hospitalised after being detained. Delhi Police registered 20 FIRs, including for assault on police (The Hindu, Hindustan Times), and served notices on CJP leaders (The Economic Times). Hindustan Times says four men were held after right-wing groups threatened and assaulted protesters. Many people were also detained near Shivaji Park in Mumbai, and in Goa (The Hindu).

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) founder, Abhijeet Dipke, was released and went back to his village in Maharashtra after his grandmother's death, Hindustan Times reports. He told Mint and The Economic Times that the protests will go on and that he has signed no bond promising otherwise. A dispute has grown over the funeral: his family says police delayed his release (The Hindu), while Delhi Police released a video showing officers telling him of the death and offering him a way home, which he is shown declining (The Hindu, Hindustan Times, The Economic Times). YouTuber Dhruv Rathee, freed late on Saturday, says he was "kidnapped", not detained (Hindustan Times, Mint).

Politics is hot. Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge calls it an "undeclared Emergency" (Hindustan Times, The Hindu), while Chirag Paswan says the protests are an attempt to create anarchy and BJP leaders say the group has shifted from students' concerns to electoral issues (Hindustan Times, The Hindu). Mint reports a CJP message telling supporters already in Delhi to stay put because the "next call is on its way", and CJP leaders plan to visit a Delhi police station today to ask for a date for a "peaceful" protest at Jantar Mantar (Hindustan Times, Mint). If you are in central Delhi, expect more curbs; The Economic Times reports a Delhi Traffic Police advisory for 13 October. Treat every crowd and detention figure as one side's claim until a second outlet confirms it.

Chief Justice declines to meet INDIA bloc leaders as the opposition plans its march to the Supreme Court

Chief Justice Surya Kant has refused a request from Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi and Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, backed by other INDIA bloc leaders, to meet him on Monday about the SIR of electoral rolls and the CEC, The Hindu, Hindustan Times, Mint, The Times of India and Live Law reported. Hindustan Times says he cited judicial propriety, and The Hindu says he gave reasons. The leaders had planned to march from India Gate to the Supreme Court and meet him after 4 pm (Hindustan Times).

The refusal does not end the plan. Rahul Gandhi announced a march to the Supreme Court (The Hindu), and Congress general secretary K.C. Venugopal vows to continue the protest (The Hindu). Other legal moves are running alongside: the DYFI, the CPI(M)'s youth wing, will petition the Court over alleged illegal detention and torture of activists (The Hindu), and the Court has already issued a contempt notice (Mint).

A judge declining a private meeting with one side of a political dispute is ordinary, because courts hear parties in open court with the other side present. The real test is the petitions already before the bench: whether it asks the police and the Home Ministry to account for the detentions. That is our reading, not a reported fact. For the opposition, the march is as much about visibility as access: it keeps the SIR dispute on the front page while the CJP protests continue.

Second strike on Riyadh airport a day after 12 died; an Indian hurt on Saturday has died and 10 to 11 more were injured

A second projectile hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Sunday, a day after the Saturday strike that Saudi authorities say killed 12 and wounded more than 300. The Saudi-led coalition said a projectile struck Terminal 4 and air defences intercepted a missile near the airport (FinancialJuice, BBC). The Houthis, a Yemeni armed group, claimed the attack and said they also hit Dammam airport and Aramco sites. Saudi civil aviation said Dammam airport was targeted on Sunday evening, but the Aramco claim is unconfirmed (FinancialJuice). AP reports that Saudi Arabia extended the airport's closure, and the Financial Times says oil executives pulled out of an energy congress in Riyadh.

For India the news is direct. The Hindu reports that an Indian injured on Saturday died the next day, and Mint and Hindustan Times also report one Indian dead. Hindustan Times says External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar expressed grief and promised the family all help. Injured Indians number 10 (Mint) or 11 (Hindustan Times, The Economic Times). The Hindu says several flights between Kerala and Saudi Arabia were cancelled. Separately, India's shipping minister said the tanker Valthera, with 11 Indian crew, was hit by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and all are safe (FinancialJuice, single source).

Trump announces a Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire"; Zelensky says it was news to him

US President Donald Trump said on Truth Social on Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed an "energy ceasefire", a pause in attacks on each other's power and fuel facilities, and urged both sides not to break it (Reuters, BBC, Bloomberg, Mint). Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky told Axios the declaration was news to him but that Ukraine will agree if Russia does (FinancialJuice, citing Axios). Earlier he said Ukraine would stop striking Russian refineries if Russia stops attacking its energy network (Reuters). He later wrote on Telegram that the announced energy ceasefire is good news for everyone. We have seen no confirmation from Moscow.

The announcement follows the Trump-Putin diesel deal. Mint says Trump wants 4.8 million tonnes of diesel from Russia, a Republican senator says the deal may be illegal (Reuters), and The Economic Times says India is waiting to see how the US will buy it. The Financial Times reports that Washington had threatened to block weapons for Ukraine over its refinery strikes (FinancialJuice, citing the FT). For India the stake is fuel prices and the rupee: a calmer oil market would help, but nothing is settled until Moscow and Kyiv agree terms.

Musk keeps up his Starlink attack on Ambani, days before Jio's record IPO

Elon Musk said Mukesh Ambani's online supporters showed "absurd hypocrisy" and made "ridiculous arguments", according to The Hindu, Rediff and The New Indian Express. He also said competition would greatly benefit the average Indian and vowed an "affordable" Starlink (Mint, Hindustan Times, Reuters). Reuters notes the row comes days before the record initial public offering (IPO) of Jio Platforms, Reliance's telecom arm. BJP MP Nishikant Dubey replied that Starlink poses security threats and that India's security policy is not shaped by social media pressure (Mint, Hindustan Times, The Hindu).

Starlink, SpaceX's satellite internet service, is still waiting for a licence in India; we have seen no decision reported. Moneycontrol says Jio goes into its IPO with stronger cash generation and lower debt. The IPO date and size are not in the coverage we reviewed, so we do not give them. Each side's claims remain its own.
