Delhi under heavy curbs as the CJP presses ahead with today's protest against Election Commission chief Gyanesh Kumar

Permission was denied, metro stations were shut, trains were cancelled and the internet is to be suspended around central Delhi. The opposition says the CEC must go; the police say public order comes first.

Delhi woke on Saturday under one of its heaviest security clampdowns in recent memory, ahead of the protest that the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) has called at Jantar Mantar to demand the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar. Delhi Police refused permission on Friday, citing a breach of an undertaking given at an earlier protest, according to The Hindu, the Economic Times and Hindustan Times. The Union Home Ministry (MHA) also directed states to stop CJP supporters from moving towards the capital, The Hindu reported. The party says it will go ahead. Its founder, Abhijeet Dipke, released a video calling it a "final call" and saying he expects to be arrested when he lands in Delhi, Hindustan Times reported.

The curbs are wide. Mint, The Hindu and Hindustan Times report that entry to 57 Delhi Metro stations was restricted from 9 pm on Friday. After the Supreme Court remarked that "everyone does not have a car", the Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) revised its order, and Hindustan Times now counts 45 stations affected. It also reports 254 trains cancelled, and the Economic Times says Mumbai-Delhi services are among them. The Hindu puts the deployment at 23,000 security personnel. Mint says the internet will be suspended within a 4 km radius of Janpath and Kartavya Path from 10 pm on Friday, and the Economic Times reports that Delhi schools have moved to online classes.

The political background is the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, which the opposition says is deleting genuine voters. On Friday evening, MPs of the INDIA bloc, led by Rahul Gandhi, met President Droupadi Murmu and handed over a four-point memorandum seeking the CEC's removal, Hindustan Times reported. Gandhi said the President had asked for proof of what he calls vote chori, or vote theft, and told MPs he "guarantees" the government will fall. The BJP called his account politically motivated, The Hindu reported; we have not seen the President's side of the meeting. Madhya Pradesh police detained 14 students heading to Delhi (The Hindu), and the state High Court told police to produce a woman detained on a train (Live Law).

What to expect today: if you are in central Delhi, plan for closed stations, road diversions from 6 am (Hindustan Times traffic advisory) and no mobile data near Janpath. The key questions are how many people turn up, whether arrests follow, and whether anyone goes back to the courts. The Supreme Court on Friday refused to order an independent probe into the CJP's July protest (Hindustan Times, Economic Times). Delhi Police has also published an open letter to citizens ahead of the stir. Treat every crowd and arrest figure today as a claim by one side until a second source confirms it.

BJP wins the Nagaon Lok Sabha seat and holds Nandigram; TVK takes both Tamil Nadu bypolls

Ruling parties swept Friday's by-elections, The Hindu reported. The BJP wrested the Nagaon Lok Sabha seat in Assam after seven years, and India Today says the Congress was set to lose its deposit. Akhil Gogoi, an Assam politician, said Congress's Gaurav Gogoi had "failed completely" and should resign, Hindustan Times reported.

In West Bengal the BJP retained Nandigram with a record margin and also won Rejinagar, a seat with many Muslim voters (The Hindu, Hindustan Times). In Tamil Nadu the TVK of Chief Minister Vijay won both Madurantakam and Dharapuram. Vijay called it a victory for "political decorum", while M.K. Stalin of the DMK called it a "temporary victory for politics of betrayal" (The Hindu, Hindustan Times). The Hindu adds that the DMK kept much of its vote even though it contested alone, that Puducherry's result also favoured the ruling side, and, in its own analysis, that the AIADMK's falling vote share may deepen its troubles. Nagaon is one of 543 Lok Sabha seats, so the result does not change the balance at the Centre.

India calls Vance's remarks on Indian professionals "deeply offensive"; MEA says the green-card curbs do not advance shared ambitions

The government has pushed back against US Vice President JD Vance over his comments on Indian professionals, as the row over the suspension of firms from the green-card filing route widened. The Hindu, Hindustan Times, Mint and Moneycontrol all report that New Delhi called the remarks "deeply offensive". The External Affairs Ministry (MEA) said the American steps on PERM do not advance the shared ambitions of both countries, and noted that US history has been shaped by immigrants, according to Hindustan Times and The Hindu. It added that the arrangement offers opportunities for Indians but also helps the US.

We have not seen the full text of what Vance said, so we do not quote it. On the company side the lists differ: the Economic Times named Infosys and Wipro on Thursday, while a Mint explainer headline names Microsoft and TCS. TCS told Reuters it sees no impact on its workforce strategy. Bloomberg Television reports that Indian tech workers face new uncertainty over H-1B visas and the green card queue.

World · LawPress ✓ 6 outlets · Trump's and Israel's remarks are theirs

Indian-origin jurist Navi Pillay wins the Nobel Peace Prize; Trump attacks her and sanctions the ICC within hours

The Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay of South Africa, a former United Nations human rights chief, for her work in international justice, AP, Reuters and the BBC reported. Mint and Hindustan Times note her Indian roots, which Mint ties to the colonial labour system that took Indians to South Africa. The International Criminal Court (ICC), where she once served as a judge (Reuters), said justice is key to lasting peace. The Washington Post reports that praise poured in while Israel criticised the choice.

President Trump reposted an attack on the award and called her a "hater of America and Israel", Mint and Business Standard reported. Hours later, Reuters and AP reported, the US imposed sweeping sanctions on the ICC; the court's prosecutor condemned them and said its work will continue. Annamalai, a BJP leader from Tamil Nadu, was among the Indian voices hailing her win (The Hindu).

Musk renews his Starlink push with "Dear Prime Minister Ambani"; BJP targets Rahul Gandhi and answers on spectrum

Elon Musk again raised Starlink's pending India licence on X. Mint says his post addressed "Dear Prime Minister Ambani", and the Economic Times reports he argued that the lack of internet denies children educational opportunity. The Hindu reports that the BJP targeted Rahul Gandhi over his exchange with Musk and countered the SpaceX founder's remarks on India's spectrum policy. No licence decision has been announced.

Abroad, SpaceX's purchase of a block of spectrum has rattled telecoms. The Financial Times says US telcos lost about $45 billion in value, and Reuters reports that Deutsche Telekom shares fell 7 per cent.
