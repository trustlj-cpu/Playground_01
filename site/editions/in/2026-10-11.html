About 7,000 detained, four former chief ministers among them, as Delhi shuts down for the CJP march against Election Commission chief Gyanesh Kumar

Jantar Mantar stayed empty, the protest scattered across the city, and the CJP has called a second march for today. The opposition calls it an undeclared emergency; the Delhi government says the capital will not be held hostage.

Delhi saw one of its largest police crackdowns in years on Saturday. Police detained about 7,000 people, including four former chief ministers, Mint and the Economic Times reported; The Hindu's earlier count was over 5,000. Those held included Mamata Banerjee of the Trinamool Congress, Arvind Kejriwal of the AAP and Kerala's Pinarayi Vijayan (The Hindu, Hindustan Times), along with activists Yogendra Yadav and Prashant Bhushan and Dipankar Bhattacharya of the CPI(ML) Liberation (The Hindu). The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) had called the march at Jantar Mantar to demand the removal of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar. Its founder, Abhijeet Dipke, was detained at Delhi airport, Mint reported, and Reuters and Hindustan Times say he stayed in custody. His grandmother died that day (Mint, The Hindu); Delhi Police said he had been informed, Hindustan Times reported.

Jantar Mantar itself remained clear; the New Delhi DCP, a senior police officer, dismissed social-media claims about it (The Hindu). Protesters regrouped at spots such as Dilli Haat and Connaught Place. The Hindu reports that right-wing group members assaulted protesters at Connaught Place, and Hindustan Times says journalists were among those attacked. Two police personnel were hurt when stones were thrown near Akashwani Bhawan, The Hindu reported. Lawyers detained near the Supreme Court were released after Chief Justice Surya Kant intervened (Economic Times, Mint). Mint counted 45 metro stations shut until 10 pm, and the Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) later reopened entry and exit at all of them (Hindustan Times, Mint). The Indian Express counted 245 trains cancelled. Delhi's markets shut, with Khan Market traders warning of losses of ₹1.5 lakh (about $1,550) a day per shop (Mint), and The Hindu reports that daily-wage workers were stopped from reaching their jobs. Protests were also held in Mumbai, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kochi and Kalaburagi (The Hindu, Mint).

The political fight is sharp. Delhi Chief Minister Rekha Gupta said "Delhi will not be held hostage" (Hindustan Times, The Statesman). BJP president Nitin Nabin accused the INDIA bloc and the CJP of trying to destabilise the country (Hindustan Times), and Sambit Patra said the unrest is not India's mood (Economic Times). M.K. Stalin called the police action an "undeclared emergency" (The Hindu). On the other side, 124 former civil servants signed an open letter defending the Election Commission and the SIR of electoral rolls (Hindustan Times, The Hindu). The Hindu's analysis asks how the government will stop a largely leaderless crowd from regrouping.

What to expect today: CJP leader Ajinkya Shinde, who is leading in Dipke's absence, has called a fresh march to Jantar Mantar and says the group will not go back until the CEC resigns (The Hindu, Mint, Hindustan Times). Mint reports traffic curbs at about 30 points. If you are in central Delhi, check metro and traffic advisories before you leave. Treat every crowd and detention figure as a claim by one side until a second source confirms it.

Supreme Court to sit on a Sunday to hear a contempt plea over Delhi's metro and rail shutdown

The Supreme Court has set a special Sunday hearing of a contempt plea over the closure of metro stations and trains on 10 October, The Hindu, Mint, Hindustan Times and the Economic Times reported. Hindustan Times says the plea alleges violation of the Court's order of 9 October, and names the home secretary and the Delhi Police chief. We have not seen the petition itself.

Other legal moves are running alongside. Senior advocate Menaka Guruswamy filed a habeas corpus petition against the detention of political leaders (Live Law), and a CJP member asked the Court to trace Dipke, Saurav Das and Ranka (The Hindu). A Delhi court rejected CJP leader Ratna Singh's plea for an FIR over alleged confinement by police (The Hindu, LawChakra). The Hindu adds that legal experts are questioning the mass detentions, and Mint has an explainer on preventive detention and how it differs from arrest. Delhi Police has not yet answered the contempt allegation in anything we have seen.

A special sitting on a Sunday is unusual. The central question is whether blanket closures of public transport are a proportionate way to keep order. Hindustan Times reported on Friday that the Court had remarked that "everyone does not have a car", which suggests the bench is alert to the burden on commuters. We expect the Court to ask the police and the Home Ministry to explain the shutdown, but that is our expectation, not a reported fact.

Preventive detentionLaw

Holding a person briefly, without charge, to stop an offence or disorder that police fear may happen. It is different from an arrest on suspicion of a crime already committed.

World · West AsiaPress ✓ 6 outlets · casualty counts are Saudi authorities'

Houthi strike on Riyadh's King Khalid airport kills 12 and wounds over 300; Trump says US "may" join Saudi retaliation

Saudi authorities said a Saturday attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 12 people and wounded more than 300, AP and DW reported. The Saudi-led coalition said Yemen's Houthis were responsible and called it a war crime. Trump called it "a terrible thing" and said the US may join Saudi strikes on the Houthis (Economic Times, Mint). Secretary of State Rubio said an American was killed. UN Secretary-General Guterres condemned the attack.

For India the effects are practical. A large Indian community works in Saudi Arabia, and The Hindu reports that flights between Kochi and Saudi destinations remain disrupted. Reuters says major conferences in the Kingdom will go ahead. Separately, the maritime agency UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz, and US Central Command said it disabled a cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman (FinancialJuice; single source for both). The risk to oil supply is why crude stays near $92.

Security · North-EastPress ✓ 4 outlets · tolls differ by count

Nine killed as Naga-Kuki clashes flare across three Manipur districts in 24 hours

Hindustan Times reports nine people killed in separate incidents of communal violence across three districts of Manipur within 24 hours, while The Hindu's earlier count was at least five. A woman was shot dead in Kamjong district and prohibitory orders were imposed (The Hindu, The Times of India), and Hindustan Times says a Kuki woman was killed in crossfire near the Chassad police gate junction.

The flare-up came as Manipur's Chief Minister met Prime Minister Narendra Modi in Delhi and said he wants to advance peace in the state, NDTV and ThePrint reported. The Naga and Kuki are two of the state's main communities, and the clashes involve groups from both communities. We have not seen an official toll from the state government, so treat the figures as outlets' counts.

Economy · CurrencyPress ✓ 5 outlets · rupee level from market data

RBI opens a special dollar window for oil companies as the rupee hovers near record lows

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is opening a special window to sell dollars to oil companies, Reuters and Moneycontrol reported. The Hindu BusinessLine says it is meant to meet the daily dollar needs of state-run oil firms as crude prices rise, and Aaj Tak says it opens on Monday, 12 October. The Financial Times says the central bank is trying to shore up the rupee as it nears record lows. The rupee ended at 96.73 to the dollar (down 0.05% on 10 October).

Oil companies buy dollars every day to pay for imported crude, so lumpy demand can push the rupee down. A direct supply from the RBI takes that demand off the open market. Details such as the size of the window are not yet confirmed. An SBI research note says the RBI should raise the repo rate by 0.50 percentage points soon (Aaj Tak, single source).
