Opposition marches to the Supreme Court and files a petition to pause the voter-roll revision; Congress claims 2.5 lakh took part

Rahul Gandhi and the INDIA bloc asked the Court for an independent audit of voter deletions under the SIR. The Court flagged defects in the filing, and the Chief Justice has kept his distance.

The opposition took its fight over the voter-roll revision to the Supreme Court's doorstep on Monday. The INDIA bloc, the opposition alliance, walked through central Delhi in what Congress called a "March for Democracy" (The Hindu). Hindustan Times reports that Rahul Gandhi, Priyanka Gandhi Vadra and Neha Bora, president of the student group AISA, led it, and that lawyers joined in large numbers. The Hindu says the show of unity met heavy police deployment but little interruption, and that Congress claims 2.5 lakh (250,000) people took part in the four-hour march. That figure is Congress's own, and the paper adds that most marchers were Congress supporters who had travelled from across the country. Before the march, Delhi closed six metro stations and restricted traffic on more than 20 stretches, CNA reports.

The petition targets the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. Hindustan Times reports that it lists 10 requests, among them a pause in the SIR and an independent audit of voter deletions, and accuses Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar of the "unilateral and arbitrary" use of the Commission's collective powers. The Hindu says Rahul Gandhi asks for an independent panel to examine the SIR and the decisions behind it, and for the Election Commission of India to hand over its records and official notings. Mint describes it as a public interest litigation (PIL). NDTV reports that the Supreme Court flagged 14 defects and a ₹220 shortfall in the filing; we have seen that from one outlet only.

The words were sharper than the paper. After the march Rahul Gandhi said "you cannot beat us, threaten us, frighten us" (The Economic Times), and likened the protest to a tandav and a "Shivji ki baraat", a wedding procession where everyone is welcome (The Hindu). BJP leaders, among them Shivraj Singh Chouhan and Anurag Thakur, called the remark "anti-Hindu" and the Congress leadership directionless (The Hindu); MP Nishikant Dubey criticised the march to the Court (NDTV). The alliance is not whole: the DMK, which has left the INDIA bloc, says it need not join Congress-led protests (The Hindu). Outside Delhi the argument rests on numbers: The Hindu reports that 13.60 lakh names were dropped from Punjab's final electoral rolls after the SIR, that Telangana Chief Minister Revanth Reddy questioned a voter decline despite population growth, and that Akhilesh Yadav alleges the Commission acts at the BJP's behest.

The bench itself has stayed out of the street. The Hindu and Hindustan Times reported that Chief Justice Surya Kant declined the leaders' request for a meeting, and in open court he said democratic and political protests must take place but the Court must not be dragged into them, The Hindu reports. Kerala Congress removed the person behind a social-media post aimed at the Chief Justice (The Economic Times, The Hindu). Nothing we reviewed says when the petition will be listed; the defects have to be cured first.

Politics · Public orderPress ✓ 2 outlets

Jantar Mantar is de-notified as a protest site; the Delhi High Court is asked to hear a challenge today

Delhi Police issued an order de-notifying Jantar Mantar, long Delhi's designated protest ground, on the recommendation of a Home Ministry committee, Hindustan Times reports. The Hindu says the move follows a four-page Supreme Court order of 9 October in which the bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant urged the government to look for a "spacious, open" alternative site. Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), said Delhi Police mentally tortured him and his family and that "Modi will denotify democracy itself" (The Hindu). A lawyer asked the Delhi High Court to hear a plea against the "closure" on Tuesday (The Hindu, single outlet). Hindustan Times adds that Delhi Police took down about 300 social media posts as "misinformation", and that the CJP plans a protest in Rajasthan over alleged house arrests.

The Court asked for a better site, and the government removed the old one; we have not seen a replacement named. That is our reading. Whether one appears will decide if this looks like relocation or restriction.

Markets · EconomyPress ✓ 5 outlets · oil prices from a market wire

Rupee surges 96 paise to 95.75 after RBI curbs, yet the Sensex falls 548 points as oil climbs

The rupee gained 96 paise to close at 95.75 against the US dollar on Monday, after touching 95.57 at its best and 95.96 at its worst, The Hindu reports. Reuters says it jumped about 1 per cent after the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) redirected oil companies' dollar demand and raised the cost of some forex hedges, and The Economic Times reports a similar 1 per cent climb. A Bloomberg headline says India is drawing a line in the sand for the rupee at 97. Banks have asked the RBI for clarity and relief on how the new rules work (Reuters, The Economic Times).

Shares went the other way. Business Standard reports that the Sensex shed 548 points and the Nifty 50 ended at 22,363, with FMCG and public-sector bank indices lagging; The Hindu blames an oil price spike and outflows by foreign institutional investors (FII). A market-news wire put WTI crude at $93.74 a barrel, up 2.06 per cent, and Brent at $106.65 (FinancialJuice, single source). Dear oil is the rupee's enemy, so Monday's relief rests on the RBI's rules rather than on the oil price.

World · West AsiaPress ✓ 6 outlets · casualty counts differ by outlet

Two Indian seafarers missing after ship attacks off Oman; Doval speaks to Iran's security chief as Riyadh airport stays shut

Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal said two Indian seafarers are missing and nine were rescued after a drone attack on a tanker at Sohar Anchorage in Oman, with the local coast guard searching (The Hindu). Hindustan Times reports that India's foreign ministry said attacks on commercial ships and seafarers in the Strait of Hormuz must cease, and that the Indian embassy in Muscat is working with Omani authorities and the ship managers. The Hindu says two ships were attacked on Sunday and that the Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy has warned merchant vessels against switching off their Automatic Identification System (AIS) to avoid detection. National Security Adviser Ajit Doval then spoke by phone to Mohsen Rezaei of Iran (Hindustan Times, Mint, The Hindu); the Iranian embassy in India said on X that the talks covered regional affairs and bilateral ties (Mint).

Riyadh is the other front. Saudi Arabia has extended the closure of King Khalid International Airport until 17 October after the Houthi attacks, Mint reports, and about 60 per cent of scheduled flights were expected to be cancelled on Monday, according to Reuters citing aviation data firm Cirium. The Times of India puts the death toll from the airport attack at 13, against the 12 cited here yesterday. Iran says it is still reviewing a US reply to its proposal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters, The Straits Times). Yesterday we reported the tanker Valthera's 11 Indian crew safe, on one source; Monday's reports do not name the ship, so we cannot tell if it is the same vessel.

Economy · PricesPress ✓ 2 outlets · figures attributed

Retail inflation rises to 5.54 per cent in September, the highest in the new series, strengthening the case for more RBI rate hikes

Retail inflation, measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI), rose to 5.54 per cent in September on costlier food, the highest in the new index series released in January, The Hindu reports. Reuters says the figure nears the RBI's tolerance ceiling and boosts the case for further rate hikes. The RBI aims for 4 per cent inflation with a band of 2 to 6 per cent. Higher rates make loans costlier and usually raise the monthly instalments (EMI) on floating-rate loans.

The reading arrived on the day the RBI moved to protect the rupee and as oil rose; a weak rupee and dear crude both raise import costs. The next signals are the October inflation reading and the RBI's next policy review, whose date we have not confirmed.
