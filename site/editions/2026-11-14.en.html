Personal data of 818 ‘Everyone’s Startup’ Phase 2 participants leaked… second incident in five months after Phase 1

The Ministry of SMEs and Startups confirmed it on the 13th. Names, phone numbers and emails were exposed, and one private company taking part in the program was hacked.

The Ministry of SMEs and Startups said on the 13th that personal data of 818 participants had leaked from Phase 2 of its startup support program ‘Everyone’s Startup’. What leaked were names, phone numbers and email addresses. The ministry’s own systems were not breached; one of the 50 private artificial intelligence (AI) solution providers taking part in the program came under an outside hacking attack, and the data got out that way. The incident comes from Phase 2, which held its launch ceremony last October. In June, the email addresses and startup ideas of 5,000 Phase 1 participants in the same program had also leaked, so this is a second incident in five months. This one is smaller than Phase 1 but includes phone numbers.

In Phase 1, a joint investigation by the ministry and the National Intelligence Service concluded that non-public information, though not visible on the service screen, was contained in the API (a channel through which programs exchange data) that the server sends down, and that the encryption key for unlocking it was exposed along with it. This time, it has not been confirmed by what route the hacked provider was breached, or whether other participants or materials such as business plans also leaked beyond the 818. What the ministry has said goes only as far as confirming signs of a leak; the intrusion route and who is responsible will become clear only when the investigation results come out.

Korean and Chinese foreign ministers meet in Beijing for first time in three months… discuss Korean Peninsula issues ahead of APEC

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun held foreign ministerial talks with Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi at the Diaoyutai State Guesthouse in Beijing on the 13th. It came three months after Wang’s visit to Seoul in August. In opening remarks, Cho said, “The Korean Peninsula issue is a vital interest of the Republic of Korea,” and that peace and stability on the Peninsula are a common interest of both countries, and called for continuing to strengthen strategic communication to advance the situation. Wang said Korea–China relations had returned to a normal track of improvement and development, and said 10 million people-to-people exchanges are expected. The talks came ahead of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit to be held next week in Shenzhen, China. On the way out of the country, Cho explained that the talks would cover what the two countries must coordinate ahead of APEC and the North Korea issue, and said it was not very likely that China would invite North Korea to APEC. It has not been confirmed whether the two ministers issued a joint statement or announcement, or what specific agenda items were agreed and how far.

Korea lose 2-3 to Saudi Arabia… first time in 42 years conceding three to the Saudis

The Korean national football team lost 2-3 to Saudi Arabia in a friendly at Jassim bin Hamad Stadium in Doha, Qatar, on the 13th, local time. Having conceded an opening goal in first-half stoppage time, Korea levelled early in the second half through Oh Hyun-gyu. But they soon fell behind again and conceded one more within three minutes, falling to 1-3. In the 24th minute of the second half, Lee Jae-sung, receiving a cutback from Hwang Hee-chan, pulled one back, but Korea could not close the gap further. Korea conceding three goals to Saudi Arabia is the first time since 1984, 42 years ago, and losing to Saudi Arabia is the first time since a 0-1 defeat in the World Cup final qualifying round in August 2005, 21 years ago. With this match, the all-time A-match record stands at 5 wins, 9 draws and 6 losses.

Interim manager Robert Moreno said after the match that it was a “disappointing result” and that the aim was to prepare a turnaround in the match against Qatar on the 17th. Before the match, rumors of a rift among the squad had circulated, and the squad had issued a statement saying they would stay together. Son Heung-min started and played the full match, but the 60th goal in A-matches did not come. Moreno’s contract ends at the end of this month, and what happens after that has not yet been announced.

Court dismisses Belift Lab and Source Music’s 2.5 billion won damages claims against Min Hee-jin

Civil Division 12 of the Seoul Western District Court (presiding judge Kim Jin-young) on the 13th dismissed, in each case, the damages claims that Belift Lab and Source Music, labels under Hybe, brought against Min Hee-jin, former CEO of ADOR. Belift Lab claimed 2 billion won, saying it was harmed by Min’s remarks to the effect that ‘ILLIT copied NewJeans’, and Source Music claimed 500 million won, for a combined 2.5 billion won. A counterclaim for 5 billion won in damages that Min filed against Belift Lab was also dismissed. Litigation costs are to be borne by Belift Lab and Source Music, who sued, and by Min, who filed the counterclaim, each for themselves.

Belift Lab and Source Music expressed regret over the ruling and said they would appeal immediately. Min’s side said it intended to put an end to the draining controversy. It has not yet been confirmed on what grounds the court rejected the claims, or whether it weighed the truth of the remarks. As this is a first-instance ruling, the matter can be contested again on appeal. In February, Min also won a first-instance ruling in the 25.5 billion won put option lawsuit against Hybe.

After U.S. envoys meet Putin, European role in Ukraine peace talks raised… prisoner exchange pushed

U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Russian President Putin on the 12th. Russian presidential aide Ushakov said the talks were substantive, constructive, frank and productive. The two then met Ukrainian President Zelensky in Ireland, and Zelensky said that as a result prisoners would be released, welcoming Europe’s direct participation in the next talks. Kushner said the U.S. is moving toward the largest prisoner exchange of this war.

The EU, which held an informal summit in Dublin on the 13th, said that five countries — Germany, France, Britain, Italy and Poland — and the EU would take part in the next talks as Europe’s representatives. However, when and where the talks will be held, whether Russia has accepted European participation, and how large the prisoner exchange will be and when it will happen have not been confirmed.
