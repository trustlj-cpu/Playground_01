KOSPI closes at 5,469.47… Kosdaq plunges 3.26%

The KOSPI fell 1.46% and gave up the 5,500 level, while the Kosdaq plunged 3.26%. This week’s Samsung Electronics and SK hynix earnings, interest-rate decisions in major countries and the U.S. midterm elections are cited as the next variables.

Korea Economic Daily reported that on the 26th the KOSPI closed down 1.46% at around 5,469 and the Kosdaq fell 3.26%. Bloomingbit reported the KOSPI close as 5,469.47, and MBC reported that the KOSPI had slid to the 5,400 level for the first time in about six months while the Kosdaq plunged by more than 3%. According to Yonhap, the KOSPI opened that day down 51.25 points (0.92%) at 5,499.05 and the Kosdaq opened down 9.52 points (1.27%) at 742.17; the KOSPI then narrowed its losses, moved around the unchanged line and at one point turned higher during the session. Korea Economic Daily reported that the KOSPI fell back to the 5,400 level early in the session before narrowing its losses, with foreign investors and institutions buying. Yonhap reported that the Kosdaq was down 38% from its peak and that SK Securities assessed it as ‘building a floor.’

Kyunghyang Shinmun called this week, when Samsung Electronics and SK hynix earnings overlap with interest-rate decisions in major countries, a ‘super week’ and asked whether the KOSPI will build a floor and rebound; it reported that the KOSPI had barely held the 5,500 level last week after a roller-coaster market. In its Market View, Yonhap listed whether Samsung Electronics and SK hynix will announce additional shareholder returns as something to watch. Korea Economic Daily reported that Goldman Sachs cut its KOSPI target from 12,000 to 9,000, and Chosun Ilbo reported that Korea Investment & Securities said the market must keep open the possibility of the 5,200 level and that a quick recovery is hard to expect. According to Chosun Ilbo, Daishin Securities projected that the KOSPI should be wary of 6,700 in a ‘blue sweep’ and of 4,950 in a ‘red sweep’ at the U.S. midterm elections.

NEC special counsel to inspect 2.47 million ballots stored at Olympic Park on the 28th

Yonhap, Kyunghyang Shinmun, Korea Economic Daily and SBS reported on the 26th that the NEC special counsel will inspect 2.47 million ballots stored at Olympic Park, along with CCTV and other items, on the 28th. Korea Economic Daily wrote in its breaking-news headline ‘voluntary inspection of ballots and items to be carried out’, and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the special counsel will conduct an ‘equipment and documents check’. SBS reported that the targets are 2.47 million ballots and CCTV, and Joseilbo reported that 600 people will be deployed for the work. Yonhap stated in its headline that the purpose of the inspection is ‘verifying additions, subtractions and forgery’.

Lula wins a fourth term in Brazil’s runoff… Bolsonaro rejects the result

Yonhap reported that Brazil’s electoral commission announced Lula’s win in the runoff, giving him a fourth term overall, and Chosun Ilbo and Dong-A Ilbo carried the same in breaking news. According to Yonhap, with 10% of the votes counted it was Lula 50.41% to Bolsonaro 49.59%, and at the halfway point the race was still tight at 50.1% to 49.9%. Chosun Ilbo reported that with 85% counted Lula led with 51.43% and that Brazilian pollsters projected Lula as the likely winner. The Guardian reported that Lula won a fourth term after a historic upset of the far-right Flávio Bolsonaro.

Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that voting hours in this runoff were extended by two hours for the first time ever, with Lula’s side citing ‘traffic restrictions’ and Bolsonaro’s side ‘bad weather’. Yonhap reported that Bolsonaro said he cannot accept the election result and would ask for it to be annulled. Chosun Ilbo reported that the result put the brakes on Latin America’s ‘pro-Trump swing to the right’. Bloomberg TV aired a segment under the title that Brazilian markets were bracing for a plunge on Lula’s victory.

Political sparring after Oh Se-hoon’s acquittal in the appellate trial… ruling party: ‘politicization of the judiciary’, People Power Party: ‘fabricated prosecution’

Chosun Ilbo reported on the 26th that Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok called Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon’s acquittal in the appellate trial the ‘politicization of the judiciary’ and that the ruling party’s Choi Min-hee said ‘we should introduce AI judges’. Korea Economic Daily reported that opposition from the ruling camp to the appellate acquittal continues and that calls to ‘introduce AI judges’ have emerged. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that National Court Administration chief Noh Kyung-pil answered at the National Assembly Legislation and Judiciary Committee’s comprehensive audit that ‘it is not appropriate to say what the Cho Hee-de judiciary did’, and Dong-A Ilbo reported that the chief said ‘the Supreme Court does not interfere in trials’.

Yonhap reported on the 25th that Mayor Oh’s case had been reversed to an acquittal and that the next hurdle is the city council, where the Democratic Party holds ‘two-thirds of the seats’; on the 26th it reported that People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk said, ‘The acquittal is a crushing blow to the special counsel—this is what a fabricated prosecution looks like.’ Dong-A Ilbo reported that after Mayor Oh said in effect, ‘The general election is approaching; I will do my best for unity,’ the opposition camp is already stirring.

KB Kookmin Bank hack data leak 119 → 2,210 customers… corrected 24 days after announcement

Yonhap reported on the 26th that KB Kookmin Bank said it had confirmed an additional 2,091 customers whose information was leaked in the hack, raising the number of victims from the 119 first disclosed to 2,210. Yonhap noted in its headline that the correction came 24 days after the hack announcement. SBS reported that the bank said it was ‘sorry to its customers’, and Dong-A Ilbo reported in a headline that it was ‘not an additional attack’. In a separate article, Dong-A Ilbo criticized, ‘They said 119, now 2210… a total failure in personal data protection’, and reported that Mirae Asset Life Insurance also had customer data leaked.

In an editorial, Chosun Ilbo pointed out that it took three weeks just to decide who would investigate the hack—the police or the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency—and also ran an article saying the National Office of Investigation and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency had passed the case back and forth for three weeks. Kyunghyang Shinmun said the agency had shaken its head over the ‘AI hacking case in the financial sector’ and asked whether it is not conducting, or unable to conduct, its ‘first investigation’.
