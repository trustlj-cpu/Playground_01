ANC takes its fight to field 181 candidates to the Constitutional Court, but the hearing date has slipped

The party says a missed "submit as final" click was a software step, not a legal requirement. The IEC says the lists were never lodged in time.

The African National Congress has asked the Constitutional Court to let it contest several municipalities in the 4 November elections, after the Electoral Court rejected its appeal, according to TimesLIVE, BusinessDay, Briefly and EWN. The hearing had been set for Wednesday 7 October, but EWN reported on Monday that it had been pushed out by another week, which points to Monday 12 October; DailyDrop has that date from one outlet only and advises checking the court roll. The dispute turns on the 28 August deadline: the Electoral Commission of South Africa says the ANC did not press "submit as final" on the online nomination system before 17:00, so its candidate lists in at least six municipalities were never lodged. About 181 ANC candidates are affected.

In papers reported on Saturday, the ANC argues that section 14 of the Municipal Electoral Act does not require that click, which it calls a software function, and that the IEC's software cannot override constitutional rights. It also says its administrators could not complete the step before the deadline for reasons outside their control. The IEC's chief electoral officer, Sy Mamabolo, has said the exclusion follows from the party's own failure. DailyDrop has not seen the court's directions or an indication of when judgment will follow.

US ambassador Bozell hits back after Lamola tells him to "get out of the kitchen"

Foreign Minister Ronald Lamola told the Sunday Times that US ambassador Leo Brent Bozell III should "get out of the kitchen" if he no longer believes in diplomatic engagement, after Pretoria issued Bozell a third démarche in nine months, according to TimesLIVE, EWN, eNCA, BusinessTech and Briefly. A démarche is a formal diplomatic protest short of expelling an ambassador. DIRCO complained about Bozell's remarks on what it calls the "white genocide" falsehood and his September meeting with AfriForum.

Bozell replied on X that Lamola had personally insulted him, and said criticising the remark could itself prompt another démarche. In a Fox News interview broadcast on Sunday, he said the United States would not give up on the partnership and remained optimistic about common ground, EWN reported. Lamola also pointed out that the US had expelled South Africa's former ambassador, Ebrahim Rasool, without warning. DailyDrop reads the exchange as relations at a low point, but with both sides still saying they want engagement.

S&P Global PMI falls to 49.0 in September, the sharpest private-sector contraction since December

The S&P Global South Africa Purchasing Managers' Index dropped to 49.0 in September from 50.5 in August, putting private-sector activity back in contraction, according to Reuters via Engineering News and Polity, and Trading Economics. A reading below 50 means shrinking activity. New orders fell at the sharpest rate in two and a half years as customers held back spending amid uncertainty about fuel prices and the economy, the reports said.

There were some bright spots: exports rose for a fourth straight month and business expectations hit a four-month high. Input costs rose sharply on higher fuel prices, and supplier delivery times lengthened at the fastest rate since February 2024, partly because of delays at the Durban port and Middle East disruption. Employment was broadly stable. The reading is not directly comparable to the Absa manufacturing PMI, which rose to 50.7 last week.

Rand little changed near R16.66 on Monday as Wall Street rises and oil slips

The rand traded at about R16.66 to the dollar on Monday evening, up 0.07% from Friday's R16.65, according to the Rio Times. A Reuters report carried by Engineering News had it at 16.6825 early in the session, about 0.2% weaker than the previous close, and another daily roundup put it near R16.74. The sources differ by up to eight cents, depending on the time. The Rio Times said the All Share traded near 108,200 to 108,300 points and that an official close had not been published, so DailyDrop leaves the index ear box at a dash.

In New York, the S&P 500 closed on Monday at 7,773.95, up 0.66%. WTI crude fell 1.84% to US$89.43 a barrel and gold slipped 0.13% to US$4,156.80 an ounce. Lower oil is welcome news for local fuel prices, but Wednesday's increase is already fixed. The Rio Times noted the rand was weighed by a stronger dollar and rising global bond yields over the previous week.

Special-vote applications close at 17:00 on Monday 12 October, the IEC reminds voters

Registered voters who cannot vote at their station on 4 November can apply for a special vote until 17:00 on Monday 12 October, according to SAnews, EWN, SABC News and the Oudtshoorn Courant. There are two types. Home-visit special votes are for people who cannot get to a voting station because of their physical condition. Voting-station special votes are for people who can attend a station but cannot do so on election day. Approved voters in the second group vote on 2 and 3 November.

Applications can be made online at elections.org.za, by sending an ID number by SMS to 32249 at R1 per message (for voting-station special votes only), or by delivering a form to a local IEC office. Someone else may deliver the completed form for you. The IEC will send an SMS with the result once an application has been processed; voters who get no reply can still vote on election day. SAnews also reported that Cabinet urged people who lost their IDs to replace them before the election.
