Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer, the Vatican says, after surgery to remove part of his right lung

The announcement came late on Tuesday. The Vatican called the operation successful, and a cardinal is to lead a Mass on Wednesday while the pope recovers in hospital.

The Vatican announced late on Tuesday evening, Rome time, which is also South African time, that Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer, according to AP, NBC News, NPR, The Hill, the Evening Standard, Politico and others. He had been due to enter a Rome hospital that day for surgery on a nodule, a small abnormal growth, in his lung, as IOL, SABC News and The Citizen had reported on Monday. Le Monde and De Telegraaf reported that the nodule taken out turned out to be cancerous. The Vatican said the lower lobe of his right lung was removed and that the operation was completed successfully (The Hill, The Straits Times, Punch). The right lung has three lobes, so one of the three is gone. Australian outlets reported that he is recovering in hospital. The items DailyDrop read give no prognosis and do not say whether further treatment is planned. Indonesia's detikHealth names the type as adenocarcinoma, but we have not seen a second outlet or the Vatican say so, so we treat it as unconfirmed.

GMA News and the Philippine Daily Inquirer reported that Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle will preside over a Mass on Wednesday while the pope is in hospital. The New York Times reported an outpouring of good wishes from world leaders, and OSV News reported prayers from US bishops and lawmakers. Leo is the first pope from the United States, and the Times described the diagnosis as a test for a papacy still taking shape. La Repubblica wrote that the Vatican is stunned but cautious and will have to reconsider its agenda and travel, and Infobae reported that Argentina's government is keeping its preparations for a papal visit in place while it waits for word from Rome. Folha de S.Paulo noted that the Vatican now speaks about a pope's health through detailed bulletins, where it once stayed silent about cancer. What to watch is the next medical bulletin, how long he stays in hospital and whether any trips are put off. A diagnosis is not a forecast, and nothing published so far says how advanced the disease is. Because the Vatican speaks for the pope, its wording is the baseline, and anything that goes beyond it from unnamed sources should be read with care.

World · OilPress ✓ 5 outlets · damage figures from Saudi sources

Houthi attack causes a limited fire at an Aramco building in Dhahran as Brent stays above $100

Houthi missiles struck near Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran on Tuesday. Reuters reported loud booms near the site and Bloomberg reported that the headquarters was hit. Saudi state news, citing an energy ministry official, said the attack caused a limited fire and no casualties (FinancialJuice). The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted missiles heading for Dhahran and Haradh, and Saudi civil defence said debris from two intercepted missiles injured four people in Riyadh. The coalition had ordered an oil facility in Sanaa evacuated before it struck Houthi targets in the Yemeni capital (Reuters, the FT, the BBC). Brent crude settled at $105.69 a barrel, down 0.9%, and WTI at $94.14, up 0.4% (FinancialJuice). Reuters said oil climbed again on Wednesday on Saudi supply risk and Russian diesel flows. Reuters also quoted officials saying Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks on shipping before the US vote.

For South Africans the link is the pump. IOL reported Minister Barbara Creecy promising no more nasty fuel-tax surprises, with President Cyril Ramaphosa hinting at possible relief. Moneyweb quoted her saying that an increase in the Road Accident Fund levy in the fuel price will not happen on her watch. Neither report, as we read them, says when relief might come.

Labour · PoliticsPress ✓ 3 outlets

Cosatu's Losi admits the federation is “out of touch” as the race for its leadership intensifies

Zingiswa Losi, president of the Congress of South African Trade Unions (Cosatu), called for unity at the federation's 15th national congress despite divisions in its alliance, SABC News reported on Tuesday. News24 reported that she admitted Cosatu is “out of touch” as the contest for its leadership heats up. The Citizen reported that Gwede Mantashe irked the gathering, with delegates decrying what they called the ANC's “Big Brother” attitude towards the federation. DailyDrop has not seen, in these reports, who is standing for which post.

The congress is due to run until Thursday 15 October and to elect new leaders, as SABC News reported earlier this week. It had already been adjourned once and reconvened after a row involving Mantashe, which News24 and SABC News covered on Monday. Cosatu, the ANC and the South African Communist Party (SACP) form the governing alliance, so a federation that says it has lost touch, and delegates who resent the ANC's tone, make for an uneasy run-up to the local elections. What to watch is the vote itself, and whether the ANC and the SACP accept the outcome in public.

Policing · InquiryPress ✓ 4 outlets · hearing dates not given

Madlanga Commission seeks a statement from Sibiya's associate as his bail case returns to court

The Madlanga Commission, the judicial inquiry into alleged criminality and corruption in policing and the justice system, wants a statement from Serge Cabonge, whom eNCA calls an associate of Shadrack Sibiya. SABC News and The Citizen reported that it will question Cabonge over allegations in recordings linked to Sibiya. Separately, SABC News reported that the commission has applied for an in-camera hearing for a Dr Sifolo, and The Citizen reported that it is seeking closed-door testimony from the intelligence coordinator. The reports do not say when Cabonge will appear, what the recordings contain or whether the closed-door requests have been granted.

Sibiya, who resigned from the South African Police Service (SAPS) earlier this week, was back at the Randburg Magistrate's Court on Tuesday for the continuation of his bail hearing, IOL and SABC News reported. SABC News warned that its live coverage contained explicit content. A bail hearing decides only whether an accused person may await trial at liberty, not whether he is guilty. Resigning ended his employment but not the criminal process, and the commission's work runs on a separate track. DailyDrop has not seen the bail ruling.

Markets · EconomyPress ✓ 5 outlets · closing levels not confirmed

Chip selloff drags Wall Street lower as bank results arrive, while China's exports leap 36%

US stocks ended lower on Tuesday as chipmakers fell. The Nasdaq closed down and the Dow Jones Industrial Average ended a three-day advance, according to The Straits Times, Zonebourse and Clarín. Reuters said chip shares extended their slide on a report about Chinese memory chips and on nerves over AI spending, after South Korea's Kospi sank more than 5% earlier in the day (AP). Bank earnings began. Citigroup and Goldman Sachs beat profit forecasts and Wells Fargo's profit rose, while JPMorgan posted what Reuters called a rare profit miss. Bank of America was fined more than $1.2 billion by US authorities for failing to police suspicious activity (Reuters, the FT, Bloomberg). Williams and Collins of the US Federal Reserve (Fed) both said another rate rise this year is possible or appropriate (Bloomberg, Reuters). US inflation figures are due today. DailyDrop could not confirm Tuesday's closing levels from two outlets, so no index figures are shown.

China's customs data showed September exports up 36.3% on a year earlier in dollar terms, against a poll of 25.5%, and a trade surplus of $133.5 billion against a poll of $118.1 billion (FinancialJuice). Reuters said exports blew past forecasts and the FT put the rise at 36%, driven by AI-related shipments. Producer prices rose 5.0% on the year, while consumer prices rose 0.9%, below the 1.0% poll (FinancialJuice, Reuters). The International Monetary Fund (IMF) warned this week of risks to the world economy from a correction in AI shares, war and debt (Reuters). Asian shares edged up on Wednesday as the chip selloff eased, Reuters reported.
