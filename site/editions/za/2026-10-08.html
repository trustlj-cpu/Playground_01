Western Cape school-bus operators halt learner transport over diesel costs, then agree to resume on Wednesday

About 21,900 learners were affected on the first day of term four. The education department says it will respond on Friday and that the province is the only one discussing diesel relief.

Operators who ferry pupils to school in the Western Cape suspended services on Tuesday 6 October, the first day of the fourth term, saying they can no longer cover rising diesel costs at their fixed contract rates, according to EWN, the provincial government and IOL. The Western Cape Education Department (WCED) said it received reports that 21,934 learners across the province were affected. The operators said they had received no fuel subsidy, and the department accused them of using learners as "pawns".

After talks with Education MEC Jaco Londt, the Small Bus Operators Council agreed to resume services on Wednesday 7 October, EWN reported. The WCED said it would give a "substantive response" on Friday 9 October after an internal meeting on the cost implications, and that the Western Cape is the only province actively discussing diesel cost relief, citing budget limits. Petrol and diesel prices rose on Wednesday by R3.33 and up to R3.24 a litre. DailyDrop has not seen the operators' full demands or a figure for the subsidy they want.

Durban: about a thousand asylum seekers leave Home Affairs queue as groups with sticks arrive

A line of about a thousand foreign nationals formed on Wednesday 7 October along Che Guevara Road, leading to the Home Affairs Durban Refugee Reception Centre, after the department told offices not to turn away people who want to apply for asylum, according to EWN, Briefly, eNCA and SABC News. The directive followed a recent Constitutional Court ruling that struck down parts of the Refugees Act, including a requirement that applicants without documents show "good cause" first. Armed police were present.

Then groups carrying sticks and sjamboks arrived, and the people in the queue left out of fear, EWN reported. Nkosikhona Ndabandaba, who leads the group Insizwa Nobunsizwa, said it came to demonstrate peacefully against the court ruling. Police advised migrants to leave the area for their own safety. IOL reported that access had been blocked by the March and March movement. Briefly reported that the US Consulate General in Durban issued a security alert on 7 October about reported looting and violence in the CBD, which some residents disputed. DailyDrop has not seen a count of arrests or injuries for Wednesday. The Inter-Ministerial Committee on Migration has said the ruling does not grant refugee status automatically.

MK Party says Zuma is "well and alive" and discharged, as accounts of his hospital visit still conflict

MK Party spokesperson Sifiso Mahlangu said on Wednesday that former president Jacob Zuma, 84, was at a routine check-up, not in hospital for an illness, and was back at his Nkandla home, according to EWN, TimesLIVE and BusinessDay. "He is very much well and alive; it was a regular check-up," he said, adding that Zuma has two such check-ups a year and will continue campaigning.

The Witness newspaper had reported on Tuesday that Zuma was admitted to hospital after falling ill, and an independent source said he went to a private hospital in Msinga after a Monday MK rally. TimesLIVE and BusinessDay led with the conflicting accounts. DailyDrop has seen no medical statement, hospital confirmation or public appearance since, so the cause remains unconfirmed. The party said the visit took place the day before its statement.

Rand weakens to about R16.70 as fuel prices rise; Wall Street slips and gold falls 1.1%

The rand traded at 16.5850 to the dollar early on Wednesday and had weakened to 16.7025 by 13:01 GMT, as a stronger dollar and higher oil prices weighed, according to Reuters reports carried by CNBC Africa. DailyDrop could not confirm a closing level for the rand or an official All Share close, so the ear boxes show an intraday figure and a dash. The Rio Times noted the rand had lost around 2% over the previous week.

In New York, the S&P 500 closed at 7,801.77, down 0.22%. WTI crude fell 1.30% to US$88.28 and gold dropped 1.11% to US$4,140.70 an ounce. Trading Economics put Brent near US$102.60 on 5 October after a week of swings between roughly US$100 and US$108, and said US President Donald Trump had rejected Iran's seven-day proposal. Goldman Sachs estimates Gulf exports recovered to 23.3 million barrels a day in September, including some shipped with tracking off.

Economy · FuelPress ✓ 4 outlets · rates per the DMPR

First day of the new fuel prices: petrol up R3.33, diesel R2.84-R3.24, and knock-on effects begin

New prices took effect on Wednesday 7 October: petrol 95 up R3.33 a litre to R30.25 inland, petrol 93 up R3.12, 0.05% diesel up R2.84 and 0.005% diesel up R3.24, according to IOL, TopAuto, the Rio Times and Reuters. The Rio Times called it a record high for petrol. ETM Analytics told Reuters that "the combination of much higher oil prices and the depreciation of the ZAR has heavily impacted prices".

The effects are already visible. Western Cape school-bus operators stopped work on Tuesday over diesel, and IOL reported that Cape Town's MyCiTi and Golden Arrow bus commuters would avoid an immediate fare rise. Golden Arrow burns about 25 million litres of diesel a year, so each R1 on the diesel price costs it roughly R25 million, EWN reported earlier this year, and it says further fare reviews may be needed in coming months. No taxi fare changes have been confirmed.
