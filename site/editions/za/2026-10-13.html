Deputy police commissioner Shadrack Sibiya resigns while facing sexual-offence charges

The resignation was reported on Monday and SAPS ended his contract with immediate effect. He was already suspended and had denied the allegations, the BBC reported.

Shadrack Sibiya, South Africa's suspended Deputy National Commissioner of Police, has resigned from the South African Police Service (SAPS), according to the BBC, The Citizen, SABC News, IOL and eNCA. The news emerged on Monday 12 October. SABC News reported that his SAPS contract was terminated with immediate effect and that a disciplinary case against him was still open when he left. The Citizen recalled that he was first placed on special leave in July 2025 and was later suspended. The BBC reported that he is charged with sexual offences and had denied grooming a 16-year-old and raping an 18-year-old. Those allegations have not been tested at trial. DailyDrop could not confirm from two outlets whether he is in custody or out on bail today. The deputy national commissioner is one of the most senior posts in the service, directly below the National Commissioner, which is why a case against its holder drew wide attention. The Citizen's daily round-up led its Monday bulletin with the resignation, and eNCA ran a reaction piece headlined with a remark that it was "just the start of the momentum". DailyDrop has not been able to read what that remark referred to beyond the headline, so we do not build on it. Police have not, in the items we read, said how the vacancy will be filled or what happens to the open disciplinary file now that he has gone.

The manner of his exit is already being questioned. In a column, IOL's editor Lance Witten wrote that the dishonourable end to Sibiya's SAPS career, with the service letting him resign rather than face dismissal, raises hard questions about accountability and public trust. It helps to separate the two tracks. The disciplinary process decides whether an officer keeps his job, and it is run by his employer. The criminal case decides guilt, and it is run by the prosecution and the courts. A resignation ends the first track, and it does not end the second. EWN reported on 1 October that his bail hearing was continuing at the Randburg Magistrates' Court, and Daily Maverick covered the same application. None of the Monday reports DailyDrop saw gave the next court date, and none said who will now act in his post. Readers should also keep in mind that a charge is not a finding of guilt, and Sibiya has denied the allegations, according to the BBC. What to watch is whether police leadership explains why the disciplinary process stopped when he resigned, whether the court sets a date soon, and whether the service names a replacement. Readers who follow policing should treat today's news as the end of one process, not of the case, and should expect more comment from politicians and civil-society groups over the coming days.

Constitutional Court reserves judgment on the ANC's bid to put 181 candidates back on the ballot

The Constitutional Court heard the African National Congress (ANC) on Monday and reserved judgment, according to SABC News, IOL and The Citizen. The party is appealing against the exclusion of 181 of its candidates from next month's local government elections. SABC News said the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) argues the ANC missed the deadline for submitting its candidate lists. The Citizen reported that the ANC risks having its candidate missing from six municipalities.

Earlier reporting by EWN and Business Day described the dispute as turning on the IEC system's "submit as final" step. EWN reported that the 181 candidates were 130 on proportional-representation lists and 51 ward candidates, and that the Electoral Court had already dismissed the party's appeal. The ANC says it completed the forms, uploaded documents and paid deposits, and that the button is a software function, not a legal requirement. In court, IOL quoted the ANC's counsel comparing the matter to a lecturer who would not refuse to mark a draft. SABC News said the DA warned that a ruling in the ANC's favour could create significant administrative difficulties. EWN reported that the IEC has said it cannot hold the 4 November vote in the affected municipalities if the matter is not settled in time, because ballots are already being printed. No date for judgment has been reported.

Zuma is expected to leave hospital, but accounts of his condition conflict

IOL reported on Monday that former president Jacob Zuma was expected to be discharged from hospital that day, and that a source close to him dismissed reports that he was in a critical condition. SABC News quoted Mzwanele Manyi, spokesperson for the Jacob Zuma Foundation, as saying Zuma was recovering well, and Manyi thanked the public for their good wishes. Minister in the Presidency Khumbudzo Ntshavheni said only Zuma's doctors can speak about his health, with his consent, IOL reported. eNCA reported that he remained in hospital ahead of the launch of the uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party's manifesto, and Cape Town ETC wrote that conflicting reports were fuelling uncertainty.

The Citizen reported that the Foundation also criticised President Cyril Ramaphosa, saying his commitment should show in practical and meaningful action, and SABC News carried the Foundation's complaint that the Department of Public Works has neglected Zuma's Nkandla home. IOL and Financial Mail both wrote that his stay has opened questions about who would lead the MK Party in future, with IOL naming Duduzane Zuma in that debate. DailyDrop has not confirmed that he has left hospital, and no official medical statement has been published in the reports seen. The manifesto launch date was not given in the items we read.

Cosatu congress resumes amid a row over Mantashe, with new leaders due by Thursday

The 15th national congress of the Congress of South African Trade Unions (Cosatu) was reconvened after an adjournment, and eNCA carried Ramaphosa's address to delegates live. News24 reported that Gwede Mantashe's refusal to speak before the South African Communist Party (SACP) set off more chaos at the gathering, and SABC News reported that Mantashe walked out; eNCA aired a segment on him being accused of undermining Cosatu.

SABC News quoted Parks as saying most of the issues that forced the adjournment have been resolved. The congress is due to run until Thursday and is expected to elect a new leadership. The federation is a key ally of the governing ANC, so a bruising leadership contest matters to the party ahead of the 4 November local elections. DailyDrop has not seen the names of any candidates for the top posts in the reports we read. DailyDrop did not see a report that explains exactly what the SACP slot involved. The outcome matters because Cosatu, the SACP and the ANC form the governing alliance, and public rows between them tend to spill into the elections. Delegates, according to SABC News, are expected to vote before the congress closes, so the picture should be clearer within days.

Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh and a disputed Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire" keep oil prices high

Oil rose again on Monday as attacks on Saudi Arabia and a claimed energy ceasefire in Ukraine kept supply fears alive, IOL and Bloomberg reported. FinancialJuice reported that the Houthis said they fired ballistic missiles at Riyadh airport on Monday, and that the Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed a missile aimed at the capital. Reuters reported that airlines cancelled flights to Saudi Arabia after the airport attacks. Moneyweb reported that Saturday's strike killed 12 people and wounded 309, and that an attack on Sunday killed one and injured 11. FinancialJuice recorded Brent crude settling at $106.65 a barrel, up 1.84%, and US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) for November at $93.74, up 2.06%.

Reuters reported that Ukraine accused Russia of violating the energy ceasefire announced by US President Donald Trump, and that the European Union (EU) adopted nearly 1,600 new sanctions listings against Russia. The BBC and FAZ noted that Moscow has not confirmed the truce. For South Africans the link is fuel. The Citizen reported that President Cyril Ramaphosa is weighing relief for motorists while Minister Creecy rejects a higher fuel levy, and Moneyweb quoted the Boxer retailer's chief executive citing rising oil costs as pressure on shoppers. Monday's Wall Street closes were not confirmed by two outlets when we went to press, so the S&P 500 figure below is Friday's, up 0.59%. Monday's closes will be in tomorrow's edition.
