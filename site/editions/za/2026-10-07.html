Conflicting reports over Zuma hospital visit as MK Party plays it down as a routine check-up

The Witness reported on Tuesday afternoon that the former president was admitted after falling ill; another source said he was taken to a private hospital in Msinga after a Monday rally.

The Witness newspaper reported on Tuesday afternoon that former president Jacob Zuma, 84, leader of the uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party, was admitted to a hospital after apparently falling ill earlier in the day, with an eyewitness saying he arrived by ambulance, according to TimesLIVE, BusinessDay, EWN and Briefly. An independent source told the media he was rushed to a private hospital in Msinga, KwaZulu-Natal, after an MK rally on Monday. Briefly noted that the nature of any illness has not been disclosed, and outlets differ on the facility.

The MK Party's account was that Zuma had attended a routine medical check-up, which it says he has twice a year, and that his campaign programme for the 4 November local government elections continues. A party spokesperson, Sifiso Mahlangu, was quoted as describing the visit as regular. DailyDrop has not seen a medical statement, hospital confirmation or Zuma's own comment. Zuma's health has been a recurring question since reports of falls in 2024, so the dispute over the facts matters for the party's campaign.

Maynier confirms an open criminal investigation into Sasol over alleged dumping into the Klipspruit River

Environment minister David Maynier told Parliament that the allegations that Sasol Secunda dumped hazardous waste from its Benfield operations into the Klipspruit River catchment, which feeds the Vaal River, are part of an ongoing criminal investigation, according to a written reply reported by Daily Investor, BusinessTech and TopAuto. He said releasing details before a decision on prosecuting would be premature. The reply was to MP Noluthando Makasi, who asked what scientific methods investigators used when no river samples were taken at the time.

The case rests on former Sasol employee Ian Erasmus, who alleged in 2019 that off-specification chemical solutions and washwater were diverted into stormwater systems between 2012 and 2019. A six-count indictment was filed in 2022 and provisionally withdrawn in July 2025 so new witness evidence could be examined. The National Prosecuting Authority executed fresh warrants at Secunda in August, Daily Maverick reported. Sasol says its clean and contaminated water circuits are separate and that it operates within its water-use licences. It denies any pollution pathway.

Tau: R23.4 billion in IDC funding went to 48 black industrialists in a year

Trade, Industry and Competition Minister Parks Tau told Parliament that the Industrial Development Corporation allocated R23.4 billion towards black industrialists in 2025 and that 48 of them benefited, according to a written reply to MP Thokozani Langa dated 30 September, reported by Daily Investor and the Parliamentary Monitoring Group. DailyDrop's arithmetic suggests an average above R480 million each, but the reply does not break the amount down and the average may hide large differences.

Reports differ on whether the money was allocated or disbursed. The IDC's chief executive said in April that the corporation has supported 73 companies under the Black Industrialists Scheme with about R7.28 billion approved, a different measure. Several black-owned firms have faced liquidation over loan repayments, and the IDC says black-owned enterprises make up about 60% of its portfolio.

Government · Foreign affairsPress ✓ 1 outlet · two linked EWN reports

DIRCO tells MPs it needs R300 million a year to fix crumbling foreign mission buildings

The Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) told Parliament it needs more than R300 million a year to maintain its overseas properties, EWN reported on Tuesday 6 October. At least 30% of the portfolio is in poor condition and only 28% is rated good. The department took the portfolio over from the Department of Public Works in 1999 without a matching budget or staff, and now has only seven people managing buildings in 114 foreign missions.

A parliamentary reply reported by EWN on 28 September put renovation needs at more than R830 million, based on condition reports compiled over three years, and the portfolio is valued at about R5 billion. DIRCO has been selling under-used properties to cut costs. The R300 million is an annual maintenance need, not a request for a new allocation, and DailyDrop found no approved increase. The DA has called for the budget to be reprioritised toward embassies and economic diplomacy. Both reports come from one outlet, so the figures stay attributed to EWN.

Markets · South AfricaPress ✓ 2 outlets · levels differ by source and time

Rand eyes a fifth weekly slide near R16.65 with Brent around US$100.90 and bond yields up

The rand traded between R16.65 and R16.68 to the dollar on Tuesday, aiming to extend a four-week losing run as a stronger dollar and a coming fuel price rise weighed on sentiment, according to BusinessTech and a Reuters market report. Oil was about US$100.90 a barrel. South Africa's 2035 benchmark government bond yield rose 2 basis points to 8.88%. The reports say that petrol will rise by up to about 12% and wholesale diesel by about 10% from Wednesday. DailyDrop could not confirm an official All Share close, so the left ear box shows a dash.

In New York, the S&P 500 closed at 7,818.93, up 0.58%. WTI crude edged up 0.01% to US$89.44 and gold gained 0.73% to US$4,187.10 an ounce. BusinessTech's midday figure for gold was US$4,121.90, which shows how much it moved in the day.
