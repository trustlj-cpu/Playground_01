Cars torched in Soweto and Durban as protests over the asylum ruling turn violent

Police counted 10 burned vehicles in Soweto and 14 in Durban, with three arrests in Durban. The acting police commissioner promised to find those behind the burnings.

Violence broke out on Thursday 8 October in Soweto and in central Durban during protests against a July Constitutional Court ruling on access to the asylum system, according to Al Jazeera, AP, TimesLIVE, EWN, eNCA, Briefly and Arab News. Acting national police commissioner Lt-Gen Puleng Dimpane gave the police count as 10 vehicles torched in Soweto and 14 in Durban, with three arrests in Durban; AP reported at least 24 vehicles and looted shops. EWN counted at least 11 cars burned in the Durban CBD and TimesLIVE at least 10, so the Durban number differs by source.

In Soweto the violence started near the Home Affairs office in Orlando West. A metro police spokesperson, Xolani Fihla, said police received reports of shots fired toward protesters, which triggered the retaliatory burnings, and that no injuries were reported. Community leader Tshepo Mposula said the march began peacefully before residents moved to shut foreign-owned convenience stores. In Durban, TimesLIVE said the fires followed an apparent revenge attack after a demonstrator was allegedly assaulted by foreign nationals, and police said three foreigners were arrested over the assault. AFP reported police firing rubber bullets. Dimpane said crime intelligence would work around the clock. DailyDrop has not seen a death toll.

Government says the asylum ruling has been misrepresented and blames disinformation for the protests

The Inter-Ministerial Committee on Migration said the Constitutional Court judgment does not legitimise illegal immigration and does not automatically grant refugee status or permanent residence, according to EWN, Briefly, AP and Al Jazeera. Government spokesperson William Baloyi said the ruling had been widely misrepresented and that a disinformation campaign had spread false claims that migrants could vote or claim SASSA grants. Home Affairs denied the voting claim, Briefly reported.

The court struck down parts of the Refugees Act, including a rule that required applicants without proper documents to show "good cause" before applying. Home Affairs then told reception offices not to turn people away, which led to crowds at the Durban Refugee Reception Centre. EWN reported on Thursday that the government is seeking legal clarity on the ruling. DailyDrop has not seen the text of any new directive.

Pretoria High Court sets aside Tau's legal sector BEE code on procedural grounds

The Pretoria High Court set aside Trade, Industry and Competition Minister Parks Tau's promulgation of the BBBEE Legal Sector Code and remitted the decision to him for a fresh decision, according to BusinessDay, TimesLIVE, Polity, BusinessTech and Devdiscourse. The court found that, on his own affidavit, Tau saw his role as merely accepting and gazetting what the legal sector had agreed, rather than exercising his own discretion. It expressed no view on the merits of the transformation targets.

The challenge was brought by the law firms Deneys Reitz, Bowmans, Webber Wentzel and Werksmans, together with the union Solidarity. The code would have required law firms to ensure that 30% to 50% of equity directors are black legal practitioners within five years. Solidarity chief executive Dirk Hermann called the judgment "a huge victory over the government's reckless racial policy". Tau's spokesperson had not responded in the reports seen. Some reports describe the code as scrapped, but the order only sends it back, so its future is not settled.

South African mechanic held hostage in Mozambique since September is freed unharmed

A 30-year-old mechanic from Phalaborwa in Limpopo who was taken hostage in northern Mozambique last month has been released, the Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) said, according to TimesLIVE and EWN. DIRCO spokesperson Nelson Kgwete said he was freed unharmed on Sunday 4 October and left Mozambique on Wednesday 7 October.

He was one of five South Africans affected by a September attack in which rebels believed to be linked to an Islamic State affiliate raided a hunting lodge and the town of Marangira in Niassa Province. He worked as a mechanic at a camp in Marrupa district and was among three civilians taken from the lodge. DIRCO credited the South African High Commission in Maputo and thanked the Mozambican government. DailyDrop has not seen details of how the release was negotiated or the fate of the other two civilians.

Oil jumps 3.6% in New York as Wall Street slips; no local close confirmed
