Two ANC campaigners shot dead in the Durban area as election tension rises

Reports from News24 and SABC News describe a deadly attack on door-to-door canvassers, while the ANC faces restless supporters and the MK Party faces its own candidate-list fight.

Two people campaigning for the African National Congress were shot dead during a door-to-door drive in the Durban area, according to reports published on Thursday. News24 said two ANC members were gunned down while canvassing in Durban. SABC News, in a separate report, said an ANC coordinator and a volunteer were killed in a shooting at Umgababa, south of the city, and that two other volunteers were wounded and were reportedly in a critical condition in hospital. The two reports appear to describe the same incident, but neither extract we reviewed says so outright, and neither names a suspect or a motive.

The violence lands while the governing party is already dealing with unhappy supporters. A separate report, carried by two outlets, had frustrated ANC supporters telling Deputy President Paul Mashatile, who has returned after an illness, to “act right”. The Citizen’s evening roundup also noted that the ANC won a Constitutional Court battle against the Electoral Commission of South Africa (IEC). That headline is the only detail we have, so the nature of the case and the ruling should be read in full before anyone draws conclusions.

The ANC is not the only party wrestling with its lists. SABC News reported that MK Party members in the Free State are demanding transparency, saying that many of the names they submitted do not appear in the candidate lists published by the IEC. That is an allegation by members, reported by one outlet, and the IEC’s answer was not part of what we saw.

Why it matters beyond party politics: local government elections decide who runs municipalities, and with them water, electricity, refuse removal and roads. KwaZulu-Natal has a long record of killings linked to candidate selection and campaigning, so early claims about who is responsible should be treated with care until police and the IEC speak on the record. Today’s watch list is below. In practical terms, three things are still unknown from what we saw: who fired the shots, whether the victims were targeted because of their party, and how the ANC and the police will respond. Until those answers arrive, the safest reading is that a serious attack happened, that it is being treated as campaign-related by two newsrooms, and that everything beyond that is unconfirmed.

Eight guards on watch as Jacob Zuma stays in hospital

IOL reports, in a story it labels an exclusive, that former president Jacob Zuma remains in Netcare St Anne’s Hospital in Pietermaritzburg under heavy security, with eight guards on watch. The extract we reviewed is cut off before it says why he is there, and we have not seen any medical statement, so we are not guessing at his condition. The detail IOL does give is the scale of the security around him. Netcare St Anne’s is a private hospital, which means any official update is more likely to come from the family or a spokesperson than from a public health department. Only IOL carries this account in our monitoring, and it has not been matched by a second newsroom, so treat the guard count as that outlet’s reporting. Mr Zuma remains a prominent figure in South African politics, which is why even a hospital stay draws attention during an election season. Watch for any statement from his family, his party or the hospital, and be wary of social media posts that claim to know his diagnosis. For now, the verifiable facts are narrow: a hospital, a city, a guard count and a single outlet’s name on the story.

Maimane asks Treasury to shield motorists from a fuel price rise

Mmusi Maimane, leader of Build One South Africa, held a media briefing in Parliament on Thursday urging National Treasury to cushion consumers against an expected fuel increase. The briefing came ahead of the Medium-Term Budget Policy Statement (MTBPS), the October update on the state of the public finances. Two outlets carried the report. Neither extract we reviewed gives the size of the expected increase, so we are not putting a number on it. It is a political appeal rather than a policy decision, and there is no sign in what we saw that Treasury has responded.

Treasury’s lever is the general fuel levy, a tax charged on every litre that it sets in the Budget. The rest of the pump price follows the international oil price and the rand. On the oil side, TD said oil markets are still “far” from normal even as some problems around the Strait of Hormuz ease, a view reported by three outlets. Our own price tape showed US crude around US$96 a barrel late on Thursday, but that is a single-source reading and should not be treated as confirmed. Consumers feel the result at the pump and again in taxi fares and food prices, which is why opposition parties often press for relief in the weeks before the MTBPS.

Business · Energy✓ 1 outlet each (Daily Maverick, Moneyweb) · two separate pieces, figures are company claims

Gqeberha factories pay for diesel as outages bite

Daily Maverick reports that power outages in Gqeberha are forcing manufacturers to spend heavily on fuel to keep the lights on. Its example is Isuzu, which faced a R380,000 diesel bill for a single day after an outage. The figure comes from that report and has not been matched by a second outlet, but it shows how failing electricity feeds straight into factory costs in a city whose economy leans on car making. A diesel generator is a stop-gap, not a plan, and the cost of running one all day is far above the cost of grid power.

The wider question was taken up by Moneyweb, which carried Standard Bank’s Lebo Buthelezi reflecting on what it will take for South Africa to build resilience to global energy shocks. Separately, a Daily Maverick World Food Day opinion piece argued that a looming El Niño, oil shocks and hunger mean the country needs a synchronised food strategy. These are opinion and analysis pieces rather than news events, and they are single-source, but together they point at the same worry: higher energy costs travel quickly into prices on the shelf.

Society · Health and safety✓ 1 outlet each (IOL, SABC News) · case counts and cause not confirmed

Tshwane probes up to 160 stomach illness cases; 200 rescued from alleged illegal rehab centre

IOL reports that the City of Tshwane is investigating up to 160 cases of diarrhoea and stomach-related illness in Bronkhorstspruit. The figure is described as “up to”, the cause has not been established in the extract we saw, and it is a single-outlet report. Residents in the area should follow guidance from the city and use safe drinking water until the cause is known. Illness clusters like this are usually traced to water, food or hygiene, but no cause has been given in the reports we saw.

Separately, SABC News reports that about 200 people were rescued from an alleged illegal rehabilitation centre in Siyabuswa. The word alleged matters here: we have not seen a statement on charges or on the conditions inside. Both stories are early and one-source, so details may change. More short items from the day are in the briefs below. Unregistered rehabilitation facilities have drawn official attention before because residents can be held without proper care or oversight, though we cannot say whether that applies here.
