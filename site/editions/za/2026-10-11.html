Trump attacks Navi Pillay after her Nobel win and says he "settled eight wars"

In a long late-night post after a rally, the US president said few had heard of the South African laureate. CNN called his main claim false.

US President Donald Trump attacked the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize laureate Navi Pillay in a long Truth Social post late on Friday 9 October, US time, after a rally in Syracuse, New York, according to CNN, The Hill, Ynet, the Daily Beast and Gulf News. He wrote that, to the best of his knowledge, no one had heard of her other than that she "very obviously, hates Israel", and he said he had settled eight wars and should have won eight peace prizes, not one. The Daily Beast described a 590-word post made around midnight.

Pillay led a UN commission that concluded Israel had committed genocide in Gaza, which the Israeli government rejected, according to reports of the post. CNN said Trump's central claim, that he settled more than half a dozen wars, is false. One report said Pillay answered with a joke at a conference in Nuremberg, telling an applauding crowd she might share the prize with Trump if they clapped more. DailyDrop could not verify a recording of that remark, and has not seen a statement from the South African government on the post. The prize ceremony is on 10 December.

Police arrest 15 more suspects after the Durban unrest, bringing the total to 19

Police said they arrested four people on Thursday and 15 more on Friday night after vehicles and buildings were burned in Durban, bringing the total to 19, according to IOL, eNCA and Daily News Egypt. The charges mentioned include public violence and malicious damage to property. Gauteng police were still pursuing suspects in Soweto, where the violence started near the Home Affairs office in Orlando West. Police have not confirmed any deaths and no consolidated injury count has been published. The vehicle count is 24 by police figures, though one outlet says 23.

The Department of Home Affairs withdrew its 28 September asylum directive on Friday and said it will develop a new operational approach to comply with the Constitutional Court order. Daily Maverick published an explainer on the July ruling on Friday. Anti-illegal-immigration groups had vowed earlier in the week to keep disrupting refugee reception offices, according to EWN. A report that soldiers had been deployed on the streets of Durban came from a single outlet, the Rio Times, and DailyDrop could not confirm it. Acting national commissioner Puleng Dimpane has promised to find those behind the burnings.

Sundowns beat Pirates 2-0 to win the MTN8 and end four years of Pirates dominance

Mamelodi Sundowns won the MTN8 final 2-0 against Orlando Pirates at Moses Mabhida Stadium in Durban on Saturday 10 October, according to The Herald, SuperSport, Africa Soccer, SNL24, MDNtv and EB News Daily. Teboho Mokoena scored early in the first half, with reports giving the 11th or 12th minute, and Iqraam Rayners added a late penalty. Pirates had won the trophy four years in a row and were chasing a fifth.

Gamphani Lungu's 63rd-minute penalty for Pirates was saved by Ronwen Williams. It was Sundowns' first MTN8 title since 2021 and coach Miguel Cardoso's first domestic trophy with the club, the reports said. The winners take R10 million. The match went ahead despite unrest in Durban earlier in the week. DailyDrop has not seen attendance figures or any incident report from the stadium.

Ten-year driving licence cards are still only a proposal, with Cabinet approval next

The Department of Transport gazetted draft amendments on 17 August that would give light-vehicle and motorcycle licence cards (codes A1, A, B and EB) a ten-year life, according to BusinessTech, TopAuto and the Western Cape government. The licence itself would remain valid, but the plastic card would still need renewal. Cards for heavier codes (C1, C, EC1 and EC) would stay at five years, and professional permits at two years.

The comment period closed in September, and the next step is for the regulations to go to Cabinet and then to be published in the National Road Traffic Regulations by the Minister of Transport. A BusinessTech report on 6 October said the department was still at that stage. The Western Cape summary says holders of expiring five-year cards could keep using them for up to three months while waiting for replacements. DailyDrop has not seen a start date. Some headlines suggest the change is "hitting" motorists now, but nothing has taken effect.

A busy Monday: ANC Constitutional Court bid, the Lungisa ruling and the special-vote deadline

Three election matters converge on Monday 12 October. The ANC's Constitutional Court bid to have 181 excluded candidates reinstated has been reported by EWN as pushed out by a week from 7 October, though DailyDrop has the new date from one outlet and the court roll should be checked. Separately, the Johannesburg High Court is expected to rule that day on Andile Lungisa's challenge to his removal from the ANC's Nelson Mandela Bay list, according to EWN, Briefly and TimesLIVE.

Applications for special votes close at 17:00 on Monday, according to SAnews, EWN and SABC News. After that, the Medium-Term Budget Policy Statement is due on 21 October, BusinessTech reported, and fuel price data from the Central Energy Fund will show whether the November adjustment is nearer R1 or R4 for petrol. Home Affairs' new asylum approach has no date yet. The local government elections are on Wednesday 4 November.
