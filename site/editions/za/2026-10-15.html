Zuma stays in hospital as the MK Party scraps a rally and his inquiry testimony is put off

Five outlets agree that the former president is still in hospital. None of the reports DailyDrop read carried a medical statement, so his condition is not confirmed.

Jacob Zuma, the former president and leader of the uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party, was still in hospital on Wednesday, according to IOL, SABC News, The Citizen, The South African and News24. The knock-on effects are mounting. SABC News reported that his testimony before the TRC Cases Inquiry has been deferred, saying the postponement follows his admission to hospital. The Citizen reported that the MK Party has scrapped a rally ahead of the elections and wrote that speculation about his health has intensified in recent days. The South African said the party had pulled the plug on a major event just days before a key political occasion. It did not say which one in the summary DailyDrop saw, so we cannot tell whether it is the same rally. IOL reported in an exclusive that eight guards are on watch while he remains in hospital. The reports we read did not say who deploys them.

A court development landed on the same day. News24 reported that Zuma has failed to challenge a ruling that forces him to face trial in 2027, and that he has dropped all his appeals. The Citizen's evening round-up carried it as “Zuma drops appeals”. Neither summary says whether the timing has anything to do with his health, and DailyDrop draws no link. The politics are more immediate. The MK Party is campaigning for the local government elections on 4 November, and Zuma is its best-known face, so an empty stage costs it campaign time in the last three weeks. The Jacob Zuma Foundation, his family and his doctors have issued no new statement in the items we read. No hospital, diagnosis or date of admission has been confirmed by two outlets. Readers should treat social-media claims about his condition with caution. What to watch is a statement from his doctors or family, a new date for his inquiry testimony, and whether the MK Party replaces the cancelled event before polling day.

Labour · PoliticsPress ✓ 2 outlets · “break” is Daily Maverick’s framing

Cosatu elects Mike Shingange as president and opens the door to campaigning for other left parties

Mail & Guardian reported that Mike Madala Shingange was elected president of the Congress of South African Trade Unions (Cosatu), defeating Mabutho Cele, as the federation announced its new national leadership. Daily Maverick wrote that Shingange, once the elective conference had reconvened, called for unity with the ANC and the SACP while allowing members to campaign for several left-leaning parties in the coming elections. The paper headlined this as Cosatu breaking with exclusive ANC support to back a left-wing “super pact”. That is Daily Maverick’s reading. DailyDrop has not seen a written Cosatu resolution, the other office-bearers or the vote count, and no second outlet used the phrase.

The shift matters because Cosatu is the ANC’s largest union ally and has often supplied campaign workers. Daily Maverick also reported that longtime ANC supporters in Ekurhuleni told Deputy President Paul Mashatile, back from illness, to “act right”, citing electricity costs, disputed bills, refuse collection, unemployment and corruption. SABC News reported that Mashatile urged workers at a union congress to be patient amid the cost-of-living crisis. Taken together, the reports show a party under pressure from its own base three weeks before the vote.

Hot US inflation and AI-debt worries hit chip stocks, and the sell-off spills into Asia

Wall Street fell on Wednesday after US consumer prices rose faster than forecast in September. Reuters reported that prices accelerated on costlier petrol, with core inflation, which strips out food and energy, also firmer. Bloomberg called it the fastest increase in inflation since April and said stocks and bonds fell together as traders bet the Federal Reserve will keep raising interest rates. The FT reported that US chip stocks slid, naming Nvidia, Oracle and CoreWeave, on inflation and AI-debt fears. Bloomberg reported that Bank of America fell after a $1.21 billion fine, and that the S&P 500 ended its longest spell of calm since 2020. DailyDrop could not confirm the index closing levels from two outlets, so no figures are given here.

Fed officials sounded measured. Reuters reported in an exclusive that Daly said rate hikes are likely needed but not urgently, and Reuters later wrote that the dollar retreated from an 18-month high as officials tempered hike bets. Overnight, Reuters reported that Asian markets sold off as AI trades crumbled, FinancialJuice reported Taiwan stocks down more than 2%, and Bloomberg said the Taiwan dollar and the South Korean won led losses among Asian currencies. Bloomberg also reported that gold held a loss. For South Africa, a weaker appetite for risk tends to weigh on the rand and on JSE-listed shares. The JSE and rand ears show a dash because Wednesday’s levels were not confirmed by two sources.

Ukraine says allies gave no warning of the Kyiv strike, as Starlink limits and a Russian-assets plan strain ties with Washington

A Russian missile attack on Kyiv killed two people, Reuters reported, and further reports said missiles also hit a power plant in the capital. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine received no warning and that it was the second night without allied warnings, according to Reuters and CNN. Allied governments had not replied in the items we read. Ukraine also complained that SpaceX has limited Starlink operations there. Reuters reported a presidential aide as saying the limits disrupted military missions, and Ukraine accused the company of crippling drone missions. Elon Musk said the US government may be behind a slowdown in drone speeds, the BBC reported. Zelensky said he regrets the difficulties, SABC News reported. Eutelsat shares jumped on the news, and Reuters noted Europe has no quick fix.

On diplomacy, the FT reported that Washington floated unfreezing some Russian assets to restart peace talks, and that European officials rejected the idea; Reuters and FinancialJuice carried both strands. Zelensky said it is painful when Trump chooses a different side, Reuters reported. Ukraine also said it freed more territory in a counteroffensive in the Donetsk region, a claim reported by Reuters alone that we cannot yet confirm. South African readers feel this war mainly through fuel prices.

World · OilPress ✓ 4 outlets · settlement prices from FinancialJuice

A container ship sinks near Hormuz as Brent stays above $100 and Washington weighs easing shadow-fleet sanctions

Reuters reported, citing sources, that a container ship sank near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile. A separate Reuters report, citing officials, said Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks on shipping before a US vote. FinancialJuice recorded Brent crude settling at $106.06 a barrel, up 37 cents, and West Texas Intermediate (WTI) for November at $95.09, up 95 cents. Bloomberg said oil gained as hostilities in the Middle East and Russia imperil flows. By Asian trade on Thursday, Reuters reported oil was lower as investors weighed the supply outlook and Middle East tensions.

Reuters reported, and FinancialJuice repeated from an official, that Washington is discussing easing some sanctions on shadow fleet vessels to lower global freight rates and lift oil flows from the Middle East. The shadow fleet is the loose group of older tankers that carry sanctioned oil outside normal insurance. For South African motorists the link is the basic fuel price, which follows international oil and the rand, so any relief on shipping costs would reach the pump only after a lag. Minister Creecy said an increase in the Road Accident Fund levy in the fuel price will not happen on her watch, Moneyweb reported.
