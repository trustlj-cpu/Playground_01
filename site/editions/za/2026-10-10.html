South African jurist Navi Pillay wins the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize

The committee honoured the former UN human rights chief, 85, for efforts to promote peace and international law. The ceremony is on 10 December.

The Norwegian Nobel Committee announced in Oslo on Friday 9 October that the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize goes to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, a South African jurist and former United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, according to Al Jazeera, the committee's own announcement, EWN and eNCA. Committee chair Jørgen Watne Frydnes made the announcement, and the committee said it could not reach Pillay before it went public. The citation honours "her efforts to promote peace and international law".

Pillay, 85, was the first woman of colour to serve on South Africa's High Court, was a lawyer for anti-apartheid activists, and later served as a judge and president of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda before becoming the UN's rights chief. The committee said she has been instrumental in ensuring that war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide are prosecuted. She received the Sydney Peace Prize in November 2025 and is due the 2026 Olof Palme Prize, with fellow lawyer John Dugard. The Nobel ceremony is on 10 December. President Cyril Ramaphosa called the award a "profound tribute" to her courage, integrity and commitment to justice (SAnews, DIRCO, Polity), and UN Secretary-General António Guterres and the Nelson Mandela Foundation also congratulated her. Pillay said she first thought it must be a joke and dedicated the prize to survivors of international crimes, CNN reported.

Home Affairs withdraws its asylum directive a day after the violent protests in Soweto and Durban

The Department of Home Affairs said on Friday 9 October that it has withdrawn the directive that told refugee reception offices to receive asylum applicants, according to News24, TimesLIVE, Reuters via CNBC Africa, allAfrica and Smile 90.4FM. The directive was issued by the director-general on 28 September after a July Constitutional Court ruling. The department said implementation overwhelmed Refugee Reception Offices within days, put officials at risk and raised public safety and public order concerns, and that it will develop a new operational approach that complies with the court order.

Police said the situation in Durban had calmed, with spokesperson Robert Netshiunda saying a swift response prevented further damage. On Thursday, police counted 10 burned vehicles in Soweto and 14 in Durban. Minister Khumbudzo Ntshavheni said earlier that the government would implement the ruling within its administrative capacity while considering its legal options, and would review the directives to avoid confusion, SAnews reported. The Inter-Ministerial Committee on Migration says the judgment does not automatically grant refugee status. DailyDrop has not seen what the new approach will be or any timetable.

Early November fuel data shows a sharp swing: from a R4 petrol rise to just over R1

The Central Energy Fund's first daily figures for the November fuel price review pointed to a petrol increase of about R4.28 to R4.57 a litre and diesel up R2.56 to R2.91, according to IOL, 2oceansvibe, Scrolla and Archynewsy. Another outlet said inland 95 could move towards R35 a litre. A later snapshot looked much milder: under-recoveries of between R1.27 and R1.34 a litre for petrol and roughly zero to two cents for diesel, with one analysis suggesting a rise of just over R1 for petrol and a neutral outcome for diesel.

The figures are the fund's unaudited daily data, issued for indication only, and they can change before the final calculation. The official adjustment is expected at the end of October, with new prices due on Wednesday 4 November, the day of the local government elections. Reuters said the rand was heading for its first weekly gain in five weeks, which would help the November outlook.

Markets · South AfricaPress ✓ 3 outlets · closes provisional

Rand on course for its first weekly gain in five weeks as gold jumps 1.4%

The rand traded at 16.5275 to the dollar at 08:30 GMT on Friday, about 0.4% stronger than the previous close and about 0.7% up on the week, putting it on track to snap a four-week losing streak, according to Reuters carried by CNBC Africa and Business Recorder. A firmer gold price and a subdued dollar helped. Trading Economics put USD/ZAR at 16.50 on 9 October, down 0.60%, which DailyDrop treats as provisional. The rand had hit 16.70 on Wednesday, its weakest since late July.

In New York, the S&P 500 closed at 7,811.54, up 0.59%. WTI crude rose 0.39% to US$91.85 and gold jumped 1.43% to US$4,216.30 an ounce. Reuters noted that the Reserve Bank raised its repo rate by 25 basis points to 7.25% last month and that markets price two more increases within six months. DailyDrop could not confirm an official All Share close.

AA asks Treasury to cut the general fuel levy by R3 a litre and scrap the RAF levy

The Automobile Association (AA) called on the National Treasury to cut the general fuel levy by R3 a litre, repeating an April 2026 relief measure, and to scrap the Road Accident Fund (RAF) levy, according to BusinessTech and Daily Investor. It made the call after inland 95 petrol passed R30 a litre on 7 October. The RAF levy is R2.25 a litre, and the fund has proposed lifting it to R3. The AA said motorists cannot absorb more.

The April cut was temporary. Treasury estimated that the relief cost R17.2 billion in lost revenue for April to June, and it was phased out from June, with outlets differing on the exact end date. A BusinessTech economist wrote on 15 September that Treasury may be unable to repeat it. The Medium-Term Budget Policy Statement is due on 21 October. DailyDrop found no Treasury response to the AA.
