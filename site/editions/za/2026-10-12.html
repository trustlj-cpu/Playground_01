Government will ask the Constitutional Court to oversee its asylum ruling as protests spread

Ministers said on Sunday the court should supervise how its judgment is carried out. Police report 30 arrests since Thursday in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng.

The government said on Sunday 11 October that it will go back to the Constitutional Court and ask for a supervisory order on how the court's ruling on asylum applications is put into practice, according to IOL, The Citizen, SABC News and News24. The Citizen explained that such an order would let the court keep oversight of the process and guide how its judgment is carried out. It had earlier described the step as a way of buying Home Affairs time. The plan was set out by Justice Minister Mmamoloko Kubayi after the inter-ministerial committee (IMC) on migration briefed the media. The ruling, SABC reported, holds that foreign nationals can apply for asylum even when they are in the country illegally. Courts reach for supervision when they doubt that a department can meet a deadline on its own, which is why the request is being read as an admission that Home Affairs needs more time and a clearer plan. The Citizen reported that Kubayi said Home Affairs plans a phased registration model by 10 November. SABC News reported that Home Affairs will add a biometric system to stop applicants abusing the process, and quoted Home Affairs Minister Leon Schreiber as saying the new system will let the department build in more security features. eNCA carried a warning from Schreiber that "chancers" would be caught out. Those controls matter because the pressure on the asylum system has fed public anger at Home Affairs offices. The department has not said how many people are waiting for decisions, and DailyDrop has not seen the court application or a filing date, so it is not yet clear when judges could hear the request.

The announcement came while the streets stayed tense. Acting National Police Commissioner Lt-Gen Puleng Dimpane said intelligence-led operations had produced 30 arrests in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng since Thursday, IOL reported. SABC News said protesters were camped outside the Home Affairs office in Durban and vowed to block its reopening, after foreign nationals were allowed to lodge asylum applications. Daily Maverick described Durban as being in an uneasy calm and asked whether the state can keep a lid on the violence. Parliament's Portfolio Committee on Home Affairs, The Citizen reported, said South Africans come first and that their concerns are legitimate, and that it will call an urgent meeting to question how the judgment is being implemented. The date has not been announced. The risk for the government is plain: a court that supervises can set deadlines, and protesters who feel ignored can set their own. For the government the dilemma is that the protesters want fewer foreigners registered, while the court has said the right to apply cannot be denied. Readers should expect Home Affairs queues, office closures and reopenings in the next few days to depend on what police and the court allow. No deaths from the unrest have been confirmed in the reports DailyDrop has seen.

Presidency asks the public to stop speculating as Zuma stays in hospital in KwaZulu-Natal

Minister in the Presidency Khumbudzo Ntshavheni urged the public and the media on Sunday to respect former president Jacob Zuma's medical privacy, according to IOL, The Citizen, SABC News, News24 and EWN. She said only his doctors can say how he is, and only with his consent. IOL reported that the government has sent a team of specialist doctors to KwaZulu-Natal to treat him, and SABC reported that President Cyril Ramaphosa asked the Surgeon General to oversee his care.

The uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party, which Zuma leads, condemned rumours about his health and challenged a report that linked his condition to a possible deployment of troops in KwaZulu-Natal, IOL reported. The party warned that such talk could fuel anxiety and political uncertainty. Ntshavheni said, in a line reported by The Citizen, that holding a position in society does not entitle anyone to violate a person's privacy. Johannesburg Sunday World wrote that the MK Party was "in a spin" over the stay, a characterisation DailyDrop could not verify. Business Day described conflicting reports about the hospital stay, and Scrolla raised questions about his role in the election campaign and a Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC) hearing. DailyDrop has not seen an official diagnosis, a discharge date or any confirmation that soldiers will be deployed. Local government elections are on 4 November.

Australia take charge of the first Test in Durban as the Proteas fall 106 runs behind

Australia ended the third day of the first Test at Kingsmead in Durban on top, according to SuperSport, News24, The Citizen and The South African. The Proteas were bowled out 106 runs behind on first innings, and SuperSport put Australia's overall lead at 139 once their second innings had begun. Sky Sports named Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc and Nathan Lyon among the wickets as the Australians took command of the match.

The Citizen noted that the visitors bowled well to put the Proteas on the back foot with two days remaining, and The South African wrote that only bad light might save the home side from defeat. DailyDrop does not have a confirmed stumps score for Australia's second innings and has not checked the weather forecast. Play resumes on Monday 12 October.

Trump announces an "energy ceasefire"; Kyiv says it is awaiting details and Russia keeps striking

US President Donald Trump said on Sunday 11 October that Russia and Ukraine had agreed an "energy ceasefire", according to Reuters, CNBC, The Hill, BBC and Benzinga. Reuters reported that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Kyiv was awaiting details from Washington and that Russia was maintaining its attacks. The Hill reported that Russia struck a Ukrainian power plant after the announcement, and The Kyiv Independent said it was the Kharkiv plant. Zelenskyy also said Ukraine would halt strikes on Russian refineries if Russia stops attacking energy sites, Reuters reported.

The announcement came with a US deal for Russian diesel that drew criticism. A prominent Republican senator said the arrangement may be illegal (Reuters), and the Financial Times said lawmakers from both parties object. Trump also said it was time for Ukraine to get a new president, and Reuters reported that Germany backed Zelenskyy in response. For South Africans the link is the pump price: diesel and oil costs feed straight into the monthly fuel adjustment. Moscow has not confirmed any truce in the reports DailyDrop saw.

World · Middle EastPress ✓ 5 outlets · 12 dead, 300+ hurt per Saudi officials

Missile strike on Riyadh's main airport kills 12; a second hit follows on Sunday and oil firms pull out of a congress

A suspected Houthi missile strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday 10 October killed 12 people, according to AP, Reuters and the Guardian. Saudi authorities said more than 300 were wounded (AP, CBS News, USA Today). On Sunday the Saudi-led coalition said an unidentified projectile hit Terminal 4 and caused a fire, Bloomberg reported. The Houthis, a Yemeni armed movement, claimed responsibility and renewed a warning against using Saudi airports (Reuters). The coalition vowed to respond firmly, the Guardian reported.

The Financial Times reported that BP and other oil companies pulled out of the World Petroleum Council congress in Riyadh. Reuters said Saudi stocks slid after the first attack. Wall Street closed the week higher on Friday 9 October: the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq 0.64% and the Dow 0.83%, while WTI crude gained 0.39% to 91.85 dollars a barrel and gold rose 1.43% to 4,216.3 dollars. Fuel prices in South Africa have been hitting records, Moneyweb reported. Markets in Johannesburg reopen on Monday.
