Inland petrol passes R30 a litre on Wednesday after a R3.33 October hike

Brent crude averaged about US$101 a barrel in the review period, up from US$87.89. Diesel rises by R2.84 to R3.24 a litre, and the slate levy edges higher.

The Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources set the October fuel adjustments, which take effect on Wednesday 7 October, according to IOL, Carmag, SAnews and BusinessTech. Petrol 95 goes up by R3.33 a litre and petrol 93 by R3.12. Inland, 95-octane petrol reaches R30.25 a litre in Gauteng, and R29.38 at the coast, based on the reports. A year ago the inland price was R21.63. Wholesale diesel with 0.05% sulphur rises by R2.84 a litre. The 0.005% grade rises by R3.24 a litre in most reports, although one outlet gave R3.58, so DailyDrop uses the majority figure.

The department put the increase down to the average Brent crude price rising from US$87.89 to US$101 a barrel over the review period, with higher shipping costs and falling global stocks also cited. The Central Energy Fund, which publishes the daily under- and over-recovery data, flagged the direction in its 2 October release. The Self-Adjusting Slate Levy rises from 83.28 to 87.66 cents a litre, adding 4.38 cents to petrol and diesel. Rio Times and SA People both call the petrol rise a record. DailyDrop has not seen a comparison table from the department, so it does not repeat that label as its own. Retail diesel is expected to land between about R33 and R35 depending on the forecourt.

Markets · South AfricaPress ✓ 3 outlets · rand levels per Rio Times and Reuters

Rand ends a fourth straight weekly loss at about R16.64 as weak US jobs data lifts miners on the JSE

The rand closed at about R16.64 to the dollar on Friday 2 October, stronger by 0.33% on the day from R16.70, but it still lost around 2.2% over the week, its fourth weekly loss in a row, according to the Rio Times. Reuters, carried by Engineering News and Polity, put the rand at 16.6825 in early trade on Monday, about 0.2% weaker than the previous close, which implies a Friday close near R16.65. The two sources differ by a cent.

The Satrix 40 exchange-traded fund, which tracks the Top 40, gained 0.81% to R102.77 on Friday. Capitec rebounded 4.4% to R4,372.69, and Glencore, Anglo American and BHP rose. DailyDrop could not confirm an official All Share closing level from the JSE or two outlets, so the left-hand ear box shows a dash. The Rio Times said the All Share traded near 108,200 to 108,300 on Monday morning, an unofficial estimate. The lift came after US payrolls rose by only 29,000 in September.

Madlanga Commission moves behind closed doors for two weeks from today, public hearings return on 9 November

The Madlanga Commission, the judicial inquiry into police corruption and political interference, will hear evidence in camera for two weeks from Monday 5 October, according to TimesLIVE, eNCA, EWN and Briefly. Its spokesperson, Jeremy Michaels, said the material relates to ongoing investigations and is confidential, and that managing media coverage and livestreaming would be hard around the elections. Public hearings are due to resume on 9 November, five days after the 4 November local government vote.

During the pause the commission plans a two-day seminar in October to prepare for drafting its final report. Phase two, which includes testimony from suspended police minister Senzo Mchunu, is to be completed in the remaining months of 2026, and phase three is expected to bring KwaZulu-Natal police commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi back to the stand. Reports differ on the final report date: SAnews gave 16 November in mid-September, while newer reports say 25 January 2027, which DailyDrop treats as current.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 4 outlets · Iranian statements attributed

Iran says the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until its seven conditions are met, keeping oil near US$100

Iran's chief negotiator said on Sunday 4 October that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the United States meets Tehran's seven conditions, according to Al Jazeera, Kurdistan24, Pajhwok and Seoul Economic Daily. The strait carries about a fifth of the world's oil. Washington has not accepted the terms, and the reports describe a standoff rather than a negotiation in progress.

For South Africa the effect shows up at the pump. The fuel price review used an average Brent price of about US$101 a barrel, and a US$100-plus market feeds directly into November's adjustment. South Africa imports most of its refined fuel, and some of it has moved from Persian Gulf sources to the Atlantic Basin during the conflict, according to local reports. WTI closed at US$91.11 on Friday, down 1.90%. DailyDrop did not find confirmation of any reopening.

Politics · ElectionsPress ✓ 5 outlets · counts per the IEC

Four weeks to the local elections: 136,790 candidates certified and the biggest ballots yet

The Electoral Commission of South Africa has certified 136,790 candidates for the local government elections on Wednesday 4 November, up from 95,427 in 2021, after disqualifying 5,096 nominations, according to figures reported by BusinessDay, EWN, Briefly and IOL. President Cyril Ramaphosa set the date on 30 April; municipal terms end on 2 November, and the law requires a vote within 90 days of that.

The numbers by area show the scale. In the Western Cape, 106 parties and 33 independents are fielding 14,348 candidates for 868 seats, EWN reported on 25 September, and the IEC is preparing 8 million ballots. In KwaZulu-Natal, 111 parties and 104 independents field 26,497 candidates for 2,166 seats, and Briefly reports no political no-go areas. In the big metros, Johannesburg has 75 parties on its proportional ballot, Tshwane 70, eThekwini 69, Ekurhuleni 67 and Cape Town 55. A crowded field also complicates coalition talks after the vote. A public holiday on polling day is likely but was not officially confirmed in the reports DailyDrop saw.
