KOSPI plunges 3.48% to close… sell-side sidecar on Alphabet shock, Hynix down 7%

Weak earnings at US big tech shook a market leaning heavily on AI chips. The Kosdaq also sank, to 724.38.

On the 30th the KOSPI closed at 5,730.74, down 3.48%. It lost 207 points in a day, having given up the 6,000 level the day before and now slipping to the early 5,700s. It started in the US. Alphabet reported revenue short of market expectations, its shares plunged and the Nasdaq fell 1.38%. As doubts grew over whether the money poured into AI is coming back as revenue, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, which had risen on AI chip demand, weakened together. The KOSPI opened at 5,760.35 (down 2.99%), then widened its losses into the 4% range, and a sell-side sidecar was triggered at around 10 a.m., after 11 trading days. A sell-side sidecar is a device that cushions the shock by suspending the effect of program selling orders for 5 minutes when futures plunge and the spot index also drops sharply at the same time. Toward the close Samsung Electronics was down 3% and SK Hynix down in the 7% range.

The loss, which had widened into the 4% range during the session, narrowed to 3.48% at the close. The Kosdaq fell 3.83% to 724.38. Samsung Electronics had announced Q3 operating profit of 107 trillion won the day before, yet its shares slid as much as the 4% range at one point; it fell again on the day, so weakness after its record results has lasted a second day. Not yet confirmed are the size of foreign and institutional net selling and the direction from the next trading day onward. Whether the Alphabet shock carries on in US stocks, and how the market mood changes after next week’s US midterm elections, remain the variables.

Actor Choi Bul-am, the ‘eternal Crime Squad chief’, dies at 87

The actor Choi Bul-am died on the 30th at the age of 87. The first news of his death came at dawn, and a funeral altar was set up at Asan Medical Center in Seoul. In the crime drama ‘Crime Squad’ he played Chief Park and was the face of the living-room screen from 1971 to 1989, and in the rural family drama ‘Village Diary’ he played Chairman Kim and led a long-running series that ran from 1980 to 2002. With his trademark ‘Pa-ha~’ laugh he has been called the ‘eternal Crime Squad chief’ and ‘the nation’s father’. Crime Squad was a long-running detective series that lasted 18 years from 1971, and Village Diary portrayed rural families changing with industrialization for more than 20 years. For as long as the two shows ran, several generations have remembered him as a father’s face. The cause of death, the funeral date and the funeral arrangements have not yet been officially confirmed.

Kim Gi-hyeon ‘luxury bag’ case: not guilty at first instance; special counsel appeals Oh Se-hoon’s second-instance acquittal to Supreme Court… 8 soldiers also acquitted at first instance

Court rulings came in quick succession on the 30th. The Seoul Central District Court found People Power Party lawmaker Kim Gi-hyeon not guilty at first instance on charges of violating the Improper Solicitation and Graft Act by giving Kim Keon-hee a Roger Vivier bag in the 2.6 million won range. The special counsel’s sentencing request at the Sept. 28 closing hearing was 1 year in prison. Kim’s spouse, tried alongside, was fined 5 million won. The same day the special counsel filed an appeal to the Supreme Court against the Seoul High Court Criminal Division 7 appeal trial ruling that found Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon not guilty of violating the Political Funds Act by having Myung Tae-kyun pay for opinion polls.

Eight soldiers, including Gu Sam-hoe, former commander of the Army 2nd Armored Brigade, and Bang Jeong-hwan, former Defense Ministry innovation planning officer, who were suspected of plotting the Dec. 3 emergency martial law at a meeting known as the ‘hamburger meeting’ (the Lotteria meeting), were also found not guilty at first instance. The court judged that a purpose of subversion of the constitutional order, meaning an aim to bring down constitutional functions, was not established.

World’s largest exchange Binance suspected of massive hack… deposits and withdrawals fully halted, Korea issues ‘withdrawal caution’

Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange, fully halted deposits and withdrawals on the 30th, saying it appeared to have suffered a massive hack. Its founder said the exchange appeared to have been a target of hacking, and the company said it would compensate the damage in full. The scale of the damage is cited in the billions of dollars, but it varies by tally and there is no official confirmation. There were also reports that stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze $2.3 billion worth, but there is no official confirmation of that either.

Prices of Bitcoin and other crypto assets fell on the news. Korea’s won-market exchanges issued one notice after another urging caution over deposits and withdrawals with Binance. The exact damage, the route of the leak and the effect on Korean users’ assets are not yet confirmed.

Ulsan thermal power plant collapse that killed 7: investigation finds ‘columns cut too much’

The cause has been identified in the collapse of the boiler tower at the Ulsan thermal power plant last November, which killed 7 workers and injured 2. The finding is that columns were cut too much during work to weaken the structure ahead of blast demolition, and this led to the collapse. This work is called weakening work: cutting away part of the load-bearing columns and similar members in advance so that the building comes down as planned when explosives are set off.

Right after the accident, police and labor authorities searched the contractor’s head office and have investigated mainly the chain of work instructions between the prime contractor and the subcontractor, the construction method and the safety management system. At what stage, on whose instruction and how much of the columns was cut, and how far responsibility extends, remain to be seen from the investigation’s results.
