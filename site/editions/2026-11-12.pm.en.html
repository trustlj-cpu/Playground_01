Nvidia board preparing to replace CEO Jensen Huang, sources report… no announcement from the company; Republican senator urges resignation

A report citing people familiar with the matter has appeared. Nvidia reportedly did not respond to requests for comment, and the company has announced nothing.

A report emerged on the 11th (local time) that Nvidia’s board is preparing to replace founder and chief executive Jensen Huang. Citing people familiar with the matter, the report said the board could meet as early as the 11th to discuss CEO Huang’s future. Nvidia reportedly did not respond to requests for comment, and there is no indication that a dismissal has been decided or announced. In a message to employees on the 8th, CEO Huang admitted to an affair and apologized. The board had earlier started an investigation through a committee of independent directors with the help of outside counsel, saying it retained confidence in CEO Huang and management. Allegations of a relationship with an in-house executive were then reported, citing testimony from former employees, but the positions of those involved have not been confirmed.

At the center of the controversy is an allegation that a woman close to CEO Huang worked for Chinese intelligence. The woman is known as an executive of a Singapore company that is a customer of Nvidia, and there are reports that U.S. intelligence authorities see it that way, but this is not an officially confirmed fact. A senior Republican on the Senate Banking Committee reportedly urged CEO Huang to resign. There is also a report of a warning that a subpoena could be used to compel CEO Huang to appear if the CEO does not explain to Congress, but who issued the warning has not been confirmed. Some lawmakers are pushing to tighten export controls on advanced chips to China. Analysts say that replacing a CEO is a matter for the board and can be done on the board’s judgment even if no criminal penalty has been finalized. On the U.S. stock market, Nvidia shares rebounded on the 11th for the first time in three days. The outcome of the board meeting is not yet known.

People Power Party Supreme Council to check basis of ethics committee member’s claim that Han Dong-hoon’s discipline was “rigged”… leader Jang Dong-hyeok rebuts: “shaking the leadership”

In closed-door discussions at its Supreme Council meeting on the 12th, the People Power Party said it would check the basis of the claim, made the day before by Central Ethics Committee member Hwang Gyeong-seong, that the discipline of Han Dong-hoon was ‘rigged,’ and would contact Hwang. In a statement on the 11th, Hwang claimed that past party disciplinary actions against independent lawmaker Han Dong-hoon and lawmaker Jin Jong-oh had been rigged, and apologized. Leader Jang said there was no problem and rebutted that it was an attempt to shake the leadership. Hwang said that on Oct. 27 the party audit office demanded that a seal be put on a resignation letter, saying it was the leadership’s wish, and claimed to have secured related evidence. Suspicion was also raised that Jang Dong-hyeok and ethics committee chair Yoon Min-woo had a secret agreement over the discipline. Han demanded an apology from Jang, and Jin demanded Jang’s resignation. The affair also revived the question of Han’s return to the party. Lawmaker Ahn Cheol-soo said of the talk of return, ‘better to just found a new party,’ and there were also arguments that anyone wanting to return should first get to the bottom of the party members’ bulletin board issue. Whether the discipline was actually rigged, and whether there was a secret agreement, has not been confirmed.

President Lee and Samsung Chairman Lee Jae-yong attend groundbreaking for Chungcheong megaproject in Asan, South Chungcheong… Samsung Onyang Campus breaks ground on HBM fab

President Lee Jae-myung on the 12th attended, together with Samsung Electronics Chairman Lee Jae-yong, the groundbreaking ceremony for a Chungcheong-region megaproject held in Asan, South Chungcheong. The event marks the first shovel of construction for a fab that will produce high-bandwidth memory (HBM) at Samsung Electronics’ Onyang Campus. President Lee expressed deep thanks for Chairman Lee’s bold investment decision and swift execution, and said the focus would be on spreading the results to the region. Chairman Lee said Onyang will turn into an HBM hub and that the megaprojects in Honam and Yeongnam will also be actively pursued.

The project is one of the three major ‘Great Leap for Korea’ megaprojects announced in late June. Investment figures for Onyang differ from report to report, at 56 trillion won, 46 trillion won and so on, and the exact scale of this groundbreaking work has not been confirmed. The schedule of the Honam and Yeongnam projects that Chairman Lee mentioned has not been confirmed either.

Science · Space✓ 4 outlets · arrival is scheduled for the 18th and has not yet happened

Voyager 1 due to reach ‘one light-day’ from Earth on the 18th… about 25.9 billion km

NASA said on the 10th (local time) that Voyager 1, the uncrewed probe launched in 1977, will reach a point about 25.9 billion km from Earth at around 5:16 a.m. (U.S. Eastern time) on the 18th. This distance is about 173 times the distance between Earth and the Sun. Light takes a full day to cover this distance, so it is called a ‘light-day’ distance. It is the first time an object made by humanity has reached a point one light-day from Earth, 49 years after launch.

At a distance of one light-day, a command sent from Earth takes a day to reach the probe, and the probe’s reply takes another day to come back. The arrival time on the 18th is the scheduled time NASA calculated, and has not yet happened.
