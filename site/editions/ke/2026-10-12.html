Riyadh airport attacked a second time in two days as Houthis claim Dammam and Aramco sites; Saudi Arabia vows a major response

Saturday's missile strike killed 12 and wounded 309, according to Saudi authorities. On Sunday a new attack set the airport on fire, the Houthis claimed more targets in the Eastern Province, and oil executives pulled out of an energy congress in Riyadh.

King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh was attacked again on Sunday 11 October, a day after a missile strike that killed 12 people and wounded 309, Saudi authorities said, as quoted by the Washington Post. The BBC called Sunday's attack the second strike in two days. The Saudi-led coalition said the new attack caused a fire, Mint reported, the Inquirer in the Philippines reported that a terminal was reportedly struck by a projectile, and AP and Politico reported that the closure of the airport was extended. Saudi Arabia vowed a major response, according to the Washington Post.

The Houthis claimed Saturday's strike. Le Monde reported that they announced large-scale operations against airports and energy sites, and FinancialJuice, a market news feed, quoted them as saying they hit Dammam airport and Aramco facilities in the Eastern Province. Saudi civil aviation said Dammam airport was targeted on Sunday evening and that damage was being assessed; DailyDrop has seen no confirmation of damage at Aramco. CBS News reported a fresh Houthi threat to Saudi airspace, and Reuters that the group renewed its warning to airlines and travellers against using Saudi airports.

Foreign nationals were among the wounded: the Philippine government said four Filipinos were hurt, three of them in intensive care (Inquirer, GMA), and India said 11 Indians were injured (Hindustan Times, The Economic Times). Saudi Gazette reported that the health minister visited Riyadh hospitals, and the Economic Times asked why Saudi air defences struggle to stop Houthi attacks. Reuters also reported a strike on Sanaa airport in Houthi-held Yemen, according to Houthi-run television. Russia condemned the attack on the airport, according to a report carried by FinancialJuice from Interfax, and Antara reported Yemeni government troops attacking the Houthis in Marib. The shock reached markets and business. Reuters reported that Saudi stocks slid, and the Financial Times that oil executives pulled out of an energy congress in Riyadh. India said an Indian national died after the attack (Hindustan Times, The Hindu); it is not clear whether he is counted in the 12. For Kenya the effects run through flights that connect via Gulf hubs, oil prices and Kenyans who work in Saudi Arabia. The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority holds its next monthly pump-price review on Wednesday 14 October, and a rise in crude would feed into it. If you are due to fly through Saudi Arabia, check with your airline before leaving for the airport.

Trump announces a Russia–Ukraine "energy ceasefire"; Zelenskyy says it is news to him and Russia hits power plants

President Trump said on Sunday 11 October that Russia and Ukraine had agreed an energy ceasefire effective immediately, according to Reuters, USA Today and CNA. NBC News reported Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy as saying it was "news for me", and Reuters that Kyiv was waiting for details. Zelenskyy said Ukraine was ready to agree and would halt strikes on Russian refineries if Russia stopped attacking Ukraine's energy sites (Reuters, Ukrainska Pravda). ABC News, the Inquirer and Punch reported that Russia struck Ukrainian power plants after the announcement, and Ukrainska Pravda that an energy facility in Kharkiv had just been hit.

Trump also called for Zelenskyy to be replaced, and Germany backed the Ukrainian leader (Reuters, Bloomberg). Zelenskyy told ABC he would organise elections if Russia agrees to a 60-day ceasefire, Reuters reported. Russia's Yandex said a data centre in the Vladimir region was shut down after a drone attack, Reuters reported, a sign that strikes are reaching far from the front line. The Financial Times reported separately that the United States threatened to block weapons for Ukraine over its refinery attacks. For Kenya the link is diesel and electricity inputs: a real pause could ease world fuel prices and, later, EPRA's review, but nothing has changed yet because neither side has confirmed terms. A Republican senator said Trump's Russian diesel deal may be illegal (Reuters).

Kipchoge wins in Melbourne and Kipruto in Chicago as Kenyan runners take Sunday's marathons

Eliud Kipchoge, 41, won the Melbourne Marathon on Sunday, his first marathon win in three years, and set a course record, according to the BBC and ABC (Australia); Al Jazeera, The Straits Times and Asharq Al-Awsat also reported the win. In Chicago, Amos Kipruto won the men's race in a dominant run, The Straits Times, CNA and Telegraaf reported, and L'Equipe said he and women's winner Hawi Feysa both set personal bests.

Sharon Lokedi was held off by Feysa for second place in the women's race, CNA reported. DailyDrop has not confirmed the finishing times, so it gives none.

World · Horn of AfricaPress ✓ 5 outlets · toll rose through the day

Boat capsizes off Djibouti: at least 139 migrants dead and 84 missing

A boat carrying about 305 migrants capsized off the coast of Djibouti, leaving at least 139 dead and 84 missing, according to PBS NewsHour, CNA, Mint and Le Temps. Reuters first reported 109 dead, including 15 children, citing a UN agency, before the toll rose. The boat was on the route from Djibouti to Yemen.

The sea route from the Horn of Africa to Yemen is a known migrant route, and the war in Yemen makes the destination more dangerous. Kenyans seeking work in the Gulf should use licensed agencies.

Ruto on Sunday: warns the opposition, sets April 2027 for the Rironi–Mau Summit highway and urges Kenyans to stop splitting family land
