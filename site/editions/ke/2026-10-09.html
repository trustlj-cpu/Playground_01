Oil jumps as Iran steps up tanker attacks and Riyadh is hit again; WTI ends 3.6% higher

Brent touched about US$105 a barrel on Thursday and Wall Street fell. For Kenya, with its pump price review five days away, the move is the main risk to fuel costs.

Oil prices jumped on Thursday 8 October after a tanker attack and slowing flows through the Strait of Hormuz, according to the Financial Times, The New York Times, NBC News and The Guardian. NBC reported Brent at about US$105 a barrel, with stocks tumbling as Trump considered renewed strikes on Iran, in its headline. Reuters headlined that Iran vowed to block more Hormuz routes, and ET Infra that vessel traffic through the strait fell to a two-month low after tanker attacks. WTI closed at US$91.50, up 3.60%.

The S&P 500 closed down 0.47% at 7,765.36 and gold edged up 0.20% to US$4,157.0. For Kenya, which imports its refined fuel, the move comes five days before the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority's monthly price review on the 14th. DailyDrop cannot say what the review will decide; it depends on landed costs over the review period. The Strait of Hormuz, Brent and WTI prices reported by different outlets differ by contract and time of day.

Houthis claim a fresh strike on Riyadh airport as Saudi Arabia counts three dead from the earlier attacks

The Houthis claimed a new attack on Riyadh's airport on Thursday, according to AP and CBS News, and Reuters reported smoke rising from an aircraft there. The New York Times described explosions rattling Riyadh as Saudi Arabia reeled from the deadliest Houthi attacks so far, and DW, the Financial Times and The Japan Times put the toll from the earlier strikes on Saudi airports at three dead.

Reuters reported that Syria is considering helping Saudi Arabia in the Yemen war, while RFI said Turkey ruled out sending troops against the Houthis. DailyDrop has not seen independent confirmation of damage at Riyadh airport beyond the Reuters and AP reports.

Kenya says it has no Ebola outbreak, but one imported death stirs fears of wider spread in East Africa

Al Jazeera, SBS and RFI all reported on Thursday 8 October that Kenya's single imported Ebola death has raised fears of wider spread in Africa, with SBS calling it a wake-up call for East Africa. RFI also carried a report that Kenya denies there is an outbreak in the country, and asked how a confirmed case could travel from the Democratic Republic of Congo to Kenya.

The facts from earlier this week stand: one confirmed case and one death, contacts under monitoring, and no further confirmed case in what DailyDrop has seen. The concern is regional: other countries are watching how Kenya's airport screening failed to flag a very ill traveller on 3 October.
