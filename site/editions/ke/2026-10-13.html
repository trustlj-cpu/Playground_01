World · Middle EastPress ✓ 8 outlets · death toll differs between outlets

Riyadh airport to stay shut this week as Houthis fire again on Monday evening and airlines cancel Saudi flights

Saudi authorities said Saturday's strike killed 12 and wounded 309; CBS News and The Times of India put the dead at 13. On Monday evening the Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed another ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh, and Malaysia urged its citizens to postpone Umrah trips.

Riyadh's airport will stay shut this week as airlines cancel services, the Straits Times reported on Monday 12 October, and the Indian outlet Mint reported that Saudi Arabia closed it until 17 October. Reuters reported airlines cancelling flights to Saudi Arabia, and The Hill that Delta postponed its Atlanta–Riyadh service. The airport, King Khalid International Airport, was struck on Saturday 10 October, when Saudi authorities said 12 people died and 309 were wounded (Washington Post), and again on Sunday. CBS News and The Times of India put the dead at 13, and CBS counted four Houthi attacks on the airport in a week.

A new alarm came on Monday evening. FinancialJuice reported a warning in Riyadh and a witness hearing three booms, the Saudi Gazette reported that coalition forces intercepted a ballistic missile fired by the Houthis towards Riyadh, and GMA reported that the Houthis claimed an attack on the airport. Saudi authorities said the threat had passed (FinancialJuice). Earlier, AP, via PBS NewsHour, reported that Saudi officials were investigating how the air defences failed, and Reuters that Turkey, Pakistan, Egypt and Saudi Arabia condemned the strikes. A Houthi official warned of a "swift, painful response" if the United States enters the Yemen war (Straits Times, Vanguard, Le Figaro), and the Straits Times reported that Iran offered to mediate between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia. The Houthi claims of hits on Dammam airport and Aramco sites remain claims: Saudi civil aviation confirmed only that Dammam airport was targeted.

Business and travel reacted. Reuters reported that top oil executives were dropped from an energy event in Riyadh, CNA and the Straits Times that Malaysia urged citizens to postpone Umrah, and the Japan Times that Japan's trade minister urged oil stockpiling. NBC News reported that energy prices rose on Monday, a move it linked mainly to the failed Russia–Ukraine ceasefire (see right). For Kenya the effects run through flights that connect via Gulf hubs, fuel prices and the many Kenyans who work in or travel to Saudi Arabia. The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority normally announces new maximum pump prices on the 14th of each month, so Wednesday's review is the first test of how far crude has moved. If you plan an Umrah trip or a flight through any Saudi airport, check with your airline or tour operator before travelling.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · Kyiv and Moscow disagree

Trump's "energy ceasefire" does not hold: Kyiv says Russian strikes on power sites go on, and the Kremlin wants a full peace deal

President Trump announced on Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed an energy ceasefire, which Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy said was news to him. On Monday PBS NewsHour reported Zelenskyy as saying Russia was still striking energy facilities, and AP reported the same from Kyiv. Reuters said Ukraine accused Russia of violating the ceasefire, and that the Kremlin rejected Zelenskyy's idea of a 60-day ceasefire because it wants an overall peace deal. Australian outlets, the Sydney Morning Herald and The Age, reported on Tuesday that Ukrainian energy sites were hit after the announcement.

NBC News reported that energy prices rose because the ceasefire failed to move either side. Reuters also reported that the European Union adopted nearly 1,600 new sanctions listings against Russia; that figure comes from one outlet. For Kenya, which imports refined fuel, the link is the world diesel price that feeds into EPRA's monthly review. A real pause in the strikes could ease it, but no terms have been confirmed by both sides.

Pope Leo XIV goes into hospital today for surgery to remove a lung nodule, the Vatican says

The Vatican said Pope Leo would be admitted to hospital on Tuesday 13 October ahead of surgery to remove a lung nodule, according to Reuters, ABC News and CBS News. NBC News reported that the pope was keeping his normal schedule before the operation, and CBS described the public announcement as a rare move for a pontiff.

PBS NewsHour published an explainer on the planned surgery. A nodule is a small growth that can be harmless or not, and DailyDrop has not seen a diagnosis in the outlets it checked, so it gives none.

Trans Nzoia governor Natembeya defiant after skipping President Ruto's tour amid a row over projects

The Star, a Kenyan newspaper, reported early on Monday that Governor George Natembeya of Trans Nzoia was defiant after skipping President William Ruto's tour of the county amid a row over projects. DailyDrop has the headline only, not the governor's words or the details of the dispute, and no second outlet carried it in the window.

Background reported earlier this year: People Daily and the Nation described Natembeya as an opposition figure in western Kenya, and AFP reported in August that Kenya votes in just under a year with Ruto seeking a second term. Treat this item as unconfirmed beyond the headline.

World · EconomicsPress ✓ 6 outlets · prize citation as reported

Americans Robert Hall and Michael Woodford win the Nobel economics prize for work on stabilising economies

Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford won the 2026 Nobel memorial prize in economics, announced on Monday 12 October in Stockholm, according to Reuters, AP, the New York Times, CBS News, USA Today and the Japan Times. Reuters and USA Today described the work as being about stabilisation, and PBS NewsHour as studying how to deal with economic shocks.

The topic matters in Kenya because central banks, including the Central Bank of Kenya, are judged on how well they steady prices and jobs after shocks such as an oil spike. Hall is generally associated with research on unemployment and recessions and Woodford with how central banks guide interest rates; DailyDrop has seen the citation only in headlines, so it does not go further.
