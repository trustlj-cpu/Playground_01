Kenya confirms its second Ebola case, a relative of the first patient, and says there is no evidence of wider spread

The patient, aged 39 according to Al Jazeera and Premium Times, tested positive while in quarantine and is being treated in isolation. CNA reported that 128 contacts of the first case have been identified and 113 traced.

Kenya's Health Ministry confirmed its second Ebola case on Wednesday. Reuters, the BBC, CBC, the Telegraph, Al Jazeera and SABC reported that the patient is a relative of the first case. CBC said the first case was a man who died two days after arriving from Congo; the BBC placed his death last week, and the Telegraph said he died in Nairobi and was thought to be Kenya's first-ever case. Al Jazeera and Premium Times gave the new patient's age as 39, and CBC and Premium Times said the person tested positive, or developed symptoms, while already in quarantine. France 24 noted that, unlike the first case, this person had not travelled.

The government's message was reassurance. Channels TV, CNA and France 24 reported that the ministry sees no evidence of wider spread. CNA added that the patient is being treated in isolation and that authorities have identified 128 contacts linked to the first case and traced 113 of them, a figure from one outlet that implies 15 are still to be traced. The New York Times said the case is likely to raise alarms and increase scrutiny of the government's surveillance measures. Al Jazeera wrote that the infection raises concerns of cross-border spread and, in a separate piece, that the scare exposed gaps in how far outbreak readiness reaches remote communities. None of the items we checked named the hospital or county, described the patient's condition, or said which strain of the virus is involved.

The source of the danger is next door. ABC News, Al Jazeera and FAZ reported this week that confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo have passed 9,000, and ABC and Al Jazeera said containment efforts are faltering. CBS News and Qazinform reported on Thursday that deaths have passed 4,400. Reuters reported that the outbreak has spread to a new province bordering the Central African Republic, while Xinhua quoted Africa CDC saying it is slowing as hotspots shift. SABC noted that Ebola spreads through direct contact with the body fluids of infected people, which is why health authorities rely on contact tracing and quarantine. Follow the Ministry of Health's own notices, and treat unsourced messages about cases in your area with care.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 3 outlets · release totals relayed by FinancialJuice

IEA agrees to speed up its oil stock release, with over 20 million barrels of diesel, as the US weighs easing sanctions on shadow-fleet tankers

The IEA's governing board agreed on Thursday to finish releasing the 100 million barrels still outstanding from March's emergency action over the Strait of Hormuz crisis (Bloomberg, FinancialJuice). Reuters and FinancialJuice said the accelerated release includes more than 20 million barrels of diesel as soon as possible and that the total will exceed 400 million barrels; FinancialJuice put the amount released so far at about 330 million. Reuters also reported that Washington is considering easing some sanctions on shadow fleet vessels to cut freight rates, and that the Kremlin says Russia has restarted diesel exports after a deal with President Trump.

The supply risk has not gone away. FinancialJuice relayed US statements that a civilian container ship sank while being towed to shore and that the M/V GFS Pride was hit by a projectile from Iran's IRGC, and Bloomberg reported a container ship sinking in the strait. Ukraine's president told the FT his forces will resume strikes on Russian refineries (Reuters, Bloomberg, FinancialJuice), and Reuters said a drone attack started a fire at Russia's Ryazan refinery. FinancialJuice reported that Brent settled on Thursday at $105.92 a barrel, down 14 cents, and US crude at $97.97, up $2.88. Kenya imports most of its refined fuel, so diesel prices matter for transport and food costs; we saw no pump-price announcement in the window.

Horn of Africa · SecurityPress ✓ 5 outlets · Eritrean losses are a militia claim

US and UK press Ethiopia and Eritrea to step back as Ethiopian forces expand control in Tigray

Al Jazeera reported on Thursday that Ethiopian forces have expanded their control in Tigray, the northern region bordering Eritrea, while a militia claims Eritrean losses; Eritrea denies any troop movement inside northern Ethiopia. Clarín, Infobae and Punch reported that Washington urged both governments to de-escalate and talk, and Infobae quoted Rubio saying the Horn of Africa cannot tolerate more conflicts. Somalia Today reported that the US warned Abiy against border incursions in a call. The UK government published its own statement on the escalating tensions on 15 October; the items do not give its wording.

Other reports show how tense the mood is. De Telegraaf quoted the International Crisis Group warning that the two countries are dangerously close to total war, and Fox News said the US embassy warned Americans of long-range threats after attacks in Addis Ababa. Both are single-outlet accounts, and we have seen no second outlet confirming attacks on the capital. Separately, the BBC, the Guardian and the Telegraph reported that the UK is threatening visa bans on Eritreans over the government's refusal to take back rejected asylum seekers. Kenya borders Ethiopia, and a wider war would touch trade routes and refugee flows; nothing in the items points to a direct effect yet, and we saw no Kenyan government statement.

World · Economy · MarketsPress ✓ 3 outlets · headline figures differ by outlet

US producer prices post their biggest rise in 4½ years as the AI-led share sell-off deepens

Reuters reported that US producer prices recorded their largest increase in four and a half years in September, and Bloomberg said the rise was the biggest since 2022, with energy costs the driver. FinancialJuice relayed the figures: producer prices up 1.3% on the month against 0.6% expected, and 6.3% on the year. A day earlier, Reuters said consumer prices accelerated in September on costlier gasoline, with core inflation firmer. Fed official Daly told Reuters that more rate hikes are likely needed but not urgently, and FinancialJuice relayed Goolsbee calling the consumer-price data "discouraging". Reuters said the dollar retreated from an 18-month high as officials tempered hike bets.

Shares took the hit. Bloomberg said the S&P 500 on Wednesday ended its longest stretch of calm since 2020 as the AI trade sank, and that the Russell 2000 slid into a correction. On Thursday Reuters said US chip stocks were set to lose $2 trillion in the latest sell-off, while Bloomberg described a $1 trillion chip rout; the figures differ and we could not reconcile them from the headlines. Reuters and Bloomberg reported CoreWeave's shares and bonds sliding on debt fears, and Bloomberg said Treasuries rallied. Reuters also reported a Nasdaq network disruption affecting US equities data feeds. At 19:00 GMT on Thursday the session had not yet closed. A firm dollar and high US yields raise the cost of dollar debt and imports for emerging economies; USD/KES was not confirmed in two sources.

World · Technology · WarPress ✓ 9 outlets · SpaceX and White House accounts not confirmed

Ukraine says Starlink is limiting its service as Musk blames the US government and Europe finds no quick fix

Ukraine said that Starlink, the satellite internet service run by Elon Musk's SpaceX, is limiting its service and disrupting military operations (Shacknews, vijesti.me). Poland's Sikorski asked Musk whether he is playing politics (The Standard HK, Around Prague). Musk said the US government may be behind the curbs (BBC) and denied that SpaceX restricted access, blaming Washington (UPI); the Independent's headline put it as Musk blaming Trump. Reuters reported that Europe has no quick fix and explained Starshield, SpaceX's military version of Starlink. A post on r/worldnews says the White House denies restricting Starlink; we have not seen that in a news outlet.

The row sits on top of a stalled peace effort. Reuters reported Zelenskyy saying it is painful when Trump chooses a different side, and the FT, relayed by Reuters and FinancialJuice, reported that Washington floated unfreezing some Russian assets to restart talks while European officials rejected the idea. The Guardian reported that Wes Streeting urged the US in Washington to back Ukraine after the Starlink row. For readers in Kenya the effect is indirect: the war keeps fuel, fertiliser and grain prices high, and the dispute shows how much a country can depend on a single private network.
