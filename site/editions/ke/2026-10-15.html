Kenya confirms a second Ebola case in a relative of the first patient, found in quarantine, and says there is no evidence of wider spread

The Ministry of Health said on Wednesday that a relative of the man who died in Nairobi last week had tested positive; Al Jazeera and Premium Times put the new patient's age at 39. CNA reported that 113 of 128 contacts of the first case have been traced.

Kenya's Ministry of Health confirmed the country's second Ebola case on Wednesday 14 October, according to Reuters, the BBC, Al Jazeera and CBC. The patient is a relative of the first case, the man who died in Nairobi last week after arriving from DR Congo (BBC, The Telegraph, CBC), and he tested positive while in quarantine (CBC). Al Jazeera and Premium Times, a Nigerian outlet, say he is 39 years old, and Premium Times adds that he developed symptoms during quarantine. France 24 pointed out that, unlike the first case, this patient is not someone who had travelled, so this is not a case that arrived from abroad.

The ministry said there is no evidence of wider spread, a line carried by CNA, Channels Television and France 24. CNA added that the patient is being treated in isolation and that officials have identified 128 contacts of the first case and traced 113 of them. That leaves 15 still to be reached, by DailyDrop's subtraction, and the figures come from one outlet. The relative was already under watch when he fell ill, which is what quarantine and contact tracing are meant to achieve: it shortens the time a sick person can pass the virus on. Al Jazeera said the infection raises concern about cross-border spread.

The wider picture is grim. In DR Congo, where the 17th Ebola outbreak in the country's history is in its fifth month (RFI), confirmed cases have passed 9,000 (Al Jazeera, CTV News, Die Zeit) and more than 4,000 people have died (RFI, FAZ). The World Health Organization has declared a public health emergency of international concern (RFI, NZZ). Africa CDC said the outbreak is slowing as hotspots shift (Xinhua), while Al Jazeera and CTV reported that efforts to curb transmission are faltering. Ebola spreads through direct contact with the bodily fluids of infected people (SABC), which is why health workers and families caring for the sick are the most exposed. Follow the Ministry of Health for updates rather than forwarded messages, and seek medical advice early if you develop a fever after contact with a sick person.

US urges Ethiopia and Eritrea to step back as fears of a new war between the neighbours grow

Reports published on Wednesday evening said the United States had called on Ethiopia and Eritrea to pursue de-escalation and dialogue amid fears of war (Punch, Clarín). Infobae quoted Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying the Horn of Africa cannot tolerate more conflicts, and Somalia Today carried a report that Washington warned against border incursions in a call with Ethiopian prime minister Abiy Ahmed. Clarín added that the US stressed respect for territorial integrity and noted troop movements near the shared border after a revival of hostilities.

The New York Times wrote that the two countries, which fought on the same side in the Tigray war that killed hundreds of thousands, are now edging towards fighting each other, and FAZ reported that Addis Ababa is threatening retaliation over Eritrea's role in the new conflict in northern Ethiopia. DailyDrop has not seen either government's account of the border events confirmed by two outlets. Ethiopia is Kenya's northern neighbour, so a wider war could send refugees towards Moyale and disrupt trade and regional diplomacy.

World · MarketsPress ✓ 9 outlets · S&P 500 closing level not confirmed

Hotter US inflation of 3.7% hits tech shares on Wall Street, and the sell-off rolls through Asia on Thursday

US consumer prices rose 0.6% in September from August and 3.7% over the year, Bloomberg reported, and the FT and Le Temps tied the jump to energy prices linked to the Iran war; Bloomberg put the rise in gasoline at 9.1%. Wall Street had its worst day since July on Wednesday: the S&P 500 fell 1.1% and the Nasdaq 1.9% (SCMP, Clarín), while the Dow slipped 48 points, or 0.1% (SCMP). Bloomberg and the FT tied the chip-led fall to worries about interest rates and the debts of AI companies. By Thursday morning, Reuters, the FT and Bloomberg described a broad tech sell-off in Asia, with Japan's Nikkei sliding at the open (Straits Times) and South Korea's Kospi down more than 5% (Chosun Ilbo, Infobae).

Federal Reserve officials sent a mixed message. Reuters reported Daly saying more rate hikes are likely but not urgently, Bowman saying there is time to wait and see, and Collins seeing another hike on the table before the end of 2026. Reuters added that the dollar retreated from an 18-month high despite the hot data as those comments tempered hike bets. For Kenya the channel is the dollar: a strong dollar makes imported fuel, medicines and machinery dearer in shillings, and higher US rates raise the cost of foreign loans. Kenya · SportPress ✓ 4 outlets · full remarks not seen

Coe says Kenya's doping fight is improving, but admits much work remains before it hosts the 2029 World Championships

World Athletics president Sebastian Coe said progress is being made in the fight against doping in Kenya, while acknowledging there is still significant work to do as the country prepares to host the 2029 World Athletics Championships, in remarks reported from Nairobi on 14 October (Straits Times, CNA). Ouest-France said Coe judged that Kenya has come a long way and that hosting the championships would help the fight, and ANSA's headline made the same point.

The agency summaries note that more than 140 Kenyan athletes have been sanctioned for doping offences since 2017 (Straits Times, ANSA). Kenya's standing in distance running is a source of national pride and income, which is why the doping record matters beyond sport. DailyDrop has seen only the summaries, not Coe's full remarks, and has no detail on testing, funding or the host city's preparations.

World · EnergyPress ✓ 6 outlets · IEA warning and Brent price are single-source

A container ship sinks near Hormuz and a second vessel is reported sinking, as officials say Iran is stepping up attacks on shipping

Reuters reported on Wednesday that a container ship had sunk near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile, citing sources; Bloomberg, citing Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority, reported a cargo ship sinking after a strike that started a fire and forced its crew to be evacuated. The Guardian and FinancialJuice reported that a second, dry-bulk vessel was sinking after a separate hit, and the Guardian described the sharpest uptick in Iranian attacks on shipping. Reuters said officials believe Iran is shifting tactics to step up attacks before the US vote. Iran's own account was not in the reports DailyDrop saw. In Saudi Arabia, Reuters reported that the Houthis vowed more attacks on airports and oil facilities after hitting Aramco's headquarters, and AP quoted Riyadh residents hearing new explosions.

Reuters also reported that the International Energy Agency says diesel shortages could last well into 2027, a warning DailyDrop has seen from one outlet. Reuters and FinancialJuice added that US officials are discussing easing some sanctions on shadow fleet tankers to bring freight rates down and push more Middle East oil to market. FinancialJuice, alone, put Brent at $106.06 a barrel at Wednesday's settlement. Kenya imports refined fuel through Mombasa, so these are the prices behind the monthly review by EPRA. The review due on 14 October was not in the sources DailyDrop reads, so check the regulator's own notice for the new maximum pump prices.
