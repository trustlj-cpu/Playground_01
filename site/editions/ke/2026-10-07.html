Kenya confirms its first Ebola case: a man back from Congo dies at Nairobi Hospital

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale said the Bundibugyo-strain case was imported. Officials are tracing 28 contacts and 23 passengers from the Jambojet flight he took from Kampala.

Kenya confirmed its first Ebola case on Tuesday 6 October, according to the BBC, Al Jazeera, The New York Times, The Standard, Citizen Digital and ABC News. The patient, a Kenyan citizen who had lived in the Democratic Republic of Congo for about seven years, died at Nairobi Hospital. Reports say he fell ill about a month before returning, travelled by road to Kampala and flew to Nairobi on Jambojet on Saturday 3 October, and was taken from the airport to Nairobi Hospital by relatives and friends, where he was isolated. Samples tested positive for the Bundibugyo virus. The Ministry of Health calls it an imported case, not an outbreak.

Duale said 28 contacts, including family members and health workers who cared for the patient, have been identified, and that another 23 passengers from the same flight are being traced. Patrick Amoth, the ministry's director, said contacts will be monitored for 21 days and released after negative tests. AP reports there is no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, and that the outbreak in eastern Congo, declared in mid-May, is growing fast. Reports differ on Uganda's status: AP says it declared its own outbreak over in late August, Al Jazeera says Ebola-free in July. DailyDrop has not seen any further confirmed case in Kenya.

Iran steps up ship attacks in Hormuz even as oil flows recover, and oil bosses say buffers are nearly gone

Iran has stepped up attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz even as oil and gas flows through it climb, according to Bloomberg and CNBC on Tuesday 6 October. CNBC says the rebound in exports is vulnerable to tanker attacks. Separately, Bloomberg and Reuters report oil executives warning that the world has nearly burned through its stockpile buffer, echoing the warning from Aramco's chief a day earlier.

For Kenya, which imports its refined fuel, the mix of recovering flows and thin buffers keeps pump prices exposed to attacks. The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority's next monthly review is due on the 14th. WTI closed Tuesday at US$89.44, almost unchanged. S&P 500 and Nasdaq reach record highs as oil slips and Treasury yields fall

The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,818.93 on Tuesday 6 October, up 0.58%, with the Nasdaq also at a record, according to CNBC and Reuters headlines. CNBC and Bloomberg both reported falling Treasury yields as oil slid, which eased bets on further US interest rate rises and left gold steady. Gold closed at US$4,187.1, up 0.73%, and WTI at US$89.44, up 0.01%.

For Kenya, cooler US yields and a calmer dollar can ease pressure on the shilling and on emerging-market borrowing costs. The US Federal Reserve's meeting minutes are due later on Wednesday, and officials such as the Fed's Daly and Schmid made remarks on Tuesday about inflation.
