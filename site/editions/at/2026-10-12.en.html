Trump announces “energy ceasefire” in Ukraine, Kyiv awaits details – shortly afterwards Russia hits a power plant in Kharkiv

New since yesterday: the US president declares a ceasefire on energy facilities as agreed. Kyiv is waiting for details, Moscow is initially silent, and the attacks continue, according to Zelensky.

US President Donald Trump declared in a post on his platform Truth Social on Sunday, October 11, that an “energy ceasefire” in the war between Russia and Ukraine was in effect from now on; both sides had agreed (Salzburger Nachrichten, BBC, Bloomberg, Reuters). According to Salzburger Nachrichten, this means a mutual renunciation of attacks on energy infrastructure. DailyDrop has no details on duration or monitoring; it is also unclear who is to check compliance. Ukraine was apparently taken by surprise: President Volodymyr Zelensky said, according to Axios, that the announcement was news to him. Later he wrote on Telegram that it was good news and important that it become reality; Kyiv supported any honest and reliable format for ending the fighting and was waiting for details from Washington (Reuters). There was initially no statement from Moscow (BBC). A member of the State Duma suspected, according to Kronen Zeitung, that Trump was only concerned with the oil price. Zelensky had earlier told the US broadcaster ABC that he would organize elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire (Reuters). British Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Canada’s Prime Minister Carney spoke in favor of an energy ceasefire; the European Commission supports Zelensky’s call for a ceasefire and wants to increase funding for Ukraine’s drones and missiles (FinancialJuice).

Shortly after the announcement, Russia hit a power plant in Kharkiv, according to Ukrainian reports; Zelensky said Moscow was continuing its attacks (Reuters, The Hill, Kronen Zeitung). The extent of the damage is not confirmed. In Russian attacks on two Ukrainian cities, six people died, according to Reuters; Russia, for its part, reported a drone alert at Moscow airports with flights temporarily suspended (Kurier, Krone), and the internet company Yandex had a data center in the Vladimir region shut down after a drone attack (Reuters, ORF). For both sides, energy facilities are a lever: Russian strikes on power plants hit the electricity and heating of Ukrainian households, Ukrainian strikes on refineries hit Russia’s fuel supply and revenues. At the same time, the pressure on Kyiv is growing: according to the Financial Times, Washington is threatening to block weapons deliveries if Ukraine keeps attacking Russian refineries (Reuters, FT). Zelensky had said in the morning that Kyiv would stop these strikes if Russia ended its attacks on the energy supply. Trump had also called for a change at the top of Ukraine; Germany and other Europeans backed Zelensky (Reuters, ORF). In the US Congress, resistance is stirring against the diesel deal with Moscow, which yesterday’s edition reported on: an influential Republican senator considers it possibly unlawful (Reuters, FT). On Monday, the EU foreign ministers want to decide in Luxembourg on sanctions against almost 1,600 individuals and organizations that support Russia’s defense industry (Salzburger Nachrichten, according to EU officials). For Austria, the energy price matters most: the WTI oil price closed on Friday at $91.85; how the markets will react to the ceasefire is open. As of Sunday evening, there was no confirmation from the Kremlin. Without confirmation from Moscow and without a monitoring mechanism, the ceasefire remains, for now, an assertion by Trump.

Houthi attacks on Riyadh airport: twelve dead, another strike on Sunday

King Khalid Airport in Riyadh has been hit twice within a day. In the attack by the Yemeni Houthi militia on Saturday, October 10, twelve people died and hundreds were injured (ORF, AP; Salzburger Nachrichten put the death toll at 13). On Sunday afternoon, a projectile struck Terminal 4, according to the Saudi-led military coalition; the Saudi civil aviation authority reported injuries (Salzburger Nachrichten, ORF, BBC). The airport remains closed, according to AP. After the first attack, the Houthis had threatened further strikes and are warning travelers against Saudi airports (Reuters). According to Kurier, Sunday’s strike was already the third attack within a week.

According to the Houthis, Saudi Arabia then bombed the airport of the Yemeni capital Sanaa; this was reported by the Houthi-controlled agency Saba and has not been independently confirmed (Salzburger Nachrichten, Reuters). The Houthis also said they had hit Dammam airport and facilities of the oil company Aramco. The Saudi civil aviation authority confirmed that Dammam was the target of an attack on Sunday evening; for the Aramco facilities there is no second source. Britain advises against non-essential travel to Jeddah, oil executives canceled their attendance at an energy congress in Riyadh, according to the Financial Times, and Saudi shares fell (FinancialJuice, Reuters). According to Kurier, Trump does not rule out taking part in strikes against the Houthis. Russia and Pakistan condemned the attack on the airport; the foreign ministers of Iran and Saudi Arabia spoke about the escalation, and Tehran offered support for a dialogue (FinancialJuice).

Judiciary protests for a week: no hearings from today until Friday

From Monday, October 12, to Friday, October 16, Austria’s judges and prosecutors are largely laying down their work. In the “Action Week for the Rule of Law,” there are no hearings, no decisions are issued and no court office-day appointments are given; urgent cases, including custody cases, are exempt (Salzburger Nachrichten, MeinBezirk). The protest is carried by the Association of Judges, the Association of Public Prosecutors and the GÖD federal representation; they do not call it a strike in the strict sense.

According to the professional associations, around 250 judges and 60 to 70 prosecutors are missing nationwide; average capacity utilization is about 120 percent. As a first step they demand 100 additional budgeted posts and criticize that the double budget provides for no new judge positions. The Ministry of Justice points to the tight budget (MeinBezirk). The figures come from the associations and have not been independently verified.

World · VaticanPress ✓ 6 outlets · finding so far described only by the Pope himself

Pope Leo XIV to undergo lung surgery: small nodule discovered

Pope Leo XIV announced during the Angelus prayer on Sunday, October 11, that he will undergo surgery on his lung. A nodule had been discovered in an early stage and in a clearly delimited spot, he said; the doctors considered an operation necessary (Salzburger Nachrichten, ORF, Reuters, Guardian). According to Kurier, the procedure is to take place in the coming days.

Kronen Zeitung reports that the Pope still wants to make his planned trip to Peru in November. Whether the nodule is benign is not known; DailyDrop has no information on the clinic or the length of recovery.

Markets · Start of the weekPress ✓ 3 outlets · prices per finanzen.at, boerse-social, ECB

Vienna Stock Exchange starts after weekly loss: ATX last at 6,543.45 points, euro at $1.1206

The ATX closed on Friday, October 9, at 6,543.45 points (−0.26 percent) and stood 2.59 percent lower on a weekly basis, but about 22 percent higher since the start of the year (finanzen.at, boerse-social). Among the biggest losers were Porr (−3.84 percent), AT&S (−2.93) and OMV (−2.90); voestalpine gained 5.19 percent. OMV had announced that state-imposed “solidarity contributions” in Austria and Romania will weigh on the third quarter by about €150 to 200 million (finanzen.at only). The Vienna Stock Exchange reopens today after the weekend.

The ECB reference rate was $1.1206 per euro on Friday; on Sunday evening the euro last traded at around 1.1202 (market data). On Wall Street, the indices closed higher on Friday: S&P 500 +0.59 percent at 7,811.54 points, Dow Jones +0.83 percent, Nasdaq +0.64 percent. WTI rose 0.39 percent to $91.85, gold 1.43 percent to $4,216.3. The Nobel Prize in Economics will be announced today.
