ATX plunges 2.65 percent to 6,611.99 points: bank stocks and high bond yields weigh

After three straight days of gains, the biggest one-day loss in weeks for Vienna’s benchmark index. Erste Group loses 4.9 percent; OMV is the only larger stock to rise.

The ATX closed on Wednesday, October 7, 2.65 percent lower at 6,611.99 points; the intraday low was 6,562.76 (finanzen.at, trend.at, wallstreet-online, boerse-social, it-boltwise). For the week it is down 1.57 percent, and it remains up about 24 percent since the start of the year. Bank stocks led the losses: Erste Group fell 4.90 percent to €110.60, BAWAG 3.79 percent to €170.30 and Raiffeisen Bank International 3.91 percent to €57.75. Andritz (−4.26 percent) and voestalpine (−3.59 percent) also declined. The biggest winner was OMV, up 2.39 percent to €72.90.

The market reports give a sell-off in European bank shares and high bond yields, especially on French government bonds, as the reasons. According to the reports, analysts also point to political uncertainty in Germany, France and Spain and to caution ahead of the earnings season; in addition, there was little corporate news from Vienna. The ECB reference rate was $1.1177 per euro, after 1.1269 on Tuesday. On Wall Street the S&P 500 closed down 0.22 percent at 7,801.77 points, WTI lost 1.30 percent to $88.28, and gold 1.11 percent to $4,140.7.

2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry goes to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for their research on mirror-image molecules

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry on Wednesday, October 7, to the Frenchman Henri B. Kagan and the Japanese Kenso Soai (SN, Spektrum, science.ORF.at, ZDFheute, LABO). They are honored for the discovery of nonlinear effects and of autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis, that is, for methods that make it possible to produce, in a targeted way, one of the two mirror-image forms of a molecule.

Kagan, formerly of Université Paris-Sud, found a method in 1986 by which one of the two forms is produced in excess. Soai, of the Tokyo University of Science, demonstrated a reaction in 2003 in which practically only one form is produced. This matters for medicines because mirror-image active substances can behave differently in the body. The prize is worth twelve million Swedish kronor (about €1.06 million).

Fuel price cap since October 1: according to E-Control, prices have so far fallen by 8.1 cents

The two-month fuel price cap has been in force since October 1 and runs until November 30 (news.at, Handelsblatt, Börse-Express, kontrast.at, dolomitenstadt.at). It combines a cut in the mineral oil tax of 6.7 cents per liter with a limit on the margin of 3.5 cents; the government expects relief of about 12 cents per liter for gasoline and diesel. According to the regulator E-Control, average prices have fallen by 8.1 cents since the introduction; the full effect is expected after about a week. On October 1 the average price was €2.169 for diesel and €1.862 for regular gasoline.

An economist at the IHS expects a dampening effect on inflation of about 0.2 percentage points. Criticism comes from the Greens (“watering-can subsidy”) and the FPÖ (does not go far enough); the energy trade warned of filling station closures, and National Bank Governor Martin Kocher argued for targeted relief, for example for commuters. The state secretariat points out that prices depend heavily on the price of crude oil and will not fall back to the level at the start of the year. On Wednesday ORF asked on “Aktuell nach fünf” whether the cap works.

Vienna-Favoriten: first tips reach police after rape of an 85-year-old woman

In the night to Tuesday, October 6, at about 1:35 a.m., an 85-year-old woman on her way home from the main train station was pulled into the inner courtyard of a residential building on Arsenalstraße in Vienna-Favoriten by an unknown man and raped (wien.ORF.at, oe24, Heute, 5min.at, meinbezirk.at). Passersby heard calls for help and saw a man run away. The woman was taken to hospital by Vienna’s ambulance service, the Berufsrettung.

The police released a wanted photo. According to police spokesman Philipp Haßlinger, “several quite concrete tips” have already come in, and the Vienna State Criminal Police Office (South branch) is evaluating them. The police accept tips, also confidentially, at 01-31310-57800. No perpetrator has been arrested; the presumption of innocence applies to every suspect.
