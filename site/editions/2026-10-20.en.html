Kim Yo-jong: “Southern border extremely unstable”… North reviews resuming loudspeaker broadcasts, reinforcing tactical strike assets

General Affairs Department director Kim Yo-jong took issue with the South Korean military’s warning broadcasts and warning shots in a statement on the 19th and raised resuming loudspeaker broadcasts.

North Korean Workers’ Party General Affairs Department director Kim Yo-jong said in a statement on the 19th that “the situation on the southern border is now highly unstable” and that the North is reviewing whether to resume loudspeaker broadcasts toward South Korea, Yonhap and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported. According to Asia Economy and Hankook Ilbo, Kim said the North’s military leadership is also reviewing, as a “preemptive and overpowering measure,” a reinforced deployment of tactical strike assets near the southern border line, and is weighing any other steps it deems necessary. Korea Economic Daily and Chosun Ilbo carried the same account and reported that the statement was aimed at the South Korean military’s response. After the statement came out, Dong-A Ilbo headlined that Kim had made a threat by invoking a “tactical strike buildup,” and Newdaily described it as brazen blame-shifting after the mine provocation. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the statement was released through the Korean Central News Agency. Kim also claimed that “the Lee government’s anti-republic confrontation moves far surpass those of the Yoon government,” Yonhap and Korea Economic Daily reported.

What Kim took issue with is the South Korean military’s response along the Military Demarcation Line. Jaekyung Ilbo reported that Kim claimed South Korean forces carried out loudspeaker warning broadcasts 2,870 times and fired warning shots 201 times from August 1 to October 17. According to Jaekyung Ilbo, Kim cited, among others, DMZ guard posts of the Army’s 7th, 15th, 21st and 22nd divisions on the central and eastern fronts. Seoul Shinmun reported that Kim indicated the North would respond to South Korean warning shots. Newdaily and Jaekyung Ilbo reported that the statement came after a mine planted by North Korean troops exploded in the DMZ last month. The South Korean government had earlier stopped loudspeaker broadcasts toward the North and begun removing the loudspeakers, and Kim issued a statement last year saying the loudspeakers had never been removed. No reports said the North had actually resumed broadcasts or moved strike assets as of early on the 20th, and no official response from the Joint Chiefs of Staff had been confirmed.

President Lee at impromptu Chunchugwan session: “We must take back OPCON early… without nuclear forces, we rank fifth in military power”

President Lee Jae-myung dropped in unannounced on Cheong Wa Dae’s Chunchugwan press room on the 19th and held an impromptu session with reporters for about 90 minutes, Jaekyung Ilbo and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported. According to Yonhap and Dong-A Ilbo, Lee said, “This is not a situation in which we can leave wartime operational control (OPCON) entrusted and neglected,” and “We must take it back early.” Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that Lee said the words ‘recovery’ or ‘restoration’ should be used rather than ‘transfer,’ and that consultations between Seoul and Washington are going well toward getting it back. Yonhap, Korea Economic Daily and Chosun Ilbo reported that Lee said, “Without nuclear forces, Korea ranks fifth in military power,” and “Would we fight a real war with nuclear weapons?” Yonhap and KBS reported that on differences between the National Intelligence Service and U.S. intelligence authorities, he said, “There may be some, but they are fully manageable.” Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that, on the U.S. position that it will hand over only after confirming the South Korean military’s ability to operate independently, Lee said he is ‘grateful for the goodwill, in a way, too.’ The same day, Yonhap reported that the government expressed concern over USFK Commander Brunson’s remarks on OPCON and the DMZ and that the U.S. side responded with sympathy. No specific timing for the handover was given.

KOSPI opens down 3.17%, then reverses to close up 1.76% at 5,768.01… foreigners and institutions both net buyers

The KOSPI opened on the 19th down 3.17% from the previous session at 5,488.41, then made up all of the loss and closed up 99.96 points, or 1.76%, at 5,768.01, Yonhap and Financial News reported. Yonhap reported that the index rose for a second straight day, extending its rebound. The Kosdaq also rose 1.8% and regained the 800 level, Korea Report reported. According to Financial News, on the main KOSPI market foreigners were net buyers of 624.7 billion won and institutions of 878.8 billion won, while individuals sold heavily. Yonhap and SBS reported that over the past week, when the index had slid to the mid-5,000s, foreigners sold nearly 9 trillion won of “Samjeon-Nix” (Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix) but bought exchange-traded funds that bet on a rising index. The early plunge was due to continued selling of AI and chip stocks on the U.S. market last week, Segye Ilbo and Ajunews reported. Seoul Economic TV reported that foreigners and institutions bought together and narrowed the early losses.

Heads of five major banks appear at audit for first time in four years, apologize in turn over hacking; Lee Chan-jin: “We will hold CEOs responsible for internal controls”

Shinhan Bank’s Jung Sang-hyuk, KB Kookmin Bank’s Lee Hwan-joo, Hana Bank’s Lee Ho-seong, Woori Bank’s Jung Jin-wan and NH Nonghyup Bank’s Kang Tae-young appeared as witnesses at the National Assembly Political Affairs Committee’s parliamentary audit of the Financial Supervisory Service on the 19th and apologized for a string of hacking incidents, Yonhap and Chosun Ilbo reported. Tech M and inews24 reported that it was the first time in four years, since 2022, that the heads of all five major banks stood together as audit witnesses. TheElec and Electronic Times reported that the bank heads pledged to increase security budgets and staff and to build an AI-based hacking defense system, and also promised compensation for affected customers. Money Today and Tech M reported that Financial Supervisory Service Governor Lee Chan-jin said CEOs would be held responsible for lax internal controls over these incidents. Yonhap and SBS reported that Lee said the planned listing of Solidigm, SK Hynix’s U.S. subsidiary, could amount to a double listing and that he is “waiting for an authoritative interpretation.”

Police release identity of ‘Songpa mother-in-law murder, wife abduction’ suspect Kim Seong-woo, 26… sent to Prosecution Service on the 20th

The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency held its Personal Information Disclosure Review Committee on the 19th and decided to release the name, age and mug shot of Kim Seong-woo (26), suspected of killing his mother-in-law and abducting his wife in Songpa District, Seoul, Korea Economic Daily and Asia Economy reported. Yonhap reported that police said the seriousness of the harm and the cruelty of the method were recognized and the evidence is sufficient. According to Financial News and Dong-A Ilbo, at around 5:38 a.m. on the 11th Kim stabbed his mother-in-law, in her 50s, to death at his in-laws’ home in Songpa and forced his wife into a car and fled; his wife was found around 10:30 a.m. the same day on a hill near Yangyang, and her life is not in danger. Korea Economic Daily reported that Kim was separated from his wife and was under investigation on a domestic violence charge. His personal information will be posted on the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency website for 30 days, and police plan to send Kim to the Prosecution Service in custody on the 20th on charges of murder of a lineal ascendant and aggravated confinement.
