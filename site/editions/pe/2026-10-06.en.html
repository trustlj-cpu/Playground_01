Politics · Elections✓ 4 outlets · ONPE figures, still partial

Lima: ONPE closed Monday with 92.7% of tally sheets counted and 1,923 sheets sent to the Electoral Juries; López Aliaga still first by about 45,000 votes

The official count stalled at 92.7% because one in fourteen tally sheets was observed. Renovación Popular has 27.7% and Avanza País 26.8%, according to El Comercio, Infobae and RPP.

At the close on Monday, October 5, the ONPE had counted 24,428 tally sheets, 92.702% of the total for Metropolitan Lima, and had sent 1,923 sheets (7.298%) to the Special Electoral Juries (JEE) to resolve observations (El Comercio, Infobae). With 92.729% of the sheets in the cut released by RPP, Rafael López Aliaga’s Renovación Popular had 27.703% and Francis Allison’s Avanza País 26.841%, a difference of about 45,000 votes.

An observed tally sheet is one with errors or doubts, such as missing signatures or figures that do not add up. If the JEE cannot correct it, a vote recount is applied, an exceptional procedure (El Comercio). The JNE speaks of about 6,930 observed tally sheets nationwide. DailyDrop has not yet seen a proclamation date for Lima, and Infobae warns that the final results could take about a month after the vote.

Markets · Peru✓ 5 outlets · BCRP close; BVL unconfirmed

The dollar closed steady at S/ 3.436 after reversing an early fall; the Lima Stock Exchange has no confirmed close

The dollar opened on Monday, October 5, at S/ 3.4175, touched a low of S/ 3.4150 and a high of S/ 3.4370, and closed at S/ 3.436, the same as Friday, according to the BCRP cited by El Comercio, Latina, Gestión and Infobae. About US$ 277 million was traded at an average price of S/ 3.4258. The exchange rate is the price of one dollar in soles. Some outlets rounded the figure to S/ 3.44 (RPP).

Other quotes differ because they measure different things: SUNAT gave 3.437 buy and 3.442 sell, and the parallel market, 3.410 and 3.445 (cuantoestaeldolar.pe, cited by El Comercio). DailyDrop could not confirm in two outlets Monday’s close of the S&P/BVL Perú General. In New York, the S&P 500 closed at 7,773.95 (+0.66%), WTI oil at US$ 89.43 (−1.84%) and gold at US$ 4,156.8 (−0.13%).

Weather · Coastal El Niño✓ 4 outlets · Senamhi and ENFEN notices

The coastal El Niño stays on alert: October will be hotter and an extraordinary event is feared through January

Senamhi and ENFEN maintain the alert status for the coastal El Niño, according to Infobae and Peru21, which report that the phenomenon could extend into November. Infobae also forecast a hotter-than-usual October in Lima because of the coastal El Niño, with high UV radiation, and an article of October 2 speaks of alerts for rains and droughts in the face of the coastal El Niño and the global El Niño.

La República notes that the coastal El Niño disrupts the spring: heat on the coast and a risk of rain toward summer. Last week’s textile emergency decree already mentions the risk of El Niño, and the Government’s request for powers includes actions to prevent it.

Sports · National team✓ 5 outlets · result of October 3

Peru closes its friendlies tour against Colombia after losing 2-0 to Canada, with a brace from Shaffelburg

Canada beat Peru 2-0 on Saturday, October 3, in Montreal, with two goals from Jacob Shaffelburg in the 19th and 35th minutes (Fox Sports México, Plano Informativo, Panamericana). El Peruano wrote that Canada “laid bare the shortcomings” of Mano Menezes’s team.

The tour included a 4-1 defeat to the United States and a 1-1 draw with Mexico. The last match is against Colombia on Tuesday, October 6, in Miami (Publimetro, Andina). That match will close the October FIFA window. One page placed the match with Canada in Vancouver, but the other sources say Montreal.

Politics · Congress✓ 3 outlets · plenary vote pending

Legislative powers: six of eight committees recommended against granting them and the plenary of the Chamber of Deputies must decide

Caretas reports that six of the eight committees of the Chamber of Deputies that gave an opinion, on a consultative basis, recommended not granting legislative powers to the Executive. President Keiko Fujimori’s original request was for 120 days and 66 measures in eight areas, and it reached the Constitution Committee, which is seeking a consensus text, according to Correo and Panamericana.

Premier Luis Galarreta has offered dialogue to the caucuses (Correo, Panamericana). DailyDrop has not yet seen a plenary vote as of the close of this issue, so the final scope, which targets security and El Niño, may change.
