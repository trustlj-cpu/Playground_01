Economy · Finance and fair trade✓ 4 outlets · lawsuit outcome and dealer-status measures undecided

15 firms hit with 1.6265 trillion won in penalty surcharges for rigging Treasury bond auctions… biggest ever in a single Fair Trade Commission case, 7 reported to the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency

The Fair Trade Commission found that 15 banks and securities firms coordinated rates and volumes in advance in Treasury bond auctions. The industry has signaled lawsuits.

On the 11th the Fair Trade Commission said it would issue a corrective order and impose penalty surcharges totaling 1.626575 trillion won on 15 Korea Treasury Bond primary dealers (PD), saying they had coordinated their bid submission rates and winning volumes in advance, or exchanged information, in competitive Treasury bond auctions. It is the largest penalty surcharge the commission has ever imposed in a single case. The previous record was 1.0311 trillion won in the 2016 Qualcomm case. The targets are 10 securities firms and 5 banks; the securities firms were fined 1.376 trillion won in total and the banks 250.5 billion won in total. By firm, KB Securities was fined the most at 332.2 billion won, followed by NH Investment & Securities at 242.8 billion won and Mirae Asset Securities at 214.8 billion won. Penalty surcharges are money an administrative agency imposes on companies that break the law, and differ from fines imposed in criminal trials.

The commission said the collusion ran from January 2020 to June 2023 and covered 150 auctions. In 145 of them, it judged, the firms went beyond exchanging information and agreed on volumes as well. Seven firms with long collusion periods and many auctions taken part in (Mirae Asset Securities, NH Investment & Securities, Industrial Bank of Korea, KB Securities, Kiwoom Securities, Korea Development Bank and Korea Investment & Securities) will be reported to the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency. The Korea Financial Investment Association said the sanctions are excessive and that it would actively support the industry’s lawsuits, and industry observers expect suits to cancel the decision and applications for a stay of enforcement as soon as the written decision is received. The Ministry of Economy and Finance said it would review whether to suspend or revoke dealer status after the decision is finalized. It is not yet known whether the penalty surcharges will be finalized or how they might change in court.

International · Ukraine war✓ 3 outlets · size and timing of prisoner exchange unconfirmed

U.S.–Russia–Ukraine talks end after two days… Russian side: “agreed in principle on largest-ever prisoner exchange,” size undecided, territorial differences unchanged

Peace talks between Russia and Ukraine, resumed after nine months under U.S. mediation and held in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, ended on the 11th (local time) after two days. The Russian side said it had agreed in principle to a large-scale prisoner exchange. The size, however, has not been set. The Russian side said it would exceed 1,000 people, while Ukrainian President Zelensky said the two sides had each discussed 1,000 to 1,500. The Russian delegation said it would soon announce the specifics of the exchange. No common ground was found on territory and the postwar European security architecture. Russia’s position is that territory should be discussed together with the European security order, comprehensively, while Ukraine and Europe hold that a ceasefire should come first, followed by discussion of security guarantees. U.S. envoy Kushner called territory the most difficult issue and said the envoy would meet Russian President Putin on the 12th and then President Zelensky. Both sides agreed to continue the talks.

Politics · Party and government✓ 4 outlets · whether differences over the real estate tax were reconciled not officially confirmed

President Lee Jae-myung holds 3-hour dinner with Democratic Party leadership… stresses “party and government of one heart and one mind,” whether differences over the comprehensive real estate tax were reconciled unclear

President Lee Jae-myung had a dinner of about three hours, from 6:30 p.m. to 9:20 p.m. on the 11th at Cheong Wa Dae, with Democratic Party leader Kim Min-seok and floor leader Han Byung-do. President Lee said, “The ruling party and the government must move with one heart and one mind so that our people’s lives can get better,” and asked the ruling party and the National Assembly to cooperate actively in passing next year’s budget, Cheong Wa Dae chief spokesperson Kang Yu-jung said in a written briefing. Leader Kim asked for bold and swift government measures to overcome polarization and boost consumption.

The dinner took place amid differences between the ruling party and the government over the comprehensive real estate tax basic deduction for non-resident single-home owners. At a high-level party-government consultation, the ruling party demanded raising the basic deduction for non-resident single-home owners to 1.4 billion won as well, the same as for residents. The government’s position is to keep the gap of 1.2 billion won for non-residents and 1.4 billion won for actual residents. The written briefing says nothing about reconciling this issue. There is a report that a Cheong Wa Dae official said real estate taxes were not discussed, but the official briefing carries no such statement.

Environment and energy · Climate✓ 3 outlets · Korea’s current electrification rate differs by count

Government backs COP31 ‘35-by-35’ electrification pledge… 35% global electricity share by 2035 is the goal, not a domestic target

On the 11th the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment said it supports the ‘35-by-35’ pledge to meet 35% of global final energy consumption with electricity by 2035, at the 31st Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (COP31) under way in Antalya, Türkiye. Final energy consumption is the total amount of energy that households, industry and transport actually use. The pledge document explains that, according to 1.5-degree pathway analyses by the International Energy Agency and the International Renewable Energy Agency, this share needs to be about 35% in 2035 and 50% or more in 2050.

An official at the climate ministry explained that the pledge does not mean raising the electrification rate inside Korea to 35%, and that the intent is to help achieve the 35% global average. The electrification rate is the share of electricity in final energy consumption. Korea’s current share differs from report to report, from 21.8% to about one third, depending on the counting basis. An explanation was also given that Korea’s high petrochemical share makes the figure come out low when counted on a domestic basis.

Society · Incident✓ 4 outlets · motive under police investigation

Man in his 50s held in emergency arrest over killing of teenage girl known as sexual-violence victim in Seoul’s Seongbuk District… gender equality minister: “anti-social crime”

Seoul’s Seongbuk Police Station placed a man in his 50s under emergency arrest on suspicion of murder at about 1:40 a.m. on the 11th. The dead person is a teenage girl, 15 years old. She is known to be the victim in a sexual-crime case committed this year by the arrested man’s son, and the son is said to be in custody on other theft charges. Police are investigating the circumstances and motive, and the motive has not yet been officially confirmed.

Won Min-kyung, minister of Gender Equality and Family, said the same day that acts that exploit the vulnerability of young people to cause harm are an “anti-social crime” that must be dealt with most sternly. The minister said that a young sexual-violence victim had not been protected by public systems, expressed a heavy sense of responsibility, and said policies would be supplemented to strengthen the youth safety net and support the recovery and independence of young victims.
