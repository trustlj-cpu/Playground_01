Saudi Arabia · SecurityPress ✓ 8 outlets · figures attributed to the coalition and Civil Defense

Coalition: the Houthis tried to hit dozens of Saudi airports, oil facilities and ports; 14 missiles and drones were intercepted over the Eastern Province, Riyadh and al-Kharj

Shrapnel from an intercepted missile hit an Arab resident in Riyadh. We found no second confirmation of the death toll the coalition cited for Riyadh airport.

The Saudi-led coalition said on Thursday that the Houthis tried to target dozens of airports, oil facilities and ports in the kingdom, according to Reuters and Financial Juice. Financial Juice alone quoted it as saying that the group had obtained advanced weapons and that 20 attacks targeted Riyadh airport in October and left 17 dead. This figure differs from what we published in an earlier issue on civil aviation, 12 dead in the strike on King Khalid Airport, and we found no second source for it, so we leave it attributed to the one who stated it.

The coalition announced, according to Asharq Al-Awsat, that it intercepted and destroyed 14 missiles and drones, among them ballistic and cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh, al-Kharj and the Eastern Province. Its spokesman, Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki, said, according to Okaz, that a drone was intercepted and destroyed in the Eastern Province, and Financial Juice carried the same report. The latter alone relayed an early warning in Najran and Jazan, then an all-clear announcement in Najran.

In Riyadh, Civil Defense announced, according to Saudi Gazette and Asharq Al-Awsat’s English edition, that shrapnel from an intercepted Houthi ballistic missile hit an Arab resident and damaged a house and a vehicle on Wednesday. The BBC wrote that the Houthis keep shelling airports and oil facilities. We found no report in the sources on the condition of the injured person, or on a date for resuming operations at King Khalid Airport.

In Yemen, Al-Watan and Asharq Al-Awsat’s English edition said that the “Yemen Dawn” operation had intensified air and ground strikes on the Houthis’ capabilities. The Yemeni government called on the United Nations and the Security Council to act urgently to lift the siege of Taiz, according to Okaz and Asharq Al-Awsat. Okaz alone published that the Houthis had buried 279 fighters, 125 of them commanders, citing Yemeni media, and we did not verify the figure.

Diplomatically, Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi, in his meeting with the Crown Prince in Riyadh on Wednesday, condemned the attack on King Khalid Airport and the attacks on civilian objects, according to Saudi Gazette and Asharq Al-Awsat. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani renewed his country’s condemnation in a call with Prince Faisal bin Farhan, according to the same two newspapers. Jordan’s Crown Prince said that Saudi Arabia’s security is the security of his own country, according to the sites Shabaka Tawasul and Sada Al-Balad. Separately, the European Union and the Gulf states postponed their summit planned in Riyadh, according to Reuters.

On another front, the Houthis said their strike on the “Saudi military operations headquarters” in Aden is part of a policy of targeting Saudi reinforcements, according to Financial Juice. The same outlet relayed, citing Tasnim citing Agence France-Presse (AFP), that Yemen rejected a Saudi request for talks in Oman, a three-link account for which we found no confirmation. AFP warned of a clip of a Saudi passenger plane exploding after a Houthi attack, saying it was generated by artificial intelligence. Reuters ran two analyses, one on how the Houthis are turning Saudi Arabia into a pressure point in Iran’s interest, the other on how Aramco keeps pumping under fire, and we saw only the headlines.

In DailyDrop’s reading, the war is widening from targeting facilities to targeting confidence: condemnations keep coming and a summit is postponed, while the figures differ between sources. Today, Friday, we are following the Civil Defense and coalition statements, the status of King Khalid Airport, and the new date for the European–Gulf summit.

Shipping · HormuzPress ✓ 7 outlets · cause attributed to sources

A container ship sinks near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile, and Washington says it sank while being towed to shore

Reuters, citing sources, said that a container ship sank near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile. Bloomberg reported, citing Singapore’s Maritime and Port Authority, that a strike started a fire and the crew was evacuated, and Asharq Al-Awsat reported that the International Maritime Organization announced the sinking of a cargo ship near the strait. Al Jazeera Net named the ship “GFS Pride”.

The Guardian wrote that a second cargo ship apparently sank, in the most intense wave of Iranian attacks on shipping since the start of the war, and CNBC headlined that Iran is escalating its attacks. Financial Juice then quoted a US official as saying the ship sank while being towed to shore. The Iranian navy said, according to state media relayed by Financial Juice alone, that a “violating” tanker is burning east of the strait.

Washington is considering, according to Reuters, easing sanctions on vessels of the “shadow fleet” to bring down freight rates. Sky News Arabia said that CENTCOM destroyed an Iranian boat that was monitoring ships, an account from one source. Reuters reported that the cost of shipping oil is rising and reaching fuel and grocery prices. We found no Iranian claim of responsibility for hitting this particular ship in the sources, nor the number of crew or its cargo. In DailyDrop’s reading, the test is shifting from scattered attacks to a lasting transport cost: whether insurance and freight rates rise again, and whether incidents spread beyond the strait.

Economy · IMFPress ✓ 5 sources · forecasts attributed to the Fund

IMF expects Middle East economies to recover to 6.6% growth in 2027, and warns the conflict could continue

The International Monetary Fund expects the economies of the Middle East, North Africa and Pakistan to recover to growth of 6.6% in 2027, according to Asharq Al-Awsat and Financial Juice. The latter alone reported that the region will contract 1.3% this year after growth of 3.3% in 2025, and that war-hit oil exporters will see a sharp contraction followed by a recovery, with output staying below pre-war forecasts. Financial Juice quoted a Fund official as saying the biggest risk is a continuation of the conflict.

On Saudi Arabia, Bloomberg said the Fund sees fiscal reserves and alternative export facilities easing the war’s effect on the economy, a headline from one source. The Fund’s managing director, Kristalina Georgieva, said, according to Saudi Gazette, that developments in Saudi Arabia could leave negative effects on everyone everywhere. On Asia, the Fund warned of slowing growth under pressure from the energy shock and rising yields, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat.

Georgieva urged the United States and rich countries to rein in debt to ease pressure on the global economy, according to Asharq Al-Awsat’s English edition, while the US Treasury Department rejected the Fund’s warning on debt, according to CNBC via Financial Juice. In Lebanon, central bank governor Karim Said said his country’s talks with the Fund are moving at a good pace, according to Bloomberg.

Markets · United StatesPress ✓ 7 sources · no confirmed close for Wall Street indexes

US inflation jumps to 3.7% in September, tech stocks fall and Asia follows, and the dollar retreats from an 18-month high

The US consumer price index rose 3.7% year on year in September, against 3.4% in August, and 0.6% month on month, according to Okaz citing the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Reuters said prices accelerated with costlier gasoline and that core inflation is stronger. Bloomberg spoke of bets on a sharper interest-rate hike by the Federal Reserve, and Daly of the Federal Reserve said, according to Financial Juice, that the risks of inflation above target remain high.

Tech stocks fell. The Financial Times headlined that chip stocks are sliding because of inflation and AI debt, and Asian bourses fell sharply on Thursday, especially in South Korea and Japan, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat. We found no confirmed closing levels for the US session’s indexes in the sources. Gold bounced more than 1% after the dollar retreated, and oil fell more than a dollar in early trading, according to Asharq Al-Awsat.

Japan’s finance minister warned of fresh intervention in the currency market after the yen touched its weakest level this month, according to Bloomberg. Bitcoin briefly fell below $80,000, according to Bloomberg alone, and Nasdaq announced a network outage that affected stock data, according to Reuters alone.

Ukraine · RussiaPress ✓ 6 outlets · statements attributed to those who made them

Washington proposes lifting the freeze on some Russian assets to revive talks and Europe refuses, while Zelensky announces a resumption of strikes on Russian refineries

The Financial Times reported, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat, that US negotiators proposed to the Europeans in Miami lifting the freeze on some Russian assets to revive talks to end the war. Financial Juice, citing the paper, said European officials rejected the plan, and the British finance minister said, according to the same service, that London is still weighing all options, a single source.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said his country will resume striking Russian oil refineries, according to Reuters, Bloomberg and The Guardian, and that it is “painful” that Trump chooses to stand on the other side, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat. Meanwhile, Asharq Al-Awsat reported that Moscow ignored the “energy truce” and targeted Kyiv, and that Ukraine says SpaceX is hampering its offensive and defensive drones via Starlink. Elon Musk demanded that Zelensky step down after the Starlink dispute, according to AFP and The Straits Times, and Britain’s Wes Streeting urged Washington to back Ukraine during his visit there, according to The Guardian. In DailyDrop’s reading, the war reaches our reader through energy prices, and any new strike on refineries touches the diesel market directly.
