Security · Egypt and Saudi Arabia✓ 3 sources for the request · the postponement comes from Agence France-Presse via The New Arab and was not confirmed by an independent source

Sisi asks parliament for a mandate to defend Saudi Arabia if asked, according to Reuters and The National

The Egyptian president says he is talking only about defense, and Agence France-Presse reports a postponement of the vote without confirmation from an independent source.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi asked parliament on Saturday, October 17, to hold an emergency session to give him a mandate to act in defense of Saudi Arabia if Cairo is asked to, according to Reuters as published by US News, The National and Middle East Monitor. Sisi said, according to Agence France-Presse, that he is not talking about going to war with anyone or attacking any party, but only about defense. Middle East Monitor reported that parliament will discuss the request. Agence France-Presse noted that the request comes as the kingdom faces near-daily Houthi attacks since early October.

On Sunday, October 18, Agence France-Presse, via The New Arab, reported that two members of parliament said the chamber had postponed the vote on Sisi’s request, with deliberations to continue on Monday. No other independent outlet confirmed the postponement. The reports did not say that Riyadh had made a formal request for intervention, and they did not specify the kind of any possible Egyptian participation or the date of any vote.

Gulf · Yemen✓ 4 sources · the decision is a call to the international community, not a designation in itself

GCC calls from Manama for designating the Houthis a terrorist group and demands firm international action

The Ministerial Council of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), at its 168th session in Manama on Sunday, called on the international community to designate the Houthi militia a terrorist group, according to Saudi Gazette, Saba and Yemen Channel. Saudi Gazette said Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan took part in the meeting. Al-Watan reported that the council escalated its stance on the Houthi attacks on the kingdom and demanded firm international action to stop them. Arab News reported that the GCC Secretary-General, Jasem Albudaiwi, described the Houthis’ conduct as criminal conduct that calls for a firm international position. Saba reported that the council renewed its support for Yemen’s unity and sovereignty. The Ministerial Council had called for a firm stance toward the group at earlier sessions, according to the website of Yemen’s Foreign Ministry. The reports did not say that any country or international body had announced a designation of the Houthis as a result of this call.

Gulf · Defense✓ 5 sources · the agenda of the talks was not published

Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman meets the UAE president and Mansour bin Zayed in Abu Dhabi

Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman bin Abdulaziz met on Sunday, October 18, the President of the UAE, Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, in Abu Dhabi, according to Okaz, Saudi Gazette, Asharq Al-Awsat, Gulf News and Arab News. Asharq Al-Awsat and Gulf News reported that Prince Khalid conveyed to the UAE president the greetings of King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, and that the UAE president asked him to convey his greetings to the Saudi leadership. Arab News said the two sides discussed regional developments and ways to strengthen cooperation and joint Gulf action.

Okaz reported that Prince Khalid also met the Vice President of the UAE, Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed, and that they reviewed the fraternal relations between the two countries. Al Ain News described the meeting with the UAE president as a discussion of strengthening relations and consolidating the region’s security. These outlets did not publish specific agenda items for the talks or any agreements that came out of them.

Economy · Central bank✓ 4 sources · full text of the speech not available

SAMA Governor Ayman Al-Sayari: the Saudi economy is resilient in the face of regional challenges

The governor of the Saudi central bank, SAMA, Ayman bin Mohammed Al-Sayari, affirmed on Sunday, October 18, the resilience of the Saudi economy as the conflict in the Middle East continues, according to Okaz and Al-Watan. Saudi Gazette reported that the governor said this before the International Monetary Fund. Al-Sayari had said in an earlier speech this month, according to Asharq Al-Awsat and Zawya, that the kingdom has maintained a large degree of resilience despite its proximity to regional tensions, and he pointed to strong foreign reserves, long-term investment in infrastructure and economic diversification under Vision 2030.

He added, according to Zawya, that the East-West pipeline helped the kingdom keep meeting its customers’ demand for oil. He warned that a prolonged regional conflict could raise inflation and slow global growth, and said global economic growth is expected to reach about 3 percent in 2026 and global inflation is expected at about 4.7 percent. These reports did not publish any new monetary policy decision.

World · Roundup✓ two or more sources for each item · arrest figures in France differ between the two sources

Macron voices “anger and shame” after the riots, Netanyahu hints at a strike on Iran without Washington, and Turkey gets a share of Russian diesel

French President Emmanuel Macron said on Sunday, October 18, that he felt “anger and shame” over the vandalism and looting that accompanied Saturday’s demonstrations, according to Asharq Al-Awsat and CNBC. CNBC said the protests began last month over a shortage of teachers and poor conditions in schools, then widened to include objections to inequality, spending cuts and the rising cost of living. Macron said, according to CNBC and TRT World, that those who looted would bear the cost of the damage and that those who tried to bring down the Republic would be held to account. The Associated Press, which CNBC cited, reported that at least 1,800 people were arrested, while TRT World, citing the Interior Ministry, put the number arrested on Saturday at about 2,400.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said on Sunday that Israel is ready to carry out a preemptive strike against Iran without American support and that the goals he has set do not depend on Washington’s approval, according to Israel Hayom, Iran International and Asharq Al-Awsat. Iran International reported that U.S. President Donald Trump said the United States could finish the job in Iran soon, and that Washington will not attack Iran before the U.S. midterm elections on November 3. On energy, Turkish Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar said, according to Okaz, Business Recorder and Ukrainska Pravda, that his country will get a share of the Russian diesel being offered on the markets under the agreement between Moscow and Washington, and he did not announce the quantities or a start date.
