Chairman of Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council announces military operations to retake all Houthi-held areas

Rashad al-Alimi announced the start of the operations in a televised address on Sunday, while the Houthis claimed the Saudi-led coalition carried out about 50 strikes in 12 hours. There is no independent confirmation of this figure.

Rashad al-Alimi, chairman of Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council, announced on Sunday, October 4, the start of military operations aimed at retaking Houthi-held areas and extending state authority across the country, according to Reuters, CNN and Al Jazeera. The announcement came in a televised address. The Houthis have controlled Sanaa and most of the north-west since 2014. Last month they launched a surprise offensive in which they seized the Bab al-Mandab strait and about 90 miles of the Red Sea coast, according to press reports.

The Houthis’ military spokesman Yahya Saree said that aircraft of the Saudi-led coalition carried out about 50 air and missile strikes over the past 12 hours on Sanaa, al-Jawf, Taiz, Amran, Hajjah and Saada, according to Xinhua. This is a Houthi figure for which we have seen no independent confirmation, and we have seen no official Saudi statement specifying the scale of air support.

Three files overlap here: a Yemeni ground operation led by an internationally recognised government, Houthi escalation against facilities in Saudi Arabia (see the next story), and the Red Sea corridor through which energy and trade shipments pass. Analysts, as relayed by media outlets, say government forces do not operate under a unified command, which may slow progress. In DailyDrop’s reading, a widening front raises the likelihood of a Houthi response on Saudi territory in the coming days.

Today, Monday, three things are being watched: which cities or sites government forces announce they have taken and whether the Houthis confirm it, coalition statements on air operations, and any attacks on Saudi facilities.

Houthis say they targeted Aramco facilities in Riyadh and Khurais with missiles and drones; Riyadh does not confirm

The Houthis said on Sunday that they fired ballistic missiles and drones at two Saudi Aramco facilities, one in Riyadh and the other in the Khurais field east of the capital, and that fires broke out in both, according to Reuters (via Dawn), Xinhua and Kurdistan 24. The Houthi spokesman was quoted as saying the operation was a response to the strikes and that the attacks would continue until what he called the “blockade” on Yemen is lifted.

Neither Aramco nor the Saudi authorities have confirmed any damage, fire or effect on production, as far as we have seen, and reports quoted coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki as calling the claims of targeting misleading. France 24 had reported smoke and flames near Riyadh airport on September 19. What actually happened at the two sites remains unconfirmed for now.

OPEC+ keeps November production targets unchanged as oil stays above $100

The seven core OPEC+ countries, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman, agreed at an online meeting on Sunday to keep production targets for November as they are, according to Reuters, CNBC and The National. One report said Saudi Arabia’s target is 10.5 million barrels a day, which we have not verified from a second source.

Gulf countries are pumping far below their quotas because of disrupted exports in the US-Israeli war on Iran, and exports have ranged between 60% and 80% of normal levels, according to Reuters. Brent was about $73 before the war in late February and closed on Friday near $102.25, according to The National. Giovanni Staunovo of UBS said the market is still tight. The next meeting is on November 1, and the production capacity review that sets 2027 quotas has been delayed by the war.

Saudi market index rebounds 1.09% to close at 10,505.84 points after four sessions of decline

The Saudi stock market index TASI rose on Sunday by 112.87 points, or 1.09%, to close at 10,505.84 points, according to Arab News, after four consecutive sessions of decline. Trading value reached about 2.26 billion riyals on a volume of 109.01 million shares, with 220 shares rising against 41 falling. The previous close, on October 1, was 10,393 points, the lowest level since January, according to Argaam.

Changes to the market-order mechanism in the main market and Nomu took effect that day, according to Argaam. We found no single confirmed explanation for the rebound; it is likely buyers returning after the drop, which is DailyDrop’s reading. The Saudi riyal’s official rate is pegged to the dollar at 3.75.

Ghalibaf: Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until Iran’s seven conditions are met, and Tehran holds to its seven-day plan

Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said on Sunday that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the seven conditions set out in the “Islamabad Memorandum” are implemented, according to Pajhwok, Kurdistan 24, Seoul Economic Daily and Al Jazeera. Iran was reported to have presented Washington, on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, with a seven-day plan: it would open the strait during that time if the United States lifted its naval blockade, released the frozen funds, exempted Iranian oil from sanctions, and halted the fighting on all fronts.

We have not seen the full text of the seven conditions or the US reply, which Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi received in Doha according to reports and whose content was not disclosed. For readers in Saudi Arabia this matters directly: Gulf oil crosses the strait, and any closure cuts exports and raises prices, as we have seen, while also making Gulf markets more sensitive to any news. In DailyDrop’s reading, Tehran’s public hard line may be a negotiating position, but we have no evidence for that.
