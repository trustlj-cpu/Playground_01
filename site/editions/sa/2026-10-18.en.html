Sisi: Egypt ready to carry out what Saudi Arabia asks, as the kingdom intercepts missiles and drones

The Egyptian president announces his country’s readiness to defend the kingdom, while coverage speaks of missiles and drones intercepted over Riyadh, the Eastern Region and the south, and an Iranian denial of any role.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi affirmed on Saturday, October 17, his country’s readiness to carry out any measure Saudi Arabia requests and to defend the kingdom, according to Okaz and Saudi Gazette. The statement tied this readiness to the directions of King Salman and the crown prince, meaning the formula came as a response to what Riyadh determines rather than an independent initiative. Media outlets also circulated another headline saying Sisi is seeking a mandate from the Egyptian parliament for any intervention Saudi Arabia requests, which makes the announcement more than a passing expression of solidarity. However, the texts we reviewed do not specify the form of the possible intervention, when the request would be put to deputies, or whether Riyadh has made an actual request.

On the same day, coverage, including that published by France 24, spoke of Saudi Arabia intercepting missiles and drones over several regions, while Iran denied involvement in the Houthi attacks. Al-Yawm Al-Sabi’ quoted the Coalition as saying it intercepted and destroyed four drones in the Riyadh, Eastern and Southern regions. The available headlines and summaries do not state the timing of each interception precisely, where debris fell, or whether there were casualties or damage, so we present the figures as they appear at their source without adding to them.

These reports coincide with developments in Yemen covered in the next story: the Arab League’s condemnation of Houthi attacks on Maashiq Palace in Aden, the Yemeni government’s request that the Organization of Islamic Cooperation designate the group a terrorist organization, and its rejection of an Iranian mediation, regarding Tehran as a party to the war. The only clear thread between these reports is that they came out on the same day; as for any causal relationship between them, no official body confirmed it in what we monitored.

To tell the accounts apart: the Egyptian statement was attributed to Sisi directly and repeated in more than one outlet; the interception figures come from the Coalition as relayed by an Egyptian newspaper; and the denial came from Iran alone. We found no separate official Saudi statement linking Cairo’s statement to a specific measure. Three things are therefore known: a public statement, a headline about a request for a parliamentary mandate, and announced interceptions. Unknown are the content of any military arrangements, the scale of any participation, and the identity of whoever fired the projectiles among the parties mentioned.

What deserves following today: the text of any request sent to the Egyptian parliament, if published; any statement from the Saudi Ministry of Defense or the Coalition on the tally of interceptions; and Tehran’s response to the Houthi accusations. Until these details become clear, the safest course is to read the news within its current limits: a declaration of readiness, not a declaration of an operation.

Arab League condemns Houthi attacks on Maashiq Palace, and the Yemeni government rejects Iranian mediation

The Arab League condemned the attacks launched by the Houthi militia on Maashiq Palace, seat of the Yemeni government in Aden, and on civilian facilities, according to Okaz on Saturday. On the same day the Yemeni government urged member states of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation to take a firm stance toward the group and to designate it a terrorist organization. The available summaries gave no human or material toll for the attack on Maashiq, nor the type of weapon used, so we cite no figure that was not given. Separately, the Yemeni government rejected the Iranian regime’s attempts to present itself as a mediator in the crisis, stressing that Tehran is a party to the war, not a mediator. For its part, France 24 reported that Iran denies involvement in the Houthi attacks, so the two accounts cross at one point: the debate over Tehran’s role has become part of the daily coverage, with no party offering published evidence. In Marib governorate, the Murad tribes announced a general mobilization and a broad call to avenge Brigadier Ahmed Buhaibih al-Muradi, according to Okaz, which also spoke of airstrikes under what it called “Fajr al-Yaman” (Dawn of Yemen). No second outlet verified these details in what we monitored, so they remain attributed to Okaz alone, and the summary does not explain the circumstances, place or date of the brigadier’s death. In a related item, the head of the Yemeni Coast Guard Authority, Maj. Gen. Khalid al-Qumali, told Okaz that the current stage requires integrated efforts and coordination, and that the authority is monitoring smuggling routes to deprive the Houthi group of external support. What is confirmed is that positions were recorded in a single day: a condemnation from the Arab League, a request for designation, and a rejection of Iranian mediation. As for the field, most of its details rest on a single source.

Al-Jadaan: Saudi investments limited the fallout from the energy shock, and SAMA governor warns of repeated shocks

Saudi Finance Minister and chair of the International Monetary and Financial Committee at the International Monetary Fund, Mohammed Al-Jadaan, said that investments by countries such as Saudi Arabia limited the fallout of the energy shock on the global economy, according to Saudi Gazette from Bangkok and Okaz on Saturday. In another statement relayed by Asharq Al-Awsat, Al-Jadaan affirmed that continuing wars and conflicts and disruptions to supply chains impose human and economic costs and raise risks to the global economy.

On the same day, the governor of the Saudi central bank SAMA, Ayman bin Mohammed Al-Sayari, affirmed that geopolitical fragmentation, technological change and repeated shocks threaten global economic stability, according to Okaz and a second economic source. The two speeches agree in the headlines that successive shocks have become a feature of the period, but the available summaries present no figures on growth, oil prices or the size of the investments Al-Jadaan referred to, so we add no number of our own.

As economic background, data from the previous day show that West Texas Intermediate crude closed at $99.02, up 2.61 percent, that gold closed at $4181.1, up 0.26 percent, and that the S&P 500 closed at 7659.44 points, down 1.09 percent, according to Friday’s readings. The published statements do not link these figures to the officials’ words; they are shown here for context only. In a related item, Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said the bank is heading toward sharply raising its inflation forecasts during October, driven by higher oil, according to Okaz.

The coverage contains no new Saudi monetary policy decision; what was published is speeches at international meetings, not measures.

13,822 violators of residency, labor and border-security regulations arrested in a week, and the foreign minister receives a call from his Jordanian counterpart

Saudi security forces announced the arrest of 13,822 residents in violation of residency, labor and border-security regulations in a week, according to Saudi Gazette from Riyadh. The figure is a weekly tally usually announced as part of joint campaigns, but the available summary does not divide it between those violating residency and labor rules and border infiltrators, and does not specify the regions, so we give only the total as it appeared.

On the diplomatic front, Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan bin Abdullah received a phone call from Jordan’s Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates, according to Okaz, and the two sides discussed regional developments. The summary does not make clear the items discussed or any agreement that resulted, and it does not appear that a second outlet published fuller details, so the story remains attributed to Okaz.

The call comes on a day when the regional security file led the newspapers’ headlines: Sisi’s statement of readiness to defend the kingdom, the interception of drones, and attacks in Aden. However, the summaries do not say the Jordanian call is connected specifically to those events, so we note the timing without assuming a cause.

On the local sports front, the Saudi league continues, with the main results being Al-Ittifaq’s win over NEOM by a goal to nil and Al-Qadsiah going top after beating Abha 2-1, news we present on the last page in the briefs.

World · RoundupPress ✓ one source for most items · figures attributed to Okaz and Asharq Al-Awsat

Protests in France in the hundreds of thousands, two drones over Romania, and Ebola in Kenya: top international news

France saw mass demonstrations on Saturday over rising living costs and falling purchasing power, and Okaz put the number of participants at about 800,000, while President Emmanuel Macron threatened the vandals. The summary did not make clear the source of the figure or whether the police announced a different tally, so we attribute it to the newspaper. In Albania, anti-government protests entered their fifth consecutive day, and Okaz spoke of street clashes besieging Edi Rama’s government.

In Eastern Europe, Romania announced on Saturday that it shot down two drones that had violated its airspace over the Black Sea, a development Okaz described as coming amid rising regional tension. The summary did not mention the drones’ origin or the identity of their operator.

On health, Kenya’s Ministry of Health announced four new Ebola cases, three of them among health workers, bringing the total number of cases to seven. In Mexico, meteorologists issued warnings of heavy rain, flash floods and dangerous coastal waves as Hurricane “Trudy” approaches the west and center of the country. On the economic front, the Bank of Canada governor said the bank will sharply raise its inflation forecasts this month, driven by higher oil.

These international stories do not directly touch Saudi Arabia, but some of them, like oil and inflation, are tied to the economy that Al-Jadaan and Al-Sayari discussed the same day.
