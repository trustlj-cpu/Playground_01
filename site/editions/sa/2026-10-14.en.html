Saudi Arabia · SecurityPress ✓ 7 outlets · figures attributed to Civil Defense and the coalition

Shrapnel from two intercepted missiles hits four people in Riyadh and damages 15 homes; an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran is targeted with no injuries

The coalition said it intercepted six missiles, and reports differ on the number of drones. We found no date in the sources for reopening King Khalid Airport.

Saudi Civil Defense announced, in a statement published by the Saudi Press Agency and relayed by Okaz, Saudi Gazette and Financial Juice, that shrapnel fell after two cruise missiles were intercepted over Riyadh, injuring four people and causing limited damage to 15 housing units. We found no details in the sources on the condition of the injured or the exact time of the interception, and the outlets carried the news after midnight Riyadh time.

In Dhahran, an official source at the Ministry of Energy said, according to the Saudi Press Agency, Okaz, Al-Watan and Saudi Gazette, that an administrative building belonging to Aramco was targeted with no injuries. Financial Juice reported from the agency that the attack caused a limited fire, and we found no second confirmation of that. Reuters had quoted sources as saying a loud boom was heard near the Aramco headquarters on Tuesday evening, and a Bloomberg headline said the headquarters was hit, while the Saudi wording is “targeting of an administrative building”. Saudi Gazette reported that the wreckage of a Houthi missile fell in a residential area of Dhahran after it was intercepted, a single source.

The Saudi-led coalition announced the interception of six missiles launched by the Houthis, two ballistic missiles and four cruise missiles, according to Asharq Al-Awsat’s English edition and Okaz. Okaz speaks of two drones destroyed, while Asharq Al-Awsat speaks of six drones, and we do not know whether the difference comes from two different statements. The coalition announced the destruction of a Houthi missile that was headed for Dhahran, and Financial Juice quoted it as saying a missile was intercepted in al-Kharj.

On the diplomatic side, the foreign ministers of Qatar and Kuwait condemned the attacks in calls with Prince Faisal bin Farhan, according to Saudi Gazette and Asharq Al-Awsat, and the foreign ministers of the UAE and Malaysia condemned them, according to Okaz. Trump told CNN, according to its Arabic website, that he spoke to the Crown Prince after the attacks, and we found no Saudi confirmation of that. He also said, according to Reuters, that the Houthis are trying to push up oil prices ahead of the US midterm elections.

On the official front, the Information Minister said life is normal in the kingdom and that raising readiness does not mean concern, according to Okaz. The Defense Minister discussed with Poland’s deputy prime minister, in a phone call, military cooperation and support for the region’s security and stability, according to Saudi Gazette, Okaz and Asharq Al-Awsat. The UN High Commissioner for Human Rights had expressed shock at the attacks on the kingdom’s airports, according to Saudi Gazette and Asharq Al-Awsat, and Asharq Al-Awsat reported that 400 medical staff took part in rescuing the injured after the attack on Riyadh airport.

As for King Khalid Airport, we found no announcement in this period’s reports on when operations will resume, after a strike that killed 12 and wounded 309 according to the General Authority of Civil Aviation, as we published yesterday, and after Reuters reported that the Saudi warning system did not work before the attacks on the airports. In DailyDrop’s reading, the question has changed: it is no longer whether missiles get through, but whether interceptions are enough to protect residential neighborhoods and energy facilities together. Today, Wednesday, we are following the Civil Defense and energy statements, the aviation position on Riyadh, and any new coalition statement.

Yemen · SecurityPress ✓ 5 outlets · the account is the coalition’s

The coalition strikes Houthi fuel facilities, saying they support missile and drone attacks, and al-Alimi: no turning back from ending the coup

Saudi Gazette reported that the coalition targeted fuel facilities the Houthis use to support their missile and drone attacks, and Okaz wrote that the strike cuts the group’s supply artery. Reuters had reported that the coalition ordered an oil facility in Sanaa evacuated before an operation, and a Financial Times headline said it is striking Houthi targets in the Yemeni capital.

Al-Alimi said there is no turning back from “Yemen Dawn” until the Houthi coup is ended, according to Okaz and Asharq Al-Awsat. Al-Watan spoke of shelling of al-Sabaha station and of fighting in the highlands that is tightening the noose on the group, an account from one source that we have not verified on the ground. Reuters reported that shelling of Taiz is intensifying and that prices are rising there.

We found no number of facilities targeted, no casualty toll and no Houthi response in the sources. In DailyDrop’s reading, the coalition links its strikes to cutting fuel for the launches, and the test in the coming days is whether the pace of launches from Yemen falls, and whether the shelling widens beyond Sanaa.

Economy · IMFPress ✓ 4 sources · forecasts attributed to the Fund

IMF raises its global growth forecast for this year and warns of a 2027 slowdown, and Al-Jadaan extends his chairmanship of the Fund’s committee by a year

The International Monetary Fund raised its forecast for global economic growth this year, and said economies will slow in 2027 and the gap between them is widening, according to Asharq Al-Awsat in its Arabic and English editions. Reuters, in its coverage, warned of the risks of a correction in the AI market and of war, debt and rising yields. The Fund expects euro area growth of 1.1% in 2026 and then a rise in 2027, according to Asharq Al-Awsat. The paper also reported that it expects the Saudi economy to grow 7.9% in 2027, a figure from one source for which we found no 2026 forecast alongside it.

At the annual meetings of the Fund and the World Bank, the Fund extended the term of Mohammed Al-Jadaan as chair of the International Monetary and Financial Committee for an additional year, according to the Fund’s statement and Saudi Gazette, and the finance minister led the Saudi delegation. In the oil market, Reuters said Saudi Arabia led the increase in OPEC+ output in September despite the disruptions of the Iran war, a single source. In DailyDrop’s reading, markets are reading the week between two risks: an energy shock and a possible correction in AI stocks, and the Fund puts both at the head of its warnings.

World · VaticanPress ✓ 4 outlets · the diagnosis comes from the Vatican

Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer after surgery to remove part of his lung

Pope Leo XIV entered hospital on Tuesday for surgery over a lung nodule, according to Reuters. The Vatican then announced that doctors removed part of his right lung and that the diagnosis is lung cancer, according to the Associated Press, Politico and Asharq Al-Awsat. Asharq Al-Awsat described the operation as successful. Many international outlets carried the news in similar wording.

We found no disease stage, treatment plan or recovery period in the sources, nor the effect on the Pope’s schedule and appointments. In DailyDrop’s reading, today we stick to what the Vatican announced and refrain from speculating about his condition, and we await the next medical bulletins and the announcement of the coming days’ program.

Ukraine · RussiaPress ✓ 6 outlets · statements attributed to those who made them

Russian missiles on Kyiv kill two with no prior warning, according to Zelensky, and Ukraine advances in EU accession talks after Hungary drops its objection

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said a Russian missile attack on Kyiv on Tuesday night killed two people, and that his country received no warning from its partners about the missiles heading its way, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat. He said the main target was an energy facility, the CHPP-4 heating and power plant, according to Financial Juice. Trump said Washington is still sharing intelligence with Kyiv, according to Reuters and Bloomberg. Reuters had reported earlier on Tuesday that Russia had not struck Ukrainian energy targets for the first time since the energy truce announced by Trump, before the night attack came.

Ukraine took a step in its EU accession talks after Hungary dropped its objection, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat. 12 members of the US Senate announced their rejection of the diesel deal with Russia, according to Asharq Al-Awsat, and Reuters spoke of growing Republican reservations about it, and Russia said its diesel exports could exceed the quantities Trump announced, according to Okaz and Asharq Al-Awsat. Okaz reported that Britain is speeding up delivery of more than five thousand defensive missiles to Ukraine, a single source. In DailyDrop’s reading, the war reaches our reader through diesel and energy, and today’s yardstick is whether Moscow confirms a commitment to ease strikes on energy.
