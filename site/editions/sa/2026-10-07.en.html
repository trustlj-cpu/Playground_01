Yemen’s army says it pushed the Houthis out of the Bab al-Mandab and Mocha areas; the Houthis deny it, and there is no independent confirmation

Government sources told Reuters and AFP that forces reached only the outskirts of Mocha, while the Houthis said they control everything they seized last month. The coalition says it continues to provide “full operational support”.

Yemen’s army said on Tuesday, October 6, that it had pushed the Houthis out of areas around the Bab al-Mandab strait and from the coastal city of Mocha, according to ITV and Al-Monitor. Government sources told Reuters and AFP that forces reached only the outskirts of Mocha, and military sources did not confirm to AFP that the city had been taken, while Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said the Houthis still control everything they seized last month. We have not seen independent confirmation from any party.

According to an analysis published by the Foundation for Defense of Democracies on October 6, the coalition says it continues to provide “full operational support” to Yemeni forces in the operation “Yemen Dawn” announced by Rashad al-Alimi. Al-Monitor, citing Reuters, reported that Saudi Arabia is expected to lead the air campaign while Yemeni forces fight on the ground, and the announcement includes retaking all Houthi areas, among them Sanaa, according to Al Jazeera.

AFP reported hearing another explosion in north Riyadh on Tuesday after explosions on Monday evening, and the Houthis said they targeted Riyadh airport and an Aramco refinery, with no Saudi confirmation of any damage. Fighting is still raging in the highlands of Taiz, and the International Institute for Strategic Studies estimates pro-government forces at about 122,000 troops, but the government’s control over some of its factions is “limited”, according to Al Jazeera.

In DailyDrop’s reading, the announcement still outruns the proof. Today, Wednesday, we are following: independent photos or testimony from Mocha and Dhubab, coalition statements, any Saudi clarification of the explosions in Riyadh, and the fate of the road between Taiz and Aden.

Yemen · BackgroundPress ✓ 4 sources · dates from Security Council reports and analyses

How the war got here: from tanker attacks in July to the government offensive in October

On July 22 the Houthis said they attacked two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea, and on July 26 they attacked two refineries, in Jazan and Yanbu, according to the Security Council’s monthly report. On July 30 Saudi Arabia, Yemen and 12 other countries announced the “Multinational Maritime Defence Coalition”. The Houthis began their campaign against the government on August 6 and reached Bab al-Mandab on September 11, according to the Foundation for Defense of Democracies analysis.

On September 19 the Security Council condemned the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, including the targeting of civilian and energy infrastructure, according to Anadolu, and it noted that at least 125,000 people have been displaced since the start of September, according to the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs. UN envoy Hans Grundberg warned in an August briefing that the risk of a return to wide-scale conflict is greater than at any time since the 2022 truce.

Brent near $102.6 according to one report, and West Texas Intermediate steady at $89.44

A report published on October 5 on the MoneyMagpie website said Brent is trading near $102.60, about 57% higher than a year earlier, and that it fell to about $97.70 days ago, linking this to the continued disruption of the Strait of Hormuz. Gulf exports remain hostage to Hormuz: Ghalibaf repeated on Sunday that the strait will not open before the seven conditions are met, and we have seen no change in Iran’s position since.

Unverified — what we could not confirm

“The Houthis destroyed Khamis Mushait air base.” A Houthi claim relayed by Blue News: they said they hit weapons depots, runways and a hangar in response to more than 300 Saudi air strikes. Saudi Arabia did not comment, and the date of the report is unclear to us, so we do not treat it as confirmed.
“The Houthis attacked a power station in Medina.” An accusation by the coalition: it said a drone hit the Taybah station on September 29 and knocked out one transformer without affecting the wider grid; the Houthis denied it and called it a lie, and there is no independent verification (Gulf News, Al-Ahram, Al-Monitor).
“Government forces took control of the city of Mocha.” A claim by Yemeni state media. Government sources told Reuters and AFP that forces reached only the outskirts of the city, and military sources did not confirm that it had been taken.
