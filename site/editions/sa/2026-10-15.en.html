Saudi Arabia · SecurityPress ✓ 12 outlets · attacks attributed to the Houthis by Saudi and coalition sources, and what the group says is attributed to it

Houthis vow to keep targeting airports and oil facilities; Reuters: extensive damage at Aramco’s Dhahran headquarters, and shrapnel from a ballistic missile hits a resident in Riyadh

The Ministry of Energy described what happened in Dhahran as a limited fire in an administrative building with no injuries, while Reuters quoted three sources as saying the fire spread across several floors. The coalition announced early Thursday that it intercepted 14 missiles and drones.

On the evening of Tuesday, October 13, an administrative building belonging to Aramco in Dhahran, in the Eastern Province, was targeted, causing a limited fire with no injuries, according to an official source at the Ministry of Energy who said the competent authorities had begun dealing with the incident, as relayed by the Saudi Press Agency, Okaz, Al-Watan and Saudi Gazette. Reuters had cited sources as saying a loud blast was heard in Dhahran near the headquarters. Bloomberg, citing people familiar, said a Houthi missile hit the headquarters and a fire broke out in part of the compound. The next day Reuters quoted three sources as saying the headquarters suffered extensive damage and the fire spread across several floors, and Financial Juice reported, citing two sources, that the company asked its employees to work from home while operations continue normally. We found no official statement specifying the extent of the damage, and we do not know whether the administrative building the ministry mentioned is the part the sources described.

In Riyadh, Civil Defense announced that shrapnel from two cruise missiles intercepted by air defenses on Tuesday hit four people, two women, a man and a child, and caused limited damage to 15 homes, according to the Saudi Press Agency, Okaz and Saudi Gazette. Residents of the capital then heard new explosions on Wednesday, according to the Associated Press, and the coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile that the Houthis launched toward Riyadh, according to Financial Juice. Civil Defense later announced that an Arab resident was hurt by shrapnel from an intercepted ballistic missile, which damaged a house and a car, according to Saudi Gazette, and the BBC carried the same report.

The Houthi military spokesman said their forces targeted Aramco in Dhahran, King Khalid Airport and an Aramco refinery in Riyadh, and that their operations against airports and oil facilities will continue in the coming days, according to Financial Juice and Reuters. We found no Saudi confirmation that the refinery or the airport was hit. The coalition announced early Thursday, according to Asharq Al-Awsat, the interception and destruction of 14 missiles and drones, including ballistic and winged missiles aimed at Riyadh, al-Kharj and the Eastern Province, then the interception of a drone in the Eastern Province, according to Okaz and Financial Juice. The coalition had announced on Tuesday night the destruction of two ballistic missiles, four cruise missiles and six drones, according to Asharq Al-Awsat, and we do not know whether Thursday’s figure includes what it announced on Tuesday night.

What was confirmed by an official Saudi announcement: an administrative building in Dhahran was targeted and a limited fire broke out, four people were injured in Riyadh by shrapnel from two cruise missiles, and an Arab resident was injured by shrapnel from a ballistic missile. What was not officially confirmed: the extent of the damage at the headquarters, the Houthi claims about the refinery and the airport, and the overall tally of interceptions.

Readings differ on the performance of the defenses: the Financial Times said the kingdom is one of the world’s largest arms buyers but is struggling to keep pace with the Houthis’ methods, and CNN Arabic presented an analysis of why the targeting succeeded despite tens of billions spent on defense each year. In DailyDrop’s reading, the threat has moved from a single documented strike to a graduated campaign that hits airports and energy together, and its impact is hard to measure while damage figures are missing. Today we are following: a statement on the damage in Dhahran, the status of King Khalid Airport, and whether the Houthis’ vow is followed by new attacks.

Economy · EnergyPress ✓ 5 outlets · Brent price from one source

Georgieva: security developments in Saudi Arabia could hurt everyone everywhere; Brent ends Wednesday’s session at $106.06, and Washington considers easing “shadow fleet” sanctions

International Monetary Fund Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said at a briefing on the sidelines of the annual meetings in Bangkok on Wednesday that developments in Saudi Arabia could have “negative repercussions for everyone everywhere,” according to Saudi Gazette, and Okaz added that she tied this to rising energy costs and growing geopolitical risks. She also called on the United States and rich countries to act urgently to rein in debt, according to Asharq Al-Awsat. At the same meetings the Fund extended the term of Saudi Finance Minister Mohammed Al-Jadaan as chair of the International Monetary and Financial Committee, the Fund’s ministerial advisory body, for another year, until January 2028, according to Saudi Gazette.

In the market, Brent crude ended Wednesday’s session at $106.06 a barrel, up 37 cents, according to Financial Juice, and Reuters had said traders were weighing Saudi supply risks against a flow of Russian diesel. Prices then fell early Thursday as investors assessed the supply outlook and Middle East tensions, according to Reuters. The International Energy Agency said a diesel shortage could last into 2027, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat, and that global oil demand will fall by 2.6 million barrels a day in 2026 because of the disruption from the war with Iran and higher prices, according to Financial Juice.

Reuters quoted a US official as saying Washington is looking at easing some sanctions on “shadow fleet” ships to lower freight rates and increase the flow of oil in the Middle East, which Financial Juice also reported. The ships and sanctions covered were not clear. In DailyDrop’s reading, this means shipping cost has become a problem Washington is addressing by loosening a restriction on sanctioned tankers, not by raising output.

Iran · ShippingPress ✓ 6 outlets · accounts of the sinking conflict

A container ship sinks near Hormuz; Tehran says it hit a mine, while Reuters sources say it was hit by a projectile

The naval force of the Revolutionary Guards announced, in a statement carried by state media, that a container ship sank south of the Strait of Hormuz after hitting a naval mine, according to Asharq Al-Awsat and Financial Juice. Sources told Reuters it sank after being hit by a projectile, and Singapore’s Maritime and Port Authority said a strike started a fire and the crew was evacuated, according to Bloomberg. Al Jazeera Net named it “GFS Pride,” while Asharq Al-Awsat cited International Maritime Organization data on the sinking of a ship called “Cape Dow” after being hit by a projectile last month, and we cannot say for certain that they are the same ship.

The Guardian spoke of the most intense escalation in Iranian attacks on shipping since the start of the war, and of a second cargo ship said to be sinking, and Reuters quoted officials as saying Tehran changed its tactics to step up targeting of ships before the US election. Financial Juice said Oman ended its talks with Iran on Hormuz and that completing the file depends on the US–Iranian talks, and that US Central Command said its forces helped Gulf partners ship more than 19 million barrels through the strait on the day before its Tuesday statement. Financial Juice quoted Iranian parliament speaker Qalibaf as saying his country does not seek an endless war or prolonged negotiations, and that an agreement with Washington does not prevent a new attack on it. Netanyahu said he does not seek to attack Iran again before the Israeli elections, according to Reuters and Asharq Al-Awsat.

Russia · UkrainePress ✓ 5 outlets · the assets report is the Financial Times’s and is relayed by the agencies

Washington proposes lifting the freeze on some Russian assets to revive talks, according to the Financial Times, Europeans refuse, and Zelensky: we received no warning before the missiles hit Kyiv

Reuters, Financial Juice and Asharq Al-Awsat relayed from the Financial Times that the United States proposes lifting the freeze on some Russian assets to relaunch talks to end the war in Ukraine, and Financial Juice, citing the paper, said European officials reject the plan. We found no official Russian or US comment on the report.

Ukrainian President Zelensky said Russia struck Kyiv’s energy facilities with ballistic missiles without any warning from partners, and Reuters reported two people killed in an attack on the capital. Asharq Al-Awsat and Okaz said Moscow is ignoring the “energy truce” and continuing the shelling. Zelensky said it is “painful” that Trump chooses to stand on the other side, according to Reuters. In a separate file, an aide to the Ukrainian president said SpaceX restricted Starlink operations in Ukraine, disrupting military missions, according to Reuters, and Polish Foreign Minister Sikorski asked Elon Musk whether he is practicing politics through the service. On the diesel file that links Washington and Moscow, Italian Prime Minister Meloni called the deal a “very risky choice,” according to Reuters, and 12 US senators denounced it, according to Asharq Al-Awsat. Reuters said Kyiv announces it has liberated more territory in the Donetsk region, and Russian Deputy Prime Minister Novak said two Russian refineries were attacked on Wednesday, according to Financial Juice, and Okaz said Britain is speeding up delivery of more than 5,000 defensive missiles to Ukraine.

Markets · United StatesPress ✓ 6 outlets · index closing figures unconfirmed

US inflation of 3.7% in September lifts bets on a rate hike and hits chip stocks from New York to Asia, and Federal Reserve officials: no rush

The US consumer price index rose 3.7% year on year in September, against 3.4% in August and expectations of 3.6%, and 0.6% month on month, according to data relayed by Financial Juice and Okaz, and Reuters said gasoline pushed prices to accelerate and that core inflation is stronger. Core inflation came in at 2.6% a year against expectations of 2.5%, according to Financial Juice. Treasury bonds fell and the dollar rose to its highest level since May 2025, according to Bloomberg, stocks fell led by chips, and Bank of America shares dropped after a $1.21 billion fine. We did not confirm index closing figures.

Asian markets sold AI stocks early Thursday, according to Reuters and the Financial Times, and Taiwanese shares fell more than 2%, according to Financial Juice. The dollar then retreated from an 18-month high as Federal Reserve officials played down rate-hike bets, according to Reuters: Daly said a rate hike is likely but not urgent, Goolsbee called the figures “discouraging,” and Collins had said earlier that a new hike is on the table before the end of the year. A senior White House adviser, Hassett, said the headline figure is “disappointing” and that the rise in core inflation this month is “incidental,” according to Financial Juice, and the banking group Citi expects a hike in December, according to the same site. Gold fell more than 1.7% to about $4,086 an ounce, its lowest since October 7, according to Al-Watan. In DailyDrop’s reading, the riyal is pegged to the dollar, so the path of US interest rates matters for borrowing costs in the kingdom.
