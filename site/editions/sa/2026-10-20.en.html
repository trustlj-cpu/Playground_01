Prince Khalid bin Salman in Abu Dhabi: two meetings with the UAE president and his deputy amid regional escalation

The Saudi defense minister visited Abu Dhabi on Sunday, October 18, and the Saudi foreign minister received two calls on Monday from his Emirati and Turkish counterparts.

Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman arrived in Abu Dhabi on Sunday, October 18, and was received by UAE President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed, according to Asharq Al-Awsat and Okaz. Okaz said the meeting covered the partnership between the two countries and regional developments, while Emirati and Gulf media, including Gulf News, reported that Vice President of the UAE Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed and National Security Adviser Sheikh Tahnoun bin Zayed attended the meeting, and that Prince Khalid conveyed the greetings of King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. In a separate meeting Prince Khalid reviewed the fraternal relations between the two countries with Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed, according to Okaz. The reports said the talks covered support for security and stability and joint Gulf action, and the official statements did not mention specific decisions or agreements.

The next day, Monday, October 19, Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan received two phone calls from the foreign ministers of the UAE and Turkey to discuss de-escalation in the region, according to Saudi Gazette, and Al Arabiya reported that the Saudi and Emirati foreign ministers discussed regional security by phone. Faisal bin Farhan had discussed on Sunday in Manama with his Russian counterpart Sergey Lavrov the escalation in Yemen and threats to navigation, on the sidelines of the Ministerial Council meeting of the Gulf Cooperation Council, according to Asharq Al-Awsat and Saudi Gazette. A Houthi projectile had fallen in Najran on Sunday, and local authorities in Yemen’s Shabwa announced on Monday the toll of a missile attack on a recruitment camp.

Houthi missile attack on a camp in Shabwa: 53 killed and 200 wounded, according to the local authority

The Houthi group fired ballistic missiles on Monday, October 19, at al-Alam camp in Jardan District north of Ataq, the capital of Shabwa governorate, where new recruits were preparing for a training session, according to Reuters and Saudi Gazette. In its first reports Reuters, citing government sources, said at least 18 people were killed and more than 30 wounded, and the local authority in Shabwa later announced that the toll had reached 53 dead and about 200 wounded, calling it preliminary and liable to rise, according to Saudi Gazette and PBS citing the Associated Press. The group acknowledged targeting the camp. Rashad al-Alimi, chairman of Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council, said that targeting recruits reflects the militia’s resort to terrorist attacks when it is under pressure, and pledged a firm response, according to Asharq Al-Awsat. Al-Alimi asked the United Nations to support international action to designate the Houthis a terrorist organization, and Okaz reported that the Giants Brigades foiled a Houthi attack in “Kahboub.” The local authority in Marib said, according to Okaz, that the governorate was hit by a Houthi attack with four ballistic missiles, and al-Alimi affirmed that the “Yemen Dawn” operation is moving ahead toward its goals.

Future Investment Initiative in Riyadh postponed to 2027, a week before its scheduled date

The Future Investment Initiative Institute (FII) announced on Monday, October 19, the postponement of its tenth edition, “The Power of Legacy,” from its scheduled dates in Riyadh between October 26 and 29 to 2027, according to Saudi Gazette, Arab News and Arabian Business. The institute’s chief executive, Princess Maha bint Mishari Al Saud, said the decision was taken to ensure that the historic edition meets the expectations of the community of investors, business leaders and international policymakers. The institute added that existing registrations and partnerships remain valid, and it did not announce new dates.

The National and Gulf Business linked the postponement to escalating Houthi attacks on the kingdom, including strikes that targeted King Khalid International Airport, and cited sources as saying they raised security concerns among international executives and investors. Saudi authorities did not publicly confirm this reason, according to the same reports.

Macron in Abu Dhabi, among the first stops of a Gulf tour to secure fuel and freedom of navigation in Hormuz

French President Emmanuel Macron arrived in Abu Dhabi on Monday, October 19, on the second stop of a Gulf tour that began in Kuwait, includes Bahrain and Qatar, and runs until October 21, and was received by Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed, according to The National, Gulf News and Asharq Al-Awsat. Gulf News said the two presidents discussed the strategic partnership and expanding it in the economy, investment, energy, advanced technology and artificial intelligence. Reports said the Élysée Palace wants a return to stability and freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, that it will seek commitments for additional cargoes of diesel and gasoline, and that the chief executives of TotalEnergies and Engie are accompanying Macron.

Macron had said on Sunday, according to Okaz, that there is no justification for violence after the riots in France and that he would visit the Gulf to secure energy, and he affirmed in a televised address that he would travel as planned. Reuters and the Associated Press reported that he rejected the resignation of Interior Minister Nuñez, while Asharq Al-Awsat quoted Macron as saying the French feel anger and shame.

Lavrov in Tehran: Moscow ready to mediate between Washington and Iran

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov arrived in Tehran on Monday, October 19, on a working visit, and met Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and then President Masoud Pezeshkian, according to Anadolu, Press TV and Okaz. Lavrov said his country is ready to help find a solution to the crisis and to mediate between the United States and Iran, and described the American and Israeli strikes on Iran as unjustified aggression, according to what the Financial Jos agency relayed of his remarks. The agency, citing IRNA, reported that Lavrov briefed Araghchi on the results of his negotiations with Arab countries, and that Moscow is committed to the investment and nuclear agreements with Tehran.

Pezeshkian told Lavrov that Tehran will not accept a state imposing its will by force and that it is open to resolving regional differences through dialogue, according to Press TV. Russia’s Foreign Ministry said the talks cover the situation in the Gulf, ways of settling the acute international crisis, and energy, transport and economic projects. Lavrov had discussed with Prince Faisal bin Farhan on Sunday the escalation in Yemen and threats to navigation, according to Asharq Al-Awsat.
