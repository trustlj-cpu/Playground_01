Twelve dead and 309 wounded in a strike on King Khalid Airport in Riyadh; the coalition calls it a “war crime” and vows a decisive response

The General Authority of Civil Aviation said Saturday’s strike killed 12 people and wounded 309, some of them critically, and all flights were diverted away from the capital. It is the third attack on the airport this week.

The Saudi General Authority of Civil Aviation announced that a strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday, October 10 killed 12 people and wounded 309, some of them critically, according to CNN, CBS, CNBC, Arab News and The Washington Post. It said all flights were diverted away from the capital and operations at the airport were temporarily suspended. This is the third attack on the airport this week.

According to the Authority, the dead are four Saudis and two Bangladeshis, and one each from the United States, Jordan, Palestine, Syria, Sudan and Egypt. The Authority’s statement did not name the party responsible, while the coalition said the Houthis carried it out, and the head of the “Houthi Media Office” said on the X platform that the group “closed” Riyadh International Airport, according to reports, whereas CBS said the group had not announced formal responsibility.

The spokesman for the Saudi-led coalition, Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki, called the attack a “fully fledged war crime” and said the coalition would respond “decisively” in line with “international humanitarian law”. The United States, Britain, Spain and Canada warned their citizens against using the airport, according to CNN. As of dawn on Sunday we had found no independent confirmation of new coalition air strikes.

In DailyDrop’s reading, this is the largest toll of civilian casualties in this escalation that we have found, and it may change calculations in Riyadh and Washington. Today, Sunday, we are following: the coalition’s response, the Saudi market’s opening on the news, the state of the airport and flights, and the US decision.

Trump: “Maybe we’ll join” the Saudi strikes on the Houthis, and plans to call the Crown Prince

President Trump said, in remarks relayed by NPR, Al Jazeera and CBS, that the United States is considering joining the Saudi strikes on the Houthis: “Maybe we will. We’ll look at it,” and that he would make a decision “quickly”. Al Jazeera said he plans to call “the Crown Prince”, Prince Mohammed bin Salman, and that his remarks came before the Saudi authorities confirmed the toll.

The US State Department condemned “the Houthis’ terrorist attacks on civilian infrastructure and airports”, and Marco Rubio said the strike cost innocent lives, among them an American. Reports quoting officials said US help included sharing intelligence, identifying targets and planning the latest attack. Trump has not yet made a decision.

All flights diverted away from Riyadh and airport operations suspended, and Lufthansa extends its suspension to October 16

The General Authority of Civil Aviation said all flights were diverted away from Riyadh on Saturday and airport operations were temporarily suspended, according to CBS and CNN. The airport had been reopened on Friday after about 45% of departing flights were cancelled on Thursday, according to Cirium via Gulf Times, and Lufthansa had announced the suspension of its flights to Riyadh until October 16.

As of dawn on Sunday we had found no date for resuming operations. In DailyDrop’s reading, how quickly services return will be decided by the foreign carriers’ decisions more than by the Saudi authority. If you have a flight through Riyadh, we advise checking its status with the carrier.

Saudi market opens Sunday after a week of attacks and a sixth straight weekly loss, with the last close at 10,376

TASI’s last close, on Thursday, October 8, was 10,376 points after a fall of 1.57%, the biggest daily drop since March, ending a sixth consecutive weekly loss, according to Reuters via Business Recorder and Trading Economics. The market did not trade on Friday and Saturday, so its response to Saturday’s attack has not yet appeared.

In the US, the S&P 500 closed on Friday, October 9, at 7,811.54 points, up 0.59%; gold rose 1.43% to $4,216.3 per ounce, and West Texas Intermediate rose 0.39% to $91.85, according to closing data. We do not predict the direction of Sunday’s open, and we are following it.

A week of attacks on airports: from Abha on Tuesday to Riyadh on Saturday, in five stages

Monday, October 5: the Houthis said they targeted Riyadh airport and an Aramco refinery, with no Saudi confirmation. Tuesday the 6th and Wednesday the 7th: the Abha and Riyadh airports were targeted, and the General Authority of Civil Aviation said the two attacks left three dead and 36 wounded, and the coalition destroyed 82 targets. Thursday the 8th: a strike on King Khalid Airport, and on Friday the Authority announced that three Saudis, including a pilot, were killed, and about 45% of departing flights were cancelled.

Friday the 9th: the airport was reopened and the coalition destroyed three launch platforms. Saturday the 10th: a strike killed 12 people and wounded 309, the coalition vowed a decisive response, and Trump said he is considering joining. This is a sequence compiled from several reports, with dates as they relayed them, and some figures may change with updates.
