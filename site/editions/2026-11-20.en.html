First-ever U.S.-China-Russia summit adopts peace declaration that “nuclear war cannot be won”… U.S. account still pending

The talks held in Shenzhen, China, on the 19th ended after about 1 hour 30 minutes, and China and Russia said they reached agreement on dialogue to reduce nuclear risks and on UN reform.

The leaders of the United States, China and Russia met together for the first time on the 19th in Shenzhen, China. In the city hosting the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin sat around a large round table, and Xi was described as seated effectively in the middle. The talks ended after about 1 hour 30 minutes. Russia’s Foreign Ministry said a peace declaration by the three leaders was adopted and released the full text. The declaration contains wording that nuclear war cannot be won and a pledge to open dialogue to reduce nuclear risks and on nuclear non-proliferation. China and Russia said they also agreed with the United States on UN reform and on expanding fuel supplies, and there were reports that it includes a passage saying bodies such as the International Criminal Court must not abuse their jurisdiction. Xi was reported to have said after the talks that they were “positive and constructive,” and said during the talks that “when the great powers are in harmony the world is at peace, and when they quarrel it falls into chaos.”

On the U.S. side, Trump said on the 19th (local time) that artificial intelligence (AI) safety was discussed with Xi in a limited way, and added an understanding of the position of President Putin, who was angry over Ukrainian forces’ attack on the Crimean Bridge. Putin said there had been no global conflict for 80 years thanks to the Yalta agreement. The Chinese side put forward the message that big countries should join forces to keep the world stable. Cheong Wa Dae issued a position before the talks that it would not be “a second Yalta meeting,” and European countries pushed back, saying “we are not Putin’s friends.” Assessments of the talks range from a declaratory meeting without a breakthrough to an analysis that nothing was decided unilaterally. It is not yet known whether the declaration contains concrete commitments to carry it out or a plan to end the war in Ukraine, or whether the U.S. government will confirm the Chinese and Russian accounts as they stand.

Anthropic prices at $83 a share in largest listing ever… report says shares rose 38% intraday on day one

AI company Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq on the 19th (local time). The offer price had been set beforehand at $83 a share, which puts the company’s value at about $1.5 trillion. The money raised in the initial public offering (IPO) was reported to have surpassed the previous record held by SpaceX. However, the exact amount raised differs by tally, from $94 billion to $107 billion. Accounts of the first day also differ by report. One report said the shares rose as much as 38% intraday, while another said they spiked above the offer price and then pulled back. Anthropic’s chief executive was reported to have said on listing day, “we will pace AI development,” and “safety is in the company’s DNA.” There is also a report that the chief financial officer (CFO) said 75% of the company’s computing resources go to research and development and that the IPO will be used to increase investment. In the Korean market, a view emerged that the listing pulled money to one side and kept worries about supply and demand going, and the question of interest was whether the unease would ease after the listing. The first-day closing price has not been confirmed, and the Korean market’s reaction will only be known once trading opens on the 20th.

22 smartphones caught at CSAT venues by emergency disaster alert… Vice Minister of Education: “no delay to the exam”

The 2027 academic year College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) was held nationwide on the 19th. About 550,000 candidates sat it. At a briefing that day, the Vice Minister of Education said 22 smartphones were caught ringing after an emergency disaster alert was sent. The candidates caught numbered 22, and the exam was not delayed. There is a report that the alert was sent one more time without warning, and those caught are reported to have their scores voided. This year the government mounted an all-out response, as concern was high about cheating with AI and advanced electronic devices.

Regional tallies also came out. In Daegu and North Gyeongsang, 19 cheating cases were caught, and possession of electronic devices was reported to be the most common. This figure uses a different criterion from the 22 nationwide, so the two cannot be added or compared. The exam committee explained that Korean history was set at an easy level, and analysis from EBS and the college-admissions industry said the Korean language section was easier than last year. Items singled out as hard were Korean item 17, on legal liability for defamation, and math item 21, on cubic functions. An overall nationwide tally of cheating has not been confirmed.

Politics · Judiciary✓ 4 outlets · timing of impeachment motion undecided

Supreme Court sends part of Han Duck-soo case back… Democratic Party decides to pursue impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae, People Power Party objects

On the 19th, the Supreme Court partly quashed and remanded the lower court ruling in the case of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo on charges of key insurrection duties. Han was indicted on that charge and had earlier been sentenced to 15 years in prison. According to one report, the Supreme Court treated two counts of taking part in the insurrection as guilty and found only the “proposal at the martial law Cabinet meeting” not guilty, and extended the detention period by 2 months. The Democratic Party held an emergency general meeting of lawmakers the same day and, after heated debate, decided to pursue the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-dae. The Democratic Party’s Kim Min-seok called the Han Duck-soo ruling “historic treason” and said the procedure and content of impeachment are delegated to the party leadership.

The People Power Party said “the reality of the insurrection witch-hunt has come out” and claimed “a fabricated indictment,” and criticized the Democratic Party’s push for impeachment as a “parliamentary coup.” Impeachment is a procedure in which the National Assembly brings the charge and the Constitutional Court decides. Within the Democratic Party, hardliners urged impeachment, and the Democratic Party side went as far as describing the ruling as a declaration siding with the insurrection. Because the lower court will hear the Han case again, the final conclusion has not yet come, and this decision does not mean acquittal. When the motion will be introduced and put to a vote has not yet been set.

International · International law✓ 2 outlets · enforcement steps and Myanmar’s reaction unconfirmed

International Court of Justice rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the judicial organ of the United Nations, ruled on the 19th that Myanmar committed mass killing, that is, genocide, against the Rohingya, a Muslim ethnic minority. The case began when Gambia sued Myanmar in 2019 for violating the Genocide Convention. In January 2020 the court issued provisional measures ordering protection of the Rohingya. Genocide means killing and similar acts carried out with intent to destroy a particular ethnic or religious group, and the convention obliges member states to prevent and punish it. The proceedings were held in The Hague, the Netherlands.

The ICJ is not a court that punishes individuals but one that handles disputes between states, so this ruling concerns whether the state of Myanmar breached the convention. ICJ rulings are final and cannot be appealed, but the means of enforcing them are limited. Whether the ruling contains concrete enforcement steps such as an order for reparations, and how Myanmar’s authorities reacted, have not been confirmed.
