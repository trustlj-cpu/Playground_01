Trump: “Could meet Kim Jong-un in China… up to Xi Jinping”… North Korean, Chinese reactions unconfirmed

U.S. President Donald Trump, setting off on an Asia tour on the 16th (local time), said a meeting with North Korea’s State Affairs Commission Chairman Kim Jong-un could happen on the occasion of APEC in Shenzhen, China.

U.S. President Donald Trump, speaking to reporters on the 16th (local time) before leaving on an Asia tour, said a meeting with Kim Jong-un, chairman of North Korea’s State Affairs Commission, was possible. Asked where, Trump replied “in China,” and said whether Chairman Kim could come to China was “up to President Xi Jinping.” The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit is held in Shenzhen, China, on the 18th and 19th, and Trump is due to hold talks with Xi on that occasion. The tour schedule also includes attending the ASEAN summit in Manila, the Philippines, on the 17th. However, this remark does not mean a meeting has been arranged. The remark gave no date, venue or North Korean intent, and no official reaction from North Korea or China has been confirmed.

Trump is the only sitting U.S. president to have met Chairman Kim in person. Their last meeting was in 2019, and summit-level negotiations have not continued since. At last year’s APEC in Gyeongju as well, Trump expressed willingness to meet, but there was no response from the North. Chairman Kim stood alongside Xi and Russian President Putin at the Beijing military parade last September, so a trip to China is not unprecedented in itself. The variable is the U.S.-China-Russia trilateral summit being mentioned for the 19th. There is no official confirmation from the Chinese side, and Russia’s presidential office said Putin will visit Tajikistan on the 19th and 20th, so it is not known how the schedules will fit together.

Speaker Cho Jeong-sik formally launches push for constitutional amendment… People Power Party: “a ploy to change the subject, no amendment under this government”

National Assembly Speaker Cho Jeong-sik held a press conference on the morning of the 16th and made the Assembly-led push for a constitutional amendment official. The Speaker launched a Constitutional Revision Advisory Committee directly under the Speaker and appointed former Speakers Kim Hyung-o and Chung Sye-kyun as co-chairs. Three principles were presented for the amendment: Assembly leadership, exclusion of a consecutive term for the sitting president, and agreement between the ruling and opposition parties and public consent. Next year was named the right time to push. The Speaker also said the question of a presidential consecutive term is considered closed. The proposal the advisory committee produces is to go on to a Special Committee on Constitutional Amendment that the ruling and opposition parties will form later. The People Power Party pushed back immediately. Its position is that Cho’s case for amendment is a ploy to change the subject and that there will be no amendment under this government. The Democratic Party welcomed it as the first step in Assembly-led discussion of amendment. An amendment is passed only with approval from at least two-thirds of the sitting members of the National Assembly, that is, 200 or more of the 300, and is finalized only after a national referendum. Some analysts say that, given the seat balance, passage is difficult if the People Power Party opposes. The content of the amendment and the timing of a vote have not been set.

Government task force activated over tax accountant exam AI cheating scandal… Prime Minister: “cancellation and eligibility suspension swiftly”

A day after the 15th, when the top passer admitted to AI cheating and surrendered, the government sped up follow-up measures on the 16th. The Prime Minister said cancellation of the exam and suspension of exam eligibility would be carried out swiftly. The government has begun the procedure to cancel that passer’s exam, and the suspension of eligibility is reported to run for 5 years. Reports differ on whether the suspension covers only the tax accountant exam or is widened to national qualification exams generally.

The Ministry of Finance and Economy formed a task force, the ‘Tax Accountant Exam Cheating Response Task Force,’ to discuss measures to prevent recurrence. After Cheong Wa Dae ordered on the 15th a full inspection of suspected AI-assisted cheating across all national qualification exams, how to change supervision at exam sites is the next task. The Korean Association of Tax Accountants said it would strengthen verification of probationary tax accountants. The 2027 academic year CSAT is two days away, on the 19th, so how supervision responds to AI is also drawing attention. Whether there are fraudulent passers besides this passer has not been confirmed.

International · Middle East✓ 5 outlets · reason for dismissal and Houthis’ current front line unconfirmed

Saudi Arabia dismisses commander of Yemen coalition forces fighting Houthis… Yemeni forces withdraw from Red Sea chokepoint

On the 16th, Saudi Arabia replaced the commander of the coalition forces involved in Yemen’s civil war. The Saudi state news agency reported that, under a royal decree, the commander, a lieutenant general, moves to become an adviser to the defense minister, and the armed forces chief of staff takes on the role of coalition commander while keeping that post. Whether the reason for the dismissal was officially stated has not been confirmed.

The replacement came as the pro-Iran rebels, the Houthis, expand their reach. The Yemeni government said coalition-side forces are pulling back from the area around Bab el-Mandeb, at the entrance to the Red Sea, and concentrating on defending Aden. Yemeni military sources said the Houthis had reached a coastal area about 100 km west of Aden, but this was not independently confirmed. The Houthis claimed on the 15th that they targeted an oil pipeline and an airport near Riyadh, but Saudi Arabia did not confirm it. It is not yet known how the balance of the war will change.

International · Chips and politics✓ 4 outlets · exact size and timing of holdings unconfirmed

President Trump holds up to $62 million in Nvidia stock… financial disclosure analysis reported

According to a U.S. media analysis of financial disclosure reports, President Donald Trump is estimated to have held between $13 million and $62 million in Nvidia stock as of the end of August. Because the filing lists amounts only in ranges, the exact size cannot be known. The maximum of $62 million was reported as about 83 billion won, but one outlet put it at 62 billion won, so the conversions differ. The report also pointed to the president’s approval, late last year, of exports of Nvidia’s high-performance H200 chip to China. The White House position has not been confirmed.

The report came amid turmoil over Nvidia’s management shake-up. On November 12, Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang would step down immediately as president, CEO and director, and Colette Kress, the chief financial officer (CFO), will serve as interim CEO. Allegations reported as the background to the resignation have not been announced as established fact, and former CEO Huang was reported to have denied them, saying there had never been a threat to national security. While online petitions demanding a return continue, reports that a return is being discussed conflict with an anonymous source’s statement that the board is not considering it.
