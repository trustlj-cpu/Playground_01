22 candidates caught not handing in smartphones by emergency disaster alert on CSAT day… no delay to the exam

Vice Minister of Education: “No advanced devices other than smartphones were used”… CSAT scores of those caught reportedly to be invalidated

The 2027 College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) was held on the 19th, and 22 candidates who had not handed in their smartphones were caught by an emergency disaster alert that sounded on mobile phones nationwide before the exam began. The Vice Minister of Education said that, of the 22 caught that day, none had used an advanced device other than a smartphone. It was reported that sending the alert did not delay the running of the CSAT. The scores of the candidates caught were reported to be invalidated. The emergency disaster alert sounds on any phone that is switched on, regardless of any refusal to receive it, so it was used to pick out devices smuggled into exam rooms. The government had announced in advance a plan to collect electronic devices switched off before the exam and to send the alert between 8:25 and 8:30 a.m., after entry to the exam rooms closed.

The catch ties into worries that ran up to the day before. President Lee said that extending the exam time would be considered if the alert disturbed the exam, and reports said that ways of turning off the emergency disaster alert were spreading online. Authorities have not confirmed whether those ways actually work. On the exam itself, the college admissions industry was reported to see the Korean language section as easier than last year’s CSAT, and a tally showed that the first-period absence rate in the Ulsan area was 7.75%, slightly higher than last year. No situation requiring a time extension has been reported this time, and the details of the penalties for those caught and the appeal procedure are not yet known.

Anthropic’s offering price set at $83… $94 billion raise expected to surpass SpaceX record

Anthropic has set its offering price at $83 per share ahead of its Nasdaq listing. That is above the range of $70–78 first indicated. Korean market reports said the amount raised would exceed $94 billion and make it the largest initial public offering (IPO) ever, surpassing SpaceX’s previous record, but how that amount is made up, including whether it covers privately allocated shares, has not been confirmed. The offering price is the first price at which new shares are sold to investors, and trading is due to begin on the night of the 19th, Korean time. In Korean and overseas markets, supply-demand concerns have been raised ahead of the listing, and the question is whether they ease after it. On the 18th in New York, the S&P 500 fell for 5 straight sessions while the Nasdaq rose. One report said about $3 billion was raised in an offering to Japanese investors and that it was at the low end of the target, but that is a single outlet’s report. The first-day share price will only be known once trading starts tonight.

Industry · Semiconductors✓ 4 outlets · the schedule for choosing a successor CEO and former CEO Huang’s future are unconfirmed

Nvidia without Jensen Huang tops $100 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time… board says it has “no plans to reappoint” after Huang voices wish to return

In its first quarterly results since Jensen Huang stepped down, Nvidia posted revenue of $115.8 billion, topping $100 billion in a quarter for the first time. The interim chief executive officer (CEO) was reported to have said the business is not being disrupted. The next-quarter forecast (guidance) was reported as around $130 billion. On the 18th, the first trading day after the results, the S&P 500 fell for 5 straight sessions in New York while the Nasdaq rose. No single cause of the decline has been confirmed.

The question of a return is unsettled. When former CEO Huang said a return was wanted, the board drew a line, saying it has no plans to reappoint and is looking for a successor. Speaking at an all-hands meeting of a company with about 47,300 employees, the interim CEO was reported to have said in effect that the passion for innovation will not change and to have hinted at pay raises. A survey saying 56% of employees want Huang back was also reported, but who conducted it and how are unconfirmed. The schedule for choosing a successor is not known.

On day of US-China-Russia trilateral summit, Trump says closer China-Russia ties are the backdrop… Cheong Wa Dae: “Doesn’t seem likely to become a second Yalta Conference”

A trilateral summit bringing the leaders of the US, China and Russia together for the first time is to be held on the 19th in Shenzhen, China, on the sidelines of the APEC summit. US President Trump cited the closer ties between China and Russia, once adversaries, as the backdrop to the talks. The three leaders exchanged their first greetings at a welcome event the day before. Cheong Wa Dae was reported to have said that this meeting will not become a “second Yalta Conference,” like the Yalta Conference of February 1945 at which leaders of great powers discussed the postwar order.

In Japan, concern was voiced that Japan could be sidelined in great-power diplomacy, and Takaichi was reported to be seeking a phone call with President Trump right after the summit. There is an outlook that nuclear arms reduction will be raised, but it is not a confirmed agenda. What was discussed and agreed at the summit has not yet been reported.

Missing-person report filed for ‘cheating suspicion’ LEET perfect scorer… police reportedly tracing movements through Gwangjin, Songpa and Gangdong

A missing-person report was reported to have been filed for a person known as a perfect scorer on the Legal Education Eligibility Test (LEET), and police were reported to have begun trying to locate the person. Police were reported to have searched the Gwangjin District area of Seoul and to be tracing movements leading on to Songpa District and Gangdong District. A suspicion of cheating had been raised against this person, and there were reports that lawyers moved to file a criminal complaint. The person was reported to have denied cheating and to have live-streamed solving the CSAT Korean language questions.

Police were also reported to have moved to obtain the CSAT exam papers the person took last year, and the Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education was reported to have said that the answer sheet in question had not been discarded. Whether the suspicion is true and the current whereabouts are unconfirmed, and will only be known once the results of the police search come out.
