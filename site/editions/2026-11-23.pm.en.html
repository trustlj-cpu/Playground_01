KOSPI plunges 3.31% to close at 4,708.54… sell sidecar two days running, government readies stabilization fund for first time in 18 years

On the 23rd the KOSPI plunged 3.31% to close at 4,708.54, and a sell sidecar was triggered intraday for the second day running. The government began preparing on the 22nd to activate the stabilization fund, but whether it will actually be deployed, and its size, have not been decided.

The KOSPI closed at 4,708.54 on the 23rd, down 161.42 points (3.31%) from the previous session. Intraday it fell more than 5%, sliding to 4,586.80, and at around 11:33 a.m. a sell sidecar, which briefly suspends the effect of program sell orders, was triggered. It followed one on the 20th, making two days running. The KOSDAQ also closed down 1.58% at 635.45, its lowest in two years. The index slid back to its level at the start of the year, with foreign investors and individuals selling and institutions buying. The size of foreign net selling varies slightly from tally to tally. The KOSPI fell 7.95% in the past week alone, and foreign investors recorded net selling of more than 4.5 trillion won over the same period.

Ahead of this, on the 22nd, the government held an emergency market-conditions review meeting and began preparations so that the Securities Market Stabilization Fund (the stabilization fund) can be activated immediately if needed. A capital commitment of 10.76 trillion won arranged during COVID-19 in 2020 is mentioned as the basis, but the amount actually paid in has not been confirmed. If money is actually put in, it will be the first time in 18 years, since the 2008 global financial crisis. Measures were also announced to lift for three months the daily limit on orders to buy back treasury shares that listed companies can place, and to put illegal short selling under intensive watch. Cheong Wa Dae said on the 23rd that the market instability is partly linked to the U.S. stock market and that the Deputy Prime Minister for the Economy would explain the stabilization fund that day. The timing and size of any deployment will be set depending on market conditions.

Samsung Securities ‘Voice of the Customer’ hacked… personal data of 8,241 customers leaked

Samsung Securities said on the 23rd that the ‘Voice of the Customer’ system on its website had been hacked and the personal information of 8,241 customers had leaked. On the 21st the company found traces of outside intrusion in the system and confirmed that some of the attachments customers uploaded when filing complaints had been taken. The items leaked vary from person to person depending on the material a customer uploaded; on top of name, email and address, some cases include an account number and a driver’s license number. Reports differ on whether the account numbers had been encrypted. Samsung Securities blocked the IP used in the intrusion, suspended the service and reported it to the Financial Supervisory Service and the Korea Internet and Security Agency. It notifies customers confirmed as affected of the items individually, and has also made this checkable through the ‘personal (credit) information leak lookup’ menu on its website. In the securities industry, after Daishin Securities announced on October 28 that the information of 38,490 customers had leaked, the damage has now continued to Samsung Securities. Whether money actually left accounts and the details of the intrusion route are not known.

Cheong Wa Dae: “Considering life sentence without parole and longer stalking no-approach periods”

Cheong Wa Dae said on the 23rd that it is considering extending the no-approach period for stalkers and introducing a ‘life sentence without parole’ that would permanently isolate violent offenders from society. Under the current Stalking Punishment Act, a court can order a provisional measure barring the offender from coming within 100m of the victim for up to three months, and with two extensions it applies for up to nine months at most. A life sentence without parole means that a person given it is excluded from parole regardless of time served or conduct record in prison.

Cheong Wa Dae explained that it will form a whole-of-government response task force to produce comprehensive measures, and that a roadmap will be included in them. Whether the sentence will be introduced, when it would take effect, and how many months the no-approach period would be extended to have not been decided.

‘Real doll disposal’: Jang Yun-gi’s father, a police superintendent, reportedly given severe discipline… apparently escapes criminal punishment under kinship exemption

It was reported on the 23rd that Superintendent Jang, father of the ‘female high school student killer’ Jang Yun-gi (23), received severe disciplinary action. Superintendent Jang was found to have disposed of a damaged real doll and burned the mobile phone the son had used while clearing the son’s rented room on May 8, three days after the incident. The National Police Agency headquarters inspection requested severe discipline on the 8th of last month, and the Gwangju Metropolitan Police Agency disciplinary committee is reported to have decided on severe discipline on the 30th of the same month. Severe discipline for police officers divides into dismissal, discharge, demotion and suspension, but because an appeal review procedure through which it can be contested remains, the exact level was reportedly not disclosed.

Under the kinship exemption provision, Superintendent Jang is reported not to have received criminal punishment such as for destruction of evidence. The National Police Agency is reported to have introduced a recusal system, under which a case involving a police officer’s family is handled by a different police station unconnected to the home area.

New phase in Cho Hee-de impeachment row… Cheong Wa Dae: “a matter for the National Assembly,” judges’ conference chair: “gone too far,” People Power Party to file complaint against Judge Lee Jin-gwan

On the 23rd Cheong Wa Dae said that the impeachment motion against Chief Justice Cho Hee-de being pushed by the Democratic Party is ‘a matter to be discussed in the National Assembly’. The same day, the chair of the National Conference of Judge Representatives said the impeachment of the chief justice has gone too far and that judges too must take action if the impeachment motion is passed. The People Power Party said it would file a complaint for legal distortion against Senior Judge Lee Jin-gwan, who presided over the first trial in the case of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo. The party’s legal support team is collecting specific cases and pushing for the complaint. The Democratic Party explains that the grounds for impeachment are procedural violations, not the content of rulings.

If the impeachment motion passes the National Assembly, the chief justice would be suspended from duty, and some point out that if some Supreme Court justices recuse themselves from trials, the en banc panel could fail to reach its quorum and the insurrection trial of former President Yoon Suk Yeol could be delayed. One poll was reported to show opposition to impeachment at 50.1% and support at 44.6%. The row arose after the Supreme Court overturned and remanded the Han Duck-soo case on grounds pointing to partial acquittal; the dates for introducing and voting on the impeachment motion have not been fixed.
