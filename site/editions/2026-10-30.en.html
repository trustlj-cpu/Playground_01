Insurrection ringleader Yoon Suk Yeol again faces a death-penalty request on appeal… life imprisonment sought for Kim Yong-hyun

At the Seoul High Court’s closing hearing the special counsel asked for the same sentence as at first instance. Final arguments come on the 3rd of next month, and the verdict date will be announced after that.

On the 29th the insurrection special counsel sought the death penalty for former President Yoon Suk Yeol at the closing hearing of the appellate trial before Criminal Division 12-1 of the Seoul High Court. Saying the life imprisonment handed down by the first-instance court on Feb. 19 was too light, the special counsel again asked for the death penalty it had sought at first instance and said there was no reason to hesitate over a death sentence. It also argued that an insurrection the people stopped is no ground for leniency. For former Defense Minister Kim Yong-hyun, who is being tried alongside Yoon, life imprisonment was sought; for Noh Sang-won, former commander of the Defense Intelligence Command, 30 years in prison; for former National Police Agency chief Cho Ji-ho, 20 years; and for former Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency chief Kim Bong-sik, 15 years. A closing hearing is the stage at which the evidence has been examined and the prosecutor states a view on the sentence. The special counsel has been asking the appellate court to accept the probative value of Noh Sang-won’s notebook, which the first-instance court did not recognize, as evidence that the martial law was prepared meticulously over a considerable period.

Yoon’s side continues to deny the insurrection charge on appeal, and the defense team rebutted the request as the true face of a political special counsel. The death penalty is the highest legal sentence, and this is the first time in 30 years, since former President Chun Doo-hwan in 1996, that a death sentence has been sought for a former president at first instance. Still, a sentencing request is not a verdict. At the hearing on the 3rd of next month the court will hear the defendants’ final arguments and final statements and then announce the date of the verdict, so that date is not yet set. With the trial over the Dec. 3 emergency martial law entering the closing stage of the appeal, what remains at issue is whether the first-instance life imprisonment will stand or change to a death sentence. The first-instance court sentenced Kim Yong-hyun to 30 years and Noh Sang-won to 18 years, and this time the special counsel raised its requests to life imprisonment and 30 years respectively.

Samsung Electronics’ Q3 operating profit hits record 107 trillion won… semiconductors alone 106.5 trillion won

Samsung Electronics released its confirmed third-quarter results on the 29th. Consolidated sales were 195.2532 trillion won and operating profit was 107.4405 trillion won, up 783% from a year earlier. That means roughly 55 won of every 100 won in sales stayed as profit from the core business. Nearly all the profit came from the semiconductor (DS) division. Semiconductor operating profit was 106.5 trillion won, an operating margin of 72%, as demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and server storage lifted results. The finished-products (DX) division, by contrast, swung to a loss, with sales of 52 trillion won and an operating loss of 800 billion won. The company said it has finished next year’s HBM supply negotiations and that all the volume is sold, yet additional requests keep coming in. The same day it resolved a third-quarter cash dividend of 4,601 won per share for both common and preferred shares, 30 trillion won in total, payable on Nov. 27. Total shareholder returns this year come to as much as around 110 trillion won, and after the 35 trillion won already fixed, the remaining sum is 55 trillion to 75 trillion won. It said it will retire a considerable amount of treasury shares, with the details to be decided in January next year. Facility investment this year is 84.4 trillion won, including 76.8 trillion won for semiconductors. Despite the record results, the KOSPI closed down 1.24% at 5,937.66 that day, giving up the 6,000 level, and Samsung Electronics shares fell more than 4% at one point.

North Korea fires about 10 short-range ballistic missiles into the East Sea… flight of about 250 km

North Korea fired about 10 short-range ballistic missiles from the Wonsan area into the East Sea at around 12:30 p.m. on the 29th, the Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS) said. The JCS first announced them as unidentified projectiles, then confirmed them as ballistic missiles, and the flight distance was put at about 250 km. The exact specifications are being analyzed by South Korean and U.S. intelligence authorities. Japan’s Defense Ministry said an object that may have been a ballistic missile fell outside Japan’s exclusive economic zone, and the Japan Coast Guard said the projectile had already fallen.

The National Security Office at the presidential office held an emergency security meeting, and the JCS stepped up surveillance and vigilance against further launches. South Korea, the U.S. and Japan are sharing related information. The launch came six days after the Defense Ministry proposed, on the 23rd, inter-Korean military talks to discuss matters such as delineating the Military Demarcation Line in the demilitarized zone, and 17 days after the launch on the 12th. North Korea has not responded to the talks proposal. Counts differ over whether this was the 17th or the 18th launch of the year.

2.47 million Songpa District ballots moved out… blockade protest likely to end after 146 days

More than 2.47 million ballots from Songpa District, Seoul, for the June 3 local elections were all moved out of the Olympic Park handball stadium on the 29th. The National Election Commission (NEC) special counsel team ran a public verification with more than 700 participants for nearly 12 hours, from around 11:50 a.m. the day before until around 12:38 a.m. that day. Of 433 boxes, the 420 judged to have no problems were moved by the NEC to a secure warehouse, while the 13 boxes (about 700 ballots) containing ballots to which observers had objected were secured separately by the special counsel for further analysis, including a detailed examination by the National Forensic Service.

People Power Party lawmakers and some observers opposed the transfer, and an overnight standoff followed; with police forming a human chain around the stadium, the removal began at 6:43 a.m. and ended around 7:20. People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyeok claimed the ballot boxes vanished as if fleeing in the night and that evidence of election fraud poured out during the verification, while Democratic Party observers rebutted that very few ballots were subject to further verification. With the boxes gone, the protest that had blockaded the stadium is effectively over after 146 days, but protesters are mentioning next locations such as Gyodae Station, Seocho-dong and Gwacheon. The result of the detailed examination is not yet out.

Hyundai Motor’s Q3 sales fall 3.4%, first drop in six years… sales target for this year cut to 4 million units

Hyundai Motor announced its third-quarter results on the 29th. Consolidated sales were 45.1102 trillion won, down 3.4% from a year earlier. It is the first quarterly sales decline in about six years, since the COVID-19 period. Fewer working days because of a strike at domestic plants and the Chuseok holiday cut global sales by 11.3%, and domestic sales fell 27.2% to 131,468 units. Even so, operating profit rose 5.5% to 2.6772 trillion won. Exchange-rate effects, cost cuts and higher hybrid sales supported profit. Net profit fell 24.2% on a foreign currency translation loss, corporate tax expense and other items.

The company lowered its annual sales target for this year from about 4.16 million units (4,158,300) to 4 million units: 664,000 in Korea and 3.336 million overseas. It cut its investment plan for the auto business from 17.8 trillion won to 16 trillion won, and lowered its consolidated operating margin target from 6.3–7.3% to 5.5–6.5%. The annual sales growth target of 1–2% was left unchanged.
