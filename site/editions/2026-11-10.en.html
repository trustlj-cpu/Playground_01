Justice minister nominee Lee Yong-chul on farmland container allegation: “lawfully reported”… asked about withdrawal of the indictment, “focus on establishing the truth”

People Power Party lawmaker Joo Jin-woo raised the allegation; the nominee gave no clear answer on whether the indictment would be withdrawn

People Power Party lawmaker Joo Jin-woo raised an allegation on the 9th that Justice Minister nominee Lee Yong-chul illegally installed a container on farmland without permission. The nominee rebutted that it was lawfully reported. President Lee Jae-myung on the 6th named the nominee, then head of the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, as justice minister nominee, and the nominee must go through a National Assembly confirmation hearing. Relevant agencies have not yet given a judgment on whether the report was lawful.

The same day the nominee said friendship with the president is not a disqualification for a minister, and said that the nominee knows nothing about the Daejang-dong case. As the opposition criticizes the pick as made with a withdrawal of the indictment in mind, asked whether the indictment would be withdrawn, the nominee answered only that the focus would be on establishing the truth about the fabricated prosecution and that the rest would follow law and principle. On whether to withdraw it, the nominee said, ‘I will sort things out a bit and say later.’

Security · Diplomacy✓ 2 outlets · government’s final decision unconfirmed

Iran’s Foreign Ministry: “Korea should refrain from sending forces to Hormuz”… concern over talk of expanding Cheonghae Unit activity

Iran’s Foreign Ministry on the 9th expressed strong concern over the possibility that the Korean navy would enter the area to restore passage through the Strait of Hormuz, and said Korea should refrain from military intervention. The Iranian government cited the risk that the situation could worsen, and also used the expression that it hopes Korea will not become an accomplice in military aggression. The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway linking the Persian Gulf with the outer sea, and restoring passage there is at the center of this discussion. In Korea, discussion continues on widening the Cheonghae Unit’s area of operations and adding forces to clear naval mines. Defense Minister Kang Sin-cheol, responding to the charge of a ‘roundabout deployment’, repeatedly stressed the principle of not getting involved in or interfering with the war, and explained that the reason for adding mine-clearing forces is to prepare for threats in a contingency. The ruling party said it would keep the principle of non-involvement in war, and the opposition called it a decision made in a dilemma. It has not been confirmed whether the Korean government has made a final decision to send the navy to the Strait of Hormuz.

Trade · U.S.-China✓ 2 outlets · U.S. reaction unconfirmed

China extends by two months, to next January, its deferral of the 24% additional tariff on the U.S. and of tightened controls on rare earths

China announced on the 9th that it will extend by two months, from the 10th to January next year, the deferral of the 24% additional tariff it had been going to impose on U.S. imports, while keeping the 10% tariff. The deferral of the measure to tighten export controls on rare earths is extended for the same period. A deferral only puts tariffs or controls off, so when the deadline ends they take effect again unless separate action is taken. After the U.S.-China summit held in Korea last November, China decided to defer the 24% additional tariff for another year, and that deadline was the 10th of this month.

Which talks with the U.S. this extension is the result of, and what steps the U.S. will take in response, have not been confirmed. Whether it will be extended again in January next year, two months from now, or whether the tariffs and controls will actually begin, has not been decided.

IT · Personal data✓ 2 outlets · actual scope and circumstances of the leak unconfirmed

Personal data leak reported for 100-odd Korean corporate accounts using Anthropic’s ‘Claude’… received by Personal Information Protection Commission

The Personal Information Protection Commission said on the 9th that it has received a personal data leak report involving 100-odd Korean corporate accounts using ‘Claude’, the generative artificial intelligence service of the U.S. firm Anthropic. It has been raised that, as an outside security firm gained unauthorized access to Anthropic’s work systems, account information of Korean customer companies may also have been exposed.

What information leaked and how much, and how much of it contained account holders’ personal information, is not yet known. That a report was received does not mean the scope of the leak has been settled.

National Election Commission special counsel asks Public Prosecution Service and National Forensic Service for forensic examination of removed ballots

The National Election Commission special counsel team led by Lee Tae-han, investigating the shortage of ballot papers at the June 3 local elections, said on the 9th that it had asked the Public Prosecution Service and the National Forensic Service for a forensic examination of ballots taken out of the Olympic Park Handball Stadium.

The number of ballots entrusted for examination and when results will come out have not been confirmed. The cause of the ballot paper shortage has also not yet been established.
