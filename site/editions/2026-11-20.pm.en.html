Samsung Electronics executive reshuffle keeps Jun Young-hyun and Roh Tae-moon as ‘twin tops’… Hwang Sang-jun and Choi Won-joon named new heads of memory and Galaxy

The reshuffle announced on the 20th keeps the broad frame of top management while changing who is in charge of two core businesses.

Samsung Electronics announced its reshuffle of top executives on the 20th and decided to continue the ‘twin-top’ setup of Jun Young-hyun and Roh Tae-moon. An executive reshuffle is the annual personnel decision that keeps or replaces a company’s highest-ranking executives. The direction of this reshuffle was reported to lean toward stability rather than big change. Even so, new faces enter the two core businesses. Hwang Sang-jun and Choi Won-joon have stepped forward as the new heads of the memory business and the Galaxy business. According to one report, 3 presidents were promoted in the semiconductor division alone. The number of promotions and retirements in other business divisions has not yet been reported.

The company left in place the existing setup in which Jun Young-hyun leads the semiconductor business and Roh Tae-moon leads the home appliance and mobile business. An explanation emerged that this reads as an intention to keep running the business stably without big change. The specific job titles of the new heads, when they take office and the full list of those promoted have not been confirmed. Whether the company will issue a separate organizational overhaul or follow-up reshuffle is also not known.

‘SM stock price manipulation’: Kakao founder Kim Beom-su acquitted at appeal trial too… prosecutors had sought 15 years in prison

Kakao founder Kim Beom-su was found not guilty on the 20th at the appeal trial on charges of stock price manipulation involving SM Entertainment shares. Manipulating a share price means buying and selling shares in large volumes to move the price artificially. The first trial ended in acquittal, and prosecutors appealed, so the case was argued again at the Seoul High Court. According to one report, the court explained that ‘every trade, to varying degrees, could in itself affect the market price.’ The prosecution’s sentencing request had been 15 years in prison. According to one report, after the ruling Kim said that ‘Kakao’s uncertainty has been resolved’ and that ‘bold change is needed.’ The court’s full reasoning has not been confirmed. Whether prosecutors will lodge an appeal to the Supreme Court has not been reported either. In a criminal case, the period for such an appeal is 7 days from the day after the verdict is pronounced. If none is filed, the acquittal becomes final.

111 electronic-device cheating cases tallied nationwide at CSAT… 54 caught by disaster alert, government vows to ‘track down to the end’

The Ministry of Education said on the 20th that a total of 111 cases of cheating with electronic devices at the 2027 College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) had been caught nationwide. Of these, 54 were devices singled out by sending an emergency disaster alert to the exam sites and finding which ones sounded. The 22 people mentioned at the Vice Minister of Education’s briefing on the 19th and the 98 people reported afterward cannot be compared directly with this 111, because one counts people and the other counts cases. The Vice Minister of Education said that, among the 22 caught earlier, there was no case of using an advanced device other than a smartphone.

The government moved on follow-up. The prime minister said a whole-of-government task force on cheating would be run to track it down to the end, and that reports would be accepted for two months with a reward of up to 100 million won. According to one report, the prime minister ordered last year’s CSAT exam papers to be kept, so a full survey reaching back to past exams is being mentioned as a possibility. In Cheonan, South Chungcheong, a candidate formally booked on suspicion of using smart glasses admitted to using the glasses in the actual exam. The scale of smart glasses cases, the total number of those formally booked and how scores will be handled have not been confirmed.

California subpoenas Nvidia… report says probe into leak of advanced chips and secrets to China

One report said authorities in the US state of California had issued a subpoena to Nvidia. The subject of the inquiry is whether advanced semiconductors and secrets leaked to China. A subpoena is a document by which an investigating agency legally demands relevant documents or testimony. The move was said to have come after former CEO Jensen Huang stepped down. How Nvidia will respond to the subpoena and the scope of the demand have not been confirmed.

Inside the company, the interim CEO said at an all-hands meeting, ‘We can do this,’ and that ‘the passion for innovation will not change.’ A report also said pay raises were hinted at. One report said 56% of employees want Jensen Huang to return, but the method and the group surveyed are unconfirmed. No announcement has been made on whether Jensen Huang will return.

Report says Anthropic rose 17% on first day of trading… opening trade at $111.50, amount raised differs by tally

A report said Anthropic’s share price rose 17% on its first day, after trading began on the Nasdaq on the 19th (local time). The price on which the gain was based and the closing price have not been confirmed. The opening trade was at $111.50, about 34% above the offering price of $83. There was also a report that the price topped the offering price intraday before slipping, so the price was said to have moved up and down during the day.

The size of the money raised in the initial public offering differs by report, with $81.3 billion, $94 billion and $107 billion all appearing. The same day, the Nasdaq Composite closed down 2.25%. The KOSPI closed down 5.21% on the 20th at 4,869.96, but whether this fall is related to the Anthropic listing has not been confirmed.
