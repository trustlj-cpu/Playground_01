Bank of Korea raises base rate 0.25 percentage point to 3.25%… two Board members favor holding, next year’s growth forecast revised up to 4.1%

The decision previewed in the morning editions has come. On the 26th the Bank of Korea raised the base rate from 3.00% to 3.25%, its third increase this year.

The Bank of Korea’s Monetary Policy Board raised the base rate by 0.25 percentage point on the 26th, from 3.00% to 3.25%. It is the third increase this year. Five of the Board’s seven members voted for the increase, and two, Hwang Geon-il and Lee Su-hyung, filed a minority opinion in favor of holding. A minority opinion is a position that differs from the majority, kept on the formal record. The base rate is the starting point for bank loan and deposit rates, so households with floating-rate loans may face a heavier interest burden. One report said the wording on further increases was kept but the wording indicating the pace of increases was dropped, but this was not confirmed elsewhere. The KOSPI pared a rise that had been around 1% at one point after the announcement and closed the day at 4,711.21, up 0.29% from the previous session.

The Bank also revised its economic outlook that day. It raised this year’s growth forecast from 3.3% to 3.9% and next year’s from the previous forecast of 2.9% to 4.1%. Inflation was held at 2.7% for this year and raised from 2.3% to 2.4% for next year. Governor Shin Hyun-song said nominal GDP this year would exceed 3,000 trillion won and semiconductor operating profit would reach 700 trillion won. Nominal GDP is the size of gross domestic product that carries price changes as they are. The governor expects private consumption to grow 2.4% next year, yet explained that the stock market slump had dampened consumption in the second half, and called this increase a preemptive response, saying inflation would converge to the 2028 target level. The timing and size of the next increase and the specific reasons of the two members who favored holding are not confirmed.

Homeplus to close all stores temporarily from the 27th… “not enough money to buy goods”; party interested in acquiring is a consortium led by a real estate developer

Homeplus will temporarily halt operations at its stores nationwide from the 27th. The company cited a shortage of funds to buy goods. One report put the number of stores halting operations at 67, but the figure was not confirmed elsewhere, so “all stores” is used here. Homeplus is a large supermarket chain in corporate rehabilitation proceedings, which had been continuing to operate while clearing its debts under court supervision. One report said funds of about 200 billion won had come in recently yet the cash crunch persisted, but this amount too was not confirmed elsewhere. The length of the closure, the reopening date and how employee pay and supplier payments will be handled are not known. News on the acquisition side came out the same day. According to one report, the party pursuing a takeover of the whole of Homeplus is a consortium led by a real estate developer. A consortium is an acquisition alliance formed jointly by several companies. While there are reports that a letter of intent, a document stating interest in acquiring, has been received, the labor union said receipt is not confirmed and demanded an answer from the company. The acquisition price and terms and the sale schedule are not confirmed.

Supreme Court dismisses final appeal in former President Yoon Suk Yeol’s ‘perjury in Han Duck-soo trial’ case… acquittal final

On the 26th the Supreme Court dismissed the final appeal on the charge of ‘perjury in the Han Duck-soo trial’ against former President Yoon Suk Yeol and made the acquittal final. The charge is that statements Yoon made as a witness in the trial of former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, concerning the Cabinet meeting at the time of martial law, were false. Perjury is the crime of a sworn witness making a false statement, and for a conviction the falsity of the statement must be proven. One report said the Supreme Court judged that a statement to the effect that ‘an attempt was made to hold a Cabinet meeting’ cannot be regarded as perjury and that a false statement was not proven, but details of the reasoning were not confirmed elsewhere.

Dismissing the final appeal means the Supreme Court accepted the appellate court’s judgment as it stood, so the case can no longer be contested. This ruling applies only to the perjury charge. Its effect on the other trials Yoon faces is not confirmed.

US floats overproduction tariff proposal on 14 countries and regions including Korea… 15% cap kept for Korea; government: “held up well despite pressure over trade surplus with US”

On the 25th (local time) the Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) put forward a proposed notice of additional tariffs on 14 countries and regions including Korea, citing overproduction. The government said the direction is to keep for Korea the 15% cap agreed in last year’s Korea-US negotiations. Industry Minister Kim Jung-kwan’s position is that it would be a solid result if the cap is defended to the end, and the government considers it a solid result if 15% is maintained even amid pressure over the trade surplus with the US. An overproduction tariff is an additional tariff imposed on the grounds that one country’s production capacity exceeds demand and cheap goods are flooding out.

However, the USTR said it would also consider raising Korea’s tariff rate to as much as 25% if it judges consultations and existing agreements insufficient. There were also reports that a report had taken issue with Korea’s fostering of strategic industries such as semiconductors. Rates are reported differently by country. Korea and Japan are reported at around 15%, with an additional 20% proposed for China and up to 20% for Singapore. The final decision, the effective date, the items covered and whether the 15% cap also applies to semiconductors and automobiles are not known.

President Lee Jae-myung’s approval at 43%, down 1 percentage point from two weeks ago… negative rating at 45%, ahead of positive

In the National Barometer Survey (NBS), a regular opinion poll, 43% of respondents rated President Lee Jae-myung’s handling of state affairs positively. That is down 1 percentage point from two weeks ago. The negative rating is 45%, 2 percentage points higher than the positive.

A report said the same survey found 44% opposed and 34% in favor of the impeachment of Chief Justice Cho Hee-de, but this was not confirmed elsewhere. The survey period, sample size and margin of error are not confirmed.
