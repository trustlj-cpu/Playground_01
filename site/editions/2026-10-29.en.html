SK hynix signs long-term contracts with 14 customers, securing half of 2027–2028 capacity… 60 trillion won in additional returns

Yonhap reported that the company announced third-quarter operating profit of 83.3338 trillion won together with quarterly sales above 100 trillion won

SK hynix announced its third-quarter results on the 28th, saying operating profit was 83.3338 trillion won, up 632% from a year earlier, Yonhap and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported. Yonhap reported that quarterly sales topped 100 trillion won for the first time, at 104.6 trillion won, and Chosun Ilbo wrote that it was a record for the sixth straight quarter. Korea Economic Daily reported that the operating margin reached 80%, achieving a ‘dream operating margin’, while another Chosun Ilbo breaking story gave operating profit as 83.3334 trillion won, so the final digits differ from article to article. Kyunghyang Shinmun and Dong-A Ilbo reported that the company announced an additional 60 trillion won in shareholder returns the same day. According to Yonhap, it will buy back 20 trillion won of treasury shares, with 10 trillion won going to share retirement and 10 trillion won to employee compensation, and will also pay a quarterly dividend of 20,000 won per common share, 14 trillion won in total. Korea Economic Daily reported that this year’s shareholder returns total 100 trillion won.

Chosun Ilbo reported that on its conference call the company said it had signed long-term contracts with 14 customers, securing half of its 2027–2028 production capacity. Yonhap reported that the company said its DRAM and HBM volumes for the three years through 2029 are effectively sold out, and that in gauging demand it will also look at customers’ financial health, ability to raise funds and the readiness of key supply chains. Korea Economic Daily reported that the company expects its demand advantage to continue through 2030. Chosun Ilbo gave ‘HBM4 mass production in full swing… server DDR5 sales up more than 40%’ as the conference-call headline, and reported that President Kwak Noh-jung, announcing a halt to the Solidigm initial public offering, said ‘it is difficult to achieve the expected effect’. Chosun Ilbo also reported that the call included an outlook that the AI data-center market will grow more than 60% in 2027. Yonhap wrote that the results fired back once more at the argument that AI has peaked, and reported that this was a judgment combining volumes already secured under long-term supply contracts with demand under discussion with customers.

Korea Media and Communications Commission fines Google and Apple 63.7 billion won for forcing in-app payments… Google 42.9 billion won, Apple 20.8 billion won

The Korea Media and Communications Commission on the 28th imposed penalty surcharges of 42.9 billion won on Google and 20.81 billion won on Apple and decided on corrective orders, Yonhap and Chosun Ilbo reported. The two fines together come to 63.71 billion won. Yonhap reported it was the first sanction for forcing in-app payments, and Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that it took three years for the sanction to be finalized. Chosun Ilbo said in its headline that the commission imposed the fines after finding the conduct amounted to ‘forcing in-app payments’. In a wrap-up article, Yonhap reported that Google and Apple expressed regret at the decision and said they would consider how to respond. KBS reported that Google and Apple pushed back against the 63.7 billion won fine and issued a position that they ‘regret the decision’. Dong-A Ilbo reported the amounts of 42.9 billion won for Google and 20.8 billion won for Apple in a breaking story. Kyunghyang Shinmun noted in a separate article that the fine was now finalized. Whether the two companies will file suit was not confirmed in the reports.

Korean and U.S. defense chiefs to meet in Washington in December, hold SCM in early next year… opposition demands dismissal over ‘Can we trust U.S. forces?’ remark

Defense Minister Kang Sin-cheol held a first in-person meeting since taking office with U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth on the 28th, Yonhap reported. According to a Kyunghyang Shinmun photo caption, the meeting took place on the sidelines of the 13th ASEAN Defence Ministers’ Meeting-Plus in Cebu, the Philippines. Yonhap and Korea Economic Daily reported in breaking stories that the two ministers will hold bilateral talks in Washington this December and, on that basis, convene the Korea–U.S. Security Consultative Meeting (SCM) in early next year. Yonhap reported that the Defense Ministry said the two ministers agreed to cooperate closely to transfer wartime operational control at the right time.

Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the U.S. Department of Defense is postponing the 58th SCM, which had been due in November, to early next year, saying it was a ‘decision made by mutual agreement because of a scheduling conflict among leadership’. Chosun Ilbo reported that the U.S. speaks of holding it in early next year, while South Korea’s Defense Ministry’s position is that it is ‘under consultation and not finalized’. Yonhap reported that the conservative opposition, including the People Power Party, demanded Minister Kang’s dismissal over the remark ‘Can we trust U.S. forces if war breaks out with North Korea?’, saying it ‘shook the blood alliance’. SBS reported that the issuance of a U.S. visa to the chair of the National Assembly National Defense Committee was refused the same day.

FTC conducts on-site inspections of four ramen makers Nongshim, Samyang Foods, Paldo and Ottogi… suspected price collusion

The Fair Trade Commission sent investigators on the 28th to carry out on-site inspections at four major South Korean ramen makers, including Nongshim, Samyang Foods, Paldo and Ottogi, Chosun Ilbo reported. According to Chosun Ilbo, the FTC is examining whether they consulted in advance in setting ramen selling prices. Yonhap and SBS reported that the FTC’s Priority Investigation Planning Unit, launched this month, is understood to be investigating the ramen collusion case after processed foods such as instant rice and sauces.

Yonhap reported that the same day the FTC went to the scene to investigate suspected bid rigging on public works at six construction firms including Gyeryong Construction, Kolon Global and Isu Construction. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the Public Prosecution Service and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency are clashing over which agency should receive collusion self-reports (leniency). In a separate article Chosun Ilbo reported that Nongshim is expanding its product lineup with stir-fried noodles to push Shin Ramyun’s overseas expansion. The ramen makers’ positions were not confirmed in the reports.

Government accepts loss in Elliott remand arbitration… files no annulment suit, about 153 billion won in damages finalized

The government decided on the 28th not to file an annulment suit in a British court against the remand arbitration award in the international investment dispute (ISDS) with U.S.-based private equity fund Elliott, Korea Economic Daily reported. Yonhap reported that the government lost for good and that the damages come to about 153 billion won, and Kyunghyang Shinmun and Chosun Ilbo also put the same figure in their headlines. Dong-A Ilbo reported that the award was handed down on the 30th of last month and that the government decided after discussions with relevant ministries, the law firm representing the government and outside experts. Dong-A Ilbo reported that the dispute over the 2015 merger of Samsung C&T and Cheil Industries, which began in 2018, has entered the wrap-up stage after eight years.

SBS Biz, citing a Justice Ministry announcement right after the remand arbitration award, reported that it orders the government to pay about $48.49 million (about 65.8 billion won) plus late-payment interest. Newsis reported that in February this year the government won the annulment suit and avoided paying 160 billion won in damages. How this 153 billion won and the earlier 65.8 billion won relate is not confirmed in the reports.
