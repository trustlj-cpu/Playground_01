KOSPI plunges 3.48% to close at 5,730.74… sell-side sidecar on Alphabet shock, Hynix down 7%

Weak earnings at US big tech shook a market leaning heavily on AI chips. The KOSDAQ also fell 3.83%, closing at 724.38.

On the 30th the KOSPI closed at 5,730.74, down 207 points (3.48%). It had already slipped to the 5,900 level the day before, and in a single day it was pushed down to the early 5,700s. It started in the US. Alphabet reported earnings short of market expectations and its shares plunged more than 12%, wiping out about 669 trillion won in market capitalization in a day. Amazon’s shares fell about 7% on worries over cash flow even though its cloud unit AWS grew 39%. Doubts have grown over whether the money poured into AI is coming back as revenue. This unease spread to Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, which had risen on AI chip demand.

The KOSPI opened at 5,760.35 (down 2.99%), then widened its losses into the 4% range, and at around 10 a.m. a sell-side sidecar was triggered for the first time in 11 trading days. A sidecar is a device that cushions the shock by suspending the effect of program selling orders for 5 minutes when futures and the spot index plunge together. The loss, which had been in the 4% range during the session, narrowed to 3.48% at the close, and Samsung Electronics fell 3% and SK Hynix in the 7% range. SK Hynix announced a 20 trillion won share buyback plan but could not avoid the decline. Samsung Electronics had announced Q3 operating profit of 107 trillion won the day before, yet its shares were pushed down as much as the 4% range at one point on a reaction that shareholder returns fell short of expectations. The size of foreign and institutional net selling has not yet been confirmed, and the direction of the next trading day depends on whether US stocks carry the Alphabet shock forward.

Actor Choi Bul-am, the ‘eternal Crime Squad chief’, dies at 87… funeral altar at Asan Medical Center in Seoul

The actor Choi Bul-am died on the 30th. The age at death was 87. The first news of the death came at dawn, and a funeral altar was set up at Asan Medical Center in Seoul, where condolence visits were received from 10 a.m. that day. In the crime drama ‘Crime Squad’ Choi played Chief Park and was the face of the living-room screen from 1971 to 1989, and in the rural family drama ‘Village Diary’ played Chair Kim and led a long-running series that ran from 1980 to 2002. With the trademark ‘Pa-ha~’ laugh, Choi was called the ‘eternal Crime Squad chief’ and ‘the nation’s father’, and the acting career spanned 67 years. Political figures, including President Lee Jae-myung and Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon, paid tribute one after another. Mayor Oh introduced Choi as the ‘longest-serving Seoul city ambassador’ and said the empty place is keenly felt. In life Choi was also known for work helping children in crisis, and left the words, “Clearing the thorn bushes from the path the people walk is the spirit of a clown.” The cause of death, the funeral date and the funeral arrangements have not yet been officially confirmed.

Economy · Industrial activity✓ 4 outlets · changes by industry and in services unconfirmed

September output up 3.0%, consumption 3.6%, investment 5.3% together… largest output gain in 15 years and 6 months

In the September industrial activity trends released by the National Data Office on the 30th, output, consumption and investment all rose together for the first time in three months. The all-industry output figure, which adds up mining and manufacturing, services and more, rose 3.0% from the previous month, the biggest jump in 15 years and 6 months. Goods consumption, as shown by retail sales, rose 3.6%, and facility investment, which gauges future production capacity, rose 5.3%. It means the output index went from 100 to 103.0.

When all three indicators rise together it is called a ‘triple increase’, and this time semiconductors and autos led the gain. Unlike the KOSPI, which plunged the same day led by chip stocks, the real-economy indicators for September show semiconductors pulling output up. The changes by industry, the detailed figures for services and the trend in October have not yet been confirmed.

Politics · Judiciary✓ 4 outlets · whether the special counsel will appeal in the Kim Gi-hyeon case unconfirmed

Kim Gi-hyeon ‘luxury bag’ case: not guilty at first instance, spouse fined 5 million won… special counsel appeals Oh Se-hoon’s second-instance acquittal to Supreme Court

On the 30th the Seoul Central District Court found People Power Party lawmaker Kim Gi-hyeon not guilty at first instance on charges of violating the Improper Solicitation and Graft Act by giving Kim Keon-hee a Roger Vivier bag in the 2.6 million won range. The special counsel had sought a prison term. Kim’s spouse was fined 5 million won. It was the spouse who handed over the bag, but the court found that a conspiracy between the couple was not proven. After the sentencing Kim objected: “A zombie special counsel’s indiscriminate indictment is political persecution.”

The same day the Kim Keon-hee special counsel filed an appeal to the Supreme Court against the Seoul High Court Criminal Division 7 appeal trial ruling that found Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon not guilty of violating the Political Funds Act by having Myung Tae-kyun pay for opinion polls. The special counsel argues there is enough evidence to back Myung Tae-kyun’s statements. The Supreme Court will make the final judgment, and in a Gallup Korea survey, 34% saw this second-instance acquittal as a ‘good ruling’ and 37% as a ‘bad ruling’.

Economy · Crypto assets✓ 4 outlets · damage and frozen amounts not yet officially confirmed

World’s largest exchange Binance suspected of massive hack… deposits and withdrawals fully halted, full compensation promised

Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange, fully halted deposits and withdrawals on the 30th, saying it appeared to have suffered a massive hack. The company said the exchange appeared to have been a target of hacking and that it would compensate the damage in full. The scale of the damage is cited in the billions of dollars, but it varies a little by tally and there is no official confirmation. There were also reports that stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze $2.3 billion worth, but there is no official confirmation of that either. A stablecoin is a crypto asset issued to match the value of a legal currency such as the dollar, and when hacked funds move, its issuer can freeze the wallets involved.

The shock spread to prices. Bitcoin fell 3.7% and traded in the $78,000 range, Ethereum and XRP also plunged, and Binance Coin (BNB) dropped in the 5% range within 4 hours. Korea’s won-market exchanges issued one notice after another urging caution over deposits and withdrawals with Binance. The exact damage, the route of the leak, the effect on Korean users’ assets and the time deposits and withdrawals will resume are not yet confirmed.
