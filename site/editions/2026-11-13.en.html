Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang announces resignation… Kress, the CFO, named interim CEO as search for a permanent successor begins

Following yesterday’s reports that the board was preparing a replacement, the company itself announced Huang’s immediate resignation on the 12th (local time). The reason for the resignation has not been confirmed.

On the 12th (local time), Nvidia said in a regulatory filing that Jensen Huang had stepped down immediately as president, chief executive officer (CEO) and director. The board named Colette Kress, chief financial officer (CFO), as interim CEO and appointed Harvey Jones as the company’s first board chair. The company’s explanation is that it will look for a permanent CEO among both internal and external candidates. Huang led the company for 33 years. What this page reported yesterday was a report from sources that the board was preparing a replacement, and there was no official announcement from the company. The new development is that the company itself announced the resignation within a day. On the New York stock market, Nvidia shares opened about 13% lower. Interim CEO Kress told employees, “This is an extremely difficult time right now,” and called an all-hands meeting.

The reason for the resignation cannot be confirmed from the filing alone. Allegations surrounding Huang’s private life, and reports that a woman involved is suspected of being a Chinese spy, have followed, but the China-related part is reporting that has not been officially confirmed. The U.S. Congress is said to be pressing for testimony even after Huang’s departure, and to be ready to issue a subpoena if there is no compliance. A subpoena is a document that legally requires testimony or the submission of materials. President Donald Trump said that Tim Cook of Apple would be the right successor, but that is only the president’s opinion, not a list of candidates released by Nvidia. When a permanent CEO will be chosen, and whether orders and investment plans will change, is not yet known.

At ‘Kim Seung-won solicitation’ trial, Public Prosecution Service seeks 4 years for broker Yang and 6 months for Genencell founder Kang

At a closing hearing held on the 12th at the Seoul Western District Court before Judge Seong Jun-gyu of Criminal Single Division 10, the Public Prosecution Service made sentencing requests of 4 years in prison and forfeiture of more than 600.11 million won for broker Yang, who was indicted on charges including promise to give a bribe, and 6 months in prison for Kang, founder of the pharmaceutical company Genencell. A closing hearing is a trial session at which, after the examination of evidence is finished, the prosecutor states a sentencing opinion. The two are suspected of soliciting approval of a clinical trial plan for a COVID-19 treatment through Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Seung-won and promising to give sponsorship money. The sentencing requests concern these two people. A sentencing request is the prosecutor’s opinion, not a judge’s decision. The date of the verdict and what the two sides said in their final statements have not been confirmed. Separately from this trial, Kang was sentenced on Sept. 30 at an appeals trial to 4 years in prison with detention in court. That is a ruling in a different case, so it should be read apart from the outcome of this trial.

Houthi rebels claim hits on Saudi East-West pipeline and Yanbu oil facilities… damage not confirmed, attack on Mecca denied

Yemen’s pro-Iran rebel group the Houthis claimed on the 12th (local time) to have struck Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline and an oil facility at Yanbu on the Red Sea coast. The Houthis also said they attacked pumping stations around Yanbu with drones. The East-West pipeline carries crude from Saudi Arabia’s eastern oil fields to ports on the Red Sea side, and is a means for Saudi Arabia to move oil around the Strait of Hormuz. The Houthis, on the other hand, denied reports that they attacked the area of the holy city of Mecca. There were also reports that Saudi Arabia carried out an interception of the Houthis’ missiles and drones. An interception is shooting down incoming weapons in the air.

Whether the strikes actually took place, and if so how much damage was done to which facilities, has not been confirmed beyond the Houthi claims. The Houthis are known to have claimed on Oct. 27 as well to have attacked a pumping station on the same pipeline. If the Strait of Hormuz were blocked, the pipeline and Red Sea ports would serve as a detour, so claims aimed at these facilities are tied to worries about crude supply. Saudi Arabia’s official position has not been confirmed.

Daejang-dong’s Nam Wook claims in court ‘Disadvantages if you don’t cooperate with the investigation… it will come out in the special counsel’s probe’, one outlet reports

Nam Wook, a private developer involved in the Daejang-dong development project, said in court on the 12th, in effect, that the prosecution (now the Public Prosecution Service) had imposed disadvantages on those who did not cooperate with the investigation, and claimed that this would come out in the special counsel’s probe. The remark is the testimony of a party to a trial in progress. The Public Prosecution Service’s response and the court’s judgment have not been confirmed.

The Public Prosecution Service is the agency that handles indictments in place of the Prosecutors’ Office, and reports call it ‘the prosecution (now the Public Prosecution Service)’. A special counsel is a separate investigative body set up to investigate only a particular case. Because Nam is a party in the Daejang-dong case, this claim also has to be read as words coming from Nam’s own position. Whether treatment really differed depending on cooperation with the investigation, and by what procedure any such fact would be sorted out, is not yet known.

KOSPI closes down 0.61% at 5,342.73… holds the 5,300 level, KOSDAQ change withheld as tallies conflict

The KOSPI closed on the 12th at 5,342.73, down 0.61% from the previous trading day. Slipping slightly from the Nov. 11 close of 5,375.36, it held the 5,300 level. The closing price is the last price when trading ends for the day. Early in the session it opened modestly higher around the 5,380 level, and for a time the tug of war over the 5,400 level continued. Individual investors were buying, and Samsung Electronics was reported to have gained in the 2% range during the session. The day before, the index had risen 2.60%, and on this day it closed lower.

The KOSDAQ’s closing change differs from tally to tally, so the figure is hard to pin down. Meanwhile one tally found that the KOSPI had fallen about 23% over the past month, the biggest drop among major markets, but that figure is confirmed in only one place. The index level of 5,342.73 shows how much the total market capitalization of all listed stocks has changed, with a base point set at 1,000.
