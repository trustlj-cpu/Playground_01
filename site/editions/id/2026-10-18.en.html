KPK names former BNPB head’s younger sibling and four others as suspects, disaster funds allegedly used to buy shares

The Ready-to-Use Fund (Dana Siap Pakai), which should have gone to the response to karhutla fires and the NTT earthquake, is at the heart of the case. KPK seized evidence worth about Rp5 billion, but the article carrying the death penalty was not used.

The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) named Bima Setiawan, the younger sibling of the former BNPB head, and four others as suspects in an alleged disaster-fund corruption case at the National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB). According to KPK’s explanation, cited by six outlets since Friday, October 16, the money at issue came from the Ready-to-Use Fund, a budget line set aside for emergency response. Its stated purpose was to deal with forest and land fires and an earthquake in East Nusa Tenggara. Bima is alleged to have used part of the money to buy shares, and the list of shares bought is the part of the case most often cited.

Investigators also seized evidence. The reported figures differ between outlets: one report gives Rp5.47 billion, another gives Rp5 billion and says it came from funds for handling karhutla and floods. No state loss figure has been published in Friday’s and Saturday’s reports, so the value of the seizure cannot yet be compared with the total funds at issue. KPK has also not publicly explained the role of each of the five suspects.

One question many members of the public ask is why the suspects were not charged under the article that carries the death penalty. The Law on the Eradication of Corruption Crimes does provide for the death penalty for corruption committed under certain circumstances, including during a disaster. KPK explained that the article was not applied because investigators were limited in time at this stage. The explanation was carried by two outlets on the same day, Saturday, October 17, and has not been followed by additional statements from other institutions.

KPK itself sent a more general message. It warned that the Ready-to-Use Fund must not become a free-for-all, because this type of fund is designed to be disbursed quickly when disaster strikes, with simpler procedures than the regular budget. That speed also makes oversight of its use a weak point. What to wait for next is further questioning, a breakdown of the flow of funds, and whether investigators add other charges as the file develops.

For readers, three things are worth noting. First, this case is not about a single figure; it concerns funds earmarked from the start for victims of fires and the earthquake, and so every rupiah diverted has a concrete face. Second, the media report details that do not fully match, so readers need to distinguish between KPK’s official explanation and the summaries in each report. Third, as of Saturday there was no widely carried statement from the suspects’ side, so everything above remains the investigators’ allegations, to be tested in court.

Klaten Regent and head of BPK’s Central Java office detained on the spot by KPK, money allegedly for 2024 Pilkada debts

KPK named the Klaten Regent and the head of the Central Java representative office of the Audit Board (BPK) as suspects, then detained both immediately. Five outlets reported the naming on Friday and Saturday. According to KPK, the head of the BPK Central Java office asked for Rp600 million, while the Klaten Regent bargained it down to Rp400 million. Investigators commonly lay out such details to show how a deal came about, but KPK has not explained in detail what was exchanged for the money. One outlet also reported an allegation that the proceeds of the corruption were used to pay 2024 Pilkada debts. The case touches two institutions that formally face each other: the regional government as the party being audited, and BPK as the auditor of its financial reports. If KPK’s allegation is proven, the relationship between auditor and audited becomes instead a channel for transactions. That is why Friday’s and Saturday’s reporting stressed two things, namely the immediate detention and the amount of money requested. KPK has not stated the amount that finally changed hands, and the suspects’ full identities are not discussed at length in the available reports. Questioning of other witnesses is still under way, including summonses for an official who has not met the schedule. In cases like this, immediate detention signals that investigators judge the initial evidence strong enough and fear attempts to destroy evidence, though the official reason for the detention is not set out in the available reports. Klaten residents can follow developments through statements from KPK and the district government.

Prabowo turns 75, Putin sends greetings and the whole cabinet is gathered at Bogor Palace

President Prabowo Subianto turned 75 on Saturday, October 17, 2026. Greetings came from at home and abroad. Russian President Vladimir Putin sent congratulations and mentioned Prabowo’s authority and contribution, according to two outlets. The Presidential Advisory Council (Wantimpres), the Ministry of State Secretariat, and State Secretary Prasetyo Hadi also offered prayers, and Prasetyo thanked the public for the prayers that poured in.

The day before, on Friday night, Prabowo gathered all cabinet members at Bogor Palace. Two outlets said the event also hosted Russia’s Deputy Prime Minister and introduced the cabinet to him. Housing Minister Maruarar Sirait, after the meeting, said he was grateful for two years of cabinet work, while the head of the National Food Agency said that food has grown ever sturdier over two years of Prabowo’s leadership. What was discussed inside the room was not detailed, so the reason for the meeting is known only from participants’ statements.

Mensesneg Prasetyo Hadi and Kemensetneg used the birthday to urge the public to keep up togetherness. The Bapanas head, in a separate statement, judged that food has grown ever sturdier over two years of Prabowo’s leadership; this claim is an official’s assessment and is not accompanied by price or stock data in the available summaries, so readers should compare it with data from Statistics Indonesia.

Environment · Forest fires✓ 4 outlets · hotspot distribution by province incomplete

Karhutla smoke detected in Jakarta, Singapore warns seven Indonesian companies

Smoke from forest and land fires (karhutla) has been detected in the Jakarta area, and Rano Karno asked residents not to panic, according to a detikcom report on Saturday, October 17. The same day the Singapore government issued warnings to seven Indonesian companies over cross-border haze originating from land fires. The companies’ names are not listed in the available summary.

On the ground, four outlets reported that BNPB used up 2.4 tons of dry ice to deal with karhutla smoke in South Sumatra. Forestry Minister Raja Juli Antoni inspected karhutla handling in Kalimantan in person and stressed that the government is not washing its hands of it. The BNPB disaster-fund corruption case being handled by KPK also names karhutla response among the intended uses of the funds, so the two issues now overlap in the coverage.

For residents, the practical questions are air quality and how long the smoke lingers. Saturday’s reports did not include Jakarta’s air quality index figures or the number of hotspots in South Sumatra and Kalimantan. The safest information to rely on is therefore local-government advisories and official data that is updated regularly.

Trump suspends Russia tariff sanctions, Ukraine protests, and White House spokesperson removed within a week

US President Donald Trump signed an executive order exempting Russia from tariff sanctions, and Ukraine strongly protested the decision, according to two outlets on Saturday, October 17. Details of which goods are exempted have not been set out in the news summary. The same day Katie Zacharia was removed as White House spokesperson after less than a week in the job, after repeated blunders, two outlets said.

In the Middle East, Iran denied involvement in a Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia and offered to act as mediator. In Myanmar, a military air strike on the village of Yesagyo killed 11 civilians, including children, according to one outlet. At home, subsidized fuel filling in Bali uses an odd-even system from October 17 to 19, and Nadiem Makarim filed a cassation appeal against an appeal ruling of 9 years in prison.

National Police Chief (Kapolri) Gen. Suyudi Ario Seto visited the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX) and pledged to investigate suspected capital-market crimes; he did not specify the case in question in the news summary. Nadiem’s case now moves to the Supreme Court, the body that will weigh the cassation appeal.
