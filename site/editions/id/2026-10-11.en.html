Flash floods hit Padang, hundreds of residents affected and evacuated from Kuranji and Koto Tangah

Heavy rain flooded homes until residents climbed into their attics. There was no official information on fatalities on Saturday night.

A flash flood hit two districts in the city of Padang, West Sumatra, on Saturday, October 10, after heavy rain, according to Okezone, Kompas, CNBC Indonesia and Hariansib. The worst-hit areas were Kuranji, including Guo Lapau Munggu, and Koto Tangah, including Lumin Park and Kampung Apa. The Regional Disaster Management Agency (BPBD), together with the Rapid Reaction Team, the TNI and Polri, evacuated residents; Kompas reported that a number of residents had to climb into their attics to save themselves.

Provisional BPBD data as of 22:45 WIB, cited by Pikiran Lampung, put the number of affected residents at 498, of whom 238 had been evacuated, and at least 120 homes flooded; the figure comes from only one source and may change. Kompas noted that this flood again struck a location hit by a flash flood in November 2025. DailyDrop has not seen official information from Basarnas or the BPBD on deaths or missing persons.

The BMKG earlier issued a heavy-rain alert for West Sumatra and other provinces for October 6 to 9, according to Kontan MomsMoney. DailyDrop has not seen a new warning for Sunday; residents are advised to monitor the official BMKG and BPBD channels.

Trump vents fury over Nobel for Navi Pillay: Norway is said to have left an indelible stain

President Donald Trump responded to the Nobel Peace Prize for Navi Pillay with two posts on Truth Social, according to Axios, the Washington Post, Forbes and Fox News. On Friday night US time he wrote a long post of about 600 words questioning Pillay’s record, calling her clearly unfriendly to Israel, and calling that rejection an “indelible stain” on Norway, while listing eight wars he claims to have settled. On Saturday morning he wrote a short post that ended with “WOW!”.

Forbes noted that Trump also reshared a post saying the Nobel committee “has disgraced itself beyond repair”. JPost quoted Trump as saying there is “something wrong in Norway” and that the prize was given to “someone nobody has ever heard of”. Pillay replied calmly that world leaders have a legal obligation to protect human rights and to refrain from war, according to AP as cited in a search summary. Trump’s “eight wars” claim was judged exaggerated by several outlets; the Nobel committee did not respond to the posts in the sources DailyDrop saw.

Russian strike in Zaporizhzhia kills 12; Zelensky calls it Russia’s way of thanking the US for the diesel waiver

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky wrote on Saturday, October 10, that Russia “thanked” the United States for easing diesel export sanctions by dropping five guided glide bombs on residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia, according to Yahoo News, UNN and Interfax-Ukraine. Twelve people were killed, including three children, and 14 were wounded; Tribune India wrote that 13 were killed, so the casualty figure could still change. He said Moscow reads the easing of pressure as permission to keep bombarding cities.

A day earlier Trump announced a diesel deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin. According to Inquirer and JPost, Trump then called for Ukraine to get a new president, blamed Zelensky for US diesel prices and accused him of ignoring US requests not to attack Russian diesel refineries. JPost wrote that Trump was also considering a joint strike on the Houthis. The Kremlin has not responded to Zelensky’s accusation in the sources DailyDrop saw.

Police raid drug village in Makassar, 104 people arrested and 98 test positive for sabu

Police raided a drug village in Suangga subdistrict, Tallo district, Makassar, on Friday afternoon, October 9, and took 104 people into custody, according to Kompas, detik, Fajar, JPNN and Kompas TV. Of them, 98 tested positive for sabu in urine tests and six were negative. Three people with the initials P, A and W were named as suspected dealers. Police seized about 10 grams of sabu, Rp49.6 million in cash, 26 mobile phones and two scales.

The operation involved about 80 Makassar Polrestabes personnel and 25 Ditresnarkoba personnel from the Polda Sulsel. Several people tried to flee over the roof, and police fired warning shots after being pelted with stones. Kapolrestabes Arya Perdana said the site had been raided before last year and the barriers that were torn down had been rebuilt. President Prabowo on October 6 spoke of the drug village phenomenon and plans to change the name of the BNN, according to CNN Indonesia and CNBC Indonesia.

IRGC claims tanker hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz; US Central Command disables cargo ship trying to run the blockade

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on Saturday, October 10, according to Iranian state media cited by JPost, that an oil tanker exploded and caught fire after hitting a sea mine while passing through the Strait of Hormuz. DailyDrop has not found independent confirmation of the claim. US Central Command (CENTCOM) said it disabled a commercial cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman the same day after its crew ignored warnings and tried to break through the naval blockade of Iran.

The IRGC Navy, through the Tasnim news agency, said it controls the strait and will not allow “aggressor forces” in the area. JPost also wrote that Tehran and Washington exchanged proposals that could open the strait “within seven days”, but the terms and status of the talks remain unclear. US markets closed Friday with WTI up 0.39% at US$91.85, and the oil market can only respond when it opens on Monday.
