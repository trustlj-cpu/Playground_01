MSCI proposes downgrading Indonesia to a standalone market, IHSG closes down 11.57 percent in one day

MSCI announced its consultation on Thursday, October 15, and IHSG fell to 5,185 on Friday. On Sunday, House Budget Committee chair Said Abdullah said the scrutiny from MSCI was unfair.

MSCI, the global stock index provider, announced on Thursday, October 15, that it is opening a consultation on a proposal to move Indonesia’s classification from an emerging market to a standalone market. The release was cited by Katadata, Bisnis.com, and Kontan. The reasons MSCI gave were a still-opaque share ownership structure and alleged coordinated trading. The freeze on Indonesian stocks in MSCI indexes remains in force until further notice. MSCI stressed that this step is only the start of a formal consultation, not a final decision, and that market participants’ input will be heard. MSCI said the final decision will be announced by the end of June 2027 at the latest, while Katadata said the consultation period runs until April 30, 2027. Bisnis.com wrote that about 6.5 months remain in the consultation window, and Kontan reported that IHSG came under immediate pressure after the announcement. The Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX) said it respects MSCI’s independence in assessing and classifying markets, but will ask for further explanation of the basis for the announcement, pointing to the reforms already carried out, according to Katadata.

The market reacted on Friday, October 16. IHSG opened down 8.01 percent at 5,393 and trading was briefly halted, according to Katadata. The index closed at 5,185, down 11.57 percent or about 678 points, according to Kontan, Telusur, and several other outlets. CNBC Indonesia called it the deepest daily fall since the 1998 monetary crisis. On Sunday, October 18, Said Abdullah, chair of the Budget Committee of the House of Representatives (DPR), said the scrutiny from MSCI was unfair because the Financial Services Authority (OJK), IDX, and KSEI have been implementing the recommended improvements step by step, according to detikcom, Liputan6, and Viva. He pointed to FTSE Russell, which still places Indonesia in the Secondary Emerging Market group together with 13 other countries, such as China, India, the Philippines, Vietnam, and Saudi Arabia, and asked why the difference in assessment between the two institutions had not been explained. Said also said foreign investors bought Rp9.18 trillion worth of shares on the day IHSG fell. He said he does not reject correction and does not accuse an institution of MSCI’s stature of conspiring to push down IHSG, but judged that MSCI’s policy benefits hedge funds. MSCI is not a religious institution and its officials are not prophets, Said said, and Disway quoted him as asking MSCI not to monopolize the truth. Said also highlighted capital-market reforms and openness of share ownership data, which he said are already under way.

World · France✓ 6 outlets · arrest count still changing

Cost-of-living protests in France turn into riots, Limoges town hall burns and more than 2,000 people detained

Protests against rising living costs spread across France on Saturday, October 17, and turned into riots in several cities, according to i24NEWS, CNN, France 24, Al Jazeera, LBC, and CBC. France 24 reported that the Limoges town hall, a building inaugurated in 1883, was “largely gone” after being set on fire by a masked mob, prefect Maurice Barate said; the roof and bell tower collapsed. In Paris, crowds tried to break into Galeries Lafayette, damaged banks, and looted a Lacoste store and an SFR phone shop, according to Al Jazeera. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez put attendance at about 550,000, while the CGT union put it at 800,000, according to CNN and detikcom. The arrest figures rose over the weekend. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said about 1,200 people were arrested and more than 150 police officers injured on Saturday night, according to CBC and i24NEWS. On Sunday, Nunez said nearly 1,800 people were arrested, almost half of them in Paris, and more than 500 officers injured; LBC and APA later reported more than 2,000 people detained and more than 600 officers injured. President Emmanuel Macron said there is no place for violence in France and that looters will be prosecuted and punished, and he was scheduled to address the nation on Sunday evening, according to LBC and Al Jazeera. The government blamed the radical left and looters, according to CBC. Indonesia’s Foreign Ministry said no Indonesian citizens were involved so far, according to detikcom.

Politics · Diplomacy✓ 5 outlets · content of Monday’s speech not yet delivered

UN Secretary-General Guterres arrives in Jakarta and meets Prabowo at Hambalang, speech at the Foreign Ministry scheduled for Monday

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres arrived at Soekarno-Hatta Airport on Saturday night, October 17, and was welcomed with a state ceremony by Foreign Minister Arrmanatha Nasir, according to Suara.com, Kompas TV, Tempo, and Kontan. On Sunday, October 18, President Prabowo Subianto received him at his private residence in Hambalang, Bogor Regency, according to Republika, IDN Times, and Minanews. IDN Times said the talks covered Palestine and UN reform, while Tempo said the visit’s agenda included reform of the multilateral system, Agenda 2030, and the situation in the occupied Palestinian territories.

According to Republika and Media Indonesia, Guterres was scheduled to visit the TNI Peacekeeping Mission Center in Sentul to meet soldiers due to serve in the UN mission in the Central African Republic (MINUSCA), and to lay a wreath for the 50 Indonesian peacekeepers who died. On Monday, October 19, he is scheduled to give a speech on the future of multilateralism at the Foreign Ministry. There has been no reporting yet on the content of that speech or on any new Indonesian troop commitments.

Law · Regional corruption✓ 5 outlets · PDIP dismissal not yet formally issued

Klaten Regent Hamenang detained by KPK together with head of BPK Central Java, PDIP ready to dismiss him Monday

The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) arrested Klaten Regent Hamenang Wajar Ismoyo in a sting operation on Thursday, October 15, according to Bisnis.com, Metro TV, and RCTI+. On Friday, October 16, KPK named Hamenang and two others as suspects and detained him after about 11 hours of questioning, together with Ahmad Luthfi, head of BPK’s Central Java representative office, and private party M Amin Wahyu Utomo, according to RCTI+ and Bisnis.com. KPK seized about Rp1 billion by an initial count, and spokesperson Budi Prasetyo described an alleged payment from the Regent to BPK officials related to a financial audit.

From PDI Perjuangan, Komarudin Watubun said the party leadership (DPP) will issue a recommendation for dishonorable dismissal, scheduled for Monday, October 19, according to Republika, Tempo, and Sindonews. He said the party has repeatedly warned its cadres not to abuse office. Outlets differ in describing his status: Rmol wrote that Hamenang has already been “kicked out,” while Republika and Sindonews said the dismissal would only take place on Monday or next week.

Politics · Survey✓ 5 outlets · sample selection methodology not detailed

PPI survey: 65.4 percent of respondents satisfied with the Prabowo-Gibran government’s performance, 32.5 percent dissatisfied

Parameter Politik Indonesia (PPI) announced a survey evaluating two years of the Prabowo-Gibran government on Sunday, October 18. A total of 65.4 percent of respondents said they were satisfied, made up of 58.6 percent satisfied and 6.8 percent very satisfied, while 32.5 percent were dissatisfied and 2.1 percent did not answer or did not know, according to Liputan6, Suara Merdeka, Merdeka, and Satelit News. The survey was conducted from September 26 to October 6 through face-to-face interviews with 1,200 respondents in 38 provinces, with a 95 percent confidence level and a margin of error of about 2.83 percent.

Among those satisfied, the most common reasons were expanded social assistance (16.7 percent), the arrest of corruptors (10.3 percent), and the Sekolah Rakyat program (9.8 percent). Among those dissatisfied, the most common complaints were a difficult economy (11.8 percent), social assistance that is insufficient or poorly targeted (11.4 percent), and high prices of staple goods (10.4 percent). PPI Executive Director Adi Prayitno said the government needs to focus on poverty, economic conditions, food prices, corruption, and the difficulty of finding work in its second year.
