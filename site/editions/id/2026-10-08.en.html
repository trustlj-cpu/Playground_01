After the Constitutional Court ruling on Gibran’s diploma, PKB and PDIP politicians push for a special committee; parties have not yet taken an official stance

Impeachment needs a two-thirds vote in parliament. Golkar is waiting for a consensus among party leaders, and PKB says no decision has been made.

A day after the Constitutional Court (MK) declared the lawsuit over Vice President Gibran’s educational requirements inadmissible, political reactions have begun to emerge, according to CNN Indonesia, detik, IDN Times, Tirto and Sindonews. PKB’s Eka Widodo, a member of House of Representatives (DPR) Commission II, proposed a special committee (pansus) to follow up on the Court’s findings and raised the possibility of impeachment, detik said. PDIP, through Komarudin Watubun, suggested Gibran resign voluntarily, while PDIP’s Deddy Sitorus judged that Gibran’s candidacy had not met the requirements from the start.

Other parties were more cautious. Golkar said impeachment depends on a consensus among political party leaders, Demokrat respects the ruling and has not discussed a parliamentary inquiry (hak angket), and Berandasulsel wrote that Gerindra asked for the controversy to be stopped. IDN Times noted that PKB has not decided anything on a pansus. Tirto explained that impeaching a vice president requires the support of two-thirds of parliament, so the proposal is still far from an official process.

Experts quoted by Hukumonline and UMY warned that the Court’s findings sit in the reasoning, not the operative part, so the ruling does not automatically mean Gibran’s candidacy is invalid. DailyDrop has not seen an official statement from Gibran or the Palace during this edition’s coverage period.

Prabowo summons 800 senior TNI-Polri officers: no sectoral ego, keep the people’s trust

President Prabowo Subianto gave a closed-door briefing to about 800 senior TNI and Polri officers at the presidential palace complex on Wednesday, October 7, according to Kompas, CNN Indonesia, detik and Antara. The directives were delivered by Cabinet Secretary Teddy Indra Wijaya: strengthen cohesion and operational readiness, do not put sectoral ego first, uphold integrity, and remember their role as a people’s army and a community police force that quickly helps citizens.

Coordinating Minister for Politics and Security Dudung Abdurachman said Prabowo instructed that future recruitment of TNI and Polri personnel put more emphasis on intellectual ability, not only physical. Utut Adianto of PDIP, chair of House Commission I, judged the briefing a consolidation after the replacement of several senior officers, according to detik; that is a personal interpretation, not an official Palace statement.

Foreign-exchange reserves dip to US$146.3 billion in September, enough for 5.3 months of imports

Bank Indonesia (BI) reported that Indonesia’s foreign-exchange reserves at the end of September 2026 stood at US$146.3 billion, down slightly from US$146.5 billion at the end of August, according to Pasardana, Kontan, Investortrust and RCTI+. The release came on Wednesday, October 7. BI Executive Director of the Communications Department Ramdan Denny Prakoso said the position was mainly influenced by the government’s drawdown of foreign loans and by tax and service receipts, while maturing government foreign-debt payments and rupiah stabilization policy weighed on it.

The reserves are equivalent to financing of about 5.3 months of imports, or 5.2 months of imports and government foreign-debt payments, far above the international adequacy standard of about three months of imports. RCTI+ wrote 5.4 months, and one Bisnis headline wrote US$146.5 billion although the article body says US$146.3 billion, so DailyDrop uses the figure used by most sources. The figure is lower than US$156.47 billion at the end of 2025 and US$148.74 billion in September 2025.

Jakarta Composite Index falls 0.75% to 6,146.72, ending a three-day rise; energy sector under most pressure

The Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) closed down about 46 points, or 0.75%, at 6,146.72 on Wednesday, October 7, ending three straight days of gains, according to Pasardana, Bisnis, Metro TV and Media Asuransi. The index briefly touched 6,214.76 before sliding and closed near the daily low of 6,136.19, Bloomberg Technoz said. A total of 390 stocks fell, 280 rose and 293 were unchanged.

The energy sector fell 1.56%, while financials edged up 0.02%. WIFI, BRPT and GOTO weakened, while INDF, MEDC and TLKM strengthened, according to Pasardana and Bisnis. Media Asuransi recorded turnover of about Rp36.19 trillion, far larger than Rp9 trillion to Rp10 trillion on previous days; DailyDrop cannot yet confirm whether that figure includes negotiated trades.

The Atlantic: White House asks Pentagon to prepare strike options on Iran before US midterm elections

The Atlantic reported on Wednesday, October 7, US time, citing administration officials, that the White House has asked the Pentagon to prepare options for strikes on Iran that could be carried out before the US midterm elections on November 3. The scale and targets are still under discussion, the report said, and it is unclear whether the administration will go ahead. A White House official said ‘all options’ remain open to President Donald Trump, according to an energy-media summary that quoted the official.

The report contradicts the common assumption that Trump would hold back from escalation before the elections. DailyDrop has not seen official confirmation from the Pentagon or the White House, and the oil market had not shown a reaction when this edition went to press. For Indonesian readers, the risk lies in oil prices, the exchange rate and foreign fund flows, not in direct changes to services.
