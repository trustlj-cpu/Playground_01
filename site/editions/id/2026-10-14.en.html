Sumatra hotspots jump to 3,663 and smoke spreads to Papua; Prabowo sends 200 pumps to Tanjung Puting, national disaster status not yet declared

The President was told that land still burning is down to just 639 hectares, far below what he had assumed. More than 60,000 personnel have been deployed, but poor visibility hampers aerial firefighting and 1,673 flights have been disrupted since August.

Indonesia’s forest and land fires, known as karhutla, entered Tuesday, October 13 with heavier numbers in Sumatra. The Meteorology, Climatology and Geophysics Agency (BMKG) detected 3,663 hotspots on the island of Sumatra, a jump in under 24 hours, most of them in South Sumatra, according to Antara, detikNews and Liputan6. Hotspots only mark possible fire; they do not measure how large a fire is. The area figure comes from the National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB): land still burning is down to 639.23 hectares, the largest part in Tanjung Puting National Park, Central Kalimantan, according to detikNews, Republika and Liputan6.

A figure that small startled the President. IDNFinancials wrote that Prabowo Subianto was surprised while chairing the forest-fire meeting because the burning area was only that size, and Tempo wrote that he had at first thought the fire was still sweeping across thousands of hectares. The head of BNPB, who gave him the report, said so, according to Liputan6. Prabowo then made Tanjung Puting a special focus (detikNews, RRI, MetroTV). When the regional military commander (Pangdam) asked for 50 water pumps, he said he would send 200, according to detikNews and Liputan6, and Suara.com and Indopolitika wrote that he was preparing Rp2 billion for 200 water-drilling points. He also wants more forest rangers and the TNI and police to help train them, according to several outlets; some put the target at 70,000 people.

On the ground, more than 60,000 combined personnel have been deployed for ground operations in Central Kalimantan and South Sumatra, said Cabinet Secretary Teddy, according to Republika and Antara. The obstacle is in the air. BNPB acknowledged that poor visibility hampers aerial firefighting (Republika), and the Forestry Ministry asked for more air support because the fire in Tanjung Puting is spreading, according to Liputan6. The Environment Minister insisted that every resource is at work and that it is untrue the government is negligent, said Antara and Liputan6. Antara noted the President prioritized a dry ice trial in South Sumatra to disperse the smoke, while Liputan6 quoted an expert that peatland is simply hard to extinguish.

The smoke is already far from the fire. A total of 1,673 flights have been delayed or cancelled since August because of the fires, according to detikNews and Republika; Republika added that the Transportation Ministry is overseeing airport operations. In the east, haze blanketed Timika with visibility of 300 meters and disrupted flights (Republika), and students in South Papua studied online (detikNews, Liputan6). Liputan6 wrote that the air in Singapore and Malaysia is also unhealthy. Malaysia raised the haze issue at ASEAN while acknowledging that Indonesia’s efforts are already maximal, said Antara, and Republika wrote that Malaysia is ready to help.

The national disaster status has not changed. The government says the fires do not yet have that status, according to Tempo. The DPR is urging it be declared (Liputan6), while the Central Kalimantan Governor has not proposed it because such a status cannot be handled casually, said detikNews. The DPD chair asked the government to study foreign assistance, and Komnas HAM asked for the recovery of victims and transparency in law enforcement (Antara).

For readers, the useful measure is not the number of meetings. Kompas.com wrote that the President received a report that the fires will improve in mid-October; that estimate depends on rain, and DailyDrop has not seen BMKG’s basis for it. Residents of Sumatra, Kalimantan and Papua should monitor air quality and local government advisories, and passengers should check schedules with their airline. What to watch today: whether the number of hotspots falls, whether the 639 hectares truly shrinks, and whether the visibility problem turns into real aerial firefighting.

KPK arrests 14 people in sting at BNPB; about Rp1.4 billion and gold seized, case linked to disaster ready-to-use funds

The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) ran a sting operation (OTT) at BNPB on Tuesday, October 13, KPK’s 22nd OTT of 2026, according to Antara. KPK secured 14 people and seized about Rp1.4 billion in cash and gold, said Antara and Republika; Republika briefly wrote Rp14 billion before the figure appeared as Rp1.4 billion. Antara quoted KPK that the case concerns procurement and disaster ready-to-use funds, and detikNews wrote that the target was the BNPB Principal Secretary (Sestama) together with others.

BNPB said it will cooperate, said Republika. A KPK deputy confirmed the OTT and said a case presentation (ekspose) was held that night (Antara); the names and legal status of the 14 people are not yet in the reports we checked. Republika wrote in its headline that the money came from forest-fire response funds, but only one outlet has linked it so far, so DailyDrop marks it as unconfirmed.

The case matters because BNPB sits at the center of the forest-fire response now under scrutiny; Liputan6 reported on Tuesday that the BNPB head told the President about the 639 hectares of burning land. Ready-to-use funds are money set aside so it can be disbursed quickly during a disaster, so any alleged irregularity will be read by the public as a threat to victims. Republika briefly wrote that those secured were being kept secret. An OTT is not a guilty verdict; wait for KPK’s official statement on suspects and the construction of the case.

Houthis hit Aramco headquarters in Dhahran and Riyadh; Riyadh airport closed until October 17, Indonesian umrah pilgrims delayed

The Houthis widened their attacks to Saudi Arabia on Tuesday, October 13. Reuters wrote that explosions were heard near Saudi Aramco’s headquarters in Dhahran, and Bloomberg reported that the headquarters was hit. Saudi state news agency, citing an energy ministry official via FinancialJuice, said the fire was limited with no casualties; an Iranian media claim that the headquarters was evacuated is unconfirmed. In Riyadh, civil defense reported four people injured by fragments of an intercepted missile and light damage to 15 homes (FinancialJuice), and detikNews wrote that Saudi Arabia intercepted cruise missiles and drones aimed at the capital. The same day, the Saudi-led coalition struck a fuel depot near Sanaa that it said the Houthis were using, according to Reuters, BBC and Guardian, while Republika wrote that the Houthis claimed their attack was retaliation.

The fallout spread to flights and pilgrims. The closure of Riyadh airport was extended until October 17 (detikNews), Gulf Air postponed its Riyadh route until the same date and Qatar Airways temporarily halted flights to Riyadh and Dammam (Antara). A total of 53 prospective umrah pilgrims from Makassar were delayed, said detikNews and Republika. Indonesia condemned the attacks on civilian infrastructure, the UN called for an investigation into the attack on the airport and China condemned it (Antara). Egypt said it is ready to support Saudi Arabia (Antara), while detikNews and Republika wrote that Israel offered help against the Houthis and that Saudi Arabia was said to be unwilling to accept it. Reuters wrote that Saudi warning systems stayed silent before the airport attack that caused casualties. Trump accused the Houthis of deliberately pushing up oil prices ahead of the US midterm elections (Reuters); Brent closed at US$105.69, down 0.9 percent, according to FinancialJuice.

Fuel queues in Bali not easing: Koster says residents are panic buying, Pertamina opens stations 24 hours; government denies subsidized prices will rise

Queues for fuel (BBM) in Bali had not eased on Tuesday, October 13. DetikNews reported that Pertalite ran out in Klungkung and Denpasar residents were willing to wait until night even though the pumps at fuel stations were empty. Governor Koster judged that residents fear running out and so are all filling up until stocks are gone, said detikNews, and asked residents to stay calm because supply will soon return to normal, according to Antara.

Pertamina is speeding up the recovery. Republika wrote that additional tankers were brought in to top up Pertalite and Biosolar; Antara reported that fuel stations (SPBU) are open 24 hours in four regencies and police were deployed to keep the queues in order, while detikNews said 112 tons of fuel are scheduled to arrive on Nusa Penida. DPR members and a Bali consumer foundation asked for the distribution pattern to be evaluated (Antara). Republika wrote that the government is being urged to take emergency steps and that the national stock guarantee is being questioned. The Deputy Finance Minister made clear there will be no increase in subsidized fuel prices, according to Antara and Republika. Outside Bali, Antara reported that Lampung tightened subsidized diesel filling as queues grew. DailyDrop has not seen a detailed explanation of why supply in Bali was disrupted in the first place; what is clear from the reports is that queues were still forming at a number of stations until Tuesday night.

Pope Leo XIV diagnosed with lung cancer after surgery to remove part of his right lung

Pope Leo XIV was diagnosed with lung cancer after undergoing surgery to remove part of his right lung, according to AP News and Politico. Reuters earlier reported that the Pope was scheduled to enter hospital on Tuesday, October 13 for an operation on a nodule in his lung, and Antara reported he underwent lung surgery in Rome. The early reports mentioned only a nodule, a small lump in the lung that is often benign; the cancer diagnosis emerged only after the operation.

DailyDrop has not seen the stage, treatment plan or recovery schedule in the available sources, and will not guess at them. The news is widely reported by international media, so an official Vatican statement will be the next reference point.

So separate the three things now circulating: the operation has been done, the cancer diagnosis has been reported, while the stage and prognosis are not known. The faithful and readers should wait for an official statement before sharing news about the Pope’s condition.
