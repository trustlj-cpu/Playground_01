Prabowo convenes ministers, TNI commander and police chief over forest and land fires; firefighting focused on Tanjung Puting, national disaster status not yet declared

A limited meeting at the Palace on Monday, October 12 ordered all resources deployed, with priority on Central Kalimantan and South Sumatra. The government considers itself still capable; one House of Representatives (DPR) member called for national disaster status.

President Prabowo Subianto held a limited meeting on forest and land fires (karhutla) at the Palace on Monday, October 12, according to CNBC Indonesia, CNN Indonesia, Kumparan and Suara.com. Coordinating Minister for Politics and Security Dudung Abdurachman said the meeting involved several ministries and agencies, including the TNI commander, the National Police chief (Kapolri), the Forestry Ministry and the National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB); some participants joined by Zoom. Dudung said the President is very serious about handling the problem, said CNBC Indonesia.

Kumparan wrote that the President asked for the response to be strengthened and firefighting sped up. According to Antara and detikNews, Prabowo ordered firefighting to be focused, among other places, on Tanjung Puting National Park in Central Kalimantan, and made Central Kalimantan and South Sumatra priorities. Antara added that the President asked South Sumatra to be the site of a trial of dry ice, to disperse the haze. Coordinating Minister Dudung also said firefighters will be rotated so they do not become exhausted, according to Antara, which added that Prabowo praised the TNI, the police and the volunteers already working in the field. The message to be sent is clear: the Palace wants to be seen in control, after weeks of complaints about smoke from residents and from neighboring countries.

The government has not declared the fires a national disaster, according to detikNews and Kompas.com; Dudung said the government is still able to handle them. House Commission IV member Daniel Johan, for his part, called for that status because the fires have burned for three months, said Antara. Home Affairs Minister Tito Karnavian asked regional heads and the regional leadership forum (Forkopimda) to firmly ban land burning, and Forestry Minister Raja Juli Antoni is preparing sanctions for five to six more companies, according to Antara. The Jambi Regional Police are handling 18 reports and have named 16 suspects, said Antara.

The Forestry Ministry has also stepped in. Antara wrote that Minister Raja Juli Antoni went to South Kalimantan and reminded people that the fire alert period still has one month left, and said the number of forest rangers will be raised to 70,000. The Environment Ministry is designing funding so that residents do not clear land by burning, said Antara, and BNPB warned that northern Sumatra should stay alert for floods even though it is in drought.

The smoke has crossed borders. Kompas.com wrote that other countries have protested the smoke from the fires, and Dudung replied that peatland is hard to put out. BMKG reported that the spread of smoke from South Sumatra extended as far as the Bangka Strait, and Antara noted that Sultan Mahmud Badaruddin II Airport in Palembang is operating normally again. On aid from Malaysia, Antara ran two reports with different tones, one saying it had not been discussed and one saying it was under discussion, so its status is not yet clear.

For readers, what matters is results on the ground, not the meeting. A Palace-level meeting speeds up coordination, but burning peat is hard to put out and rain is the biggest help. What is not yet visible in the available sources: a target for the area to be extinguished, the budget, and the government’s criteria for raising the status to national disaster. Residents of Sumatra and Kalimantan need to monitor air quality and regional government advisories, and passengers passing through affected airports would do well to check schedules with their airlines. The coming weeks will show whether the Palace’s order really lowers the number of hotspots, or merely adds to the number of meetings.

IHSG plunges 2.59% to 5,936, falling out of the 6,000 level; rupiah closes weaker at Rp17,913

The IHSG closed down 157.61 points, or 2.59 percent, at 5,936 on Monday, October 12, according to Okezone, iNews and CNN Indonesia, after opening up 15.56 points at 6,109, said Antara. Antara wrote that the decline was triggered by rising global uncertainty; market observer Elandry Pratama cited a combination of global and domestic sentiment without elaborating. CNN Indonesia counted 602 stocks down and Okezone 632 (the difference in the counts has not been explained), with a trading value of about Rp13.6 trillion according to Okezone. Antara and Media Indonesia briefly wrote 6,936, a figure that does not fit the size of the decline, so DailyDrop uses 5,936.

The rupiah closed at Rp17,913 per US dollar, weaker by 14 points or 0.08 percent, according to Antara, Media Indonesia, iNews and IDN Times. Antara linked it to a possible Fed interest rate hike; analyst Ibrahim Assuaibi added Trump’s statement on the Russia–Ukraine energy ceasefire. Reuters reported that the dollar held near an 18-month high at the start of the week. The IHSG is now below 6,000 before Monday’s Wall Street session has ended; its close appears only after this edition.

The external backdrop is mixed. Wall Street rose on Friday, October 9: the S&P 500 gained 0.59 percent to 7,811.54, the Nasdaq 0.64 percent to 27,366.17 and the Dow 0.83 percent to 51,654.95. WTI oil closed at US$91.85 (+0.39%) and gold at US$4,216.3 (+1.43%). Liputan6 quoted the Home Affairs Minister as wary of rising oil prices after the Houthi attack. For readers who hold stocks or shop for imports, those two things, oil and the exchange rate, are what show up fastest in the wallet.

Talk of a hak angket and impeachment of Gibran gains strength in the DPR; parties feel each other out, no official decision yet

Talk of impeaching Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka, rooted in the controversy over his diploma, kept rolling in the DPR on Monday, October 12. Detikcom wrote that the parties hold varied views: PKS said it is sounding out the best conditions, while PAN is still studying the Constitutional Court (MK) ruling. Golkar politician Ahmad Doli Kurnia received an audience with the AKSI group, which asked for a hak angket, and said the proposal would be considered, according to detikNews and Kompas.id.

A hak angket is the DPR’s right to investigate government policy or important events. According to observer Jamiluddin Ritonga, cited by BangsaOnline and Warta Ekonomi, a proposal must be submitted by at least 25 members from more than one faction and approved by a plenary session, so PDIP cannot go ahead alone; he considers the move difficult without Gerindra’s blessing. Separately, volunteers announced that Gibran will show an A Level certificate equivalent to senior high school, according to CNN Indonesia and Republika; that is a statement by volunteers, not by Gibran himself.

DailyDrop has not seen an official DPR decision, and we have not set out the full content of the MK ruling because only the headline and summary were available. For readers, the road to impeachment is long: under the 1945 Constitution, the DPR states an opinion, the MK examines, then the People’s Consultative Assembly (MPR) decides. What decides it is whether Gerindra and the other big parties are willing to open that road.

Persib U-18 bus attacked at Batang toll road rest area; police arrest one suspect, PSSI and I.League condemn

A convoy of the Persib Bandung U-18 team was attacked by a group of people at Rest Area KM 360 on the Trans-Java Toll Road, Batang Regency, Central Java, on the night of Sunday, October 11, according to Republika, Antara and detikcom. Republika wrote that the bus windows were smashed and suitcases looted; Liputan6 wrote that an official was beaten by a group. Police secured a man with the initials BPA (33) suspected of being the perpetrator, said Antara, and Republika quoted the Batang City Police criminal investigation chief (Kasatreskrim) as saying the attackers were Persija supporters.

PSSI strongly condemned the attack, and Secretary General Yunus Nusi said there is no tolerance for the perpetrators, according to Republika. I.League, APPI, Bandung Mayor Muhammad Farhan, the West Java DPRD and Persib management also condemned it, said Antara; Persija asked that the case be pursued fully. The group had just taken part in the Elite Pro Academy (EPA) tournament, a youth competition. DailyDrop has not seen a police explanation of the number of perpetrators and the full motive.

Houthi attack on Riyadh still ripples to Indonesia: umrah postponed, Ministry of Hajj and Umrah urges caution, Bahlil says energy logistics disrupted

The impact of the Houthi attack on King Khalid Airport in Riyadh has spilled over to Indonesian pilgrims. Republika wrote that a number of umrah organizers (PPIU) postponed departures and the Association of Umrah and Hajj Organizers (HIMPUH) highlighted flight cancellations to Saudi Arabia; Antara reported that the departure of pilgrims from South Sulawesi was delayed, and Reuters wrote that airlines cancelled flights to Saudi Arabia. The Ministry of Hajj and Umrah (Kemenhaj) asked Indonesian citizens and pilgrims to raise their vigilance; pilgrims in Makkah and Madinah were reported relatively safe, said Republika.

Coordinating Minister for Downstreaming and Energy Transition and Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM) Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said the Saudi–Houthi conflict is worsening energy logistics worldwide, according to Antara and Republika. Reuters reported that a number of top energy executives were struck from the schedule of the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh, while Saudi Arabia went ahead with the event. AP wrote that Saudi Arabia is investigating why its defense system failed to prevent the attack.
