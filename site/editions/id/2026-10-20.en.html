National police chief: a corporation from a neighboring country is involved in forest fire cases, Malaysia offers help

National Police Chief Suyudi Ario Seto said foreign parties are involved in 98 corporate cases that have moved to the formal investigation stage, while Malaysia has offered helicopters and proposed ASEAN-level cooperation.

National Police Chief (Kapolri) Gen. Suyudi Ario Seto said on Monday (October 19), after a meeting with President Prabowo Subianto at the Merdeka Palace, that a foreign party in the form of a corporation is involved in forest and land fire (karhutla) cases. According to detikNews, Kompas.id, and TV One, the Criminal Investigation Agency (Bareskrim) of the Indonesian National Police (Polri) has raised 98 corporate cases to the formal investigation stage. Suyudi confirmed that the foreign party came from a neighboring country, but did not name the country or the company because investigators are still sorting out which cases involve foreign investment and which do not. He said Polri will soon announce suspects, including company owners and their shareholders. Earlier, iNews reported a Bareskrim update as of October 13: 273 police reports, 223 suspects across 12 regional police commands (Polda), and 5,157.89 hectares of burned land. Suyudi has served as National Police Chief since the handover from Listyo Sigit Prabowo on September 2, according to detikNews.

On the regional side, Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said on Facebook on October 18 that Malaysia is ready to help Indonesia put out the fires and proposed an ASEAN-level cooperation mechanism for transboundary haze, according to detikNews and Bernama. Malay Mail and Bernama reported that the offer of water-bombing helicopters still stands, while Antara quoted Dudung as saying Malaysia sent helicopters and personnel. Antara also reported that Anwar acknowledged the Indonesian government’s seriousness in handling karhutla. Detik Health noted that schools in Malaysia were again temporarily closed for five days because of the haze. In Indonesia, the Transportation Ministry (Kemenhub) recorded 2,458 flights affected by smoke, according to Antara. Al Jazeera reported on October 9 that Malaysia had already closed schools because of smoke from fires in Indonesia.

IHSG falls again below 5,000 after MSCI proposes standalone status for Indonesia

The Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) came under pressure again on Monday (October 19) after MSCI opened a consultation on October 15 on downgrading Indonesia from an emerging market to a standalone market. Bisnis.com and Bareksa reported that the IHSG closed at 5,185.10 on Friday (October 16), down 11.57 percent in a single day after a trading halt by the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX). On Monday the index at one point fell to about 4,912, according to Pintu News, and Tempo recorded 4,923 at the end of the first session. Antara reported that the rupiah weakened to Rp18,046 per US dollar, and IDX lowered its lower auto-reject limit (ARB) to 7 percent to keep volatility in check, according to Antara and detikFinance. MSCI’s reasons include shareholding transparency, the reliability of free float data, and law enforcement over alleged coordinated transactions. MSCI’s final decision is scheduled for the end of June 2027, cited by CNBC Indonesia. Bank Indonesia holds a board of governors meeting on October 21, and Republika reported that BCA is running a share buyback program through March 2027.

UN Secretary-General Guterres meets Prabowo at Hambalang and stays at the president’s residence

UN Secretary-General António Guterres arrived in Indonesia on Saturday night (October 17) and was received by President Prabowo at the Hambalang residence, Bogor, on Sunday (October 18). Antara and detikNews reported that Guterres wore a batik with the parang motif and was welcomed by children in traditional dress. The Cabinet Secretariat said the bilateral meeting covered strategic issues and strengthening multilateral cooperation, and that Guterres spent the night at Hambalang, according to detikNews. Koran Jakarta and Maklumat said the agenda included reform of multilateralism, climate change, and Palestine. On Monday Antara reported that Guterres called Indonesia a pillar of multilateralism and peace, said batik offers a lesson for multilateralism, and appreciated the contribution of Indonesian peacekeepers. His term ends in December 2026, and Monday’s schedule includes a visit to the TNI peacekeeping training center in Sentul.

KPK detains Klaten Regent Hamenang Wajar Ismoyo after sting operation linked to BPK audit

The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) named Klaten Regent Hamenang Wajar Ismoyo a suspect and detained him after a sting operation (OTT) on October 15, according to metrotvnews and justisio. The reports said 20 people were secured, 19 in Klaten and one in Jakarta, and that Hamenang was questioned for about 11 hours. KPK spokesperson Budi Prasetyo said the case involves officials’ receipt of payments tied to goods and services procurement and permits, plus alleged payments to personnel of the Audit Board (BPK) tied to a regional government audit. Liputan6 reported that KPK is probing the role of the regent’s younger sibling behind a blue barrel containing Rp934 million, while some other reports put the seizure at about Rp1.9 billion. DetikNews noted that Hamenang is the second Klaten regent arrested by KPK after Sri Hartini.

Muhammadiyah condemns Trump’s joke about the name Mohammad at Georgia campaign rally

Muhammadiyah Central Board Chair Anwar Abbas condemned US President Donald Trump’s joke about the name Mohammad, according to detikNews and CNN Indonesia on Monday (October 19). Speaking at a campaign rally in Dalton, Georgia, on Saturday (October 17), Trump said that if you are unsure about the name of a person from the Middle East, just call him Mohammad, detikNews quoted from Reuters. Anwar judged the remark to be more than a joke because it contains an element of insult to Islam and the Prophet Muhammad, and called it hurtful to Muslims. The US midterm elections take place on November 3. Suara.com and Maklumat also reported similar reactions from Indonesian figures, including Jusuf Kalla according to Pelita Karawang.
