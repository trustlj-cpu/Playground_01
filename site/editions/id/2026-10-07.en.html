Constitutional Court declares challenge over Gibran’s education requirement inadmissible, but finds no proof of a high-school-equivalent diploma

The petitioner was found to have no legal standing and Gibran was not disqualified. The petitioner’s lawyer says the finding opens the way to impeachment.

The Constitutional Court (MK) declared the petition in Case No. 01/PHPU.PRES-XXIV/2026 on the educational requirement of Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka inadmissible, according to Kompas, Hukumonline, Investortrust and detik. The ruling hearing took place on Tuesday, October 6; one report gives a different date, but most sources use October 6. The panel found that the petitioner had no legal standing because the petitioner was not a participant in the 2024 presidential election (Pilpres), since under Constitutional Court Regulation No. 2 of 2024 only candidate pairs may file this kind of dispute.

Although the case failed on procedure, the ruling’s reasoning says the Court found no convincing evidence that Gibran holds a foreign diploma equivalent to senior high school; the document attached is said to be only an attendance statement (certificate of attendance), according to Hukumonline and detik. The Court said it could not annul or disqualify Gibran because he was already sworn in on October 20, 2024. Experts quoted by UMY and Hukumonline cautioned that findings in the reasoning are not the operative part of the ruling, so they do not automatically mean his candidacy was invalid.

The petitioner’s lawyer, Bambang Widjojanto, said the ruling opens the way to further legal and political steps, including impeachment, according to Hukumonline. DailyDrop has not seen a full official response from Gibran or the Palace during this edition’s reporting period.

Prabowo witnesses Satgas PKH hand over Rp13.2 trillion and 259,000 hectares of forest area to the state

President Prabowo Subianto witnessed the handover of money and land by the Forest Area Enforcement Task Force (Satgas PKH) at the Attorney General’s Office (Kejaksaan Agung) in Jakarta on Tuesday, October 6, according to IDN Times, Kumparan, Media Indonesia and iNews. Attorney General ST Burhanuddin handed about Rp13.2 trillion to the Finance Minister and the Forestry Minister, made up of forestry administrative fines of about Rp3.48 trillion, the Attorney General’s Office’s non-tax state revenue (PNBP) of about Rp1.54 trillion and tax revenue of about Rp8.23 trillion. Kumparan rounded the figure slightly differently.

The handover also covered the retaking of control of 259,230.73 hectares of forest area. Prabowo called this the eighth handover, with a total of about Rp52 trillion in about two years, according to Inilah.com and Indoposco, and said the corruptors’ resistance is not light. At the event he also said he would rename the National Narcotics Agency (BNN) the National Narcotics Control Agency (Badan Nasional Penanggulangan Narkotika), according to CNN Indonesia and CNBC Indonesia.

IHSG rises 1.21% to 6,192.93, second straight day of gains after a deep correction

The Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) closed up 74.07 points, or 1.21%, at 6,192.93 on Tuesday, October 6, according to Pasardana. The day before, the index rose 1.36% to 6,118.86. Bloomberg Technoz recorded a daily range of 6,120.2 to 6,216.4. Metro TV and BCA Sekuritas wrote about 6,195 and a gain of 1.11%, a gap that probably comes from preliminary reports, so DailyDrop uses the Pasardana figures.

The transportation sector rose 3.16%, technology 3.09% and energy 2.67%. TRUE shares jumped 34.78% to Rp62. Over two days the index gained about 2.6% from 6,036.89 on Friday, but Kontan recorded the IHSG at 6,678.20 on September 9, so the index is still about 7% below that level.

Houthi expand attacks to Saudi Arabia; Yemeni forces retake Mocha, Turkey and Pakistan ready to send troops

Yemeni government forces, backed by air strikes from the Saudi-led coalition, retook towns near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, including Mocha, according to Democracy Now and Al Jazeera on October 6. Critical Threats recorded that the Houthi widened the scale of their missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports and oil refineries on October 5. Turkish and Pakistani officials said they would soon send troops to Saudi Arabia under a defense pact signed in August, according to Drop Site.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the US had anticipated the retaliatory attack without detailing coordination, according to WNG. CBS News recorded that President Donald Trump was weighing whether the US should join the fight against the Houthi after the attack on the Saudi airport. On the other hand, Trump said the US was moving its bombers from the RAF Fairford base because of ‘several threats’, and defended the attack on Iran by calling high gasoline prices ‘a small price to pay’. Iran’s president called talks with the US ‘futile’. For Indonesia, the risk lies in oil prices and the exchange rate.

Brent closes at US$100.58, rupiah about Rp17,899; Wall Street up 0.58% and gold up 0.73%

Brent crude closed up 26 cents, or about 0.3%, at US$100.58 a barrel on Tuesday, while WTI rose 1 cent to US$89.44, according to Kabar Bursa citing Reuters; DailyDrop found only one outlet for that Brent figure. The move was flat after a fall the day before because OPEC+ held back November production and the G7 plans to release strategic reserves.

The rupiah closed at about Rp17,899 per US dollar according to Sumbar Bisnis (about 0.1% stronger from Rp17,917) and Rp17,901 according to Databoks; the daily direction differs because the earlier reference point differs. The S&P 500 rose 0.58% to 7,818.93 and gold rose 0.73% to US$4,187.1 according to market closing data.
