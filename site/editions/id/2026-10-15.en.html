KPK nabs 14 people in sting at BNPB, including its principal secretary; Rustian not detained, alleged misuse of ready-to-use funds traced back to 2022

The operation on Tuesday, October 13 targeted the agency that leads disaster response, amid forest fires that have not subsided. DailyDrop has not seen an announcement of suspects, and the value of the seized money still differs between reports.

The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) ran a sting operation (OTT) at the National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB) on Tuesday, October 13 and secured 14 people, according to Antara, Republika and Liputan6. Antara wrote that the evidence secured ranged from cash to gold. Republika put it at Rp1.4 billion and gold bars, but that outlet’s initial headline briefly carried Rp14 billion; DailyDrop uses the figure Republika repeated and marks it as not yet backed by a second source. A KPK deputy confirmed the operation and said a case conference would be held that night, said Antara.

BNPB Principal Secretary (sestama) Rustian was also secured: Republika wrote that KPK confirmed he was picked up along with 13 others. On Wednesday, October 14, Antara and Republika reported that Rustian was released and not detained; we have not seen the reason explained. On the funds under investigation, the sources differ in tone. Republika linked the case to the budget for handling forest fires, detikNews laid out the rules on ready-to-use disaster funds at BNPB, and Antara wrote that KPK is probing alleged misuse of ready-to-use funds since 2022. The three do not necessarily point to the same funds.

The sequence of reports helps explain why figures and names briefly changed. Republika carried news of the OTT on the night of Tuesday, October 13, Antara quoted the KPK deputy after midnight, Republika confirmed the involvement of the principal secretary only on Wednesday morning, and Rustian’s release appeared on Wednesday afternoon. One regional outlet, Suara Landak, wrote that the operation took place in Yogyakarta; other outlets gave no location, so we do not confirm it.

BNPB said it respects the legal process and ensures services to the public continue, said Antara, which added that the agency acknowledged that several rooms were sealed after the operation. On Wednesday BNPB also named Central Kalimantan and South Sumatra as forest-fire priorities, with 663.61 hectares handled, according to Antara. Reactions came from many directions: House members urged an evaluation of the oversight of disaster funds (Antara), Haedar Nashir, general chairman of the Muhammadiyah central leadership, hoped KPK would see the investigation through (Antara), and Mahfud MD said he was deeply saddened and judged that the nation is sick (Republika).

The timing is awkward. Liputan6 and Republika both noted that the arrests came while Indonesia was besieged by forest fires and thick smoke blanketed Sumatra and Kalimantan. BNPB attended the President’s limited cabinet meeting on forest fires on Monday, October 12, which we wrote up in our October 13 edition; a day later KPK came to the agency. It is the national disaster-response coordinator, and ready-to-use funds are designed to be disbursed quickly in emergencies, so oversight is always racing against the need for speed; that is what makes the alleged misappropriation feel more painful.

What remains unclear from the sources we have seen: who has been named a suspect, the value of the state’s loss, and whether the seized money came from forest-fire funds or from ready-to-use funds in general. The report that the younger brother and a confidant of the BNPB head were also arrested has so far been carried only by Republika, with the word “reportedly,” so we placed it in the unverified column. Today, watch for KPK’s announcement, Rustian’s status, and whether the House really summons the BNPB leadership or the evaluation stops at statements.

Blast at Cimanggis Market in South Tangerang kills two; police cite ethylene gas, not a bomb

An explosion shook Cimanggis Market in Ciputat, South Tangerang, on the night of Wednesday, October 14; the first report appeared around 21:00 WIB and we have not seen the exact time of the incident. Detikcom reported that two people were killed, buried under rubble, while Republika wrote that two body bags were evacuated and preliminary data counted 12 casualties. Residents described a very loud boom, “like a bomb” according to Republika and one that felt as if it lifted the body according to detikcom. The first report of a body appeared on Republika around 22:00 WIB, while detikcom wrote of two dead after midnight.

Police sent the Brimob Gegana bomb squad and bomb technicians, said Republika, and detikNews wrote that the Jakarta Regional Police (Polda Metro Jaya) sent a Gegana team to inspect the site. Detikcom reported that police found ethylene gas and confirmed the blast was not a bomb; the site turned out to be a fruit storage facility. Ethylene is commonly used to ripen fruit and is flammable at certain concentrations, but police have not explained how the gas built up or ignited. Republika wrote that police are also looking into the possibility that the blast originated in a supply warehouse of an SPPG, a kitchen of the free nutritious meals program, but that link has not been confirmed by a second outlet.

DailyDrop has not seen an explanation from police on the exact cause, the source of the fire, or the condition of the building. The casualty count may change and should be read as provisional; we have not seen details on the injured. Residents’ accounts describe only the scale of the boom, not its cause, so conclusions should wait for the results of the police investigation. Residents around Ciputat should follow the instructions of police and the local government around the market.

Forest-fire haze disrupts airports in Kalimantan and Maluku, schools in Balikpapan switch to home learning; Bareskrim names nine corporate suspects

Smoke from forest and land fires (karhutla) disrupted flights in Kalimantan and the eastern region. Tjilik Riwut Airport in Palangka Raya temporarily halted flights because of limited visibility, according to Antara and Republika, and detikcom wrote that Sampit Airport closed completely throughout October. Antara reported that Pattimura Airport in Ambon canceled 17 flights, while Republika noted smoke blanketing Timika with visibility of 300 meters. In Balikpapan, detikcom reported that schools switched to home learning for three days.

Enforcement and firefighting are under way. Bareskrim named nine suspects in corporate forest-fire cases, said Antara. Antara also wrote that the Forestry Minister concentrated firefighting at four locations in Tanjung Puting National Park and that the Kodam military command prepared 200 bore wells. On help from Malaysia, which had been reported earlier in a different tone, the government denied rejecting it and said it is communicating through the Foreign Ministry, according to Kompas.com and Antara. The Ombudsman urged regional governments to issue early warnings on the thickness of the smoke, the National Human Rights Commission (Komnas HAM) is investigating the impact of the fires on the rights of West Kalimantan residents, and the Social Affairs Ministry is preparing aid for victims, according to Antara.

The smoke crosses borders. Liputan6 wrote that the air in Singapore and Malaysia is still very unhealthy, and detikcom noted that ASEAN highlighted the haze crisis ahead of the Manila summit. AFP Fact Check judged that a number of AI-generated images exaggerate the scale of the fires in Indonesia; the fires are real, but photos in circulation without a proper source deserve suspicion.

Energy · Regional economyPress ✓ 3 outlets · Antara, Republika, detikNews

Fuel scarce in Bali, residents queue for hours; Pertamina cites tanker problems, Ombudsman finds service disruptions in 11 provinces

Fuel queues in Bali continue. Detikcom wrote that residents fainted from queuing for hours, and Pertamina cited problems unloading tanker ships as the cause. The Oil and Gas Director General said the queues stem from distribution problems and that stocks are safe, according to Antara and Republika. Pertamina mapped 52 fuel stations open 24 hours in Bali, said Antara. The House Speaker asked for more supply (Antara), and a House member urged Pertamina to normalize supply within 24 hours (Republika).

The problem is not only in Bali. The Ombudsman found fuel service disruptions in 11 provinces, is preparing surprise inspections and a distribution investigation, and disclosed indications of the cause of the queues, details of which we have not seen (Antara). Bank Indonesia in Bali judged the impact on the regional economy to be limited, according to Antara and Republika. In West Aceh, the transportation agency (Dishub) set hours for biosolar distribution to ease congestion, and the Energy and Mineral Resources Ministry (ESDM) with the provincial government assured that fuel in South Sulawesi is safe with sufficient quota, said Antara. The two official explanations, tankers and distribution, are not necessarily the same. For motorists in Bali, a 24-hour fuel station is the surest choice for now.

World · Middle East · IndonesiaPress ✓ 5 outlets · Reuters, AP, detikNews, Antara, Republika

Houthis hit Saudi Aramco headquarters in Dhahran; two Indonesian citizens injured in Riyadh have recovered, hundreds of umrah pilgrims from Tangerang fail to depart

A Houthi attack from Yemen targeted the Saudi Aramco headquarters in Dhahran on the night of Tuesday, October 13 local time. Reuters reported that a boom was heard near the headquarters, and detikcom and Republika wrote that the building was targeted, with a fire. On Wednesday Reuters quoted a source saying the headquarters was badly damaged and wrote that the Houthis vowed to attack more Saudi airports and oil facilities. Saudi Arabia said it destroyed eight Houthi missiles and drones in its airspace (detikcom); in Riyadh, missile debris injured four people, said detikcom and Antara.

The Foreign Ministry said two Indonesian citizens were injured in the attack at Riyadh airport and have recovered, according to Antara and detikcom; an earlier detikcom report still said there were no Indonesian victims, so the news changed. Antara and Republika reported that hundreds of umrah pilgrims from Tangerang failed to depart (Antara wrote canceled, Republika wrote delayed), and Republika noted that the Singapore Ulema Council asked for umrah departures to be postponed. The rupiah is under pressure from oil prices rising again, according to Antara and Republika, while Reuters wrote that oil prices rose as the market weighed Saudi supply risks and the flow of Russian diesel. Antara wrote that the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah was asked to ensure pilgrims’ safety, and Republika reported that MUI asked Saudi Arabia and the Houthis to stop the war.
