Prabowo and El-Sisi agree to form a shared investment fund, sign several MoUs, and speak with one voice on the Nile and Gaza

The Egyptian president’s state visit to Jakarta ended on Sunday with pledges to widen market access and cooperate on defense and education. The size of the investment fund has not been announced.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi ended his state visit to Jakarta on Sunday, October 11, after meeting President Prabowo Subianto at the Merdeka Palace, according to Antara, detikcom, Republika and Liputan6. The two agreed to form a shared investment fund (joint investment fund) and to expand market access; Antara wrote that they also pushed for an economic partnership agreement (EPA). In the reports DailyDrop saw, the size of the fund and its target sectors were not mentioned.

The two countries signed several memoranda of understanding (MoU) in the diplomatic and maritime fields. Antara also reported agreements to strengthen the defense industry, combat illegal fishing, and increase scholarships for Indonesian students. In front of El-Sisi, Prabowo affirmed Indonesia’s support for Egypt in securing its water rights to the Nile River, according to detikcom, Antara and Metro TV. He also said that Indonesian is now taught at Al-Azhar University and asked El-Sisi to regard Indonesia as his second home.

On the Middle East, the two pushed for de-escalation through diplomacy, affirmed support for peace and the reconstruction of Gaza, and condemned the construction of illegal Israeli settlements, according to Liputan6, detikcom and Antara. El-Sisi thanked Indonesia for championing Palestine at the Board of Peace (BoP), according to detikcom. Prabowo himself saw his guest off at Halim Perdanakusuma Air Force Base, said Antara, detikcom and Republika. Antara also noted that Prabowo invited El-Sisi to return to Indonesia, and Antara reported that the two leaders affirmed their commitment to Palestinian independence. That makes the visit more a matter of political signals and frameworks than of decisions with clear costs.

In his remarks, Prabowo called the visit a milestone in strengthening the partnership between the two countries and recalled Egypt’s support for Indonesian independence, according to Antara and Republika. El-Sisi responded by praising Jakarta’s warm welcome and urging that cooperation quickly become real projects, said Antara. Detikcom noted that the Egyptian president was escorted by a mounted cavalry unit on his way to the palace, part of the state-guest welcoming ceremony. The joint statement came a day after the attack on Riyadh airport mentioned in the neighboring story, so the two leaders’ call for de-escalation sounds more urgent.

For readers, the value of this visit lies in what follows. A joint investment fund means something only if it comes with figures, sectors and a timetable; MoUs mean something only if they lead to contracts. Support for Nile water rights is a politically sensitive position for the countries upstream on that river, so their reaction deserves watching. DailyDrop has not seen the official text of the MoUs, so the content of each agreement above follows media summaries. What remains open: how much capital each side puts in, who manages the fund, and whether the EPA has entered formal negotiations or is only an intention. Readers in export, fisheries or the defense industry would do well to wait for announcements from the Ministry of Trade and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, which have not appeared in the available sources.

Houthi attack on Riyadh airport kills 12; follow-up attack on Sunday heightens tension

Saudi authorities said the attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 12 people and injured more than 300, according to AP, Politico and USA Today. The Houthis, an armed movement in Yemen, claimed responsibility and renewed a warning that airlines and travelers should avoid Saudi airports, according to Reuters and CBS. AP reported that Saudi Arabia extended the airport’s closure. On Sunday the Saudi-led coalition said it had fended off a follow-up attack, and detikcom wrote that a cruise missile was successfully intercepted; Haaretz reported that another projectile started a fire, and the new casualty count is not yet clear.

The Indonesian Embassy (KBRI) said no Indonesian citizens were among the victims, according to Antara and detikcom, and flights from Soekarno-Hatta Airport to Jeddah and Medina were running normally. Livemint and Hindustan Times reported that an Indian citizen was killed. According to detikcom, President Donald Trump opened the possibility of the US joining strikes on the Houthis; Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attack. Reuters reported that the Saudi stock market plunged. The coalition’s demands for revenge (Guardian) raise the risk of a wider war. The UN Secretary-General, the European Commission, Russia, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Kuwait condemned the attack, according to Antara, detikcom and FinancialJuice, while FT, of which only the headline was visible, wrote that oil bosses had cancelled their attendance at the Riyadh energy congress.

Trump announces Russia–Ukraine ‘energy ceasefire’; Zelensky backs it, Moscow’s response not yet seen

US President Donald Trump announced on Sunday, October 11 that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an energy ceasefire taking effect immediately, according to Reuters, BBC, USA Today, NBC and Bloomberg. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky initially told Axios that the announcement was news to him. Later, on his official Telegram channel, he wrote that Ukraine supports the ceasefire and is waiting for details from the US. Reuters reported that Ukraine would stop attacking Russian oil refineries if Russia stopped attacks on energy facilities.

In an interview with ABC, Zelensky said he would hold elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire, according to Reuters. According to FinancialJuice, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen backed Zelensky’s call. DailyDrop has not seen the Kremlin’s response to Trump’s announcement, so it is not clear whether Moscow accepts its terms. For Indonesia, any news that eases attacks on refineries and energy ports can affect oil prices and the cost of fuel subsidies, but markets can only respond at Monday’s open.

Bezzecchi wins MotoGP Mandalika, Jorge Martin back in the standings lead; crowd breaks record with 157,000

Marco Bezzecchi won the Indonesian MotoGP at the Mandalika Circuit, Lombok, on Sunday, October 11, his fifth win of the season, according to Republika and Antara. Detiksport noted the dominance of Aprilia, Bezzecchi’s team, and Antara wrote that Jorge Martin is back at the top of the riders’ standings. Bezzecchi said tire pressure was the challenge, while Marc Marquez struggled in the main race, according to Antara. In the Moto3 class, Republika wrote that Quiles won and Veda Ega finished sixth.

Youth and Sports Minister Erick Thohir said attendance topped 157,000 people, a record, according to Antara. Immigration issued 1,351 visas to support the event, said Antara, and Antara also wrote that tourism players in West Nusa Tenggara (NTB) hope the race continues. The next questions are the measurable local economic impact and the continuation of the hosting contract, which the reports DailyDrop saw do not explain.

Indonesia women’s U17 team thrash Singapore 5-0 and qualify for the U17 Women’s Asian Cup 2027

Indonesia’s women’s U17 national team beat Singapore 5-0 on Sunday, October 11 and secured a ticket to the U17 Women’s Asian Cup 2027, according to two Antara reports. Youth and Sports Minister Erick Thohir said he was proud of the achievement. DailyDrop saw only Antara for this story, so details of the scorers and the group standings have not been confirmed by other media. The qualification adds good news for national women’s football amid the spotlight on MotoGP.

The Asian Football Confederation (AFC) organizes this tournament; the location and schedule of the final round were not mentioned in the reports seen. Watch for PSSI announcements on preparations and the coach for next year’s tournament.
