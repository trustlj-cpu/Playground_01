Gulf · SecurityPress ✓ 4 outlets · Toll attributed to Saudi authorities

Toll from Houthi missile attack on King Khalid airport in Riyadh rises to 12 dead and more than 300 injured, according to Saudi authorities

The attack came on Saturday, October 10, two days after Thursday’s attack that killed three Saudis, including an airline captain. This is the Saudi authorities’ toll; we have not seen an independent one.

Saudi authorities said, according to the Financial Times, the New York Times and the Mirror, that a Houthi missile attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday, October 10 left 12 dead and more than 300 injured. The Straits Times published a report headlined that a loud explosion was heard at Riyadh airport and that airlines had cancelled more flights.

This follows Thursday’s attack on October 8, which killed three Saudis including an airline captain, according to the General Authority of Civil Aviation, and earlier attacks on Saudi airports that killed three foreigners and injured 36 at the start of the week. The Houthis had claimed responsibility for Thursday’s attack.

We have not seen a Houthi claim of responsibility for Saturday’s attack, nor a detailed statement identifying who was responsible, nor the names or nationalities of the victims. It matters to the Egyptian reader in two ways: Egyptians working and performing umrah in Saudi Arabia, and the oil markets, which are affected by any threat. Watching today: a final toll, Saudi civil aviation data on the airport’s status, the coalition’s response, and the opening of the markets.

Egypt · The NileSingle source: Egypt Today · Trump’s remarks; we have not seen the full text

Trump calls funding of the Renaissance Dam “stupid” and says Ethiopia “took all the water” from the Nile

Egypt Today reported that US President Donald Trump described the funding of Ethiopia’s Renaissance Dam as “stupid” and said Ethiopia “took all the water” from the Nile, adding that the dam was “of course funded by America,” on Saturday, October 10. Reports said Ethiopian officials rejected the claim, but we found neither the text of their response nor its date.

We have not seen the full context or where the remark was made; it was a statement, not a post, as we understand it. Egypt has sought for years a binding agreement on filling and operating the dam, and the Alamein summit had reaffirmed the no-harm principle days earlier. In DailyDrop’s reading, the remark raises international attention to the issue but does not change the dam’s legal status.

Yemen · Bab al-MandabPress ✓ 3 outlets · Battlefield gains are claims not independently confirmed

The Guardian: Yemeni government forces and allies retake Bab al-Mandab strait from Houthis; Al Jazeera: no independent verification

The Guardian reported in the headline of its October 10 report that Yemeni government forces and their allies had retaken the Bab al-Mandab strait from the Houthis in Taiz, while Al Jazeera reported that government forces claim control of Dhubab and Mocha port and that the Houthis denied some of the claims, and that it had not independently verified the extent of the gains. A UN envoy described the situation as Yemen having returned to “full-scale war.”

The Saudi-led coalition said, according to Arab News, that it destroyed 82 military targets in response to attacks on Saudi civilian infrastructure. For the Egyptian reader, Bab al-Mandab is the Red Sea’s entrance to the Suez Canal. We have not seen an announced effect on ship traffic, and the claims of both sides cannot be verified at present.

Russia · UkrainePress ✓ 4 outlets · Zelensky’s accusation attributed to him

Russian attack on Ukrainian city kills 12, a day after Trump eases diesel sanctions on Moscow

The Straits Times, Reuters, the Associated Press and the New York Times reported that a Russian attack on a Ukrainian city killed 12 people a day after Trump announced a diesel deal with Putin and an easing of sanctions restrictions. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky wrote on Telegram that Russia “thanked” the United States for easing the sanctions by dropping guided bombs on homes in Zaporizhzhia.

This is Zelensky’s reading, not an independent finding of a causal link between the two. We have not seen Moscow’s response or the White House’s. The news matters to readers in Egypt because the diesel deal bears on global fuel supplies, and any tension over its implementation could move prices.

Egypt · AsiaPress ✓ 3 outlets · Saturday’s events from a single source

Sisi in Malaysia: meeting with the king and talks with Anwar Ibrahim, then Jakarta, according to Egypt Today

The programme of the state visit to Malaysia, according to the presidential spokesman, included a meeting with the king on Saturday, an official lunch, then a press conference and talks with Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, according to Parlmany, The Star and Bernama. Egypt Today alone reported that the president held talks with Anwar Ibrahim on Saturday and was received on arrival in Jakarta, Indonesia.

We have not seen the texts of the signed memorandums or the statement upgrading relations to a strategic partnership, so we treat that as expected until it is issued. The tour had begun in South Korea on Thursday, and trade with Malaysia had risen 74.7% in eight months, according to The Vibes.

Unverified — what we could not confirm

“Inflation in Egypt is 12.7%… no, 14.5%.” Two consecutive headlines from Al-Ahram Online about August: 12.7% for annual overall inflation nationwide, according to the Central Agency, and 14.5% for urban inflation in the central bank’s statement. Both figures are correct for two different baskets, and some headlines confuse them.
“The death toll in the Riyadh airport attack is 3.” That is the figure for Thursday’s attack on October 8, according to the General Authority of Civil Aviation; the toll for Saturday’s attack is 12 dead and more than 300 injured, according to Saudi authorities.
“Trump confirms America funded the Renaissance Dam.” A single source reported that he said the dam was “of course funded by America,” and he said Ethiopia “took all the water.” We found no verification of the first claim in other coverage; it is a statement, not a documented fact.
