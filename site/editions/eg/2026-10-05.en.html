Alamein four-way summit: Egypt, Sudan, Eritrea and Somalia reject recognition of “Somaliland” and affirm the “no-harm” principle on the Nile

Sisi, al-Burhan, Afwerki and Hassan Sheikh Mohamud held their first four-way summit in New Alamein on Sunday, and a joint declaration was issued that backs Sudan’s unity and holds that Red Sea governance is a matter for the states that border it.

President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi, the head of Sudan’s Sovereignty Council Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, Eritrean President Isaias Afwerki and Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud held a four-way summit in the city of New Alamein on Sunday, October 4, the first to bring them together in this format according to reports, on the same day the city hosted the African Union coordination meeting. Egypt Today, Al-Monitor, Arab News and Qatar Tribune reported that the leaders issued a joint declaration backing Sudan’s unity and territorial integrity, rejecting parallel entities, and rejecting support for mercenaries.

On the Somali file, the declaration rejected recognition of “Somaliland”, describing it as a violation of Somalia’s sovereignty, and called for regular funding of the African Union mission in Somalia. The leaders affirmed that governance of the Red Sea rests with the states that border it and with freedom of navigation there, and stressed that transboundary rivers rest on no-harm, prior notification and consensus, and rejected unilateral measures that harm Egypt’s and Sudan’s uses of water. According to reports, a permanent track will be set up to follow implementation, meeting periodically.

The summit comes amid tension with Ethiopia. Al-Monitor reported that Addis Ababa accused Egypt, Eritrea and Sudan of backing an armed alliance fighting its government, and that the three countries deny it; we found no independent confirmation of the accusation. For their part, former Egyptian officials told Arab News that the summit is not aimed at a third party. In DailyDrop’s reading, what matters to the Egyptian reader is the water and navigation message: the Nile is the source of water, and the Red Sea is the road to the Suez Canal.

Today, Monday, two things are being watched: whether Ethiopia issues an official response to the declaration, and whether a date for the first meeting of the permanent mechanism, and its composition, is announced. We have not seen any details of financial or military commitments accompanying the declaration.

Egypt hosts the eighth African Union coordination meeting and the Alamein African Forum, and Sisi calls for tangible projects

Sisi opened the African Union’s eighth mid-year coordination meeting in New Alamein, and said, according to Daily News Egypt, that African capacities must complement one another and that coordination should lead to tangible projects that benefit peoples. He called for cooperation on transboundary rivers on the basis of international law, consultation and avoiding harm.

According to Sani Wiz and Egypt Today, the agenda covered institutional reform, accelerating the Continental Free Trade Area, and following up the 2026 theme on water and sanitation, and South African President Cyril Ramaphosa took part. Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said the accompanying forum brings together the private sector, financial institutions and officials to align political and investment priorities. We have not seen any specific investment deals or commitments announced.

Egyptian Exchange rises across the board: EGX 30 jumps 1.61% to 53,911.1 points

The main index, the EGX 30, closed Sunday’s session, October 4, up 1.61% at 53,911.1 points, according to Arab Finance and Egypt Today. The EGX 70 index of small and mid-sized stocks rose about 3.04% to 19,952.98 points, and the broader EGX 100 rose 2.77% to 26,366.99 points, according to Arab Finance.

Shares of 178 companies rose against 33 that fell, and market capitalisation reached about 4.26 trillion pounds. Trading value differed: 9.71 billion pounds according to Arab Finance and 10.27 billion according to Egypt Today. Egypt Today reported that foreigners sold 222 million pounds on a net basis and that Egyptians bought 302 million. We found no confirmed explanation for the rise, and linking the move to de-escalation or to regional news is guesswork we have no evidence for.

Al-Alimi announces military operations to retake Houthi-held areas, and the Houthis claim to have targeted Aramco

Rashad al-Alimi, chairman of Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council, announced on Sunday the start of major military operations to retake Houthi-held areas, and said the chances of de-escalation were exhausted, according to Reuters as relayed by The Star, Al Jazeera, Khaleej Times, Egypt Today and ARY. Reuters quoted regional and Western officials as saying the offensive relies on a Saudi air force and Yemeni forces on the ground.

The Yemeni government’s military spokesman said forces carried out 257 precision strikes on Saturday and that Houthi deaths were at least 700; these are government figures not confirmed by an independent source. For their part, the Houthis said they launched ballistic missiles and drones at Aramco facilities in Riyadh and Khurais, and Saudi Arabia did not confirm it.

For the Egyptian reader, the Suez Canal passes through the Bab al-Mandab strait at the entrance to the Red Sea, and any widening of the fighting could affect navigation. We have not seen any announced effect on ship movements so far.

OPEC+ keeps November production targets unchanged in a short virtual meeting

The seven core OPEC+ countries, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman, agreed in a short online meeting on Sunday to keep production targets for November as they are, according to Reuters as relayed by Khaleej Times, ARY, the Qatar News Agency, Hellenic Shipping News and Enerdata. Reports indicate that other changes to production policy are unlikely before next year.

Gulf countries are producing below their quotas because of the Iran war, and their exports ranged between 60% and 80% of the normal level in recent months, according to Reuters. The review of production capacity that will set the 2027 quotas has been delayed. The next meeting is on November 1. Egypt imports part of its energy needs, so oil prices matter to it, but we have not seen an official Egyptian estimate of the decision’s effect.
