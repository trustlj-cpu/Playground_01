Yemen · Bab al-MandebSingle source for developments on the ground: Al Jazeera · Government claims not confirmed by any independent party

Yemeni government forces announce gains near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait as part of Operation “Yemen Dawn” and head toward Sanaa

Two days after Rashad al-Alimi announced the start of operations, government forces say they have made significant gains near Bab al-Mandeb, at the entrance to the Red Sea, while analysts warn that defeating the Houthis will be hard.

Al Jazeera said in its coverage on Tuesday, October 6, that Yemeni government forces claim to have made significant gains near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, as part of Operation “Yemen Dawn”, which the government announced on Sunday with the aim of retaking all areas held by the Houthis, including the capital, Sanaa. Al Jazeera reported that it had not independently verified the extent of these gains and that claims from both sides are hard to verify. We found no second report confirming the details on the ground.

On October 5, Al Jazeera published an analysis arguing that government forces are heading toward Sanaa but that defeating the Houthis will be a challenge. The Houthis had reportedly seized a coastal city on the Red Sea in September in an attempt to advance toward Bab al-Mandeb, according to Euronews and the South China Morning Post.

For the Egyptian reader, the interest lies in the fact that Bab al-Mandeb is the entrance to the Red Sea, through which ships pass on their way to the Suez Canal. The canal’s revenues rose 23% to about $4.6 billion in the 2025/26 fiscal year, according to Al-Ahram Online, and remain below their level before the disruption to shipping. We have seen no announced effect on ship traffic over the past few days. In DailyDrop’s reading, the effect would show if ships were attacked or insurance premiums rose, not merely because the map changes.

Egypt · 53rd anniversaryPress ✓ 2 sources · text of the greeting attributed to the presidency

Sisi congratulates Egyptians and the armed forces on the 53rd anniversary of the October victory

President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi congratulated the Egyptian people and the armed forces on the 53rd anniversary of the glorious October victory, according to Al-Ahram Online. Reports said he posted the greeting on his official Facebook page, recalled the sacrifices of the heroes and prayed for security and prosperity for Egypt. Egypt Independent reported that the Coptic Orthodox Church congratulated the president and the armed forces on the occasion.

Al-Ahram Online also carried headlines about wreaths being laid at the Unknown Soldier Memorial and at the tombs of Sadat and Abdel Nasser, but we have not seen the details. The occasion came a day after a meeting of the Supreme Council of the Armed Forces, and ahead of the Thursday, October 8 holiday.

Markets · Egyptian ExchangePress ✓ 2 sources · pressure from foreigners and Arabs, according to Al-Youm Al-Sabea

Foreign pressure pushes the Egyptian Exchange lower: EGX 30 loses 0.48% to 53,298.14 points

The EGX 30 closed Tuesday’s session, October 6, down 0.48% at 53,298.14 points, according to Arab Finance, in a second straight losing session after a 0.66% loss on Monday. Al-Youm Al-Sabea reported that market capitalisation fell about 26 billion pounds to 4.213 trillion pounds, and that trading value reached about 9.1 billion pounds, with 2.3 billion securities traded.

Shares of 64 companies rose, 146 fell and 11 were unchanged. The pressure on the market came from selling by Arab traders and by foreigners, while Egyptians bought, according to Al-Youm Al-Sabea. We have not seen a confirmed explanation for the selling, and this session comes before the Thursday holiday, when trading is halted.

Economy · Suez CanalSingle source: Al-Ahram Online · earlier financial figures, given as background

Suez Canal revenue rose 23% to $4.6 billion in fiscal year 2025/26, and its chairman expects $8 billion in 2026/27

Al-Ahram Online reported on June 28 that the canal’s dollar revenue rose 23% to about $4.6 billion in fiscal year 2025/26. Canal Authority chairman Osama Rabie said the number of transiting ships rose 10% and cargo tonnage 22%, and that both indicators remain below the target set before the disruption to shipping. In earlier remarks Rabie projected $8 billion in 2026/27, an estimate whose realisation we have not verified.

We cite these figures today because developments at Bab al-Mandeb bear directly on the canal. We have not seen figures for October 2026, and so far we have found no announced effect of the operations in Yemen on transit traffic.

Currencies · The poundPress ✓ 2 sources · prices differ from one bank to another

Dollar steady at about 52.27 pounds to buy at the National Bank of Egypt, with the highest price at 52.40 pounds

Al Mal News and Al Khaleej reported that the lowest dollar buying price on Tuesday, October 6, was at the National Bank of Egypt and Arab Bank, at 52.27 pounds, and the highest was 52.40 pounds at Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank, Al Ahli Bank of Kuwait and Nxt Bank. Al-Youm Al-Sabea reported that prices were unchanged at state and private banks at dawn on Wednesday.

The National Bank of Egypt’s price thus stays at the level recorded on Sunday, October 4, which points to a stable exchange rate over the week. Banks close on Thursday, October 8 and reopen on Sunday, October 11, so no new prices will be published during the holiday.
