Kim Beom-su acquitted for good in “SM price manipulation” case… Public Prosecution Service drops appeal on the last day of the deadline

The Public Prosecution Service chose not to take the case to the Supreme Court by the appeal deadline of the 27th, so the acquittal on appeal stands.

The trial of Kakao founder Kim Beom-su, head of the Future Initiative Center, on charges of “SM Entertainment price manipulation” ended in acquittal on the 27th. The Seoul Southern Public Prosecution Service decided not to send the case to the Supreme Court by that day’s deadline, so the acquittal on appeal, reportedly handed down by Criminal Division 4-1 of the Seoul High Court on the 20th, became final. A final appeal is the procedure of asking the Supreme Court to rule again after disagreeing with a second-instance judgment, and if it is not filed within the deadline, the judgment becomes fixed. The Public Prosecution Service is reported to have reviewed the content and intent of the first and second trial judgments and the chance of success in a final appeal, and decided to drop the appeal. Kim was indicted for violating the Capital Markets Act over an attempt, during the February 2023 takeover of SM Entertainment, to keep SM’s share price above the 120,000 won tender offer price of rival Hybe in order to block Hybe’s tender offer.

The trial reached the same conclusion from the first instance. The Seoul Southern District Court acquitted on October 21, 2025. Prosecutors had sought 15 years in prison and a fine of 500 million won, but the court found that the consistency and credibility of key testimony fell short, and pointed out that obtaining testimony by pressuring related persons through investigations of separate cases can distort the truth. Counting from February 2023, when the SM takeover battle took place, this is a final conclusion reached after 3 years and 9 months. The specific reason the Public Prosecution Service dropped the appeal and Kakao’s official position have not been confirmed.

Rail union work-to-rule on day two: metropolitan commuter trains 20+ minutes late up from 235 to 445… 2 regular trains halted

The National Railway Workers’ Union’s safe-running campaign (work-to-rule) disrupted train service again on the 27th, its second day. By the Korea Railroad Corporation (Korail) count as of 5 p.m., 2 regular trains were halted and 454 trains in all were late. 9 regular trains were 30 minutes or more late and 445 metropolitan commuter trains were 20 minutes or more late. Since 235 metropolitan commuter trains were 20 minutes or more late on the first day, the number nearly doubled in a day. No KTX high-speed train was 20 minutes or more late, and at Daejeon Station a Seoul-bound KTX was at most 14 minutes late. Work-to-rule is a labor action that, instead of stopping work as in a strike, slows service by observing every safety rule and working-hour limit one by one. Around 5:30 p.m., at the evening rush hour, “chaos on the commute home” broke out on Seoul Subway Line 1, where Korail trains run. There is concern that because weekend travel is heavier than usual, overlapping delays could worsen crowding. The union’s specific demands, the negotiation schedule and how long the work-to-rule will continue have not been confirmed.

Budget subcommittee ends work after 11 days… all Future Fund projects “shelved”; report says government keeps plan for 30% discretionary spending changes

On the 27th, five days before the legal deadline for passing next year’s budget (December 2), the Budget Adjustment Subcommittee of the National Assembly’s Special Committee on Budget and Accounts finished its work after 11 days of operation. A report said all Future Fund projects remained “shelved” at the subcommittee stage. The Future Fund is reported to refer to the 162 trillion won Future Response Fund, built from extra tax revenue from the semiconductor boom. Tax revenue is the income the government collects in taxes. Even within the ruling party, there were remarks that funding the basic child allowance from this fund, which requires continuing fiscal input, is “hard to accept.”

The dispute over how the fund will be run also continues. At last week’s bill review in the National Assembly’s Strategy and Finance Committee, the Ministry of Planning and Budget is reported to have submitted a revised plan that keeps a provision allowing spending on major items of the fund to be changed at discretion by up to 30% without a National Assembly vote. In exchange, the law would state that the fund’s spare money can be used not only to cover tax revenue shortfalls but also for government bond repayment. The government did not change course despite criticism that the fund could be used like pocket money. The final handling of the shelved projects and the negotiation schedule ahead have not been confirmed.

SK hynix doubles pace of share buyback as stock falls… orders to buy 700,000 shares a day

SK hynix will double the pace of its ongoing share buyback. This became known on the 27th, and reports said it is a response to the recent share price drop, with daily buy orders at about 700,000 shares. A share buyback is when a company uses its own money to buy its own shares on the market. It reduces the shares in circulation and raises the portion going to shareholders, so it is used as a way of returning value to shareholders. Since the pace is being doubled, the earlier level would have been around 350,000 shares a day. A simple calculation with the share price at around 1 million won gives buy orders of around 700 billion won a day.

The KOSPI plunged 6.16% on the 25th, then rose 4.34% on the 27th to close at 4,915.68, and SK hynix shares were down 1.80% at 1,034,000 won as of 9:39 a.m. that day. The total buyback size, deadline and funding have not been confirmed.

Remittance firm E9Pay reportedly hacked of about 2.5 billion won… Financial Supervisory Service starts on-site inspection

E9Pay, a domestic overseas remittance firm, is reported to have had about 2.5 billion won stolen in a hacking attack. The Financial Supervisory Service started an on-site inspection on the 27th. An on-site inspection is a procedure in which a supervisory authority visits a firm in person to check how an incident happened and its internal controls. E9Pay is a small-amount overseas remittance firm that brokers small overseas transfers. According to one report, the company came under 90-plus hacking attacks, reported to the financial authorities after noticing the incident, and the incident is also being reported to Cheong Wa Dae. The report added an explanation that this is the largest theft of funds among domestic hacking incidents. These details come from only one outlet and have not been officially confirmed.

The company is preparing to list on the KOSDAQ within the year, and there is speculation that the offering schedule could be affected. The same report said the money that left was customer funds, but the number of affected customers, the compensation plan and the hacking route have not been confirmed.
