World · Middle EastPress ✓ 8 outlets · death and injury figures from the Saudi aviation authority; on Saturday 12 or 13 dead were reported depending on the outlet

A Houthi missile hit Riyadh airport, killing at least 12 and injuring more than 300; on Sunday a second attack killed one more person and Washington and London warned their citizens

Yemen’s Houthis claimed responsibility for the attack on Saturday, October 10 and threaten more. Saudi Arabia reported one more death and 11 injured on Sunday; oil fell on Monday on other news.

King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the capital of Saudi Arabia, was hit on Saturday, October 10 by a missile that left at least 12 dead and more than 300 injured, according to outlets such as Straits Times and Manila Times; the BBC and CNA put the toll at 13. It was the deadliest attack on Saudi soil since the war with Iran began, according to the Mirror. The Houthis, the Yemeni rebel movement allied with Iran, claimed responsibility for the attack, according to RTP, and the Saudi General Authority of Civil Aviation suspended airport operations. Among the injured are Filipinos and Indians, according to the Philippine Department of Migrant Workers and Indian media.

On Sunday the 11th came a second blow. The aviation authority reported one dead, a Pakistani resident, and 11 injured, according to Al Jazeera, Dawn and Saudi Gazette. The Houthis renewed their threats against Saudi airports, and Kuwait Airways joined the airlines cancelling flights, according to El Mundo. The United States and the United Kingdom issued alerts for their citizens, according to Infobae, and the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) urged caution to US airlines flying to or over Saudi Arabia, according to Straits Times. Iran reportedly offered to mediate between the Houthis and Riyadh, according to CNBC; DailyDrop saw no official confirmation. Final casualty figures are not yet settled.

World · VaticanPress ✓ 5 outlets · the pope’s own announcement, no surgery date

Leo XIV announces he will undergo surgery for a lung nodule and will be hospitalized for about a week; the November visit to Argentina is still on for now

Pope Leo XIV, 71, said on Sunday, October 11 that he will undergo surgery “in the coming days” for a lung nodule detected “at a very early stage” during medical checkups, according to New Straits Times and Philippine Daily Inquirer. The outlets report that he will remain hospitalized for about a week. The Vatican did not specify the date of the surgery in the sources DailyDrop reviewed.

The news surprised the Argentine government and Church, but the national, provincial and municipal operations for the planned visit of November 8 to 11 continue as scheduled, according to Infobae. The tour would also include Uruguay and Peru, according to El Comercio. President Javier Milei said they join “in prayer for his health” (La Nación) and the Argentine Episcopal Conference sent a message of support. Specialists consulted by Infobae maintain that air travel after lung surgery depends on the patient’s recovery.

Society · JusticePress ✓ 6 outlets · prison capacity of 2,232 places according to La Nación and Perfil (Ámbito: 2,240)

Transfer of prisoners from Devoto jail to the new Marcos Paz prison begins

The government began this weekend to move prisoners from the Devoto prison, in the City of Buenos Aires, to the new Marcos Paz complex, in Buenos Aires province, according to La Nación, Infobae, TN, Clarín, Ámbito and Perfil. The operation is gradual and aims to relocate about 1,500 inmates; the new prison has four maximum-security units and capacity for 2,232 detainees (Ámbito gives 2,240) and will also receive prisoners from Buenos Aires City police stations and alcaidías, according to Perfil and La Nación.

The plan is an agreement between the City and the national Ministry of Security, according to Infobae. The head of the Buenos Aires City government, Jorge Macri, called it “historic”. TN reported that the transfers are carried out with vans, motorcycles and federal-force escorts, and that each inmate wears a wristband with a QR code to track their movements. The CELS questioned the transfer and, according to Clarín, the operation will be paused for a time during the pope’s visit to the prison. DailyDrop did not confirm how many prisoners have been moved so far.

World · UkrainePress ✓ 8 outlets · the announcement is Trump’s; Moscow did not confirm it in the sources reviewed

Trump announces an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine; Kyiv says it knew nothing of the deal and Russia hit power plants

President Donald Trump said on Sunday, October 11 on his Truth Social network that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy ceasefire” effective immediately, that is, to stop attacking each other’s energy infrastructure, according to Reuters, the BBC, Perfil and Ámbito. Volodymyr Zelensky seemed surprised, but said Ukraine would back it if Moscow did the same, according to New Straits Times and Livemint. Moscow stayed silent in the sources reviewed.

Hours later Zelensky denounced that Russia hit several Ukrainian power plants, according to Philippine Daily Inquirer and The Guardian. France, the United Kingdom and Canada backed the initiative, according to Infobae. The announcement comes after the diesel deal, under which Russia would supply 300,000 tonnes to the United States and the world market (Globe and Mail), which seven senators from both parties consider illegal, according to New Straits Times. Brent fell 1.47% on Monday, according to Business Recorder.

Economy · The weekPress ✓ 5 outlets · no confirmed Friday close for the Merval and the wholesale dollar

Monday holiday in Argentina: no local trading session; Caputo is in Bangkok for the IMF, Milei travels to Washington on Tuesday and the opposition aims at the Thursday the 15th session

Monday, October 12 is a holiday in Argentina and local markets are closed, according to La Nación and Perfil; in the United States, which celebrates Columbus Day, stocks trade. Country risk, the JP Morgan index that measures the premium the country pays on its debt, fell 10% over the past week and ended below 600 basis points, according to Infobae. For Friday the 9th close, the published S&P Merval figures do not match across outlets, and DailyDrop could not confirm the wholesale dollar with two sources, so both ears show “—”.

Economy Minister Luis Caputo is traveling to Thailand, where the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank meet this week, to seek funds and reinforce the financial program, according to Clarín and Infobae. President Milei will leave for the United States on Tuesday night and return on Friday, according to Perfil. In the Chamber of Deputies, Martín Menem said the Thursday the 15th session to repeal DNU 70/23 seeks to “sow chaos”, according to La Nación and Cronista, and ruled out Caputo’s departure, according to Perfil.
