‘Fabricated-prosecution special counsel act’ passes Assembly… 167 for, 1 against; People Power Party skips vote, takes to the streets from the 7th

The National Assembly passed the fabricated-prosecution special counsel act at a plenary session on the 6th, 167 to 1. The People Power Party did not take part in the vote and begins a street campaign on the 7th.

The ‘fabricated-prosecution special counsel act’ passed a plenary session of the National Assembly on the 6th. Its formal title is the ‘Act on the Appointment of a Special Counsel, etc. for Uncovering the Truth Behind Allegations of Fabricated Investigations and Fabricated Prosecutions by State Agencies Including the Yoon Suk Yeol Government’s Prosecutors’ Office, National Intelligence Service and Board of Audit and Inspection.’ The Democratic Party ended by a vote, after a little over 24 hours, the filibuster (unlimited debate) the People Power Party had kept up since the 5th, and at the plenary session at 4 p.m. that day it passed the bill with 168 members present, 167 in favor and 1 against. The one vote against was cast by Rep. Lee Ju-young of the Reform Party, and People Power Party lawmakers did not take part in the vote. It is the seventh special counsel since the Lee Jae-myung government took office. When the Democratic Party, the People Power Party and the Rebuilding Korea Party each recommend one candidate, President Lee Jae-myung appoints one of them, and after a 20-day preparation period a special counsel team of up to 309 members is formed.

The investigation covers six cases in which President Lee has been indicted. The version that passed is the one the Legislation and Judiciary Committee revised on the 4th, and the contested clause on withdrawal of prosecution was dropped. The bill has the special counsel look into allegations of fabricated investigations and fabricated prosecutions, such as selecting or omitting evidence and using illegally collected evidence. The People Power Party called it ‘bulletproof legislation’ aimed at President Lee’s cases and said it would begin a street campaign from the 7th. Former Prosecutor General Lee Won-seok criticized it as unconstitutional legislation that destroys the separation of powers. Rep. Chung Sung-ho of the ruling Democratic Party said in a radio interview that day, “I get the feeling that a bit more deliberation was needed.” Cho Kuk, head of the Rebuilding Korea Party’s Innovation Policy Institute, wrote on Facebook that the provision having the special counsel apply the old Criminal Procedure Act, which has already lost effect, raises problems of unconstitutionality and illegality. The date the law will be promulgated and the schedule for recommending special counsel candidates have not yet been announced.

Authorities waive 140% margin loan collateral ratio maintenance duty at brokerages for 3 months… curbing forced selling to soften the drop

The government decided on the 6th to waive for three months the duty of securities firms to maintain the collateral ratio on margin loans. The measure came after Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Finance and Economy Lee Hyung-il held a meeting with the Financial Services Commission, the Financial Supervisory Service, the Bank of Korea and others at the Government Complex Seoul. A margin loan is money an investor borrows from a securities firm to buy stocks. Securities firms have required the value of the collateral to stay at 140% or more of the amount lent, and if share prices fall and the ratio drops below that, the firm can forcibly sell the pledged shares. This is called forced liquidation. During the exemption period, firms do not have to dispose of the shares right away even if the collateral ratio falls below 140%. Authorities explained that the aim is to reduce forced sales of the shares of investors whose collateral value has dropped and so ease further downward pressure on the market. That day the KOSPI triggered a circuit breaker three minutes after the open and closed at 5,059.69, down 6.02%. The government has said it will add market-stabilization measures if volatility in the market grows.

Gopax suspends withdrawals and trading after hacking-threat email… hot wallet assets moved to cold wallet, no actual damage yet

The virtual asset exchange Gopax received an email on the 6th threatening a hack and moved to protect users’ assets. Gopax said it blocked withdrawals from 2:55 p.m. that day and is moving most of the assets that were in its hot wallets, which are connected to the internet, to cold wallets, which are disconnected from the internet. From 3:53 p.m. it also temporarily suspended virtual asset trading. A hot wallet is a wallet kept connected to the internet so that deposits and withdrawals can be processed right away, and a cold wallet is one whose connection has been cut. Moving assets to a cold wallet lowers the risk of their being taken by outside intrusion. The step is a preemptive response to the threat, and no actual damage is known to have occurred so far. While the assets are being moved, users cannot use deposits, withdrawals or trading. It was the day the KOSPI plunged on a circuit breaker, so users could not place trading orders during the hours when prices were swinging sharply. The identity of the sender of the threat, what was demanded and when service will reopen have not been confirmed.

US jobs up 165,000 in October… above expectations, December rate hike outlook strengthens

In the October jobs report released by the US Bureau of Labor Statistics on the 6th, nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000. It was a ‘surprise’ gain, far above market forecasts. One of the prior forecasts was a gain of 59,000, and the September gain was 29,000, far short of the forecast of 84,000. Nonfarm payrolls count the jobs added in a month across all industries except agriculture. The unemployment rate fell from 4.2% to 4.1%. When employment comes in better than expected, a central bank has more room to raise rates to hold down prices, so expectations strengthened in the market that the US Federal Reserve will raise rates in December. That day Wall Street opened mixed, digesting the data as tech stocks rebounded. Kavana, a strategist at BofA, said on the 5th that it is too early to say the Fed’s tightening phase is over. Hassett argued in a broadcast interview that the data does not mean the Fed must raise rates. No official reaction from Fed officials has been confirmed.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claims “2 tankers hit mines and caught fire in Hormuz”… maritime authorities do not confirm

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed on the 6th (local time) that two tankers trying to pass through the Strait of Hormuz collided with naval mines and caught fire. The Revolutionary Guard also said that day that it shot down a US drone. Maritime authorities have not yet confirmed the damage to the tankers, and the US position on the drone shoot-down has not been confirmed either. Iran has kept up pressure even after the US midterm elections. Earlier, on the 5th, Iran also announced that three tankers had been damaged in the strait, and the number of damaged ships is reported differently from announcement to announcement. A naval mine is an explosive laid underwater that goes off when a ship touches it or comes close.

On the diplomatic front, a senior Iranian official said Iran had delivered a proposal for nuclear negotiations to the United States through Qatar and that the United States is reviewing it. On the 5th the South Korean government decided to extend the Cheonghae Unit’s area of operations to open waters south of Oman near the Strait of Hormuz and is going through the procedure for National Assembly consent. What to follow next is whether the claim of tanker damage is confirmed and whether the United States responds militarily.
