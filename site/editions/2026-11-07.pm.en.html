President Lee names Defense Acquisition chief Lee Yong-chul as justice minister nominee… Choi In-ho for political affairs secretary, Ahn Deok-soo for tax service head

On the 6th President Lee Jae-myung named Lee Yong-chul, head of the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, as nominee for minister of justice. The People Power Party objected, saying the pick is aimed at having the indictment withdrawn, and the nominee said prosecution reform would be completed.

On the 6th President Lee Jae-myung named Lee Yong-chul (66), head of the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, as the new nominee for minister of justice. Presidential Chief of Staff Im Kwang-hyun announced the picks that afternoon at the Chunchugwan press center of Cheong Wa Dae. Choi In-ho (60), former president of the Housing & Urban Guarantee Corporation, was named senior presidential secretary for political affairs, and Ahn Deok-soo (55), head of the Seoul Regional Tax Office, nominee for National Tax Service commissioner. The picks came 48 days after Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Seung-won stepped down as justice minister nominee. The nominee is a lawyer by background and was active in Lawyers for a Democratic Society. Right after the nomination, the nominee said that, drawing on experience with prosecution reform under the Participatory Government, the reform would be completed without a hitch.

The People Power Party objected at once. Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk wrote on Facebook, “After all the going around, it is the Gyeongseong line in the end,” and the party named the nominee as part of the ‘Seongnam line’ and someone implicated in the Daejang-dong case, asking whether the pick is meant to engineer the withdrawal of the indictment against President Lee. Chief of Staff Im answered that the Daejang-dong allegations were taken into account in the vetting and that the nominee was expected to explain the details in person at the confirmation hearing. The same day the National Assembly passed the fabricated prosecution special counsel bill, which puts six cases involving President Lee under investigation, and the clause on withdrawal of the indictment had already been dropped at the amendment stage in the Legislation and Judiciary Committee. To become minister, the nominee must go through a National Assembly confirmation hearing, and no date has been set yet.

Mirae Asset Securities hack leaks account numbers of about 3,800 customers and data of 35,255 employees… no monetary loss so far

Mirae Asset Securities said on the 7th that personal information of customers and employees was leaked in an external hacking attack. According to the company, abnormal outside access occurred on the 4th to the mobile web login service that uses mobile phone authentication, and in the process the account numbers of about 3,800 customers were taken. On the 5th, signs of an external attack on the in-house groupware were confirmed, and the names and employee numbers of 35,255 employees, including retirees and outsourced staff, were leaked. For some, email addresses, mobile phone numbers and encrypted passwords were also included. The company explained that unique identification information such as resident registration numbers and passport numbers was not among the data leaked. Mirae Asset Securities blocked the access route, activated its emergency response system and reported the incident to the Financial Services Commission, the Financial Supervisory Service and the Personal Information Protection Commission. No monetary loss has been confirmed so far, and the website and major financial services are operating normally. The company urged people to beware of impersonation calls and of phishing and smishing that use the leaked information. In the financial sector hacking incidents keep coming: at the end of October a leak of information on about 38,000 customers was confirmed at Daishin Securities. Details of who carried out this attack and how they got in have not been confirmed.

New York stocks rebound after one day… Nasdaq up 1.09% as chip stocks surge, Dow down

On the 6th (local time) New York stocks moved in different directions by index after the previous day’s plunge. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite rose 1.09% to close at 24,579.42, and the S&P 500 rose 0.31% to finish at 7,376.86. The Dow Jones Industrial Average (Dow 30) fell about 0.5%. Chip stocks moved sharply: AMD rose 11% and Nvidia also rose. On the 5th, the day before, the Nasdaq fell 5.52% and the S&P 500 fell 4.36%. The Nasdaq’s gain that day was about one-fifth of the previous day’s drop, so if the previous day’s loss is taken as 100, about 20 was recovered.

In the US October jobs data released the same day, nonfarm jobs rose by 165,000, beating expectations. In Korea the KOSPI had already closed on the 6th down 6.02% at 5,059.69. New York’s rebound will first be reflected when the market opens on Monday the 9th. No institution has officially explained the reason for the rebound.

Prosecution Service rejects arrest warrant for Nonghyup chief Kang Ho-dong: “charges insufficiently substantiated”; warrant for business partner’s head also rejected

The Seoul Central District Prosecution Service on the 6th rejected the arrest warrant for National Agricultural Cooperative Federation (Nonghyup) Chairman Kang Ho-dong. The warrant had been requested on the 3rd by the Anti-Corruption Investigation Unit of the Wide-Area Investigation Division of the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency. The warrant for the head of a company that does business with a Nonghyup affiliate, suspected of handing Kang about 100 million won, was rejected as well. The Prosecution Service’s Non-referral Review Division 1 said it had closely examined the evidence the police gathered and the account given in interviews with the suspects before the warrant was requested, and that the main charges were not substantiated enough to seek a warrant. It added that, given that the investigation has run for more than 1 year and 6 months and the suspects have been attending questioning, it is also hard to recognize a need or reasonableness for detention at this stage.

Kang is suspected of receiving more than 100 million won in two installments, around the January 2024 election for Nonghyup chairman, from the head of a company that does business with an affiliate. Allegations of misappropriating public funds and of violating the Improper Solicitation and Graft Act over condolence money for a mother-in-law’s funeral are also under investigation. The warrant did not even reach a court review, and how the police will respond is not yet known. A rejected warrant is a judgment on whether to detain, not a ruling on whether the charges exist.

Labor minister convenes all 49 regional office heads: “heavy responsibility for employers who put cost before safety”

Employment and Labor Minister Kim Young-hoon on the 7th gathered the heads of 49 employment and labor offices nationwide for an emergency meeting of agency heads on stepping up the response to industrial safety. Kim said it must be made clear that employers who put cost cutting ahead of safety even when workers die bear heavy responsibility. Kim ordered that companies where the same type of serious industrial accident has recurred be inspected at a level comparable to a special inspection, and told offices to re-inspect, without notice, even workplaces whose checks and inspections are already done. Instructions also came to act sternly under a zero tolerance principle once violations are confirmed.

For the Daebul National Industrial Complex in Yeongam, South Jeolla, Kim called for a close check of aging facilities, labor and management’s safety awareness and efforts, and any shortcomings in the Ministry’s response. On the 8th of next month the Act on the Execution of Duties of Labor Inspectors takes effect, delegating part of the Ministry’s inspection authority to local governments. A serious industrial accident means an industrial accident with major harm, such as a worker’s death.
