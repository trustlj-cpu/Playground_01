Douglas Ruas confirmed as Rio governor in the first round, with 50.88%, after the TSE annuls Garotinho’s votes

The re-tabulation by the TRE-RJ on Saturday the 10th avoids a runoff in the state. Eduardo Paes gave up on appealing, and about 30 students protested outside the court.

The Regional Electoral Court of Rio de Janeiro (TRE-RJ) confirmed on the afternoon of Saturday, October 10, the election of Douglas Ruas (PL) as governor of the state in the first round. The PL is the party of Jair Bolsonaro and of Flávio Bolsonaro, who is running for president. To win without a runoff, a candidate needs more than half of the valid votes, which do not include blank or null ballots. According to G1, the session was scheduled for 3 p.m. and lasted ten minutes, and the court’s president, appeals judge Cláudio de Mello Tavares, announced that Ruas reached 50.88% of the votes, above the half needed to win without a runoff, Agência Brasil and Valor also report. The change comes from a TSE decision made on Thursday the 8th: by 5 votes to 2, the court rejected the candidacy of former governor Anthony Garotinho (Republicanos) and annulled the 274,411 votes he received on October 4, according to coverage by Diário do Nordeste and DGABC. The rapporteur, Justice Floriano de Azevedo Marques, found that Garotinho had his political rights suspended by a conviction for improbity when he joined the party. In the original count, Ruas had 4,271,199 votes (49.27% of valid votes) and Eduardo Paes 3,706,984 (42.76%), according to Correio da Manhã. Without Garotinho’s votes, the total of valid votes falls, and Ruas’s share rises above 50%. The re-tabulation, therefore, is not a recount: the ballot-box data remain the same, and the court merely redoes the sum without the annulled votes.

Paes, who finished second and depended on a runoff to return to the race, gave up on appealing after the Supreme Federal Court (STF) denied a request to suspend the TSE decision, G1 reports. About 30 protesters, most of them students, called for a runoff outside the Palace of Democracy, seat of the TRE-RJ, according to Valor. Ruas is 37, a career civil police officer, a lawyer, a state representative and president of the Rio Legislative Assembly (Alerj), and was born in São Gonçalo, in the metropolitan region, according to G1. In an interview with RJ2, he said public safety will be the government’s priority and promised to call up, right at the start of the term, those who passed civil-service exams, G1 and Valor report. Celebrating, Ruas cited “renewal” and “change,” Poder360 reports. Also on Saturday, he took part in an evangelical event at Maracanãzinho, according to G1. The running mate on the ticket is Fernanda Louback, a former beauty queen and friend of Michelle Bolsonaro, Poder360 and UOL report; UOL recalls that Ruas is close to Cláudio Castro, who is under investigation by prosecutors. In the states where the governor runoff is maintained, voting is on October 25. Monday the 12th is the national holiday of Our Lady of Aparecida, and the B3 does not open.

Lula campaign takes Meta to the TSE, Ratinho Jr backs Flávio and Ipespe gives Flávio 52.7% to 47.3%

In the presidential runoff between Lula (PT) and Flávio Bolsonaro (PL), Saturday was a day of disputes in the Electoral Justice. Lula’s campaign accused Meta, owner of Instagram and Facebook, of favoring right-wing content and of not delivering the president’s profile, and said it will go to the TSE and the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD), according to G1 and Valor. Folha notes that the complaint grew after Lula’s videos broke viewing records. Justice Marco Aurélio Bellizze gave the company 48 hours to explain the alleged restrictions, Valor, CNN Brasil and Poder360 report. The same justice ordered Flávio’s running mate, congressman Alfredo Gaspar (PL-AL), to take down from Instagram within 24 hours a video that linked businessman Daniel Vorcaro to the financing of a film about Lula, according to Valor. Electoral Inspector General Ricardo Villas Bôas Cueva gave Flávio 72 hours to explain a request to take down Janones’s pro-Lula channel, according to Valor and Folha. In politics, Paraná governor Ratinho Jr (PSD) declared support for Flávio, Valor, Poder360 and UOL report. Ipespe/Abrapel gave Flávio 52.7% of valid votes against Lula’s 47.3%, according to CNN Brasil, Poder360 and Folha. Voting is on October 25. On the street, Lula held a rally in Natal, and Flávio took part in a motorcycle motorcade in Rio, Agência Brasil reports. Valid votes exclude blank and null ballots and undecided voters.

Military police fired 45 times and killed a 20-year-old with 27 shots in Moema after mistaking a cellphone for a gun

Gustavo Munis de Santana, 20, died in the early hours of Saturday the 10th during a stop by military police officers in Moema, in the South Zone of São Paulo, according to G1, Folha, Terra and UOL. According to the police report cited by G1, four officers fired 45 times, and 27 shots hit the young man. He ran across a footbridge and headed toward Alameda dos Guaramomis before being shot, G1 reports. The officers said they mistook a cellphone for a firearm, Terra and G1 report.

On Saturday night G1 published a piece on what is known and what remains to be known in the case. An R7 report describes a man in a mental-health crisis who died in a clash with police, a version the other outlets do not record. As of this edition’s publication, no forensic reports or the family’s version had been released, and the sources consulted do not detail the progress of the investigation.

Attack attributed to the Houthis on Riyadh airport leaves 12 dead and 309 injured, and Trump says he is considering backing Saudi strikes

An attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, killed 12 people and injured 309 on Saturday the 10th, according to Saudi authorities cited by AP, Al Jazeera and CNN Brasil. The Saudi-led coalition blamed the offensive on the Houthis, an armed group from Yemen, Al Jazeera reports. Flights were cancelled and airport operations were suspended, according to Reuters and Terra. Business Times describes the attack as the deadliest against the country in a decade. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attack and confirmed that an American is among the victims, according to Saudi Gazette.

Donald Trump called the attack unacceptable and said the US is considering supporting Saudi actions against the Houthis, ANSA, Livemint and CNN Brasil report; no decision has been announced. Attacks on Saudi airports on Tuesday and Wednesday, the 6th and 7th, had left 3 dead and 36 injured, according to Al Jazeera, and the coalition said it hit 82 military targets in response, Business Today reports. Organizers of major conferences in the kingdom say the events are going ahead, Reuters reports.

Trump says Ukraine needs “a new president” in clash with Zelensky over Russian diesel, and Iran reports tanker hit by mine in Hormuz

US President Donald Trump on Saturday the 10th pushed back against Volodymyr Zelensky’s criticism of the deal with Russia that eases sanctions on Russian diesel and said it is time for Ukraine to have a new president and hold elections, according to G1, Straits Times and Japan Times. The exchange of accusations came on the day Russian attacks killed 21 people in Ukraine, Straits Times reports. Germany said it is keeping its sanctions against Russia, Reuters reports. In the Gulf, Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said a tanker exploded and caught fire after hitting a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, G1 and CNN Brasil report; the UKMTO, the British body that monitors shipping, spoke of a tanker hit by a projectile, according to GMA News. The US Central Command (Centcom) said it disabled a cargo ship that tried to break the naval blockade of Iran in the Gulf of Oman, Reuters and FT report.

Markets are closed on the weekend. In New York, Friday the 9th ended higher: the S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54 points, the Nasdaq closed at 27,366.17 (+0.64%) and the Dow Jones at 51,654.95 (+0.83%). The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 5.244%, WTI at US$ 91.85 (+0.39%) and gold at US$ 4,216.30 (+1.43%). Over the week, diesel prices and the Russia sanctions remain at the center of the US inflation agenda, according to Reuters.
