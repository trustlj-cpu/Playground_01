Runoff enters the home stretch with Flávio Bolsonaro and Lula tied in the polls

Futura and PoderData/AYA, cited by CNN Brasil, show a gap of at most 1.6 points between the two; Datafolha and Quaest are described as a technical tie.

The race for the presidential runoff reached the end of the week with polls pointing to a balance between Senator Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) and President Lula (PT). According to CNN Brasil, the Futura institute registered 50.8% of valid votes for Flávio and 49.2% for Lula, and PoderData/AYA showed 50% for each. Those two figures were released by a single outlet, CNN Brasil, and are attributed to it here. In parallel, the press described the picture in the Datafolha and Quaest polls as a technical tie, and ten international outlets repeated the phrase “tight race in the home stretch”.

On Friday (16), Folha de S.Paulo, G1 and Google News reported a new cut of the Datafolha poll: 96% of the electorate say they intend to vote in the runoff and 1% say they do not intend to show up. A French outlet, also cited among the day’s coverage, wrote that Lula had narrowed the gap to Flávio in one poll. The reports consulted do not carry, in the material available, the margin of error or the fieldwork dates of each survey, and for that reason the polls cannot be compared with one another.

The candidates’ schedules also saw movement. Flávio gave an interview to the journalist Renata Lo Prete, of Globo, and G1 published a #FATO or #FAKE fact-check of his statements. According to G1 and Google News, he said that naming his father “is not on the radar” and that Congressman Eduardo Bolsonaro will not be foreign minister in a possible government. Lula campaigned in Juiz de Fora (MG) and, according to G1, said that Governor Cleitinho “only has something to gain” if he stays neutral in the race.

External factors entered the conversation. G1 reported that a letter from the Donald Trump administration says it supports the Brazilian electoral process and defends action against Justice Alexandre de Moraes, the PCC and the CV. The Superior Electoral Court (TSE) began judging on Friday new rules for linking WhatsApp channels to campaigns. What remains unconfirmed, in this edition’s sources, is the fieldwork date and the sample of each poll cited.

Congress promulgates the end of the 6x1 schedule, with Lula present, and the measure appears in the Official Gazette

The National Congress promulgated the end of the 6x1 schedule at a ceremony attended by Lula, and the proposed constitutional amendment (PEC) was made official in the Official Gazette on Friday (16), according to G1. Six outlets covered the promulgation, and a daily digest also listed it among the highlights. G1 published an explanatory text on when the rule takes effect and which professions are affected, but the exact deadlines are not in the excerpt consulted for this edition. Folha de S.Paulo reported that Brazil had 14.8 million jobs with a signed work card on the 6x1 schedule in December 2025, or 3 in every 10 formal contracts, according to a study using official data. Speaking to TV Bandeirantes, Finance Minister Dario Durigan said the government is preparing credit and training for companies affected by the change, according to Folha. A tax lawyer interviewed by the paper, Roberto Quiroga, said in another interview that the current government’s tax measures closed loopholes.

Brent hits US$ 110 for the first time since May and big tech posts its biggest weekly drop since April 2025

G1 and Google News Economy reported that Brent crude reached US$ 110 a barrel, the first level like it since May. Folha reported that big tech shares registered their biggest weekly drop since Donald Trump announced tariffs in April 2025. G1 also reported that the US public deficit rose to US$ 1.99 trillion, the largest in five years.

At Friday’s (16) close, the Ibovespa ended at 205,430 points, up 0.54%, and the dollar at R$ 5.0693, down 0.58%, according to the edition’s quotes table. WTI rose 2.61% to US$ 99.02, and the S&P 500 fell 1.09%. Folha also noted a UN warning of a possible global food crisis after the energy shock.

Justice · Banco Master✓ 2 outlets per topic · part of the information comes from messages cited by a magazine

New messages link Daniel Vorcaro to a Supreme Court justice and a sealed inquiry; PF asks for 90 more days in the Dark Horse case

G1, citing a magazine, reported that messages show that the former banker Daniel Vorcaro, jailed since March in investigations into Banco Master, organized a party with escorts for Supreme Federal Court (STF) Justice Kassio Nunes Marques. Folha de S.Paulo reported, based on messages, that Vorcaro had access, through more than one intermediary, to data from a sealed inquiry into Nelson Tanure and put spies to work. Estadão published that the owner of Banco Master edited the scripts of Gabriela Prioli videos sold as spontaneous opinion.

Also on Friday (16), the Federal Police (PF) asked STF Justice André Mendonça for a 90-day extension of the Dark Horse case inquiry, according to G1. The information from the messages has not yet been confirmed by court documents in the sources consulted.

Meta denies interfering in the election, leads fundraising and Google changes AI mode

Folha and Valor reported that Meta was the company that raised the most in Brazil’s election, amid accusations from the PT and from researchers that it favored Flávio Bolsonaro’s campaign. G1 noted that the company denies political interference and cites technical incidents on Lula’s networks. On other topics, Google announced it will give links more prominence in AI mode, and Tesla presented the Roadster, described as a flying car.
