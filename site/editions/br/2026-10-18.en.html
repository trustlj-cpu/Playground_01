Flávio Bolsonaro takes his campaign to Vitória and Salvador as dispute with Lula over Meta reach lands at the TSE

One week before the October 25 runoff, the two candidates split their schedules between street rallies and a legal fight over the boosting of content on social networks.

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) spent Saturday (17) at campaign rallies in Espírito Santo and Bahia, according to G1 and Times Brasil. In Vitória, according to Folha de S.Paulo and Valor Econômico, he spoke alongside São Paulo Governor Tarcísio de Freitas and called his father, former President Jair Bolsonaro, “professor”. In Salvador, the portal BNews reported that supporters had already held a motorcade through the Pituba and Barra neighborhoods on Friday night (16), as a warm-up for the next day’s rally. On Saturday, Times Brasil and Folha de Curitiba reported that the candidate spoke from atop a trio elétrico on the Barra waterfront, accompanied by ACM Neto, Tarcísio and Congressman Nikolas Ferreira, among others. According to ICL Notícias, an appeals court judge that same day overturned the suspension of the ceremony in which Mayor Bruno Reis presented Flávio with the title of Cidadão Soteropolitano (honorary citizen of Salvador), which critics had challenged as an electoral use of the honor. Times Brasil recalled that in the first round Lula took 65.26% of valid votes in the Bahian capital, against 27.03% for Flávio.

Away from the streets, the dispute advanced at the Superior Electoral Court (TSE). CNN Brasil reported that Flávio’s campaign asked to join as an interested party in the suit that Lula’s (PT) campaign has brought against Meta, owner of Facebook and Instagram. According to the network and Band, the PL’s lawyers argue that the senator is harmed more by the platform than the president is, because negative ads against him would remain online, and ask the court to bar, until the end of the runoff, the paid boosting of negative advertising against either candidate, with a daily fine of R$ 500,000 for noncompliance. IstoÉ Dinheiro and Band reported that Meta answered the TSE by denying any favoritism toward Flávio, called the accusation by the PT campaign unsubstantiated and acknowledged a technical failure of global reach on Oct. 7, which, according to the company, was not aimed at any campaign. Before that, according to Diário do Grande ABC and Times Brasil, Justice André Mendonça had already barred the boosting of attack videos by both campaigns, one decision for each side.

Lobbyist under investigation with Lulinha says she wants a plea deal and will not spare Lula

Businesswoman Roberta Luchsinger, investigated by the Federal Police (PF) in inquiries that also involve Fábio Luís Lula da Silva, known as Lulinha, said in an interview with CNN Brasil on Saturday (17) that she intends to seek a plea deal and does not intend to spare anyone, neither the president nor his son. According to Gazeta do Povo and Revista Oeste, she called Lula a “bad character”, a “liar” and a “coward” and said Lulinha frequently met with ministers. Estadão and Metrópoles also recorded the statement. According to Gazeta do Povo, Luchsinger did not say whether formal negotiations with the PF or the Attorney General’s Office (PGR) are already under way, and the mentions of a Portuguese construction company were presented as conversations she says she overheard, with no documents. The Presidency’s Communication Secretariat denied irregularities and said Lula is unaware of the facts reported. The three inquiries in the case are proceeding at the Supreme Federal Court (STF), and Lulinha’s defense denies any connection to the payments under investigation, according to earlier reports by Correio da Manhã and Revista Oeste.

IFI puts the Lula government’s 2026 package of measures at R$ 298.4 billion

The Independent Fiscal Institution (IFI), linked to the Senate, estimated at R$ 298.4 billion the set of economic measures and benefits announced by the Lula government this year, according to Gazeta do Povo and Times Brasil. Of the total, according to the reports, R$ 93.8 billion has a direct impact on the primary balance and R$ 185.7 billion is subsidized-interest credit lines, which are expected to return to the Union. The IFI itself noted that the figures add up operations of different natures and do not amount to a single additional expense. The list includes the expansion of the income tax exemption, the Bolsa Família increase, credit programs, incentives for microentrepreneurs and fuel subsidies. Folha de S.Paulo published on Saturday (17) its own survey of the package and its effect on public debt. IFI director Alexandre Seijas said the primary surplus target currently in force is not enough to stabilize the debt, which the institution calculates would require a primary surplus of 2.1% of GDP. The government denies the measures have an electoral character, according to Gazeta do Povo.

Trump suspends tariffs and sanctions under new law against Russia, and Zelensky says the move only helps Moscow

US President Donald Trump signed on Friday (16) an order suspending the sanctions and tariffs set out in the law on sanctions against Russia and Iran named for Senator Lindsey Graham, which he himself had signed into law last month, CNBC and NBC News reported. The penalties would have taken effect this Sunday (18). According to CNBC, the law provided for raising tariffs on Russian products by up to 500% and imposing tariffs of up to 100% on countries such as China and India that buy Russian oil; earlier sanctions, based on executive orders, remain in force. NBC reported that Trump defended the decision as “very good” and that it adds to a recent agreement with Vladimir Putin to release Russian diesel onto the world market. Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelensky, called the move wrong, said it only helps the Russians and said he expects further relief, according to CNBC, The Hill and G1.

Cost-of-living protests draw more than half a million in France and end with Limoges city hall in flames

Demonstrations against the cost of living and education cuts brought about 550,000 people onto the streets of France on Saturday (17), according to an Interior Ministry estimate cited by CNN; the CGT union confederation put the figure at 800,000. Most of the rallies were peaceful, but violent groups attacked public buildings and shops, Australia’s ABC reported. In Limoges, according to ABC and G1, masked people burned a municipal police car and then set fire to the city hall, built in 1883, whose roof collapsed. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu reported about 1,200 arrests and 150 police officers injured and blamed the far left, according to i24 and CNN. President Emmanuel Macron promised to punish those responsible. In Lyon, demonstrators set fire to the city’s tourist office, according to CNN.
