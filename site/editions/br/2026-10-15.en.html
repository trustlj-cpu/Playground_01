Quaest gives Flávio 46% and Lula 44% in the first runoff poll, a technical tie

The survey, commissioned by Globo, shows nearly equal rejection rates, 54% and 53%. Datafolha releases its own poll tonight, and the first debate is on Sunday.

Quaest released the first presidential poll of the runoff on Wednesday, October 14, commissioned by Globo and the newspaper O Globo, according to G1. Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) has 46% of voting intentions and Lula (PT) has 44%, report G1, Poder360, UOL and Reuters, which describes the senator as slightly ahead. In the analysis by Felipe Nunes published by G1, Flávio could be between 44% and 48%, and Lula between 42% and 46%, which is two points up or down and amounts to a technical tie. The rejection rate, which measures who says they will not vote for the candidate under any circumstances, is 54% for Flávio and 53% for Lula, according to Valor, which also records a drop in disapproval of the government. The Lula campaign sees a “slight improvement” in the numbers, according to UOL, and bets the debate will be decisive, according to Metrópoles. Among those who cast a blank or spoiled ballot or skipped the first round, 43% say they will vote for Lula, G1 reports. Valor adds that 43% of respondents fear the return of the Bolsonaro family to power more, the same share as those who fear Lula’s reelection more. The sources consulted do not give the number of respondents or the fieldwork dates here.

State polls by Quaest released the same day point in the same direction for Flávio: in Tocantins he has 48% to Lula’s 42%, and in Espírito Santo, 51% to 38%, G1 reports. G1 also records that 10% of Flávio’s first-round voters decided their vote on election day, against 5% among Lula’s. Poder360 says the result confirms PoderData/Aya, an earlier survey that already had Flávio ahead, and Veja details where the votes of candidates who did not make the runoff are going. A poll aggregator cited by UOL puts the gap between the two at 3.6 points, a number that depends on a single source. The poll came out hours before the Senate approved the end of the 6x1 schedule, which the Lula campaign treats as a trump card, according to Valor. The calendar until the vote, on October 25, concentrates the next tests. Datafolha releases a new poll today, early in the evening, G1 reports. Globo interviews the two candidates on the 15th and 16th and holds its debate on October 23, according to G1. The first meeting between Lula and Flávio will be the Band debate, on Sunday the 18th at 8 p.m., in São Paulo, and both have confirmed they will attend, according to Terra and Valor. A poll is a snapshot of the moment, and a technical tie means the observed difference may fit within the margin of error.

Senate approves in two rounds the PEC that ends the 6x1 schedule and cuts the workweek to 40 hours

The Senate on Wednesday, October 14, approved the Proposed Amendment to the Constitution that ends the 6x1 schedule and guarantees two days off a week with no pay cut, report G1, Valor and BBC. To speed up passage, both rounds were voted on the same day, according to G1. In the first round the vote was 75 in favor and one against, that of Senator Hamilton Mourão (Republicanos-RS), report G1 and BBC. The maximum workweek drops from 44 to 40 hours, and the BBC calls the change the biggest since 1988. The text goes to promulgation by Congress, and G1 describes a transition rule, whose deadlines do not appear in the sources consulted. Lula called the approval a “historic achievement” and a “victory for workers,” according to Revista Fórum and G1. Flávio voted in favor, remotely and without making a statement, report G1, Folha and Poder360, and Lula’s team says the conversion is false. The PL leader said the party will push its proposal of pay per hour worked after the election, according to G1 and Folha. Fiesp spoke of a “ballot-box PEC,” Folha reports, and Valor records that the health sector estimates an impact of up to R$ 12 billion.

Stronger US inflation sinks tech in New York and Asian markets; the Ibovespa fell more than 1% at one point and erased its losses

US consumer prices accelerated in September, driven by more expensive gasoline, and the core index, which excludes volatile items, also firmed, Reuters reports. The figure, released on Wednesday, October 14, reinforced bets on further interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve, according to Bloomberg, and US stocks fell, led by technology, report Bloomberg and the Sydney Morning Herald. Fed officials signaled caution: Bowman spoke of waiting and seeing before further moves, and Daly told Reuters that rate hikes will probably be needed, but without urgency. The Beige Book, the Fed’s report on the regional economy, showed that a third of districts saw inflation accelerate, Valor reports. The decline reached Asia overnight: Reuters describes heavy selling of stocks tied to artificial intelligence, and AP says the South Korean and Japanese markets plunged. Reuters also records that the dollar retreated from its 18-month high and that oil fell as supply was reassessed.

In Brazil, the Ibovespa fell more than 1% at one point on Wednesday and ended with no losses, according to InfoMoney, and the spot dollar closed up 0.14%, at R$ 5.0298, according to Reuters as republished by Investing.com. Each of these figures depends on a single outlet, and that is why the boxes at the top of this edition show “—”. The Central Bank announced that on October 19 it will resume traditional currency swap auctions to roll over contracts expiring on November 3, according to a statement from the institution. In New York, Apple closed with a market value above US$ 5 trillion for the first time, report G1 and Infobae. TSMC reported higher profit in the early hours of today on demand for artificial intelligence, the FT reports, and Bloomberg speaks of results above expectations.

Chamber approves MEI debt forgiveness 461 to 4, and the Senate votes today on the Public Security PEC

The Chamber of Deputies on Wednesday, October 14, approved the government bill that forgives debts of individual microentrepreneurs and raises the revenue and employee limit for the category, by 461 votes to 4, report Valor, G1 and Poder360. Valor says the forgiveness reaches about 3.6 million MEIs registered in the federal active debt roll. Folha recalls that the measure was proposed by Lula on the eve of the runoff and, in another report, says the government will have to adjust up to R$ 39.9 billion in the 2027 Budget to accommodate electoral measures, a figure only it provides. The sources consulted do not give the bill’s next steps.

In the Senate, the Constitution and Justice Committee (CCJ) approved the Public Security PEC, which reorganizes the work of the security forces and expands integration among the Union, the states and the municipalities, report G1 and Valor. The plenary is expected to vote on the text today, Thursday, October 15, according to G1 and O Globo, and the government says it intends to create the Ministry of Public Security after approval, according to G1. Also on Wednesday, the Chamber rejected the proposal of rapporteur Marcelo Queiroz (PSDB-RJ) for the bill that changes the bank resolution rules, and Chamber President Hugo Motta (Republicanos-PB) closed the session, Valor reports. The text opened a loophole for a BRB rescue by the FGC, and Folha also records the defeat of the centrão.

Gulf remains under attack, with Aramco headquarters damaged and a ship sunk near Hormuz; Zelensky vows to hit Russian refineries again

Reuters reported on Wednesday, October 14, that the headquarters of Saudi Aramco suffered extensive damage in an attack by the Houthis, according to sources, and that the group vowed more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities. AP recorded accounts from Riyadh residents of new explosions. The same day, Reuters reported that a container ship sank near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile, according to sources; Valor speaks of two ships sunk in the region, and Bloomberg TV covered the case. In the early hours of today, Saudi state TV issued an early warning of a possible threat in Najran and Jazan, according to FinancialJuice, a single source. The effects reach diesel: the International Energy Agency assesses that the shortage may persist until 2027, Reuters reports, and G1 shows stocks “at the limit” among farmers in Rio Grande do Sul. Oil fell overnight as supply worries eased, report Reuters and Bloomberg. Reuters and FinancialJuice cite CNN, according to which the US military is considering using the bomb known as the “mother of all bombs” in possible attacks on Iran.

In Ukraine, Zelensky told the Financial Times that the country will resume attacks on Russian refineries, report Reuters, Bloomberg and FinancialJuice. A refinery in Ryazan caught fire after an overnight drone attack, according to the region’s governor, report Reuters and FinancialJuice. Reuters, citing the FT, also says the US is studying releasing part of the frozen Russian assets to restart peace negotiations. Zelensky also said it is “painful” when Trump chooses another side, according to Reuters. In New York, Wednesday ended lower, and the figure is in this edition’s economy story.
