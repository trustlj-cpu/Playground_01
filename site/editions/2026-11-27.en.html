Meritz Financial Group Vice Chairman Kim Yong-beom to head capital market reform body… staffed entirely by private-sector members, with findings reported straight to the president

A private-sector financier takes charge of the reform body the president ordered. The chair picks the members directly, and the first meeting is to be held soon.

The government has decided to put Kim Yong-beom, vice chairman of Meritz Financial Group, in charge of the capital market reform body that President Lee Jae-myung ordered. The body, provisionally called the “Capital Market Grand Transformation Committee,” will be made up entirely of private-sector members. Vice Chairman Kim, as chair, will choose the members directly, and the findings will go straight to the president. Its job is to identify problems in the capital market through fact-finding surveys and consultation, find reform tasks, propose policies and check progress. At a capital market policy meeting on the 13th of this month, the president told officials to quickly set up a separate body in which private-sector participants, including politicians from the ruling and opposition parties and the financial investment industry, would join to investigate problems and draw up improvement tasks. The appointment follows from that order, and the Financial Services Commission announced it on the 26th after meeting Vice Chairman Kim. It comes right after the KOSPI plunged 6.16% on the 25th to close at 4,697.51.

Vice Chairman Kim put forward as the core principle of the reform that one share must be worth the same whether held by a major shareholder or an ordinary shareholder. A major shareholder holds a large share of a company’s voting rights, while an ordinary shareholder is an investor who bought a small amount of stock on the market; the point is that a single share should be treated the same whichever of them holds it. However, reports differ in wording, some saying “designated” and others “formed,” so it is unclear whether the formal appointment process is complete. The member list, the date of the first meeting, the body’s legal powers, its term of activity and its budget have not been confirmed. The Financial Services Commission said it plans to discuss how to form and run the private-sector committee and then hold a first meeting to launch it as soon as possible.

Police seek advance detention warrant for top scorer in “AI cheating” tax accountant exam… 11 days after the surrender

On the 26th police sought an advance detention warrant for Han (34), the top scorer who surrendered after saying artificial intelligence (AI) was used to cheat on the tax accountant exam. It came 11 days after Han turned in on the 15th of this month. The charge is obstruction of official duties by deception, among others. As the reasons for seeking the warrant, police cited the seriousness of the charge of undermining the fairness of a national exam and its large social impact. Some reports say risk of destroying evidence and flight risk were also weighed, but the two accounts differ, so it is unclear which is the main reason. An advance detention warrant is a procedure in which a request is made to the court first, without an arrest, to rule on detention. In a written statement of surrender, Han is reported to have written of going into the exam hall with a smartphone hidden on the body, photographing the questions with a tiny camera, sending the photos to a laptop at home through a modified calculator and checking the answers produced by generative AI on a tiny monitor. Police secured and analyzed the answer sheets for the first and second rounds and questioned six exam-hall supervisors as reference witnesses. They applied for an overseas travel ban and are also checking through digital forensics on electronic devices whether there were accomplices. The date of the warrant review hearing and whether Han will be detained have not yet been made known.

Rail union’s safe-running campaign on day one: 235 metropolitan trains delayed 20+ minutes… 4 regular trains halted

The National Railway Workers’ Union began its safe-running campaign at 9 a.m. on the 26th, and train service was disrupted from the first day. By Korail’s count, 235 metropolitan commuter trains in the Seoul area ran 20 minutes or more late by 5 p.m., and 4 regular trains stopped running. The halted trains were Mugunghwa trains from Yongsan to Yeosu and to Gwangju, and two ITX-Saemaeul services between Yongsan and Iksan. Of these, the Yeosu-bound Mugunghwa train was canceled after its vehicle was released more than an hour late, leaving 431 ticket holders unable to board. On the evening commute, passengers kept complaining that “the train we waited 30 minutes for was already packed.”

A safe-running campaign is a kind of labor action that, instead of stopping work as in a strike, slows service by observing every rule and safety procedure one by one. The union is demanding better promotion rules, extra allowances and more staff, and the campaign is set to run through the 4th of next month. Korail activated an emergency transport headquarters and put in extra staff, and passengers on halted trains get a full refund. The scale of delays during the morning commute on the second day, the 27th, and the damage by line have not yet been confirmed.

Judiciary · Business✓ 3 outlets · whether the Supreme Court has decided and scope of second final appeal not confirmed

Chey Tae-won’s side offers to pay all 944 billion won within 7 days of a settlement recommendation becoming final, with 5% annual late-payment interest… Roh So-young’s side refuses a “grace period the ruling never had”

It emerged on the 26th that Chairman Chey Tae-won of SK Group’s side had offered to pay the full 944 billion won in property division owed to Roh So-young, director of Art Center Nabi, within 7 days of the day a settlement recommendation becomes final. This is in the request for a settlement recommendation that Chairman Chey’s lawyers filed with the Supreme Court on the 23rd. The offer carries a condition of 5% annual late-payment interest if the deadline is missed. Applied to 944 billion won, 5% a year comes to about 47.2 billion won a year, or about 130 million won a day.

Director Roh’s side told the court it does not agree to the request and demanded that Chairman Chey withdraw the final appeal. Its reasons: the 7-day grace period was not in the lower court ruling, and interest begins to accrue the day after the ruling becomes final. Director Roh’s side is also reported not to agree to a no-suit clause, a promise not to sue each other again. Chairman Chey’s side explained that 7 days is normal for moving a sum close to 1 trillion won and that there is no intent to delay on purpose. One report said Chairman Chey has filed a second final appeal over 244 billion won of the sum, but this was not confirmed elsewhere. Whether the Supreme Court has issued a settlement recommendation is not known.

Politics · Appointments✓ 3 outlets · hearing schedule not confirmed

President Lee signs off on confirmation hearing requests for 5, including NIS director nominee Kang Keum-sil… National Assembly must finish hearings within 20 days of receipt

On the 26th President Lee Jae-myung approved confirmation hearing requests for NIS director nominee Kang Keum-sil and the four minister nominees named recently. The five are Kang Keum-sil for director of the National Intelligence Service, Lim Woong-soon for Foreign Affairs, Hong Ik-pyo for Unification, Han Jun-ho for Interior and Safety, and Kang Yu-jung for Culture, Sports and Tourism. They were named on the 23rd. Under the Confirmation Hearing Act, the National Assembly must finish the hearing process within 20 days of receiving the request. There is a report that if Kang completes the hearing and takes office, Kang would become the first woman to head the National Intelligence Service, but this historic significance was confirmed in only one place.

Hearing dates and committee schedules have not been reported as set. The opposition has already begun its attack. Rep. Kim Jang-gyeom called the nomination of Han Jun-ho a “shield against investigations” appointment and demanded it be withdrawn at once. Kang Keum-sil reported to the hearing preparation office for the first time that day but did not answer reporters’ questions. It was also pointed out that the hearings could overlap with the deadline for passing next year’s budget, but specific hearing dates have not been confirmed.
