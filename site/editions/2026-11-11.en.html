Financial Security Institute hacked too, with some financial firms’ security-assessment data leaked… Institute: “No sensitive information such as resident registration numbers”

The “Continuous Information Security Assessment” site, which shows financial firms’ security status as scores, was breached. The Financial Services Commission demanded a root-cause analysis and measures to prevent a recurrence.

The Financial Security Institute, an agency under the Financial Services Commission that handles the detection of and response to cyber threats against financial firms, was hit by a hacking attack and some information was leaked. The Institute confirmed this on the 9th, cut off access to the system concerned and reported it to the Financial Services Commission and the Korea Internet and Security Agency, among others; the incident became known on the 10th. The breached site is the Continuous Information Security Assessment site, a service that evaluates financial firms’ security status and shows them scores. Because it is a window through which financial firms check their own security level and exchange documents, user information and forms had piled up there. What leaked was identified as about 200 administrative forms, such as sample personal credit information consent forms; 300-odd items of personal information, such as names and dates of birth, contained in those forms; and 350-odd items of system-user information, such as names and mobile phone numbers, in partly obscured (masked) form. The Institute said there was no sensitive customer information such as resident registration numbers or personal credit information.

The incident came as the hacking wave in the financial sector, which began in the banking industry in late September, has run on for more than a month. Major commercial banks, large securities firms and virtual-asset exchanges, among others, had been hit by hacks one after another, and now even the agency that assesses and monitors security for the financial sector has become a target. The Financial Services Commission said it had demanded that the Institute carry out a thorough analysis of the cause and take measures to prevent a recurrence. However, how many of the assessed financial firms had their data taken, and by what route the attacker got in, have not been officially confirmed. The investigation is under way, so the scope of the leak could change. The Institute said the leaked information contained no sensitive data such as resident registration numbers, but how the names and dates of birth in the forms could be used will not be known until the investigation results come out.

Bank of Korea Governor Shin Hyun-song elected chair of the Bank for International Settlements’ Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures… first from outside the U.S. and Europe, three-year term

Bank of Korea Governor Shin Hyun-song was elected chair of the Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures (CPMI) on the 10th (local time) at the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) Global Economy Meeting in Basel, Switzerland. The term is three years. It is the first time someone from a region other than the United States or Europe has chaired the committee. The CPMI is a body under the BIS Governors’ Meeting that sets and manages international standards for the payment and settlement systems through which money moves and for financial market infrastructure. Twenty-eight central banks, including the U.S. Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Bank of England, are members. Governor Shin was elected a BIS board member last May, and in 2023, while working at the BIS, put forward the “unified ledger” concept of issuing and circulating tokenized money and financial assets on a single platform. The Bank of Korea said the election reflects the international community’s recognition of its expertise in digitalization and tokenization. Tokenization means turning assets such as deposits or bonds into digital tokens based on blockchain technology. The specific agenda and schedule the chair will take up have not yet been made public.

Russia–Ukraine peace talks resume in Abu Dhabi after nine months… Zelensky: “constructive and substantive”

U.S.-mediated peace talks between Russia and Ukraine began on the 10th (local time) in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. It is the first time in nine months that three-party talks have been held. The U.S. delegation met separately with Ukrainian and European representatives before the talks, and after the first day’s session, which ran for nearly four hours, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky assessed the talks as “constructive and substantive.” The release of prisoners was mentioned among the topics discussed. A deadly Russian air strike had also taken place a few hours before the talks began.

Asked whether the resumption of Black Sea shipping is on the agenda, the Russian Kremlin said it would not answer while the talks are under way. U.S. President Donald Trump said this week’s Abu Dhabi talks could be a breakthrough. On the postwar security order, the U.S. is reportedly putting forward a new security framework and Europe the strengthening of the existing system. Some observers of the talks say the two sides agreed on a large-scale prisoner exchange, but neither side has officially confirmed it. Nothing on territory or ceasefire terms was settled by the first day’s results.

International · U.S. monetary policy✓ 4 outlets · December rate decision undecided

Fed Governor Waller: “I support a December rate hike”… 50bp ‘big step’ also to be considered if inflation picks up pace

Christopher Waller, a governor of the U.S. Federal Reserve (Fed), said on the 10th (local time) that a vote in favor of a rate hike is planned at the December regular meeting. The baseline idea is a 0.25 percentage point (25bp) hike, and if price increases accelerate, a 0.5 percentage point (50bp) hike should also be considered, Waller said. Raising rates by 0.5 percentage point at once is commonly called a ‘big step.’ Governor Waller said the labor market is solid and supply and demand are broadly in balance, but the breadth of the price increases is a particular worry.

Governor Waller explained that the Fed’s internal analysis points to further rises in producer prices and the possibility that the energy price shock will last longer. Waller said that at least the three so-called ‘insurance cuts’ must be reversed, and drew a line, saying there is no wish to weigh the economy down and that the stance is not excessively hawkish. On the same day another Fed official again stated the need for further hikes. Lorie Logan, president of the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas, said rates must be raised at least two more times. The December decision is set by a vote of the Federal Open Market Committee, and nothing has been finalized yet.

Industry · Semiconductors✓ 3 outlets · November revenue not yet released

TSMC October revenue hits record monthly high of 586.8 billion Taiwan dollars… up 59.7% from a year earlier

Taiwan’s TSMC, the world’s largest foundry (contract chip manufacturer), said on the 10th that its consolidated revenue for October was 586.89 billion Taiwan dollars. That is up 14.7% from the previous month and 59.7% from a year earlier. It also topped the previous monthly high, 514.81 billion Taiwan dollars in August, making it a record on a monthly basis. TSMC is a company that manufactures many advanced-process products, including artificial intelligence (AI) chips.

Cumulative revenue for January–October this year was around 4.49 trillion Taiwan dollars, up more than 43% from the same period a year earlier. This figure is the monthly revenue data that TSMC releases ahead of its full results; quarterly results are announced separately. November revenue is usually released around the 10th of the following month. TSMC’s own explanation of the cause of this increase has not been confirmed.
