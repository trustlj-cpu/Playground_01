Mandelblit begins testifying at Netanyahu trial: “There were serious investigative actions that I did not approve”

The former attorney general, who decided to file the indictments, is testifying for the defense 16 days before the election. Netanyahu sat in the courtroom, and the testimony continues today.

Avichai Mandelblit, the former attorney general who decided in 2019 to indict Benjamin Netanyahu, took the witness stand on October 11 at the Jerusalem District Court. He is testifying for the defense, and according to Israel Hayom seven days of testimony are expected. The prime minister came to the courtroom despite the security situation, and security arrangements became complicated as a result. According to reports, this is the first time Mandelblit has testified in the trial, about seven years after he brought Netanyahu to trial (Haaretz).

The dispute concerns whether the opening of the investigations was approved as required. The defense has argued since the start of the trial that investigative actions in Cases 1000, 2000 and 4000, known as the “Thousands Cases,” began before approval was given as required by law. The prosecution replies that Mandelblit approved the investigation orally (Globes). In his testimony Mandelblit said, according to Haaretz, that in his view there was no need for written documentation of an approval to open an investigation.

But he also admitted, as Israel Hayom and Ynet reported, that there were serious investigative actions he did not approve, and that he canceled one of them because it made his blood boil. Asked by attorney Hadad, one of Netanyahu’s defense lawyers, whether he had said the police acted behind his back, he replied: “It could be.” Even before the testimony began, a confrontation broke out in the corridor between the journalist Ayala Hasson and Mandelblit, who complained about it to the judges (Maariv, Ynet).

Reactions came immediately. In the Likud they called to “arrest him and close the Netanyahu trial” (Ynet), and MK Amir Ohana appealed to President Isaac Herzog to pardon Netanyahu. Maariv also quoted jurists who accused the defense of turning the defendant into the accuser. The testimony takes place 16 days before the election, and Maariv estimated that it would echo outside the courtroom as well.

DailyDrop notes: testimony on direct examination is not a ruling. The court has not yet addressed the defense’s claims, and the quotations here come from reports of the hearing, not from an official transcript. Whether oral approval is sufficient, and what the unapproved actions mean, will be decided in the verdict, not in testimony.

WhatsApp messages attributed to Netanyahu adviser Yonatan Urich leaked online; authenticity not yet verified

On October 11, pirate accounts that published screenshots of conversations attributed to Urich, one of Netanyahu’s close advisers, began to spread on Telegram, WhatsApp and social networks. According to Ynet, if the material is genuine, the conversations were held with seven people. Maariv reported that they include conversations with the commentators Amit Segal and Yaakov Bardugo about coordinating messages and publications, and Haaretz added that photos and videos from the phone of Ambassador Yossi Shelley also leaked. The publishers announced that they would release more material.

Segal replied, according to Israel Hayom, that this is an accepted relationship between a journalist and the person he covers. Urich did not address the authenticity of the material at first, and later threatened lawsuits (Globes). In the background: last month Haaretz exposed a security breach in a media-monitoring tool that Urich built, and Israel Hayom reported that he had connected his private WhatsApp to servers in the United Arab Emirates. DailyDrop does not quote the content of the conversations, which was allegedly stolen and whose authenticity has not been proven.

Houthi attack on Riyadh airport: 12 dead and more than 300 injured; another strike on Sunday

On October 10 a missile hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh. According to Saudi authorities, quoted by AP, 12 people were killed and more than 300 injured. The Houthis, an armed movement in Yemen linked to Iran, claimed responsibility and warned against airports in Saudi Arabia (Reuters). The airport was closed, and Saudi Arabia extended the closure. On October 11 the Saudi-led coalition reported another strike that caused a fire (Haaretz, Bloomberg).

The coalition promised a decisive response (Guardian), and US Secretary of State Rubio condemned the attack and said the United States stands with Saudi Arabia. Netanyahu condemned it as “terrorism,” a step described as unusual (Israel Hayom). Bloomberg warns of a danger that the conflict will widen, and the FT reported that senior oil-company executives, including from BP, withdrew from an oil conference in Riyadh. The Saudi stock exchange fell (Reuters). The casualty figures are Saudi Arabia’s and have not been independently verified.

Trump announces an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine; Kyiv awaits details and Russia keeps attacking

On October 11 Trump announced that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to refrain from attacking energy facilities (Reuters, BBC). According to the BBC, neither Kyiv nor Moscow responded at the time of the announcement. Zelensky said afterward that Ukraine would stop attacking refineries if Russia stopped attacking energy infrastructure, and that it was waiting for details of the proposals; Russia, he said, continues its strikes (Reuters).

Meanwhile the FT reported, according to Reuters, that the United States is threatening to block weapons supplies to Ukraine over the strikes on refineries. Trump said recently that Ukraine should have a new president, and Zelensky told ABC he would hold elections if Russia agreed to a 60-day ceasefire. Germany backed Zelensky, and a senior Republican senator said Trump’s Russian diesel deal could be illegal (Reuters). DailyDrop cannot confirm that the pause has taken effect.

15 days to the election: Kan 11 poll gives 53 seats to the Netanyahu bloc and 50 to the Eisenkot bloc; surplus-vote agreements are signed

A Kan 11 News poll, published on October 11, gave the Netanyahu bloc 53 seats and the Eisenkot bloc 50, a drop for the latter (Haaretz, Kan 11). Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar! remains the largest party, ahead of the Likud, according to Maariv. Ben-Gvir’s Otzma Yehudit rose to nine seats from eight last week and ranks fourth, ahead of Yair Golan; Shas, Religious Zionism and Yisrael Beytenu each lost a seat. Ofer Winter’s party passed the electoral threshold.

Surplus-vote agreements, agreements between parties that pass each other surplus votes so as not to lose a seat, have been signed in recent days. Shas and United Torah Judaism signed such an agreement (Haaretz), and Ynet sums up that the Change bloc has closed all the corners, that the Haredi and Arab parties have reached understandings, and that Netanyahu and Smotrich have joined hands. A question remains whether Ben-Gvir will go with Winter. Winter published a draft plan under which up to 25% of the Haredi draft cohort would be recognized as Torah students (Ynet). A single poll is a snapshot, not a forecast.
