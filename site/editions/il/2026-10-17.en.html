Israel is preparing for a possible strike on Iran, and Netanyahu convened a security discussion

According to Globes, the prime minister spoke with US Secretary of State Rubio and convened the heads of the defense establishment. Security sources in Israel assess that a strike plan is taking shape, and an adviser to Netanyahu also says Israel is “very close.”

In recent hours, reports in Israel have intensified that a strike on Iran is being seriously considered. According to Globes, which published the item on Friday evening, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu spoke with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and then convened a security discussion with the heads of the defense establishment. The article cites an assessment in Israel that a strike plan is already taking shape, and describes military moves identified in Iran, alongside ongoing dialogue between Israel and the Americans.

At the same time, Maariv quotes a senior adviser to Netanyahu, who said that Israel is “very close” to action on the Iranian issue. Such a statement is not a decision, and it gives no detail on timing, targets or scope. The two sources, Globes and Maariv, agree on the general direction: intense security discussion and an expectation of change. Neither reports a final decision, and there is no official announcement from the Prime Minister’s Office or the IDF confirming a strike.

It is important to distinguish between three levels of information. The first is facts that can be verified: the call with Rubio and the security discussion. The second is assessments by officials in Israel, which the press presents as a mood and not as a datum. The third is speculation about the timing and manner of action, for which there is not yet a source that can be relied on. Reports of this kind also tend to serve political aims, such as pressure on Tehran or on Washington, so it is worth reading them with caution.

Meanwhile the regional arena continues to develop. The BBC reported that the number of reports of attacks on ships near the Strait of Hormuz has reached a peak and that oil exports are slowing, while President Trump said Israel had helped Saudi Arabia in the war against the Houthis. All of this reinforces the sense of a region under high tension, but none of it proves a direct link between the events.

What remains open: whether there is a decision, when, and in what coordination with the United States. Anyone looking for answers will find mostly questions today. We will keep following official announcements, and will publish only what is confirmed by more than one source.

Trump: Abraham Accords are back on the table, and Israel helped Saudi Arabia against the Houthis

In a conversation with reporters at the White House, President Trump said that the “Abraham Accords” with Saudi Arabia were back on the table, and that Israel had helped Saudi Arabia in the war against the Houthis. The remarks were published by Ynet, Haaretz, Maariv, Israel Hayom and the Jerusalem Post, and all of them quote the president along the same lines: Saudi Arabia appreciates the Israeli support, and he is optimistic about its joining normalization agreements.

Haaretz adds that a diplomatic source told it this week that Netanyahu is trying, with American mediation, to persuade Saudi Arabia to allow Israel to intervene in the war in Yemen. Maariv presents this from another angle: whether the Houthi war will bring Israel closer to normalization. Here too this is analysis and not fact, and the material reviewed contains no Saudi statement confirming Trump’s words.

The meaning for the Israeli reader is twofold. On the one hand, if Saudi Arabia does join the agreements, it would be an enormous strategic change. On the other hand, statements by an American president about future agreements have been heard many times before, and in practice the process depends on Saudi conditions that also touch on the Palestinian situation. We will keep checking whether confirmation arrives from Riyadh.

New White House spokeswoman fired after five days

Katie Zacharia, who started on Monday as White House spokeswoman in place of Karoline Leavitt, was fired by President Trump after less than a week. Ynet, Haaretz, Calcalist, Zman Yisrael and Maariv reported it on Friday, and three of them note that the firing came after a single press briefing.

According to Haaretz and Ynet, Zacharia presented herself more than once as a lawyer, but at the briefing admitted that she had not passed the bar exams. This caused an uproar, and Maariv quotes the president saying “frankly” that her time was up. The precise details of the decision come from press reports and not from an official announcement that was checked.

The incident adds to a sense of instability around the administration ahead of the midterm elections. Haaretz reports that in the United States they are bracing for a blue wave in Congress, amid public anger at Trump. It is not yet known whether the spokeswoman’s firing will affect the campaign, but it provides an uncomfortable headline for the White House.

Ahead of the election: Netanyahu, Ben-Gvir and Bennett in a public dispute

Maariv quotes Bennett on Netanyahu: “This is the first time I ask myself — what is going on here?” Israel Hayom reports a message from Netanyahu to National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir, according to which he “will not be National Security Minister again,” and notes that Israel’s ambassador to the United States, Yechiel Leiter, told CNN that Netanyahu wants a broad unity government after the election. This is a single-source report, and so it is not a proven fact.

Ynet reports on the “Big Party” campaign of Netanyahu and Eisenkot, while Globes and TheMarker address the prime minister’s conduct regarding the October 7 investigation. TheMarker quotes Netanyahu on Channel 14, who said that he does not know whom the Egyptian intelligence representative met before October 7, and that he wants to know the truth.

These are political claims in the midst of an election period, and not data. The reader needs to distinguish between what was actually said and the newspapers’ interpretation.

Wall Street slides and bonds swing, oil climbs

Globes reported on Friday sharp declines on Wall Street: the Nasdaq lost about 2% and the fear index soared, and US 10-year government bond yields fell this week to about 5.09%, compared with 5.31% at the peak. The report was written before the close of trading, and so it contains no verified closing values.

The day before, according to TheMarker, the Dow Jones added about 1% amid a pullback in chip stocks, as the producer price index in the United States recorded the sharpest rise since 2022. Britain’s economy grew more than expected for the fourth consecutive month.

At this stage we will not present market indexes as final data. We will check the close and update in the next issue.
