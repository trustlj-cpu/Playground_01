Report: about 1,240 aircrew from countries with no relations with Israel entered the country in the past year

The figure was presented in a closed discussion in a Knesset subcommittee after the flydubai affair. At the same time, it was reported that a US delegation will examine Israelis’ visa waiver for the United States.

Israeli media reported on Tuesday, citing a closed discussion in a Knesset subcommittee, that about 1,240 aircrew members of foreign airlines, among them pilots and co-pilots from Syria, Lebanon and Oman, entered Israel over the past year. The report appeared in the Times of Israel, ynet, Haaretz, All Israel News and JFeed. The figure was not published officially, so it is presented here as a report only.

Channel 12 reported, as carried in the Times of Israel, that a US delegation will arrive in the coming days to check whether Israel meets the security requirements of the visa waiver program, which Israel joined in 2023. One of the requirements is that Israel receive and check information on passengers and aircrew entering the country. The US State Department said it does not comment in advance on possible steps.

The Times of Israel also reported, citing a report that was not officially confirmed, that the Air Force launched fighter jets during the affair and closed the airspace, and that they were called back when it became clear the plane was heading for Saudi Arabia. DailyDrop has seen no official confirmation of these details, and they remain reports.

Zelensky hosts an Israeli delegation in Kyiv and calls for close relations; warns of Iranian-Russian drones

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky hosted an Israeli delegation in Kyiv on Tuesday, led by Ze’ev Elkin, which came for a ceremony marking 85 years since the Babi Yar massacre. The Times of Israel and the Jerusalem Post reported this. Zelensky called for strong relations between the two countries.

According to the reports, Zelensky warned the delegation that the drones Iran is developing together with Russia, along with the operational experience the two are gaining, could also become a threat to Israel. He said Russia received the technology to produce these systems from Iran. Overnight Kyiv was attacked with ballistic missiles and drones, and the delegation stayed in a temporary shelter set up by the Israeli embassy.

Wall Street at a new record: S&P 500 closes at 7,818.93, up 0.58%

The S&P 500 closed on Tuesday at 7,818.93, up 0.58%, breaking its previous record from August, the Washington Post, TheStreet and Yahoo Finance reported. The Dow Jones index rose 0.49% to 51,521.28, and the Nasdaq rose 0.4% to a new record. Technology led the gain.

The oil price barely changed: WTI closed at $89.44 a barrel, up 0.01%. Gold rose 0.73% to $4,187.1 an ounce. Analysts quoted in the reports attributed the moves to expectations about global monetary policy and to the war between the United States and Iran, as well as strong earnings reports.

TA-35 falls 0.59% to 4,198.81, led by declines in defense, communications, insurance and bank stocks

The TA-35 index closed on Tuesday at 4,198.81, down 0.59%, according to reports from Investing.com and ad-hoc-news. According to the report, the declines were concentrated in defense, communications, insurance and bank stocks. On Monday the index rose about 0.13% (by calculation).

Remembrance · SecuritySingle sources ✓ Haaretz and Times of Israel · every item attributed

On the eve of the October 7 anniversary: a former intelligence officer in Southern Command appeals his dismissal, and hundreds of protests are planned worldwide

Haaretz reported on October 6 that Colonel Ari Amidror, the Gaza Division’s intelligence officer on October 7, 2023, is appealing his dismissal from the army and claims he did not bear responsibility for the warning that was missed. The article is behind a paywall and only the opening paragraphs were visible, so the details of the appeal remain unknown.

The Times of Israel reported, on behalf of a ministry, on more than 260 protests against Israel planned around the anniversary, and on an interfaith organization raising 5.2 million Israeli flags around the world in memory of the victims. The decision on the question of responsibility remains with a state commission of inquiry that has not yet been established; the opposition has pledged to set one up if it wins the election.
