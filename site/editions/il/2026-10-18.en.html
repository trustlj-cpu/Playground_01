News · Judea and SamariaPress ✓ 3 outlets · the full text of the Prime Minister’s Office statement was not published

Jewish rioters attacked European diplomats and an American journalist; the president apologized and warned of settler violence

Footage from the West Bank sparked a dispute between the President’s Residence and the Prime Minister’s Office, and in Tel Aviv people demonstrated demanding a state commission of inquiry.

At the end of the weekend, several reports of settler violence in Judea and Samaria appeared in Israel, and together they rose to the top of the agenda. The Ynet website published footage of Jewish rioters attacking European diplomats near a Christian village in the West Bank, and the item also appeared among the top headlines of Google News Israel. According to the headline of another article on the site, a diplomat who took part in the tour said that “the rioters kicked the mayor in the head” and that Netanyahu’s statement was “shocking.” These are the words of one person, and it was not reported how the Prime Minister’s Office responded to the quote itself.

Ynet also reported on an American photographer who underwent surgery and whose finger was nearly severed after an attack by Jewish rioters. Haaretz English wrote that President Isaac Herzog apologized to the Washington Post photographer, condemned settler violence and called for those responsible to be brought to justice. According to the same report, the Prime Minister’s Office, for its part, issued a warning, and its full content is not given in the summary that was published. That is, the President’s Residence and Netanyahu’s office adopted two different versions of the same event.

The report on the American photographer added an international dimension: Ynet said she underwent surgery in a hospital, and that the injury to her finger was severe enough to risk amputation. Haaretz English reports that Herzog’s apology was directed at her, and that the President’s Residence called for those responsible to be brought to justice. These statements were made, according to the report, in parallel with the statement from the Prime Minister’s Office, and the two messages were heard in close proximity and in different wordings.

On Saturday evening, demonstrators arrived at Habima Square in Tel Aviv demanding the establishment of a state commission of inquiry, against the background of the defamation suit filed by the former Shin Bet chief against Netanyahu. A former police commissioner who spoke there said, as quoted by Ynet, that “settler violence is spreading like a cancer in the body of the nation.” The same day Ynet published footage from the southern Hebron Hills in which masked men steal a flock of sheep from Palestinians in front of soldiers. According to the report, the soldiers who were there ignored what happened, and acted only afterward.

It is important to distinguish between what is documented and what is said. The attack on the diplomats, the injury to the photographer and the theft in the Hebron Hills are documented in the media reports, and Herzog’s apology was published in his name. By contrast, the quote about a kick to the mayor’s head comes from a single diplomat, and there is not yet an official statement from Israel or the European Union that confirmed its details. The political reactions are also still partial, and the work week that begins today will show whether police complaints are filed or whether ambassadors are summoned for clarification talks.

Whether this is a new escalation or an isolated outburst remains an open question, because cumulative incident data were not published in the reports at hand. What is clear is that the issue has entered the discourse in the days of an election campaign, and criticism of settler violence has been heard in the past week both from the President’s Residence and from the Tel Aviv demonstration. In the coming days it is worth following statements from the army and the police, and checking whether they are accompanied by open investigations and names of suspects. Until then, the video footage is the only fact that no one disputes.

Politics · Election campaignPress ✓ 4 outlets · the remarks about the government’s composition are attributed to the ambassador and were not confirmed by Netanyahu

Liberman goes on the attack against Eisenkot, Bennett turns to the right, and Israel’s ambassador talks of a unity government without Ben-Gvir

Political headlines over the weekend revolved around one question: who will sit with whom after the election. According to Maariv, Israel’s ambassador to the US, Yechiel Leiter, said in an interview with CNN that the prime minister wants a broad unity government after the election. Maariv headlined it “Netanyahu plans a government with Eisenkot, without Ben-Gvir,” and Israel Hayom published that the message to Ben-Gvir is “he will not be National Security Minister again.” Both papers attribute the remarks to the ambassador, and we have no statement from Netanyahu himself.

Avigdor Liberman responded in two tones. Ynet reported that he is aiming for repeat elections without Netanyahu and quoted him: “We will oust Ohana and pass the defendant law.” A day earlier, according to Maariv, Liberman sharply attacked Gadi Eisenkot and accused him of “vote siphoning,” and then changed his tone and called on people to vote only for four parties. Ynet also attached a headline about Yinon Magal, who attacked Eisenkot with a harsh expression.

Naftali Bennett turned directly to right-wing voters, according to Israel Hayom, and said: “We will not establish a Palestinian state.” The appeal was aimed at voters of the Likud, Religious Zionism and Otzma Yehudit. A Haaretz opinion column wrote that the change camp will not beat Netanyahu with “silk gloves” and needs “boxing gloves”; this is the position of one writer and not a factual finding.

Four outlets reported the exchange of messages, but most of them rely on quotes and headlines. The full text of the interview with Leiter, an official statement from the Prime Minister’s Office and a new poll will help clarify whether this is a declaration or a campaign.

Foreign policy · Washington and the GulfPress ✓ 5 outlets · it was not specified what Israeli aid was given to Saudi Arabia

Trump: Abraham Accords are back on the table, and Israel helped Saudi Arabia in the war against the Houthis

According to Ynet and Haaretz, President Donald Trump said on Friday that the Abraham Accords were back on the table and that Israel had helped Saudi Arabia in the war against the Houthis. According to the headline, this is a single statement linking two issues: the possible expansion of the normalization agreements and the security situation in the south of the Arabian Peninsula. Five outlets published it.

Haaretz added that this week a diplomatic source said that Netanyahu is trying, with US mediation, to persuade Saudi Arabia to allow Israel to intervene in the war. This is information from a single anonymous source that does not appear in the other reports, and so it is presented here as a report only. It was not stated in what way Israel helped Saudi Arabia, whether with intelligence or other aid, and no official Saudi response was given in the material at hand.

In other news from Washington, Haaretz reported that Trump froze a package of sanctions against Russia a few days before it took effect. The package included imposing tariffs on goods from Russia and sanctions on purchasing oil and gas from the country, and the presidential order noted that the freeze serves an interest that the summary does not specify. The text of the order itself was not published in full in the material collected.

For the Israeli reader there are two separate matters here. The first is that the discourse about Saudi Arabia is returning to the agenda in the midst of an election campaign, and it may become an argument in the hands of all camps. The second is the price of oil, which stood at about $99 a barrel at Friday’s close, and is sensitive to any change in sanctions on Russia and to tension in the Gulf.

At this stage, confirmation from Riyadh and the text of the order are missing. Until then it is worth reading the statement as a message from Washington and not as an agreement, and following Saudi reactions.

Economy · Higher educationPress ✓ 5 outlets · the exact amount of the donation and its terms were not fully specified

John Paulson donates a quarter of a billion dollars to the Hebrew University, and the Mount Scopus campus will be named after him

TheMarker, Globes and other outlets reported that American businessman John Paulson is donating a quarter of a billion dollars to the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. According to TheMarker, following the donation the university’s Mount Scopus campus will be named after him. Globes quoted the headline “Solidarity with Israel” that accompanies the announcement.

Globes notes that Paulson’s wealth is estimated at $11.2 billion, and that he became famous for a bet against the American mortgage market, which brought him billions on the eve of the financial crisis. This was given as biographical background, and the summary of the reports does not specify how the sum will be distributed over the years or the purposes to which it will be directed.

The reports did not include a response from the university’s management, and it was not stated whether the donation is conditional on constructing buildings or establishing research institutes. The question of the name, that is, whether the entire campus or only part of it will be named after the donor, is explained only in TheMarker’s headline.

In the donations market, a sum of a quarter of a billion dollars is exceptional even by international standards, and so it may affect the university’s budget and scholarships. The Hebrew University is a public institution, and so the details of the donation are also a matter of public interest.

For readers who want to understand what it means: it is worth waiting for an official announcement from the university, seeing how the money is distributed, and checking whether there will be changes in tuition or scholarships. Until then this is an announcement that is still waiting for details.

Security · October 7 trialsPress ✓ 2 outlets · it was not stated how the government ministries responded to the demand

Ben-Gvir demands cutting about $250 million from government ministries’ budgets in favor of the tribunal for Nukhba terrorists

Haaretz reported on Saturday that National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir is demanding to cut hundreds of millions of shekels from the budgets of other government ministries to fund the military tribunal for the Nukhba criminals who carried out the October 7 massacre. Haaretz’s English version mentions a sum of $250 million. The two reports by the same outlet summarize the same demand in two wordings, and so they should not be seen as separate confirmation.

According to Haaretz, the Prison Service and the Ministry of National Security have not yet begun preparing for the trials, although the law on the matter and its budget were approved in May. The trials themselves, according to the same report, will not begin before 2028. That is, the budget demand refers to a cost that will begin to be invested long before the proceedings start, and not to proceedings under way.

The report does not present a response from the government ministries from which the money is requested, or from the Finance Ministry. It is unclear whether the demand was submitted formally to the government or raised in internal correspondence, and there is no information on when it will be discussed. This is a report by a single outlet, and so we cannot say whether the numbers will be accepted.

The issue touches a wide public: the trial of the Nukhba terrorists depends on a budget, on manpower and on a detention facility. The question of how much of the budget will be directed to this and how much to other ministries stands at the center of an election campaign.
