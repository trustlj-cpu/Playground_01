Trump announces support for Netanyahu nine days before the election, and the prime minister thanks him on X

The US president, who had so far avoided openly backing anyone, posted full support for the prime minister on Sunday, and Netanyahu thanked him on X.

President Donald Trump announced on Sunday, October 18, his support for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, nine days before Israel’s election, to be held on October 27, according to reports by Al Jazeera, France 24 and Axios. According to the Forward and the Jerusalem Post, in a post on Truth Social Trump described Netanyahu as a strong leader and a partner in the confrontation with Iran, and declared full support for him. Fox News alone reported that Trump said that if Israel chooses a different leader, the United States would have to finish the job against Iran alone, and this remark was not confirmed by another outlet. Globes wrote that for many weeks the president had avoided expressing support for any of the candidates, and Ynet and Haaretz wrote in their headlines that Trump is intervening in the election for the first time in favor of Netanyahu.

Al Jazeera reported that Trump had earlier declined, in an interview with Time, to express support for Netanyahu, and AP reported at the beginning of the month that, asked about it, he said: “I can only say that I am no longer involved in politics.” Axios wrote that this is the first time a US president has openly backed a candidate in an Israeli election. Netanyahu thanked Trump on X, France 24 reported. According to NBC News, public support for Trump in Israel has fallen following disagreements over Iran, so the endorsement may not give Netanyahu a significant boost. Axios said the polls do not point to a clear path to power, and that in most of them Netanyahu’s coalition is below the 61 seats needed to form a government.

Netanyahu warns Israel may face a hostile US administration and plans an additional roughly 450 billion shekels in defense spending

Ynet reported on Sunday, nine days before the election, that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel may in the future have to deal with a hostile American administration, which would threaten military aid and the strategic relationship with Washington. According to the report, he called for building new alliances, including with India and with countries in Latin America, and said he intends to allocate about 450 billion additional shekels to defense spending over the coming decade. Ynet wrote that Netanyahu presented this as a reason to use the remaining years of President Trump’s term to strengthen Israel’s standing. The JFeed site said Netanyahu told security officials that the goals he set do not depend on American consent, and that in an interview with i24NEWS he said all of Israel’s enemies want him to lose the election. The reports do not specify in which areas the money would be invested or what the source of funding is.

Ben-Gvir claims Netanyahu promised the Europeans to keep him out of the next government; Netanyahu denies it and says he will form a coalition with his party

Haaretz and the Times of Israel reported on Sunday that National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir claimed the prime minister promised European officials that his party, Otzma Yehudit, would not be part of the next government. According to Ben-Gvir, messages on the matter reached him from the Prime Minister’s Office. Netanyahu denied the claim, according to Haaretz, and told reporters he intends to form a coalition with Ben-Gvir and Smotrich after the election. Ynet reported that Netanyahu reassured Ben-Gvir that he would remain in the government, and that if he forms the next government it will be based on the current coalition and on the principles of the current government.

Separately, the Times of Israel and Middle East Monitor reported that Netanyahu ruled out appointing Ben-Gvir as defense minister or as National Security Minister in the next government. Otzma Yehudit said in response that Netanyahu’s intention to leave the Interior, National Security and Justice ministries in Likud hands indicates a plan to push it out of the coalition and bring in Gadi Eisenkot. The Jerusalem Post reported that people around Ben-Gvir estimate that Netanyahu made an initial decision to try to form a broad national government, even at the price of leaving Ben-Gvir outside the coalition.

Foreign policy · Judea and SamariaPress ✓ 4 outlets · the exact time of the incident and the text of the official statement were not given

EU condemns attack on European diplomats near Taybeh and demands accountability; Netanyahu’s office: convincing evidence is required

The European Union’s foreign policy spokesman said on Sunday that the Union strongly condemns attacks by violent settlers in the West Bank, including attacks on European diplomats, according to Haaretz English, the Jerusalem Post and Anadolu. The statement came a day after Belgian Foreign Minister Maxime Prévot condemned settler attacks on Palestinians, European diplomats and a French mayor. According to the reports, the incident took place near Taybeh, where masked men confronted a delegation of European representatives and local activists who were watching the olive harvest, and a window of one of the Belgian consular vehicles was smashed. Army Radio reported that army and police forces arrived at the scene three hours after they were called.

The European Union said it notes the condemnation by Israeli officials, and expects full accountability and steps to prevent further violence against Palestinians and journalists. It called on Israel to act in accordance with international humanitarian law and to stop the illegal expansion of settlements. The Prime Minister’s Office said that the more serious the accusation, the more convincing evidence is required, and added that violence against diplomats is unacceptable.

Electricity Authority limits each new server farm to 200 megawatts, as developers request connections of 27,000 megawatts

Globes reported on Sunday that the plenum of the Electricity Authority decided that each new server farm would be limited to 200 megawatts. Calcalist reported separately that the Authority is working to limit each new data center to that size, and that the draft decision is expected to be released for public comment. According to Calcalist, developers requested grid connections totaling 27,000 megawatts, far beyond the country’s installed generation capacity. Calcalist said that planned projects such as Mega DC’s farm in Hadera, at 1,100 megawatts, and SDS’s in Nesher, at 875 megawatts, do not fit the limit. Calcalist and Runtime Wire also reported that the Authority instructed Noga, the grid operator, to stop handling new requests to connect server farms for 140 days, in projects requesting 8 megawatts or more.
