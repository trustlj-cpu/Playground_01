The flydubai affair: co-pilot reportedly planned to crash at Ben Gurion Airport; Israel assesses that the rest of the crew was not involved

The investigation has widened beyond the Middle East, and in Israel the dispute continues between the Transport Ministry and Shin Bet over who was supposed to vet the pilot.

On Monday NPR reported, citing the findings of the investigation, that the co-pilot of flydubai flight FZ1073 intended to crash the plane at Ben Gurion Airport. The motive is still unknown. The report adds to the words of the attorney general of the United Arab Emirates, who called the incident a terror attack, and to Prime Minister Netanyahu’s assessment that the suspect had undergone Islamist radicalization. Trump hinted at a link to Iran but presented no details, the Washington Post reported.

According to the Washington Post, the investigation has widened from the Middle East to New Zealand as well, and the authorities are trying to understand how a person who had been flagged in Oman over extremist views was allowed to fly. The Times of Israel reported that Israel does not believe other crew members were involved, and that Shin Bet agents are taking part in the investigation in the Emirates. The Transport Ministry issued a new security directive for foreign flights.

In Israel, where aircraft from the Emirates have been landing since the Abraham Accords, the sparring over responsibility goes on. The AP agency reported at the end of the week that the Emirates had passed the pilots’ names and citizenship to Israel before the flight, and that Israel raised no suspicion. Shin Bet placed responsibility on the Transport Ministry, and Minister Miri Regev wrote on X that the security bodies are responsible for flight security and that the ministry had received no warnings. Channel 12 reported, according to the Times of Israel, that Regev had ignored three earlier warnings; this was not confirmed by a second source and therefore appears here as a report only.

21 days to the election: polls are tight and the campaigns brace for the final week

A Zman Yisrael poll published on October 2 gave the Likud and Yashar!, led by Gadi Eisenkot, 21 seats each, and the bloc backing Netanyahu 53 seats out of 120. In a September poll published in Haaretz, Eisenkot’s bloc was ahead of Netanyahu’s bloc, 57 to 51. The Jerusalem Post wrote that Eisenkot still leads in most polls, but Netanyahu is narrowing the gap.

On October 5 Haaretz published a review of campaign strategies in the closing weeks, according to which neither bloc holds a majority without the Arab parties, and the outcome may depend on voter turnout. DailyDrop reminds readers that polls are a snapshot, not a forecast, and that the gap between channels stems, among other things, from different sampling methods.

Iran: Strait of Hormuz to stay closed until the US accepts the conditions; Trump rejected the offer

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said at the end of the week that Tehran’s position has not changed: the strait will remain closed until Washington meets the conditions Iran has presented, Al Jazeera reported. The speaker of the Iranian parliament spoke of seven conditions based on the Islamabad Memorandum.

According to the reports, Trump rejected the Iranian offer, and an official in the US administration described the indirect contacts as positive and useful but said there will be no agreement unless the nuclear issue is addressed. Qatar and Pakistan are mediating. DailyDrop has seen no independent confirmation of the wording of the conditions, so they are presented as an Iranian position only.

Lebanon · SecurityPress ✓ 3 outlets · shelling reports attributed to Lebanese state media

Israel continued shelling in south Lebanon; Netanyahu: we will keep striking Hezbollah and look for an opportunity for peace

According to tracking by the US Council on Foreign Relations (CFR), based on Lebanese state media, the villages of Haddatha and Bar’ashit in south Lebanon were shelled on October 5. Netanyahu said the same day that Israel will keep striking Hezbollah while trying to create an opportunity for peace with Lebanon. No casualty figures for the day were found.

The fighting is taking place under a ceasefire dating from June 19. According to Long War Journal, the IDF is shifting in south Lebanon to a forward-defense posture and preparing for winter, and ynet reported, citing security officials, that Hezbollah used the period to prepare the ground. DailyDrop stresses that these are reports from different sides with no independent confirmation from the field.
