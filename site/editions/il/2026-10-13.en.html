14-year-old boy killed when a dud explodes on a school trip near the Lebanon border; 24 injured

The education minister halted trips in the north for 48 hours. The IDF assesses that it was a bomblet from a cluster bomb that failed to explode, and its origin is still under review.

On the afternoon of October 12, a dud, that is, a munition that failed to explode at the time, exploded on the annual trip of students of the Ort Maalot yeshiva high school on the Ramim Ridge, not far from the Lebanon border. A 14-year-old boy, an eighth-grader, was killed. According to Israel’s ambulance service, quoted by Reuters, 24 people were injured, students and teachers, and according to Haaretz and Ynet three of them seriously. In the first hours about 20 injured were reported (Haaretz, Ynet), and the number rose later to 24 (Reuters, Globes, Maariv).

In a preliminary check by soldiers at the scene, the IDF assessed that the explosion was most likely caused by a bomblet from a cluster bomb that had not exploded (IDF statement on Telegram). According to Globes and Haaretz, it was a Hezbollah bomblet left in the field since the war. Police said, according to Haaretz, that the blast occurred after a student put the object in his bag. Ynet reported that the IDF is checking whether the object was left in the field by security forces or was fired by Hezbollah and did not explode, since the area once served as an army staging area.

At the hospital they described, according to Ynet, heroic treatment at the scene: one boy performed resuscitation on his friend, and another asked his father, in a video call, for instructions on a tourniquet. Ynet also reported that the boys held the object for more than an hour and were given no instruction on exposure to ordnance, and Israel Hayom quotes witnesses: “They took photos with the dud, and it exploded in the bag.” According to Ynet and Maariv, a student found the dud and put it in his bag; the injured were evacuated, among other places, to the Galilee Medical Center.

Prime Minister Netanyahu visited the wounded at Rambam, and a 14-year-old boy told him, according to Ynet: “I saw a friend hurt, I tried to help him.” Michael Shoshan, a teacher at the yeshiva who lost his son in the war, told Israel Hayom: “We have not yet recovered from the loss of my son Yair, we do not know how to contain this.”

Education Minister Kisch said the trip and the route had been approved in advance, and according to Maariv the briefing included a warning about duds. He announced a halt to trips on the northern border for 48 hours and a re-examination of the guidelines (Ynet, Maariv). Globes reports that the Knesset subcommittee on mine clearance and duds, chaired by MK Nissim Vaturi, has convened only twice. Israel Hayom asks who will investigate the disaster: the police, the IDF, the Education Ministry or a government commission, and it has not yet been said who will do so.

Background: a cluster bomb carries inside it dozens or hundreds of small bomblets and scatters them over a wide area. Some of the bomblets do not explode on impact, and then stay on the ground for years and sometimes look like a small, unimportant metal object. Any touch is dangerous, including by someone who wants to photograph it or show it to friends. So the usual instruction in such areas is to stay away from any suspicious object and notify the security forces.

DailyDrop notes: the boy’s name was not given in the reports available to us and we are not publishing it. The origin of the bomblet, IDF or Hezbollah, is still under review. The casualty numbers changed during the day, and the later number, 24, appears here. Questions of responsibility are open: who approved the route, who is responsible for clearing the area, and what the escorts knew about the danger.

Kfar Saba mayor and his wife in custody on suspicion of bribery; court extends their detention by eight days

Kfar Saba Mayor Rafi Saar and his wife Meital are in custody on suspicion of cash bribery and tender rigging (Maariv, which called the affair “the Sharon bribery affair”). On October 12 the court extended their detention by eight days (Haaretz). The suspicions are bribery, fraud and breach of trust, money laundering and tax offenses. A police representative said at the hearing, according to Haaretz, that the mayor’s wife “exploited her status to advance interests and receive bribe money.” According to Israel Hayom, the police claim that in recent days she acted in obstruction of the investigation.

Maariv and Israel Hayom quote the police, according to whom the wife was a “central axis” in the affair and “had a hand in everything.” The judge determined, according to Israel Hayom, that “the suspicion is of high intensity.” According to Maariv, the suspicion against Meital Saar is stronger than the suspicion against her husband, and Israel Hayom adds that it was determined that Rafi Saar knew about the receipt of the funds and acted at his wife’s demand. Maariv also reports luxury watches, bags and cash seized in their home, a detail reported by a single source. An extension of detention is not a ruling: it establishes that there is reasonable suspicion and that the investigation needs more time. The police are also checking, according to Maariv, whether the couple’s lifestyle exceeded the financial means they reported. The couple are suspects and have not been charged, and they are entitled to the presumption of innocence. What is worth following: whether the detention will be extended again, whether an indictment will be filed, and who will actually run the municipality in the meantime.

At Netanyahu trial: Mandelblit’s testimony halted by shouting between the defense lawyer and the prosecutor, then resumed

On October 12, the second day of testimony by Avichai Mandelblit, the former attorney general, the hearing was halted after an exchange of shouts between attorney Hadad, one of Netanyahu’s defense lawyers, and prosecutor Tirosh (Israel Hayom). The testimony resumed (Haaretz). The defense lawyer argued that information about a search for material against the prime minister was not brought to Mandelblit’s knowledge; he replied: “If that was done, it is serious,” and added that he would be surprised if it turned out that information had been deliberately concealed from him (Israel Hayom).

The day before, Mandelblit said there was no need for written documentation of an approval to open an investigation (Haaretz). MK Moshe Saada, a former senior official at the Police Investigations Department, said, “I told them ‘you are criminals,’ yesterday it turns out I was right,” and Nir Hefetz, a former Netanyahu spokesman, said he cannot determine whether there were illegal investigative actions (Maariv). The quotations are from reports of the hearing, not from an official transcript, and testimony is not a ruling.

World · IranPress ✓ 6 outlets · Iran denies

Britain: Revolutionary Guards very likely behind plot against Royal Air Force base Fairford; Tehran denies

On October 12 UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said that a suspected plot against the Royal Air Force base Fairford was “very likely” coordinated in Iran, and almost certainly carried out by the Revolutionary Guards (Reuters). According to DW, Burnham spoke in Parliament of “strong indications,” the government summoned Iran’s ambassador, and the incident led to the withdrawal of US Air Force B-1 bombers from the base. Tehran denied it and called the claims “baseless and malicious conjecture” (Haaretz).

At the same time Iran said, according to Reuters, that it would reply to the American proposal on the Strait of Hormuz after it finishes reviewing Washington’s response. On Sunday two ships were attacked in the area, and two Indians are missing (The Hindu, Antara). DailyDrop notes: the British accusation is the prime minister’s, and no evidence has been published. Who carried out the attacks in Hormuz was not determined in the reports available to us.

Politics · ElectionsPress ✓ 2 outlets · the poll is attributed to Channel 12 News

14 days to the election: Channel 12 News poll gives the Eisenkot bloc 54 seats, but Netanyahu’s opponents 67 in total

A Channel 12 News poll, published on the evening of October 12, estimated a majority of 67 seats for Netanyahu’s opponents (Haaretz). However, a boycott of the Arab parties by some of the parties in the Eisenkot bloc leaves the bloc itself with 54 seats, fewer than 60. Ofer Winter’s party is still far from the electoral threshold (Haaretz). Maariv wrote in a headline that “Netanyahu’s campaign is working” and called on Winter to make a “fateful decision”: a different reading from Haaretz’s of the same data.

The electoral threshold, about 3.25% of the votes, is the line below which a party does not enter the Knesset, and its votes are lost. The election is on October 27. DailyDrop notes: a single poll is a snapshot, not a forecast, and the number of seats of the Netanyahu bloc in this poll was not published in the reports available to us.
