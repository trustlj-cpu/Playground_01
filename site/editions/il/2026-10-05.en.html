After the flydubai incident: Netanyahu orders a full review of security procedures on foreign flights, and the opposition demands that Minister Regev be dismissed

The Transport Ministry confirmed on Saturday that it does not itself vet foreign pilots. According to reports, the Omani co-pilot planned to crash the plane at Ben Gurion Airport.

On Sunday, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered a thorough review of security procedures on foreign flights to Israel, the Times of Israel reported. The order came four days after the incident of September 30 on flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv. According to the reports, the co-pilot, an Omani citizen identified as Hamam al-Hamami, attacked the captain with an emergency axe and tried to take control of the plane. The UAE attorney general called the incident a terror attack, NBC News reported, and the investigation is in the hands of investigators from the Emirates and Israel.

On Saturday evening the Transport Ministry confirmed, according to reports in Haaretz and elsewhere, that it does not carry out independent background checks on foreign pilots and relies on the airlines. Following this, a letter was sent to foreign airlines, including American ones, which according to the Times of Israel concerns citizens of 27 countries. The details were provided by sources only and were not published officially. According to JFeed, the ministry has no way to verify that the airlines comply with the rules.

At the same time, Israeli and American media reported, citing unidentified sources, that the pilot planned to bring the plane down on the airport or on a building, and that he refrained from a similar attack on an earlier flight in July, as CBC and other outlets were told. Neither Israel nor the UAE has confirmed this officially. The Mossad, Shin Bet and the IDF are jointly examining how he was allowed to fly to Israel, the Jerusalem Post wrote. According to Channel 7, flydubai suspended its flights to Israel for six days.

Opposition leaders Gadi Eisenkot and Naftali Bennett demanded the dismissal of Transport Minister Miri Regev. Bennett blamed Netanyahu and Regev for direct responsibility, and Eisenkot linked the affair to the government’s refusal to set up a state commission of inquiry. Regev rejected responsibility; according to her, Shin Bet is responsible for Israeli flights and the Mossad for foreign airlines. DailyDrop has not seen the results of the investigation at this stage, so the motive, the planning and possible accomplices remain in the realm of reports only.

Opposition links the flydubai affair to the refusal of a state commission of inquiry, 22 days before the election

On Sunday, Eisenkot and Bennett linked the security failure on the flight to the government’s refusal to investigate the events of October 7. This ties in with reports from late September, according to which UAE President Mohammed bin Zayed warned Netanyahu about ten days before the massacre, as Haaretz, Australia’s ABC and CBC reported. Netanyahu called the report a complete fabrication and said that no such conversation took place. According to the New York Times, the minutes of the security meeting on October 1, 2023 do not mention such a warning.

Four leaders of the change camp, Eisenkot, Bennett, Avigdor Liberman and Yair Golan, pledged in a joint statement to set up a state commission of inquiry if they form a government. Recent polls by the news channels show that Eisenkot’s “Yashar!” is neck and neck with the Likud, and that in most of them no bloc reaches 61 seats without the Arab parties. The gap between the camps is narrow, and the outcome will be decided only in the vote on October 27.

Gaza · SecurityAP report only · casualty figures attributed to the Gaza Health Ministry

Israeli strike in central Gaza kills one Palestinian; Gaza Health Ministry: 1,445 killed since the ceasefire

On Sunday an Israeli strike hit a civilian vehicle in the center of the Gaza Strip. According to medical officials at Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital, one Palestinian was killed and at least 11 were wounded. The IDF said the strike targeted a known Hamas operative, the AP news agency reported, and the coverage in the American press relies on it.

The Gaza Health Ministry, which is subordinate to Hamas, counts at least 1,445 Palestinians killed in Israeli strikes since the ceasefire that took effect about a year ago. By contrast, seven Israeli soldiers have been killed in Gaza since then, according to the report. Local reports point to an increase in fire and strikes over the past month. DailyDrop has seen no independent confirmation of the death figures, and the affiliation of those killed with the organization cannot be verified on the ground.

TA-35 closes Thursday at 4,218 points; shekel weakens to a two-month low

The TA-35 index closed on Thursday, October 1, at 4,218.25 points, up 0.34 percent, according to Tel Aviv Stock Exchange data and the weekly report of the Israel Investment Report. Trading on the exchange was halted on Friday because of the eve of Simchat Torah, so this was the last close before this edition. The TA-125 index stood at 4,072.30, and more than half of the stocks fell this week, mainly in renewable energy and real estate.

The shekel briefly crossed 3.07 shekels to the dollar this week, for the first time since July, Globes reported. The Bank of Israel attributed this, according to the report, to interest-rate gaps with the United States, where the Federal Reserve is weighing a rate hike, to rising yields on bonds worldwide, to tensions in the Middle East and to the strength of the dollar. An official representative rate for Friday has not yet been published, and non-government sources show differing figures, so DailyDrop is not publishing a rate without verification. Today, Monday, trading on the exchange resumes as usual.

Tehran: only talks based on justice and fairness will end the war; ship traffic in the Strait of Hormuz is still low

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told ambassadors in Tehran on Sunday that only talks based on justice and fairness could end the conflict, Al Jazeera reported. His remarks came after an Iranian proposal presented at the UN in late September to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume negotiations, which President Trump rejected. The proposal included a halt to fighting on all fronts, including in Lebanon, and relied on the memorandum of understanding of June 17. DailyDrop has not seen confirmation of the wording of the proposal from a second source.

The war broke out on February 28, 2026 with Israeli and American strikes on Iran, and in response Tehran closed the strait. According to a review by the UK House of Commons Library, maritime traffic in the strait is far below its pre-conflict level. The statement should not be taken to mean that negotiations have reopened; it is a public position only.
