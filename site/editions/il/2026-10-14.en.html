Trump: “We will land a hard blow on Iran”; White House denies plan for a strike before the midterm elections

Reports in Israel and the US describe a conversation between Trump and Netanyahu about resuming the fighting in Iran. The Prime Minister’s Office denies that Netanyahu urged it, and no source available to us reports a decision.

In the early hours of October 14, remarks by US President Donald Trump on Iran were published, in Hebrew translation: “We will land a hard blow on it, we’ve had enough” (Ynet, mako, Globes). Ynet quotes him on a “chess game” and links it to the claim that Iran is changing tactics to influence the election. The day before, on October 12, he said that the midterm elections, the elections to Congress on November 3, have no influence at all on his decisions regarding Iran (Kan 11, Channel 7).

At the same time, reports were published about a conversation between Trump and Netanyahu. Axios wrote about a shared dilemma: to strike in Iran before the election or to wait. mako reported a conversation about a new operation, and the Times of Israel wrote that Netanyahu urged Trump to resume the fighting before the elections in both countries; the Prime Minister’s Office denies it. A senior White House official told Kan 11 that the reports of a US strike before the midterm elections are untrue. Channel 12 reported, according to FinancialJuice, that there is no intelligence change that would justify an Israeli-American move in the coming weeks. According to the same report, Iran carried out a series of ballistic missile launch tests in recent days, and the army estimates that its missile arsenal is being rebuilt slowly and partially.

The escalation continued in the region. On October 13 Trump said that the Houthis are trying to raise oil prices before the midterm elections, and according to Middle East Eye even “at Iran’s request” (Reuters). According to Reuters he also said that he does not believe China and Russia are helping Iran “at all,” but the WSJ published overnight, as an exclusive, that the Houthis are building an advanced missile with the help of Chinese components; this is a report by a single outlet. Houthi missiles hit the Aramco headquarters in Dhahran, and the Saudi-led coalition attacked targets in Sanaa (details in the side column). Oil prices rose, according to Reuters, as traders weighed the risk to Saudi supply.

Tension also continued in the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters reported, citing official sources, that Iran is changing tactics to step up attacks on shipping before the US election. FinancialJuice bulletins, which we cannot verify separately, quote UKMTO on a tanker hit in the strait, Iranian media on threatening missile fire at “violating ships,” and US Central Command on assisting in the passage of more than 19 million barrels of oil through the strait. And in Britain the head of MI5 said that Iran is targeting military sites and that the suspected plot against the Fairford base is “not unique” (AP); Reuters wrote that Iran and Russia are stepping up hybrid campaigns there. We reported Britain’s accusation regarding Fairford in the previous edition.

Background: the election in Israel is on October 27, and in the US on November 3, and both leaders are in campaign mode. Eisenkot also said on Army Radio, according to Israel Hayom, that setting the overthrow of the regime in Iran as a goal was a mistake. The Iranian question has entered the election debate, and that makes the reports harder to read. Those who see a close military move as a real possibility point to the public threats and to the report of a conversation between the leaders. A skeptic points to the denials, to the anonymous sources, and to the fact that Channel 12 reports no change in intelligence.

DailyDrop notes: what was confirmed is Trump’s public threat, the denials and the reports of a conversation. It was not confirmed that a strike is planned or that a date has been set, and Trump’s remarks reached us in Hebrew translation and not from a transcript. The details of the hit on Aramco came in differing versions, and a claim that the headquarters was evacuated, published in Iranian media, is unverified and sits in the “Unverified” block.

Netanyahu confirmed: Egyptian intelligence chief landed in Israel before October 7; “I don’t know who he met”

In an interview on Channel 14, on the evening of October 13, Netanyahu confirmed that the then Egyptian intelligence chief Abbas Kamel landed in Israel shortly before the massacre of October 7 (Haaretz, Israel Hayom, Maariv, Ynet). According to Haaretz English he denied meeting Kamel, said that he does not know who did receive his warning, and turned to “the relevant parties”: “I am the Prime Minister of Israel, I am turning to them - who did he meet” (Israel Hayom).

At the time of the event the Prime Minister’s Office claimed, according to Israel Hayom, that it was not aware of an Egyptian plane arriving in Israel, and so the papers speak of a change of version. In Ynet the headline is that Netanyahu said “He talked to me? Not once,” and that the video proves otherwise. Maariv writes that Netanyahu hinted at information on the matter that is being deliberately concealed, and that a single photograph says otherwise. In the details available to us there is no description of the video or of the photograph, and we cannot verify what they show.

DailyDrop notes: the landing itself is confirmed by several sources, among them the interview itself. What Kamel did in Israel, whom he met, and who received the warning about which Haaretz writes, was not given in the reports available to us. Haaretz English describes Channel 14 as a channel supportive of Netanyahu, so the quotes come from a friendly interview and not from an investigation.

After the Ramim Ridge disaster: Education Ministry says the approved route did not pass through the forbidden area, and is checking whether the group deviated from it

The boy killed in the explosion of the unexploded ordnance on October 12 was identified the next day: Elazar Yair Wilensky, 14, a resident of Ma’alot-Tarshiha, a ninth-grade student (Ynet, Haaretz, Israel Hayom, Maariv, JNS). Correction: in the previous edition we wrote eighth grade; reports since then state ninth grade. His family described him, according to Maariv, as “a child of life, smart and wise, full of joy of life,” and he was the youngest of five siblings. 24 students and teachers were injured, two of them seriously according to Haaretz English; earlier three were reported.

The central question is how the group got there. According to Ynet, it was approved to hike on the “Israel Trail 2051” route, which in the field combines two paths, one of which is “absolutely closed to entry”; hiking guides told Ynet that at the ministry “they do not have the connection to the field.” The Education Ministry said, according to Haaretz, that it had banned activity in the area in accordance with KKL-JNF restrictions, that the safety guidelines included a ban on touching unexploded ordnance and an obligation to report it, and that the approved route was not in the forbidden area. It is checking whether the group deviated from the path (Haaretz, Israel Hayom, Ynet).

Israel Hayom adds that teachers are calling to refuse to go on trips, and that the injured may be recognized as victims of a hostile act and entitled to National Insurance Institute compensation. In an opinion piece in Maariv, Avi Ashkenazi wrote that more than 31 thousand items of ordnance were fired at Israel in the war and that not all of them can be located, and that closing the hiking routes is not the solution. DailyDrop notes: according to JNS, an initial IDF assessment is that this was an unexploded Hezbollah cluster bomblet. Who approved the route, who accompanied the group, and what the escorts were instructed, was not determined in the reports available to us.

World · HealthPress ✓ 7 outlets · Vatican announcement

Vatican: Pope Leo XIV diagnosed with lung cancer after surgery; the lower lobe of his right lung was removed

Pope Leo XIV, 71, was admitted on October 13 to a hospital in Rome for surgery to remove a small tumor in the lung; Reuters wrote of a “lump in the lung,” and The Guardian described low-risk surgery. A senior cardinal in the Vatican said, according to The Guardian, that the discovery came “like a bolt from the blue.” In the evening the Vatican announced, as AP, Reuters and Ynet reported, that the suspicion that arose in the preliminary tests was confirmed during the surgery: adenocarcinoma of the lung was diagnosed, and the doctors removed the lower lobe of the right lung (Israel Hayom, Ynet).

According to Haaretz, the surgery lasted about an hour and a half and ended without complications, but it is not yet clear whether the cancer has spread to the lymph nodes. The Pope’s public appearances were canceled until October 20. Usually the answer on the lymph nodes is what determines whether further treatment is required, and so the Vatican’s next announcement will be the next important detail.

DailyDrop notes what was and was not reported. Reported were the diagnosis, the type of surgery, its duration and that it passed without complications, and the cancellation of appearances until October 20. Not reported in the reports available to us: whether the cancer has spread, what treatment will be set, and when the Pope will return to full activity. Until such information is published, it is best to avoid guesses about a medical prognosis.

Politics · ElectionsPress ✓ 5 outlets · quotes from a conference of Ynet and Yedioth Ahronoth

Less than two weeks to the election: Jabareen – “no blank check” for the Change bloc; Elections Committee: not updated on concrete security warnings

At the “People of the State” conference of Ynet and Yedioth Ahronoth, the chair of the Joint List, Yousef Jabareen, said that the party is “not in the pocket of the Change camp.” He offered a “safety net” to replace the government, and qualified: “We have demands, there is no blank check” (Ynet). According to an interview on 103FM quoted in Israel Hayom and Maariv, the conditions for recommending a candidate at the President’s Residence include a horizon for a Palestinian state on the ’67 borders, dealing with crime, investment in young people and development of the Arab communities. Maariv adds his forecast: 12 seats.

At the conference, the acting director general of the Central Elections Committee, attorney Dean Livne, said that the committee had not been updated on concrete security warnings and that he does not identify a security event that would harm turnout at the polls on October 27 (Ynet). He added that foreign states are running an influence campaign: “bots echo messages designed to make people think the elections are rigged.” Netanyahu said, according to Kan 11, that “our enemies are thinking of testing us ahead of the election.” i24NEWS reported that extending voting hours and an additional voting day are being considered because of security concerns, and Haaretz wrote about IDF preparations for the polling stations in the Gaza Strip, in Lebanon and in Syria.

Within the Change bloc: Ynet reports that Eisenkot’s people asked Blue and White for some of the polling-committee posts, because the Yashar! party was not in the outgoing Knesset and has no posts, and the bloc fears that they will go to the Netanyahu bloc. Israel Hayom and Maariv report anger in the bloc over Eisenkot’s “siphoning the votes” campaign, two weeks after the leaders of the bloc signed a joint document of principles. Hendel, chair of the Reservists, told Ynet that he would go with whoever brings more Zionist seats. DailyDrop notes: these are statements and reports about a political struggle, not poll figures.
