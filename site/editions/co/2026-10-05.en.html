World · Brazil✓ 5 outlets · TSE percentages, per Chequeado

Flávio Bolsonaro won the first round in Brazil with 47.03% to Lula’s 45.16%, and a runoff will be held on October 25

With almost all polling stations counted, no candidate topped 50%. President Abelardo de la Espriella, who backs Bolsonaro, wrote on X that “the real change is to defeat the left.”

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro came first in the presidential first round on Sunday, October 4, with 47.03% of valid votes to 45.16% for President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. The figure comes from the Superior Electoral Tribunal (TSE) with 99.99% of votes counted, according to Chequeado. Since nobody passed 50%, the two will go to a runoff on October 25. The abstention rate was 21.08%. Partial counts varied: Blu Radio published 47.15% against 45.02% with 98.64% of the ballot boxes, and NTN24 47.31% against 44.83%.

The result was a surprise because earlier polls gave Lula the lead, and he himself said, according to iProfesional, that he believed he would win in the first round. In Colombia, De la Espriella posted on X that “today my heart is with Brazil” and that the real change is to defeat the left (Publimetro, Blu Radio). Javier Milei also congratulated Bolsonaro (Blu Radio, NTN24). DailyDrop did not find a later reaction from Lula or Monday market data, so the effect on the dollar and the stock market in Colombia remains to be seen.

Economy · Energy✓ 4 outlets · decision of October 2

The Government reversed the COP 46-per-gallon increase, and regular gasoline stays at September’s price in October

On October 1, RCN Radio and Vanguardia reported an average increase of COP 46 per gallon of regular gasoline, tied to the update of the price recognized to ethanol producers. A day later, President De la Espriella wrote on X that he had asked the Minister of Mines and Energy, María Nohemi Arboleda, to reverse the October adjustment (Primicias). Vanguardia and Publimetro reported that the Government will absorb the cost and hold the price for the month.

Primicias links the increase to costlier oil worldwide. Publimetro adds that the Executive is studying a long-term formula. One of the stories consulted says that ACPM has remained frozen since the earthquake, a claim DailyDrop could not confirm with a second source. Nor did it see a Ministry statement with the fiscal cost or how long the measure will last.

Sports · National team✓ 4 outlets · match reports from Fox Sports, RCN, Vanguardia and FútbolUy

Colombia lost 1-0 to Paraguay in New Jersey and has gone two friendlies without a win; on Tuesday it faces Peru in Miami

On Friday, October 2, Paraguay beat Colombia 1-0 at Sports Illustrated Stadium in Harrison, New Jersey, with a goal from debutant Hugo Benítez in the 56th minute, according to FútbolUy, Fox Sports México and Noticias RCN. It was the second match of the tour for the FIFA window in the United States, after the 1-1 draw with Mexico, so the national team comes into it with two friendlies in a row without a win.

Néstor Lorenzo tried a three-man defense and, according to Vanguardia’s match reports, the team put in a modest performance and had trouble adapting. The last match of the tour is against Peru on Tuesday, October 6, in Miami (Minuto60, Publimetro). DailyDrop did not find any post-match statements from Lorenzo that could be quoted.

Markets · Colombia✓ 4 outlets · closes per Bloomberg Línea, Rankia, RCN and Portafolio

The MSCI COLCAP closed Friday at 2,515.02 points (−0.59%) and the dollar at COP 3,264.50, with a drop of nearly 11% on the year

The MSCI COLCAP closed on Friday, October 2, at 2,515.02 points, 15.03 less than the previous close of 2,530.05 (−0.59%), according to Bloomberg Línea. Rankia puts it 4.25% below its all-time high of 2,626.71 points, set on September 10. DailyDrop could not confirm the figure with the Colombia Stock Exchange.

The dollar closed at COP 3,264.50, COP 21.50 less than the open of COP 3,286.00, according to the Banco de la República chart cited by Noticias RCN. The TRM in force that day was COP 3,307.73 (Portafolio), which reports an 11.38% fall in the dollar so far this year. On Wall Street, the S&P 500 closed at 7,722.72 (+0.73%), WTI at US$91.11 (−1.90%) and gold at US$4,162.3 (−1.00%).

World · Economy✓ 5 outlets · figures from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, via Telemundo and Bloomberg Línea

The United States created just 29,000 jobs in September and unemployment rose to 4.2%; the S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,722.72

The U.S. economy added 29,000 jobs in September, far below the 84,000 economists expected in a Dow Jones survey, and the unemployment rate rose from 4.1% to 4.2%, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics as cited by Telemundo, Bloomberg Línea, UnoTV, El Financiero and Revista E&N. The July and August figures were revised down; July ended with a net loss of 10,000 jobs.

Average earnings rose 3% a year, below August’s inflation of 3.4% a year. Bloomberg Línea added that traders scaled back their bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike in October. For Colombia, the rate matters: if the Fed does not raise, the dollar usually puts less pressure on the peso. DailyDrop did not find a specific analysis of the impact on Colombian debt.
