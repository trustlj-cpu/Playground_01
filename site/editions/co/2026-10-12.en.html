Colombia bids farewell to Nairo Quintana at the Gran Fondo in Cartagena and Bolívar, amid tributes and the question of who succeeds him in cycling

On Sunday, Oct. 11, the farewell race of the Boyacá cyclist was held: three routes from 50 to 186 kilometers and a gathering of peloton figures. After the tribute, the debate is what comes after him.

On Sunday, Oct. 11, the Gran Fondo Nairo Fest 2026 was held, the public farewell of Nairo Quintana, on roads in Cartagena and Bolívar, according to Caracol Radio, which followed it minute by minute. The event lasted three days, from the 9th to the 11th, and offered three routes: the Gran Fondo, of 186 kilometers; the Medio Fondo, of 106; and the “Retadores” (Challengers), of 50, according to El Universal and Publimetro before the race. Those outlets expected more than 10,600 attendees, 6,900 of them cyclists, from 21 countries, and the presence of figures such as Chris Froome, Alberto Contador, Rigoberto Urán, Joaquim “Purito” Rodríguez and Samuel Sánchez. These are pre-race figures: DailyDrop found no official count of those who finally rode, although Infobae confirms that Froome, Contador, Urán and Egan Bernal accompanied him.

Caracol reported that the group rode through the Turbaco area and that the day was framed as sport and culture; the governor of Bolívar said that “it is time for the world to get to know Bolívar,” and the same station headlined that the race consolidated Cartagena as a venue for major sporting events. Quintana, born in Boyacá, won the 2014 Giro d’Italia and the 2016 Vuelta a España, and was second in the Tour de France in 2013 and 2015. After the tribute, the conversation turned to the future: Semana picked up his message to Santiago Buitrago and to the “new blood” of Colombian cycling, and Infobae gathered experts who speak of a crisis and a lack of succession in the world elite. Quintana had already warned, according to Noticias RCN, that “we could be left without cycling.” What comes next is to see whether sponsors, federations and local tours respond to that call.

Politics · JusticePress ✓ 4 outlets · all of it is allegations with no court decision

Angie Rodríguez backs Eva Ferrer against Verónica Alcocer, and De la Espriella cancels the Qatar assignment of Alcocer’s former security chief

Semana, El Colombiano, Infobae and El Tiempo followed on Sunday the case of Verónica Alcocer, former partner of former president Gustavo Petro. Angie Rodríguez told Semana about episodes of physical violence and humiliation that she attributes to Alcocer, backed the complaints of Eva Ferrer, her former adviser, and spoke of a “criminal pact” between Petro and Alcocer. Antonella Petro, Alcocer’s daughter, defended her: “Not a thousand slanders will dim your shine,” according to Vanguardia. President Abelardo de la Espriella ordered the cancellation of the Qatar assignment of Captain Kelly Lozano, Alcocer’s former security chief, according to Semana; Infobae and El Tiempo say he would have ordered her return.

It is all allegations. Ferrer’s originated in audio recordings that Semana published in August: the Fiscalía opened a preliminary inquiry, Alcocer denied it in a letter to Attorney General Luz Adriana Camargo, and Ferrer’s defense says the recordings were illegal (El Heraldo, Vanguardia, Noticias Caracol). Semana adds that a confidential inquiry for money laundering would exist and would have been “shelved”; DailyDrop found no response from the entity. There is no formal charge and no court decision.

Justice · InternationalPress ✓ 4 outlets · the press differs on the procedure

US confirms that Álvaro Pulido, an associate of Alex Saab, is in its custody; the press speaks of capture, transfer and extradition

El Tiempo, Infobae, Caracol and Semana reported that Álvaro Pulido Vargas, a Colombian businessman associated with Alex Saab, who is named as a front man for Nicolás Maduro, is in the hands of the United States, which confirmed his capture for money laundering. El Colombiano adds that DEA agents took him out of his prison in Caracas and that he would be being transferred to the US; Semana speaks of extradition and quotes the US attorney general. Caracol presents it as a detention for alleged corruption in Venezuela.

The terms differ, and capture, transfer and extradition have different legal consequences; none of the outlets cited specifies the procedure. Primicias recalled that the US offered a US$10 million reward in 2021 and charged him with conspiracy to launder money; DailyDrop could not confirm it with a second source. It remains to be seen before which court he will appear.

Security · ConflictPress ✓ 3 outlets · casualties per official sources and press

De la Espriella orders another bombing against Front 33 in the Catatumbo and says “there is no truce”; Inpec guards attacked in Barranquilla

President Abelardo de la Espriella confirmed a bombing against Front 33, a Farc dissident group, in the Catatumbo, and said that “with the narco-terrorists there is no truce” (Semana, Minuto60, IFM Noticias). Infobae says the target was a camp of the “Calarcá” dissidents; an initial tally by Semana speaks of four bodies recovered. In Cajibío, Cauca, there was combat with dissidents; De la Espriella announced two dead and a coca laboratory dismantled, according to Infobae.

In Barranquilla, two Inpec guards suffered an attack that Semana links to a “plan pistola” against officials. The president responded that “the transfers will continue” (Infobae, Semana, Caracol) and the Ombudsman’s Office asked for an investigation. DailyDrop found no figures on the wounded or confirmed perpetrators; the casualties from the bombings have no independent verification.

World · War and energyPress ✓ 4 outlets · the announcement is Trump’s; Moscow has not confirmed it

Trump announces an “energy truce” between Russia and Ukraine; Russia hits a power plant in Kharkiv shortly afterward and Kyiv says it is waiting for details

President Donald Trump said on Sunday, Oct. 11, around 14:30 UTC (9:30 a.m. in Bogotá), on Truth Social, that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy truce” effective immediately (Reuters, USA Today). Volodymyr Zelensky replied that it was news to him and then that Kyiv was waiting for the details while Russia kept up its attacks (NBC, Reuters). Russia hit a power plant in Kharkiv shortly afterward (ABC News, Reuters), and Reuters counted six dead in two Ukrainian cities.

The dispute continues: Trump called for replacing Zelensky and threatened, according to the Financial Times cited by Reuters, to halt weapons over Ukrainian attacks on refineries; Zelensky said Ukraine would stop them if Russia ceases its attacks on energy. For Colombia, a crude exporter, the figure to watch is the price: WTI closed Friday at US$91.85 (+0.39%) and Reuters reported that oil is slipping today in Asia.
