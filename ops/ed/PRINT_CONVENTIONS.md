# Print front-page conventions by country, as reference for DailyDrop editions

Research date: 2026-10-08. Owner request: "실제 각 나라 종이신문 구해와서 참고해" (get each country's actual printed newspapers and use them as reference).

## Read this first: sources and confidence

- **I could not look at any actual front-page scans.** From this container, WebFetch and curl could not reach freedomforum.org, kiosko.net, frontpages.com, wikipedia.org, w3.org or theguardian.com (DNS failures, or the proxy refused them). The only working tool was WebSearch, which returns summarised snippets. In total I ran about 50 searches.
- Every claim below carries one of two tags:
  - **[S]** means a search result supports it. The URL is in the source list for that country.
  - **[K]** means it comes from general knowledge of these papers and of locale and typography standards, and nothing in this session confirmed it. Treat [K] items as strong defaults. **Check them against a real scan before you call anything "authentic".** This applies most to masthead and dateline wording, prices and volume or issue numbers.
- Numbers, dates and quotation-mark rules follow national standards, which are stable and well documented. These are the safest parts to apply. The weakest parts are paper-specific masthead details (exact order, price and volume) and the details of Taiwanese papers.
- One alternative for verification: on a machine with open internet, look at kiosko.net, frontpages.com or freedomforum.org/todaysfrontpages, or the PressReader edition of each paper.

## KR: 조선일보 and 한겨레

**1. Text setting**
- Since the 1990s, Korean papers have been set horizontally. 한겨레 was the first general daily set horizontally (1988). 중앙일보 switched on 1995-10-09 and 조선일보 on 1999-03-02 [S].
- Body text is newspaper 명조 (serif). 조선일보 has its own house faces, 조선일보명조 and the 조선신명조 derived from it, plus 조선고딕 [S, from a font vendor]. 한겨레 introduced its own 한겨레체 in 2005 [S].
- Body size: 조선일보 went from 9.4pt to 9.8pt (2012) and then to 10.2pt (2013). It was the largest among national dailies, where the typical range is 9.5 to 10pt [S]. Papers adjusted the width ratio, tracking and leading at the same time [S].
- Columns: most general dailies run a 7-column (7단) grid. 한겨레 went to 5단 with wider margins and tightened tracking [S].
- Body is justified (양끝맞춤) with no hyphenation. A line can break between syllables inside a word, so print does not keep words together. Extra space is absorbed between words [K; W3C Korean layout notes that lines "tend to be broken in the middle of a word, with no hyphenation" S].

**2. Headlines**
- Headlines have no capitalisation issue. The lead headline is usually a heavy 고딕 (sans), and secondary or feature headlines are often 명조 [K]. Papers use typeface weight and style to signal how important a story is [S].
- Quotation marks: "큰따옴표" is the usual mark for direct quotes in headlines. ‘작은따옴표’ is used for emphasis or paraphrase [S]. Korean papers often paraphrase a speaker inside quote marks. A study found that only 24% of 조선일보 quote-headlines were verbatim [S].
- Headlines have no final period [K]. A middle dot "·" (가운뎃점) joins parallel items, and "…" is common [K].
- A subhead (부제) under the main headline is standard, often with a left bar or dash [K].

**3. Masthead and dateline [K]**
- The nameplate is at top left on 조선일보 and at top centre or left on 한겨레.
- Under or beside it runs a small information line, for example `2026년 10월 8일 목요일` plus `제3x,xxx호` (cumulative issue number). Some papers add the lunar date `(음력 8월 28일)`. I could not confirm whether 조선일보 still prints the lunar date [searched, not found].
- Price is not usually prominent on page 1. Subscription details sit in the masthead box inside the paper.

**4. Numbers and dates [K]**
- Large numbers use 만, 억 and 조 units with Arabic digits and no spaces: `3조2000억원`, `1만2000명`. The national orthography would put a space before the unit (`3조 2000억 원`), but papers usually close it up.
- Thousands commas appear only inside a unit group of four or more digits, for example `1,234명`. A run like `12,345,678원` is rare in headlines.
- Decimal point is ".". Percentages are written `3.5%`.
- Dates are written `10월 8일(목)` in body text. Headlines use `8일`.

**5. Front-page structure [K]**
- The top story sits top left, under the nameplate, with a large photo.
- 2 to 4 more stories and a 지면 안내 (index) box or strip are common.
- A large display ad (5단 광고) fills the bottom band of page 1. This is a very Korean convention.
- There is no market data on page 1.

## US: New York Times and Wall Street Journal

**1. Text setting**
- NYT body face is Imperial. Headlines are Cheltenham in many cuts: Bold Extra Condensed for one-column heads, and Bold Condensed or Medium for decks [S].
- WSJ uses Escrow for headlines and Exchange for body text. Exchange was designed for the Journal in 2002–2005. Retina is its face for tables and agate [S].
- Both papers set body text justified and hyphenated, in narrow columns of roughly 30 to 35 characters [K].
- Page 1 has 6 columns at both papers. WSJ moved back to 6 in 2008 [S].

**2. Headlines**
- NYT capitalises headlines in title case ("up style"). Content words are capitalised, and prepositions of four or more letters are capitalised too [S].
- WSJ also uses title case on A1 [K].
- Under the main head come multiple decks: one or more smaller headlines, in title case, in a lighter weight [S/K].
- Kickers are small labels above heads, for example "NEWS ANALYSIS" [K].
- Quotes in headlines use single marks: ‘…’ [K]. Body text uses double “…” [K].
- Story datelines are in caps with an em dash: `WASHINGTON —` [K].

**3. Masthead and dateline**
- NYT: the left ear carries "All the News That's Fit to Print". It first appeared on page 1 on 1897-02-10 [S].
- NYT: the right ear carries the edition name ("Late Edition") and a weather summary [K].
- NYT: under the nameplate is a single rule line: `VOL. CLXXVI . . . No. 6x,xxx` | `© 2026 The New York Times Company` | `NEW YORK, THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026` | `$4.00`. These parts are separated by spacing and rules [K; historical template S]. The volume number is in Roman numerals.
- WSJ: the line under the nameplate reads `THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026 ~ VOL. CCLXXXVI NO. 84 ★★★★ $5.00`. The star count marks the edition [K].
- WSJ: a market strip under the nameplate gives DJIA, NASDAQ, S&P, 10-yr Treasury, oil, gold, euro and yen. Each has its value and a ▲ or ▼ with the change in % [K].

**4. Numbers and dates**
- Numbers are written `1,234.5`, `$1.2 billion` and `3.5%` (AP and NYT style) [K].
- Mastheads write dates in full caps: `THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026`. Body text uses the abbreviated form `Oct. 8` [K].

**5. Front-page structure**
- NYT: the lead story goes in the right-hand column (traditionally col. 6) or across a multi-column photo [K]. Stories jump with "Continued on Page A12" [K]. There is an "INSIDE" or index panel, and small promos run at the bottom [K].
- WSJ: "What's News" is a column of briefs on the left, split into Business & Finance and World-Wide. It started in 1934, and its width has moved between 1 and 2 columns (narrowed in 2013) [S]. WSJ uses hedcut stipple portraits instead of headshot photos (since 1979) [S]. The "A-hed" offbeat feature sits in the middle [K].

## JP: 朝日新聞 and 日本経済新聞

**1. Text setting**
- Body text is set vertically (縦組み) in 明朝. Newspaper 明朝 is a flattened (扁平, 平体) design.
- In 2015 the major national dailies were all still vertical [S]. Photo captions and tables are mostly horizontal, and headlines may run either way ("縦横無尽") [S].
- Grid: a page has 15 段 traditionally. Most national dailies moved to 12 段 with larger characters around 2008 [K]. One paper (Daily Sports) still has 15 段, with characters per 段 falling from 13 to 10 between 1986 and 2016 [S].
- 朝日 reduced its line length from 12 to 11 characters (about 2023) for readability [S].
- Text sits on a strict character grid (ベタ組み). Kinsoku rules from JIS X 4051 and W3C JLReq apply: no line may start with closing brackets, 、。, small kana or ー, and no line may end with opening brackets [S].
- Commas and full stops at the end of a line commonly hang outside the measure (句読点のぶら下げ). This is widespread in newspapers [K; general S].

**2. Headlines**
- Headlines carry no case. A main head (主見出し) has one or more 袖見出し (side heads).
- 1面トップ often uses white-on-black or screened (地紋) heads [K].
- Quotation brackets are 「…」, with 『…』 for nested quotes. There is no closing period inside 「」 at the end of a quote [K].
- Headlines drop particles and end in a noun or a shortened verb form, for example 「首相、増税見送り」. The comma 、 works as a separator, and 「」 marks quoted claims [K].

**3. Masthead and dateline**
- In the morning edition (朝刊), the 題字 runs vertically at the top right of page 1. 朝日 has used a vertical 題字 since 1886. The **evening edition switched to a horizontal 題字 from 2025-04-01** [S].
- Next to the 題字 (on its left) sits a table of contents for major series and features [S].
- The date block reads `2026年(令和8年)10月8日 木曜日`, plus the cumulative issue number `第5xxxx号`, `日刊`, `朝刊` or `夕刊`, the edition (`14版`), and the publisher and office details [K]. Both the Gregorian year and the 元号 (era name) are printed [K; general era-name practice S].

**4. Numbers and dates**
- In vertical body text, modern papers use Arabic numerals set horizontally inside the vertical line (縦中横) for 2-digit numbers, for example a two-digit "10" upright in `10月8日` [K]. Fixed expressions keep 漢数字, for example 一部, 三権 [S, general rule].
- Large numbers use 万, 億 and 兆 as units: `1兆2000億円`. Nikkei-style text uses `1万3000人`. Some papers write `1万3千人` [K]. Unit groups have no thousands comma.
- In horizontal web text use Arabic numerals, for example `3.5%`.
- Dates are written `10月8日` with the weekday as `(木)`.

**5. Front-page structure**
- The lead (トップ) story sits top right and reads right to left.
- 朝日: the column 天声人語 (603 characters, no headline, no byline) sits at the bottom of page 1 [S].
- 日経: the column 春秋 (about 500 characters, no headline) sits at the bottom of page 1 [S]. Nikkei's page 1 runs from macro and economic stories to political, international and company stories [S].
- Nikkei puts an index and short explainers such as 「きょうのことば」 on or near page 1 [K/S]. Market figures (日経平均, 円相場) normally appear on the markets pages rather than as a page-1 box [K].

Sources: media-innovation.jp/article/2025/03/28/142315.html and bunkanews.jp/article/417038/ (朝日夕刊題字横型), weblio.jp/content/日本の新聞 (縦組み・題字横目次), bunshun.jp/denshiban/articles/b12185 (12字→11字), daily.co.jp/opinion-d/2016/12/20/0009764045.shtml (15段・字数推移), bookplus.nikkei.com/atcl/column/070600261/111400022/ (春秋), chuo-u.ac.jp standard2021 PDF (日経1面構成), w3.org/TR/jlreq/ and w3c.github.io/jlreq/docs/line-composition/ (kinsoku)

## GB: The Times and The Guardian

**1. Text setting**
- The Times: Times Modern was created for the 2003 compact switch, and the masthead and crest were redrawn in 2006. Both papers are justified and hyphenated in narrow columns [S/K].
- The Guardian: the body face is Guardian Egyptian (Text). The 2018 tabloid redesign changed its size, leading and setting [S].
- The Guardian's masthead and headlines are set in Guardian Headline (2018) [S].

**2. Headlines**
- Both papers use sentence case [S].
- They run a standfirst, a sentence-case summary under the head, often with the byline nearby [K].
- Quotes in headlines use single marks: ‘…’. Body text uses double “…” [K; UK practice].
- Short kickers or labels appear, for example "Exclusive" [K].

**3. Masthead and dateline [K]**
- The Times: the royal arms are in the middle of the nameplate. The line underneath reads `Thursday October 8 2026 | thetimes.com | No 7x,xxx | £2.80`. The Times writes dates month-first without commas.
- The Guardian: the nameplate takes two lines with no blue band (2018) [S]. The date line reads `Thursday 8 October 2026` with the price, `£2.80` or similar. Day-month order, no comma.

**4. Numbers and dates [K]**
- Numbers are written `1,234.5`, `£1.2bn` (headlines use bn and m), `3.5%`.
- The Guardian writes `8 October`. The Times writes `October 8`.

**5. Front-page structure [K]**
- A "skyline" of promotional puffs with pictures runs above the nameplate.
- Tabloid page 1 is one dominant story, often with a big photo, plus 1 or 2 secondary items and a turn line: "Continued on page 4".
- There is no market table on page 1.

## DE: FAZ and Süddeutsche Zeitung

**1. Text setting**
- Both papers use justified text (Blocksatz) with German hyphenation. Hyphenation is essential in narrow columns, and the usual rule is no more than three hyphenated lines in a row [S, general German typography].
- Body size: the general guide is 9 to 12pt [S]. FAZ enlarged its body text slightly in the 2007 redesign [S].
- SZ: its 2012 redesign brought house faces by Henning Skibbe and Nils Thomsen. The serif body face (SZ Text or SZ Serif) is a modernised Excelsior, and headlines are bold sans (SZ Sans) [S].

**2. Headlines**
- German capitalises nouns, but headlines are otherwise in sentence form.
- FAZ: since 2007, headlines are left-aligned with no Fraktur. Fraktur stays only in the nameplate [S].
- Each head has a Dachzeile (kicker or overline) above it and a Unterzeile below it [K].
- Quotes are „…“, with ‚…‘ nested [S for „“]. Some publications use »…« chevrons instead (book and magazine style). FAZ and SZ both use „…“ [K].

**3. Masthead and dateline**
- FAZ: the nameplate is in Fraktur. Its subline "ZEITUNG FÜR DEUTSCHLAND" is set in roman (Antiqua) [S].
- FAZ: the running line inside the paper reads `FRANKFURTER ALLGEMEINE ZEITUNG · MITTWOCH, 11. SEPTEMBER 2019 · NR. 211 · SEITE 31`. This confirms the order weekday, date, Nr. and page, with "·" separators [S].
- FAZ: the page-1 line under the nameplate follows the same pattern: `Donnerstag, 8. Oktober 2026 · Nr. 234 · D 2954 A · 4,20 €`. Edition and region codes and prices for other countries may appear in small type [K].
- SZ: the line under the nameplate reads `MÜNCHEN, DONNERSTAG, 8. OKTOBER 2026 · 82. JAHRGANG / 4x. WOCHE / NR. 234 / 4,20 EURO`, with slash or dot separators [K].

**4. Numbers and dates**
- Numbers are written `1.234,5` (thousands dot, decimal comma). Some styles use a narrow space for thousands outside money, for example `80 000 Zuschauer` [S, sources conflict]. **Newspapers use the dot.**
- Currency goes after the number: `4,20 €` or `4,2 Milliarden Euro`. Headlines abbreviate to `Mrd.` and `Mio.` [S/K].
- Dates are written `8. Oktober 2026`. Short form is `8.10.` [K].

**5. Front-page structure**
- FAZ: since 2007-10-05, page 1 carries one colour photo every day. Before that, only 33 front-page photos ran in 1949–2007 [S].
- FAZ: a big lead commentary (Leitglosse) runs on page 1, and sometimes a cartoon replaces the photo [S].
- SZ: the Streiflicht column runs down the left edge of page 1 every day since 1946, unsigned [S].
- Market data on page 1 is at most a tiny line. Neither paper runs a full DAX table on the front page [K, unverified].

## FR: Le Monde and Le Figaro

**1. Text setting**
- Body text is justified and hyphenated (French hyphenation) [K].
- Le Monde moved off Times in 1994–95 to Jean-François Porchez's Le Monde Journal family. In 2002 Lucas de Groot drew new title faces [S, partial].
- Le Figaro's 2024 redesign gave it a new, more web-like title face and a lighter layout [S].

**2. Headlines**
- French sentence case: only the first word and proper nouns are capitalised [K].
- Le Monde uses a surtitre (kicker) and a chapô (standfirst) [K].
- French quote marks are « … ». Spacing rules [S]:
  - A no-break space goes inside the guillemets. Imprimerie nationale prescribes a word-space no-break, but the press often uses a fine (narrow) no-break space.
  - A thin no-break space (U+202F) goes before `; ! ?`.
  - A no-break space goes before `:`.
  - Nested quotes use “…” [K].

**3. Masthead and dateline**
- Le Monde: the bandeau under the nameplate reads `JEUDI 8 OCTOBRE 2026 · 82E ANNÉE · NO 24 xxx · 3,80 € · FRANCE MÉTROPOLITAINE · WWW.LEMONDE.FR · FONDATEUR : HUBERT BEUVE-MÉRY`. It is in small caps with dot or dash separators [K].
- The pairing of issue number and year of publication is confirmed historically: "n° 1020 ; 5e année" (1948) [S]. The weekday price of €3.80 was confirmed [S].
- Le Figaro: the line reads `JEUDI 8 OCTOBRE 2026 · LE FIGARO · NO 2x xxx · 3,xx €` [K].
- Le Figaro: the motto runs near the nameplate: « Sans la liberté de blâmer, il n'est point d'éloge flatteur » (Beaumarchais), along with "Fondé en 1826" [K].

**4. Numbers and dates**
- Numbers are written `1 234,5`, with a thin no-break space (U+202F) as the thousands separator [S].
- Euro goes after the number with a no-break space: `3,80 €` [S/K].
- Large amounts: `1,2 milliard d'euros`, `12 millions`. Headlines may use `Md€` [K].
- Dates are all lowercase: `jeudi 8 octobre 2026`. The first of the month is `1er octobre` [K].

**5. Front-page structure [K]**
- Le Monde: one lead story and an editorial or analysis. A cartoon has historically run on page 1 (Plantu) [S]. A sommaire strip lists inside pages with page numbers.
- Le Figaro: the lead story plus "L'éditorial" on page 1, with skyline promos.

## IN: The Hindu and The Indian Express

**1. Text setting [K]**
- Body text is a justified, hyphenated serif in 7–8 broadsheet columns.
- The Hindu's 2022 redesign brought larger body type, bolder heads, gist and highlight panels, and more white space [S].
- The Hindu's 2005 Garcia redesign used Interstate and Chronicle, and was later reverted [S].
- The Indian Express redesign (5 Nov, about 2025) brought a cleaner layout, defined story boundaries, white space, a bolder typeface and a modernised masthead [S].

**2. Headlines [K]**
- Sentence case, which is British-influenced.
- Quotes in headlines use single marks: ‘…’.
- Short kickers and labels are common, for example "EXPRESS INVESTIGATION".

**3. Masthead and dateline [K]**
- The Hindu: the line reads `CHENNAI, THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026 | Vol. 149 No. 2xx | 24 pages | ₹10`. It also shows the city edition and the printing centres, and the price varies by city.
- Indian Express: the line shows edition city, date, `Vol.` and `No.`, page count and price `₹`, and the "Journalism of Courage" tagline. Exact wording is unverified.

**4. Numbers and dates**
- Indian currency uses lakh and crore, with 2-2-3 digit grouping: `₹1,50,000`, `₹1.2 lakh crore`. Foreign currency is written in millions and billions [S].
- `₹` goes before the number [S]. Older or other styles use `Rs` or `Rs.`.
- Dates are written `October 8, 2026` [K].

**5. Front-page structure [K]**
- Front-page "jacket" or wrap ads and large display ads are very common.
- The news page is dense, with many stories plus a briefs column and an index.

## AU: Sydney Morning Herald and The Australian

**1. Text setting**
- SMH: the body face was Charter in its 2000 redesign, and it went back to 9pt after complaints [S]. It has been compact (tabloid) since 2013 [S].
- The Australian: typography is Times Classic, which the website also adopted to echo print [S]. The paper is broadsheet [K].
- Both papers set justified, hyphenated columns [K].

**2. Headlines**
- Both use sentence case. SMH is cited as a sentence-case paper [S].
- Quotes in headlines use single marks ‘…’, as in UK practice [K]. The Australian Government Style Manual uses single marks for all quotes [S], but newspapers use double “…” in body text [K].

**3. Masthead and dateline [K]**
- SMH: the nameplate has "Since 1831" (searched, not confirmed). The line reads `THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026 | smh.com.au | $4.00 (incl. GST)`. Higher prices for regional or interstate copies may be listed.
- The Australian: the nameplate has a tagline. The line shows the date, price, `$` and "theaustralian.com.au".

**4. Numbers and dates [K]**
- Numbers are written `1,234.5` and `$1.2 billion`. Headlines use `$1.2bn` and `$500m`.
- Newspaper datelines use `October 8, 2026`, while the government and AU English standard is `8 October 2026`. **Verify this against a scan.**

**5. Front-page structure [K]**
- Tabloid SMH: one dominant story with a photo, plus a skyline.
- The Australian: broadsheet, with a lead, a photo, 2–3 stories and an index. A small markets strip (ASX 200 and AUD) may appear [unverified].

## CA: Globe and Mail and Le Devoir

**1. Text setting**
- Globe and Mail: Nick Shinn designed a family for it in 2007 (Globe Sans, News and Text). In 2010 it commissioned the Pratt Nova family, with variants called Sense and Sensibility. The nameplate is Pratt Pro, and it was blackletter for about 150 years until 1990 [S].
- Globe and Mail: the paper was narrowed to 12 inches in 2007 [S].
- Le Devoir: Lucie Lacava redesigned it in 1992 with only two families (Torino and Century cited for the Arts section), and again on 2018-06-07 [S].
- Both papers set justified, hyphenated text [K].

**2. Headlines**
- Globe and Mail: sentence case, which is the Canadian Press norm. "A majority of established media outlets favour sentence case" [S].
- Le Devoir: French sentence case [K].
- Quebec guillemets (OQLF): put a regular space before «, a no-break space after «, a no-break space before », and a regular space after » [S].
- Quebec spacing before other punctuation: Canadian practice keeps a no-break space before `:`, but uses **no space** (or at most a thin space) before `; ! ?`. This differs from France [S/K].

**3. Masthead and dateline [K]**
- Globe and Mail: the line reads `CANADA'S NATIONAL NEWSPAPER · FOUNDED 1844 · THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026 · $4.xx`. The weekday masthead has been red since 2010 [S].
- Le Devoir: the motto is "Fais ce que dois". The line reads `Vol. CXVII · No 2xx · Le jeudi 8 octobre 2026 · x,xx $ + taxes` [K].

**4. Numbers and dates**
- English Canadian: `1,234.5`, `$1.2-billion` (Globe and CP use the hyphenated "-billion" in money) [K].
- French Canadian: `1 234,5 $`, with $ after the number and a no-break space [S]. Decimal comma [S]. Amounts are written `1,2 milliard de dollars`.
- Dates: `Thursday, October 8, 2026` and `jeudi 8 octobre 2026` [K].

**5. Front-page structure [K]**
- Globe and Mail: glossy cover since 2010 [S]. One big photo and lead, an index rail, and a Report on Business promo.
- Le Devoir: text-heavy, with the editorial cartoon (Garnotte) and the day's "Libre opinion" teasers.

## TW: 聯合報 and 自由時報 (sources weakest here)

**1. Text setting [K]**
- Taiwanese newspapers mix vertical (直排) and horizontal (橫排) setting on the same page. Long body text and front-page stories are traditionally vertical; boxes, graphics and charts are horizontal. 自由時報 uses more horizontal blocks than 聯合報. I could not confirm the current proportions [searched; not found].
- Taiwanese books are still mostly vertical [S].
- Body face is 明體 (Ming, serif). Text sits on a character grid, justified, with no inter-word spaces.
- Punctuation is full-width and **centred in the character frame** in Taiwan and Hong Kong practice, unlike the corner-placed punctuation of mainland China [S].
- Taiwan follows its own rules, not the mainland standard GB/T 15834 [S].
- Kinsoku applies: no line starts with 、，。」』 or similar characters.

**2. Headlines**
- The main head is large and heavy (黑體 or 粗明), with 副標 (subheads).
- Quotes are 「…」 first and 『…』 for nested quotes. Book and newspaper titles take 《…》, and article titles take 〈…〉 [S, academic style guides; standard Taiwanese MoE usage].
- Full-width punctuation sits with half-width digits and Latin [S, 行政院 文書處理手冊].

**3. Masthead and dateline [K]**
- The 報頭 (nameplate) is calligraphic, top right or top left.
- Date format: `中華民國115年10月8日 星期四`. Also shown are the Gregorian year (2026), sometimes the lunar date (`農曆八月廿八日`), the issue number, the price (`零售每份 新臺幣xx元`) and the edition.
- Ads in the nameplate area on page 1 are common (報頭下 ads) [S, about 2002].

**4. Numbers and dates**
- Statistics use Arabic numerals with 萬 and 億 units: `6億3,944萬2,789元`, `1萬500元`, `新臺幣1千元`. A comma groups four digits inside a unit group [S, 行政院 公文書橫式書寫數字使用原則].
- Newspapers commonly write `3.5億元` or `新台幣1200億元` [K].
- In vertical text, numbers may switch to Chinese digits (一二〇〇) or use upright Arabic numerals [K].
- Year: 民國 for official items, Gregorian more often in news text (自由時報 uses 西元 widely) [K].

**5. Front-page structure [K]**
- The headline story is often opinion-led. 自由時報 is known for front pages that take a strong editorial stance, for example a two-day front-page campaign on the services trade pact [S].
- Large display ads on page 1 are normal [S].

# Apply to DailyDrop: per-edition checklist

## Baseline for every edition

- Set `<html lang>` per edition (`ko`, `en-US`, `ja`, `en-GB`, `de`, `fr`, `en-IN`, `en-AU`, `en-CA` or `fr-CA`, `zh-Hant-TW`). Hyphenation and quote marks depend on it.
- Format all numbers and dates through `Intl.NumberFormat` and `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with the edition locale. Never build separators by hand.
- Body text uses `text-align: justify` with a fallback of `text-align-last: start`. Allow justification only when the column is at least about 28 characters wide (Latin) or about 12 characters (CJK). On narrow phones, fall back to `start`, because rivers are worse than a ragged edge.
- `quotes:` should follow the table below. Use `<q>` or the `::before`/`::after` content `open-quote` and `close-quote` so the marks come from CSS.
- Headlines: `text-wrap: balance`. Do not add a trailing period.

## Per-edition rules

**KR**
- Body: `text-align: justify; word-break: normal` for print-like syllable breaks. Use `keep-all` if you prefer words kept whole on screen; print does not do this.
- Turn off hyphens.
- Body face is a 명조 serif, for example Noto Serif KR or 본명조, at about 1.7 line-height. Headlines use heavy 고딕.
- Numbers: units with no spaces, as in `3조2000억원` and `1만2000명`. Write a custom formatter: `Intl` compact notation gives `1.2만`, which is not newspaper style.
- Date line: `2026년 10월 8일 목요일` + ` · 제N호`. Optionally add `(음력 M월 D일)`; 조선일보 practice is unverified.

**US**
- `hyphens: auto; text-align: justify` with a serif body face (Imperial-like, for example Georgia or Source Serif).
- Headlines in title case: capitalise words of four or more letters, and all words except a, an, the, and, but, or, for, nor, at, by, in, of, on, to, up, as.
- Dateline under the nameplate: `VOL. CLXXVI … No. N` | `NEW YORK, THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026` | price. Volume is in Roman numerals and the date in caps.
- Optional WSJ-style market strip with ▲▼ and %.
- Numbers: `en-US`, written `1,234.5` and `$1.2 billion`.

**JP**
- `text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-character; line-break: strict;` plus `hanging-punctuation: allow-end` (Safari only) and `text-spacing-trim: normal`.
- Use `palt` only on headlines.
- Optional authentic mode: `writing-mode: vertical-rl` for the lead story, with `text-combine-upright: digits 2`.
- Body face is 明朝 (Noto Serif JP or Shippori Mincho). Headlines use heavy ゴシック or 明朝.
- Numbers: `1兆2000億円` and `1万3000人`, with no commas inside unit groups. Write a custom formatter.
- Date line: `2026年(令和8年)10月8日 木曜日`. Use `Intl.DateTimeFormat('ja-JP-u-ca-japanese', {era:'long', year:'numeric'})` for the era. Add `第N号` and `朝刊`.

**GB**
- `hyphens: auto; justify`.
- Headlines in sentence case.
- Date line: Guardian style is `Thursday 8 October 2026` (no commas), and Times style is `Thursday October 8 2026`. Pick one; Guardian-style day-month matches `en-GB` `Intl` output once the comma is removed.
- Price `£`. Use `bn` and `m` in headlines.

**DE**
- `hyphens: auto` is mandatory with `lang="de"`. Justify.
- Headlines left-aligned in sentence form, with a small overline (Dachzeile) above.
- Numbers: `de-DE`, written `1.234,5` and `4,20 €`.
- Date line: `Donnerstag, 8. Oktober 2026 · Nr. 234 · 4,20 €`, with middle-dot separators.
- Fraktur only in the nameplate, if at all.

**FR**
- `hyphens: auto; justify`.
- Insert U+202F before `; ! ?` and inside « ». Insert U+00A0 before `:`. Do this with a text pre-processor; CSS cannot add these spaces.
- Numbers: `fr-FR` `Intl` already outputs U+202F for thousands, giving `1 234,5 €`.
- Date line in small caps: `JEUDI 8 OCTOBRE 2026 · 82E ANNÉE · NO N · 3,80 €`. In body text, dates are lowercase.

**IN**
- `en-IN` `Intl` gives `1,50,000`. Format ₹ amounts as lakh and crore (`₹1.2 lakh crore`) and foreign amounts as million and billion.
- Headlines in sentence case.
- Date line: `NEW DELHI, THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026 · Vol. N No. N · ₹10`.

**AU**
- Justify and hyphenate. Headlines in sentence case with single quotes.
- Date line: `THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026` (newspaper style, unverified). Note that `en-AU` `Intl` gives `8 October 2026`, so override the format.
- Use `$1.2bn` in headlines.

**CA**
- en: sentence case, `en-CA` numbers, `$1.2-billion` in money (CP style).
- fr: `fr-CA`, written `1 234,5 $`. NBSP inside « » and before `:`, and **no** space before `; ! ?`; this differs from the FR edition.
- Date lines: `THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8, 2026` and `le jeudi 8 octobre 2026`.

**TW**
- `text-align: justify; text-justify: inter-character; line-break: strict`.
- Use full-width punctuation, centred. Avoid fonts that place punctuation in the corner (the mainland China style). Choose a Traditional Chinese TC font, for example Noto Serif TC.
- Quotes 「」『』, book titles 《》.
- Numbers: Arabic numerals with 萬 and 億, as in `3.5億元` and `新台幣1,200億元`. Write a custom formatter; `Intl` compact notation gives `1.2萬`.
- Date line: `中華民國115年10月8日 星期四` via `Intl.DateTimeFormat('zh-TW-u-ca-roc')`, plus `2026` and optionally the lunar date via `-u-ca-chinese`.
